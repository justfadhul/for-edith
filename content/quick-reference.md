> [!NOTE]
> This page is a memory aid for the ward. Always check doses against the **Uganda Clinical Guidelines 2023**, your unit protocol and a senior before prescribing. Doses are for adults with normal renal function unless stated.

## 1. Magnesium sulphate (MgSO₄)

**Strength check:** 50% MgSO₄ = **0.5 g per mL** → 10 mL = **5 g**. To make **20%** for IV use: **8 mL of 50% (4 g) + 12 mL water for injection = 20 mL**.

| Regimen | Loading dose | Maintenance | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pritchard (IM), the Uganda standard** | **4 g of 20% IV over 5–10 min** + **10 g of 50% IM** (5 g deep IM into each buttock, each with **1 mL of 2% lignocaine**) = 14 g | **5 g of 50% IM every 4 h**, alternate buttocks (+ 1 mL 2% lignocaine) | **24 h after delivery or last fit** (whichever is later) |
| **Zuspan (IV)** | **4 g IV over 5–20 min** | **1 g/h IV infusion** | 24 h after delivery or last fit |
| **Recurrent fit** | **2 g of 20% IV over 5 min** | Continue regimen | Think of another cause if fits continue |
| **Fetal neuroprotection (<32 weeks)** | **4 g IV over 20–30 min** | **1 g/h** | Until birth or max 24 h |
| **At HC II/III before referral** | Full loading dose (IM 10 g ± IV 4 g) | Send the next 5 g IM dose with the patient | Due 4 h after loading |

> [!DRUG]
> **Before every dose (hourly on infusion): "R-R-U"**
> - **R**espiratory rate **≥16/min**
> - **R**eflexes (patellar) **present**
> - **U**rine output **≥30 mL/h** (≥100 mL in 4 h)
>
> If RR <16 or reflexes absent → **withhold**. If urine <30 mL/h → withhold/halve. Respiratory arrest → ventilate + **calcium gluconate 1 g (10 mL of 10%) IV over 10 min**.

| Serum Mg²⁺ | Effect |
|---|---|
| 2–3.5 mmol/L (4.8–8.4 mg/dL) | Therapeutic |
| 4–5 mmol/L (~10–12 mg/dL) | Loss of patellar reflexes |
| 6–7.5 mmol/L (~15–17 mg/dL) | Respiratory depression |
| >12 mmol/L (>30 mg/dL) | Cardiac arrest |

**If no MgSO₄:** diazepam 10 mg IV slowly over 2 min (repeat once if fits recur); rectal 20 mg if no IV access.

## 2. Antihypertensives in pregnancy

**Treat urgently if BP ≥160/110** (within 30–60 min). Target **140–150/90–100** acutely; **~135/85** for maintenance. Do not drop diastolic below 80.

### Acute (severe hypertension)

| Drug | Dose | Route | Repeat | Max / notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nifedipine IR** | **10 mg** | Oral (swallow, not sublingual) | After 20–30 min if still ≥160/110 (10–20 mg) | ~30–40 mg in the first hour. Headache, flushing, tachycardia |
| **Hydralazine** | **5 mg** (5–10 mg) | **IV slowly** over 2–5 min | Every 20–30 min | **20 mg IV** per episode (WHO: or 12.5 mg IM 2-hourly). Tachycardia, headache, hypotension → fetal distress |
| **Labetalol** | **20 mg** | IV over 2 min | Then **40, 80, 80 mg** every 10 min | **300 mg** total. Avoid in asthma. Rarely available in Uganda |

### Maintenance (oral)

| Drug | Dose | Frequency | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Methyldopa** | 250–750 mg | 8-hourly (max 3 g/day) | Slow onset; **not for acute use**; switch postpartum (depression) |
| **Nifedipine MR (retard)** | 20–40 mg | 12-hourly (max 120 mg/day) | Common in Uganda |
| **Labetalol** | 100–400 mg | 8–12-hourly (max 2.4 g/day) | Avoid in asthma |
| **Amlodipine** | 5–10 mg | Once daily | Also good postpartum |
| **Enalapril** (postpartum only) | 5–20 mg | Once–twice daily | **Contraindicated antenatally**; safe in breastfeeding |

> [!REDFLAG]
> **Avoid in pregnancy**: ACE inhibitors, ARBs (fetal renal damage), atenolol (FGR), diuretics (except pulmonary oedema). **Never give ergometrine** to a hypertensive woman.

## 3. Uterotonics and PPH drugs

| Drug | Prevention (AMTSL) | Treatment of PPH | Contraindications / notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Oxytocin** (first line) | **10 IU IM** (or IV slowly) within 1 min of birth | **10 IU IV slowly** + infusion **20–40 IU in 1 L NS/RL** (e.g. 20 IU in 1 L at 60 drops/min, then 40 drops/min) | Store cool (2–8°C). Max about 3 L of oxytocin-containing fluid (water intoxication) |
| **Carbetocin (heat-stable)** | **100 mcg IM or slow IV** single dose | Not for treatment | Heat-stable; WHO-recommended alternative for prevention |
| **Ergometrine** | 0.2 mg IM | **0.2 mg IM or slow IV**; repeat 0.2 mg IM after 15 min, then 4-hourly if needed | **Max 5 doses (1 mg)/24 h**. **CI: hypertension, pre-eclampsia, heart disease**. Refrigerate |
| **Misoprostol** | **600 mcg orally** (where oxytocin is unavailable; community distribution) | **800 mcg sublingually** | Shivering and fever common. Heat-stable |
| **Tranexamic acid (TXA)** | Not routine | **1 g IV over 10 min** within **3 h of birth**; **second 1 g** if bleeding continues after 30 min or restarts within 24 h | WOMAN trial. Useless after 3 h. For any PPH cause |
| Carboprost (15-methyl PGF2α) | | 250 mcg IM every 15 min, max 8 doses (2 mg) | CI asthma. Rarely available |
| Calcium gluconate | | 1 g IV over 10 min | For MgSO₄ toxicity (and massive transfusion) |

> [!UGANDA]
> **WHO 2023 / E-MOTIVE bundle for PPH**: measure blood loss with a **calibrated drape**; trigger at **≥500 mL** (WHO 2023), or at **≥300 mL + any abnormal vital sign** (WHO/FIGO/ICM 2025 consolidated guideline); then immediately and together: **E**arly detection, **M**assage the uterus, **O**xytocic drugs, **T**ranexamic acid, **IV** fluids, **E**xamination (genital tract, placenta) and **E**scalation.

## 4. Antenatal corticosteroids

| Drug | Dose | Route | Frequency | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dexamethasone** (first choice in Uganda) | **6 mg** | IM | **12-hourly** | **4 doses** (24 mg) |
| Betamethasone | **12 mg** | IM | **24-hourly** | 2 doses (24 mg) |

**WHO 2022 conditions:** **24–34 weeks** (Uganda: usually 28–34, lower at NICU centres); **accurate GA**; birth likely **within 7 days**; **no maternal infection**; **adequate childbirth care**; **adequate newborn care** (resuscitation, thermal care, KMC, feeding, infection treatment, oxygen/CPAP). One repeat course allowed if ≥7 days since the first and still at high risk. Not routine ≥34 weeks.

**Tocolysis (only to buy 48 h for steroids/transfer):** **nifedipine 20 mg orally, then 10–20 mg every 6–8 h for up to 48 h**. Not with chorioamnionitis, APH, fetal distress/death, severe PE, or ≥34 weeks. Avoid betamimetics (salbutamol).

## 5. Misoprostol regimens by indication

| Indication | Gestation | Regimen |
|---|---|---|
| **Incomplete miscarriage** | <13 weeks | **600 mcg orally** once, or **400 mcg sublingually** once |
| **Missed miscarriage** (early fetal demise) | <14 weeks | **WHO 2022 preferred: mifepristone 200 mg orally, then misoprostol 800 mcg vaginally/sublingually 1–2 days later.** Misoprostol alone (FIGO): **800 mcg vaginally** (or 600 mcg sublingually); repeat after 3 h if needed (max 2 doses) |
| Cervical priming before MVA / surgical evacuation | <13 weeks | **400 mcg sublingually 1 h** or **vaginally 3 h** before the procedure |
| Termination (legal indication) / incomplete abortion with a uterus ≥13–14 weeks | **13–24 weeks** | **400 mcg vaginally, sublingually or buccally every 3 h** until expulsion (WHO 2022) |
| Fetal death | **14–28 weeks** | **400 mcg vaginally/sublingually every 4–6 h** (WHO 2022, ideally after mifepristone 200 mg) or **200 mcg every 4–6 h** (FIGO). Use the lower dose nearer 28 weeks (FIGO: **100 mcg every 4 h at 27–28 weeks**) and with a uterine scar |
| Fetal death | **>28 weeks** | **25 mcg vaginally every 6 h** or **25 mcg orally every 2 h** (FIGO). WHO MCPC: if no response after 2 doses, 50 mcg every 6 h; **never more than 50 mcg per dose** |
| **Induction of labour, live fetus at term** | ≥37 weeks | **25 mcg vaginally every 6 h** or **25 mcg orally every 2 h** (oral solution) |
| **PPH prevention** | After birth | **600 mcg orally** |
| **PPH treatment** | After birth | **800 mcg sublingually** |

> [!REDFLAG]
> **Previous caesarean section / uterine scar**: avoid misoprostol for induction in the third trimester (uterine rupture). Lower doses and extreme caution in the second trimester. Always stop if there is **tachysystole** (>5 contractions in 10 min) or fetal distress.

## 6. Antibiotic regimens common in obstetrics

| Indication | Regimen | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Puerperal sepsis / maternal sepsis / septic abortion** | **Ampicillin 2 g IV 6-hourly + gentamicin 5 mg/kg IV once daily + metronidazole 500 mg IV 8-hourly** (alternative: ceftriaxone 1–2 g IV daily + metronidazole 500 mg IV 8-hourly) | IV until afebrile for 48 h (WHO MCPC), then oral (e.g. amoxicillin + metronidazole) to complete about 7 days in total; longer if an abscess or complicated infection, per local protocol. Evacuate retained products; drain pus |
| **PPROM** | **Erythromycin 250 mg orally 6-hourly** | **10 days** or until labour. **Avoid co-amoxiclav** (NEC) |
| **Intrapartum GBS prophylaxis** (preterm labour, ROM >18 h, fever, previous GBS baby) | **Benzylpenicillin 3 g (5 MU) IV**, then **1.5–1.8 g (2.5–3 MU) IV 4-hourly**; or **ampicillin 2 g IV then 1 g 4-hourly**; allergy: **clindamycin 900 mg IV 8-hourly** | Until delivery |
| **Chorioamnionitis** | **Ampicillin 2 g IV 6-hourly + gentamicin 5 mg/kg IV daily** (+ metronidazole 500 mg IV 8-hourly if CS) | **Deliver**. Stop after vaginal birth if well; after CS continue until 48 h afebrile |
| **Caesarean section prophylaxis** | **Cefazolin 2 g IV** (3 g if >120 kg) or **ampicillin 2 g IV**, single dose **15–60 min before skin incision** (Uganda often uses ceftriaxone 1–2 g IV) | Single dose (repeat if >4 h surgery or blood loss >1.5 L) |
| **Pyelonephritis in pregnancy** | Admit; **ceftriaxone 1–2 g IV daily** (or ampicillin + gentamicin) | Until afebrile 24–48 h, then oral to complete **10–14 days**; urine culture; repeat culture after treatment |
| Asymptomatic bacteriuria / cystitis | **Nitrofurantoin 100 mg 12-hourly** (avoid at term) or **amoxicillin 500 mg 8-hourly** (if sensitive), or cefalexin 500 mg 8–12-hourly | 5–7 days |
| Syphilis (early) | **Benzathine penicillin 2.4 MU IM** single dose (late/unknown duration: weekly × 3) | Treat partner too |
| Manual removal of placenta / 3rd–4th degree tear | Single dose ampicillin 2 g IV (or cefazolin) ± metronidazole 500 mg IV | Single dose |

## 7. Anti-D immunoglobulin (Rh-negative, non-sensitised women)

| Situation | Dose | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| **After delivery of an Rh-positive baby** | **Uganda (usual vial): 1,500 IU (300 mcg) IM**. **UK: 500 IU IM + Kleihauer** to guide extra doses | **Within 72 hours** (some benefit up to 10 days) |
| Routine antenatal prophylaxis (where available) | **1,500 IU (300 mcg) IM** at **28 weeks** (single-dose regimen), or **500 IU at 28 and 34 weeks** (UK two-dose regimen) | |
| Sensitising event **before 20 weeks** (surgical evacuation, ectopic, molar pregnancy, miscarriage ≥12 weeks, APH, trauma) | **UK: 250 IU (50 mcg) IM** (before 12 weeks only after surgical evacuation, ectopic, molar pregnancy or heavy/repeated bleeding). **Uganda**: the vial usually stocked is **1,500 IU (300 mcg) IM**, which is also acceptable | Within 72 h |
| Sensitising event **20 weeks or more** (APH, ECV, abdominal trauma, IUFD, amniocentesis) | **UK: 500 IU IM + Kleihauer** to guide extra doses. **Uganda (usual vial): 1,500 IU (300 mcg) IM** | Within 72 h |

> [!UGANDA]
> Anti-D is expensive and not always stocked in public facilities. Check the blood group at booking, counsel Rh-negative women early, and **plan purchase before delivery**. Check the baby's cord blood group.

## 8. ANC supplements and prevention (Uganda)

| Intervention | Regimen | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Iron + folic acid** | **Ferrous sulphate 200 mg (60 mg elemental) + folic acid 400 mcg** once daily throughout pregnancy and 3 months postpartum | Anaemia treatment: **120 mg elemental daily**. Take apart from calcium and tea |
| **Folic acid (preconception)** | 400 mcg daily (**5 mg** if previous NTD, epilepsy on drugs, sickle cell) | Start 3 months before conception. **Avoid 5 mg folic acid with SP** (antagonises SP) |
| **IPTp-SP** | **3 tablets of SP (sulfadoxine 500 mg/pyrimethamine 25 mg each) as directly observed therapy** | From **13 weeks**, then **monthly** (at least 4 weeks apart) until delivery; aim ≥3 doses. **Not** in the first trimester. **Not with cotrimoxazole** (HIV+) |
| **Mebendazole** | 500 mg single dose | Second trimester |
| **Calcium** | 1.5–2 g elemental daily from 20 weeks | Pre-eclampsia prevention (low-calcium diets) |
| **Aspirin** | 75–150 mg at night from 12 to 36 weeks | High risk of pre-eclampsia |
| **ITN** | Sleep under a treated net every night | |

### Td (tetanus-diphtheria) schedule for women of childbearing age

| Dose | When | Protection |
|---|---|---|
| **Td1** | At first contact / first ANC visit | None |
| **Td2** | **At least 4 weeks after Td1** | ~1–3 years |
| **Td3** | **At least 6 months after Td2** | ~5 years |
| **Td4** | **At least 1 year after Td3** | ~10 years |
| **Td5** | **At least 1 year after Td4** | Through the childbearing years (probably longer) |

Give Td2 at least 2 weeks before delivery for newborn protection against neonatal tetanus.

## 9. Bishop score

| Score | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dilatation** (cm) | Closed | 1–2 | 3–4 | ≥5 |
| **Effacement** (%) or cervical length | 0–30% (>3 cm) | 40–50% (2 cm) | 60–70% (1 cm) | ≥80% (0 cm) |
| **Station** (relative to ischial spines) | −3 | −2 | −1, 0 | +1, +2 |
| **Consistency** | Firm | Medium | Soft | |
| **Position** | Posterior | Mid | Anterior | |

**Interpretation:** **≥8** favourable (ARM + oxytocin likely to succeed); **≤6** unfavourable (needs cervical ripening: misoprostol or Foley balloon).

## 10. APGAR score

| Sign | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A**ppearance (colour) | Blue or pale all over | Body pink, extremities blue | Pink all over |
| **P**ulse (heart rate) | Absent | <100/min | ≥100/min |
| **G**rimace (reflex irritability) | No response | Grimace | Cry, cough, sneeze |
| **A**ctivity (tone) | Limp | Some flexion | Active movement |
| **R**espiration | Absent | Slow, irregular, weak cry | Good, strong cry |

Score at **1 and 5 minutes** (and 10 if <7). **7–10** normal; **4–6** moderately depressed; **0–3** severely depressed. **Do not wait for the APGAR to start resuscitation**: follow Helping Babies Breathe (dry, stimulate, clear airway if needed, **ventilate within the Golden Minute** if not breathing).

## 11. Glasgow Coma Scale

| Eye opening (E) | Verbal (V) | Motor (M) |
|---|---|---|
| 4 Spontaneous | 5 Oriented | 6 Obeys commands |
| 3 To voice | 4 Confused | 5 Localises pain |
| 2 To pain | 3 Inappropriate words | 4 Withdraws from pain |
| 1 None | 2 Incomprehensible sounds | 3 Abnormal flexion (decorticate) |
| | 1 None | 2 Extension (decerebrate) |
| | | 1 None |

Total 3–15. **≤8 = protect the airway** (intubate/recovery position, call anaesthetist).

## 12. Shock index and haemorrhage recognition

**Shock index = heart rate ÷ systolic BP.**

| Obstetric shock index | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0.7–0.9 | Normal in pregnancy/postpartum |
| **≥0.9** | **Abnormal**: significant blood loss likely; escalate |
| **≥1.4** | Needs **urgent intervention** (resuscitation, blood) |
| **≥1.7** | **High risk of adverse outcome** (critical) |

> [!PEARL]
> Pregnant women compensate well: **BP falls late** (after ~30% blood volume loss). Tachycardia, a rising shock index, pallor, cold peripheries, anxiety and reduced urine output come first. **Rule of 30**: SBP fall of 30 mmHg, HR rise of 30, RR >30, Hb/haematocrit fall of 30%, urine <30 mL/h → about 30% blood volume lost (moderate shock).

## 13. Obstetric danger signs (teach every mother)

| During pregnancy | During labour | After delivery (mother) | Newborn |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vaginal bleeding** | Labour >12 hours | **Heavy bleeding** (soaking a pad in <5 min) | Not feeding / unable to suck |
| **Severe headache, blurred vision** | Bleeding | **Fever**, foul-smelling lochia | Convulsions |
| **Convulsions / fits** | Leaking green/brown liquor | Severe abdominal pain | Fast breathing (≥60/min), chest indrawing |
| **Fever**, very weak | Cord or hand coming out first | Convulsions, severe headache | Hot (≥37.5°C) or cold (<35.5°C) |
| **Severe abdominal pain** | Fits, severe headache | Breathlessness, calf pain/swelling | Yellow palms/soles, or jaundice in the first 24 h |
| **Fast or difficult breathing** | Fever | Painful swollen breasts, perineal wound pus | Umbilical redness/pus |
| **Reduced or absent fetal movements** | | Feeling very sad, thoughts of harm | Lethargy, floppy |
| **Leaking liquor** (water breaking) | | | |
| **Swelling of face and hands** | | | |

## 14. Normal laboratory values in pregnancy

| Test | Normal in pregnancy | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| **Haemoglobin** | **≥11 g/dL** (≥10.5 in 2nd trimester) | Anaemia <11; moderate 7–9.9; **severe <7** (WHO) |
| White cell count | 6–16 × 10⁹/L (up to ~25–30 in labour/early puerperium) | Steroids raise WBC for 72 h |
| **Platelets** | 150–400 × 10⁹/L (gestational thrombocytopenia usually >100) | <100 = think HELLP/ITP/DIC |
| **Creatinine** | **35–70 µmol/L** (0.4–0.8 mg/dL) | >~77–80 µmol/L is suspicious in pregnancy; >90 µmol/L is clearly abnormal |
| Urea | 2–4.5 mmol/L | Falls because GFR rises ~50% |
| Uric acid | Falls in early pregnancy, rises near term | High in pre-eclampsia |
| ALT / AST | Unchanged or slightly lower (<~35 IU/L) | Raised = abnormal (HELLP, AFLP, hepatitis) |
| **Alkaline phosphatase** | **Up to 2–4× normal** | Placental origin: do not over-interpret |
| Albumin | Falls (~28–37 g/L) | Haemodilution |
| **Fibrinogen** | **Rises to 4–6 g/L** | **<2 g/L in PPH = severe coagulopathy** |
| PT / APTT | Unchanged or slightly shorter | |
| **Fasting glucose** | **<5.1 mmol/L** | GDM (WHO 2013, 75 g OGTT): fasting ≥5.1, 1 h ≥10.0, 2 h ≥8.5 |
| TSH | Lower in 1st trimester (~0.1–2.5 mU/L) | Use trimester-specific ranges |
| PaCO₂ | Lower (~3.7–4.2 kPa / 28–32 mmHg) | Respiratory alkalosis compensated by lower bicarbonate (18–22 mmol/L) |
| ESR, D-dimer, CRP | Raised | Poor diagnostic tests in pregnancy |
| Proteinuria | <300 mg/24 h; PCR <30 mg/mmol | Above = significant |
| **BP** | Falls in 2nd trimester, back to baseline by term | ≥140/90 hypertension; ≥160/110 severe |
| Heart rate | Rises by 10–15 bpm | >100 at rest: look for a cause |

## 15. Fetal skull diameters

```
Presentation          Diameter (engaging)          Length
-------------------------------------------------------------
Vertex, well flexed   Suboccipitobregmatic         9.5 cm
Vertex, partly flexed Suboccipitofrontal          10.0 cm
Vertex, deflexed/OP   Occipitofrontal             11.5 cm
Brow                  Mentovertical               13.5 cm  <- too big: CS
Face (mentoanterior)  Submentobregmatic            9.5 cm
Face (partly extended)Submentovertical            11.5 cm

Transverse:  Biparietal 9.5 cm   Bitemporal 8.0 cm
Shoulders:   Bisacromial 12 cm (reduces to ~9.5 cm with compression)
```

**Landmarks:** anterior fontanelle (bregma) = diamond, 4 sutures; posterior fontanelle (lambda) = triangle (Y), 3 sutures. **Denominator**: vertex = occiput; face = mentum (chin); breech = sacrum; shoulder = acromion.

## 16. Key definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Abortion / miscarriage** | Pregnancy loss **before 28 weeks** in Uganda (WHO: <22 weeks or <500 g) |
| **Stillbirth** | Baby born with no signs of life at **≥28 weeks** (WHO international comparison); Uganda MPDSR uses ≥28 weeks |
| **Preterm birth** | Before **37+0 weeks** (extremely <28, very 28–<32, moderate/late 32–<37) |
| **Term** | 37+0 to 41+6 weeks (early term 37–38+6; full term 39–40+6; late term 41–41+6) |
| **Post-term** | **≥42+0 weeks** |
| **Low birth weight** | **<2,500 g** (VLBW <1,500 g; ELBW <1,000 g) |
| **PROM / PPROM** | Rupture of membranes before labour at ≥37 / <37 weeks |
| **Prolonged rupture of membranes** | >18 hours (UK >24 h) |
| **Primary PPH** | Blood loss **≥500 mL within 24 h** of birth (any route); **severe ≥1,000 mL**; or any loss causing haemodynamic compromise. WHO/FIGO/ICM 2025: also diagnose at **≥300 mL plus any abnormal vital sign** |
| **Secondary PPH** | Abnormal bleeding from **24 h to 12 weeks** postpartum (WHO: up to 6 weeks) |
| **Hypertension in pregnancy** | ≥140/90 on two occasions; **severe ≥160/110** |
| **Anaemia in pregnancy** | Hb <11 g/dL; severe <7 g/dL |
| **Fever** | ≥38.0°C |
| **Prolonged labour** | Active phase >12 h (Uganda) or crossing the action line; prolonged second stage: **WHO 2018 / Labour Care Guide ≥3 h nulliparous, ≥2 h parous**; NICE/RCOG-style (and older ACOG) teaching, common on Ugandan wards, uses >2 h primigravida (>3 h with epidural) and >1 h multipara (>2 h with epidural) |
| **Puerperium** | 6 weeks after delivery |
| **Maternal death** | Death during pregnancy or **within 42 days** of its end, from any cause related to or aggravated by pregnancy (not accidental). Late maternal death: 42 days to 1 year |
| **Perinatal death** | Stillbirth + early neonatal death (first 7 days) |
| **Neonatal period** | First **28 days** of life |
| **Maternal mortality ratio** | Maternal deaths per **100,000 live births** (Uganda ~189, UDHS 2022) |

## 17. Partograph rules (WHO modified partograph, used in Uganda)

| Item | How often | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Start the partograph** | When in **active labour** (**cervix ≥4 cm** on the modified partograph) | Plot the first VE **on the alert line** |
| **Cervical dilatation** | **4-hourly** VE (more often if indicated) | Plot as "X" |
| **Descent** (fifths palpable abdominally) | 4-hourly (before each VE) | Plot as "O" |
| **Fetal heart rate** | **Every 30 min** in the first stage; **every 5 min** (after each contraction) in the second stage | Normal 110–160 bpm |
| **Contractions** | **Every 30 min** (count over 10 min) | Record frequency and duration (<20 s, 20–40 s, >40 s) |
| Liquor and moulding | With each VE | C clear, I intact, M meconium, B blood, A absent; moulding 0, +, ++, +++ |
| Maternal pulse | **Every 30 min** | |
| BP | **4-hourly** | |
| Temperature | **2-hourly** | |
| Urine (volume, protein, acetone) | Every void | |

| Line | Definition | Action if crossed |
|---|---|---|
| **Alert line** | Starts at **4 cm**, slopes at **1 cm/hour** | At HC II/III: **refer** to a facility with CS capability. At hospital: reassess (contractions, position, CPD, bladder), increase monitoring |
| **Action line** | Parallel, **4 hours to the right** of the alert line | **Full assessment by a clinician**: exclude obstruction/CPD. If no CPD and membranes intact → **ARM**; if contractions inadequate → **oxytocin augmentation** (not in a grand multipara without senior review, and never with obstruction). If CPD, obstruction or fetal distress → **caesarean section** |

> [!NOTE]
> **WHO Labour Care Guide (2020)**, being introduced in Uganda, defines active labour from **5 cm**, drops the alert/action lines, and uses **time-at-each-centimetre alerts**: 5 cm ≥6 h, 6 cm ≥5 h, 7 cm ≥3 h, 8 cm ≥2.5 h, 9 cm ≥2 h; second stage ≥3 h (nulliparous) or ≥2 h (parous). Other alerts: FHR <110 or ≥160, late decelerations, pulse <60 or ≥120, SBP <80 or ≥140, DBP ≥90, temperature <35.0 or ≥37.5°C, contractions ≤2 or >5 per 10 min.
