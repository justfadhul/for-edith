## In a nutshell

A **preterm** baby is born before **37 completed weeks** of gestation. A **low birth weight (LBW)** baby weighs **less than 2,500 g** at birth, whatever the gestation. In Uganda most LBW babies are preterm, but many are **small-for-gestational-age (SGA)** term babies who grew poorly in utero (maternal malnutrition, malaria, hypertension, HIV, smoking, twins).

Why it matters: WHO estimates **13.4 million babies were born preterm in 2020**, and **prematurity is the leading cause of death in children under 5** worldwide. In Uganda, prematurity, birth asphyxia and infection together cause most neonatal deaths, and small babies are over-represented in all three.

The good news is that the interventions that save small babies are **simple and cheap**: **warmth (Kangaroo Mother Care)**, **breast milk**, **clean hands and cord care**, **early recognition and treatment of infection**, **safe oxygen and bubble CPAP**, and **caffeine**. The WHO 2022 recommendations for care of the preterm or LBW infant put **immediate KMC** and **family involvement** at the centre of care.

> [!PEARL]
> The one thing never to forget: a small baby's four killers are **cold, hunger (hypoglycaemia), infection and breathing problems**. On every ward round, ask about each one: temperature, feeds and glucose, signs of sepsis, and apnoea/respiratory distress.

## Definitions & classification

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Preterm** | Born **before 37 completed weeks** (before 259 days) |
| Extremely preterm | Below **28 weeks** |
| Very preterm | **28 to below 32 weeks** |
| Moderate to late preterm | **32 to below 37 weeks** (late preterm = 34-36+6) |
| **Low birth weight (LBW)** | Birth weight **below 2,500 g** |
| **Very low birth weight (VLBW)** | **Below 1,500 g** |
| **Extremely low birth weight (ELBW)** | **Below 1,000 g** |
| **Small for gestational age (SGA)** | Birth weight **below the 10th centile** for gestation and sex (INTERGROWTH-21st or Fenton charts) |
| Appropriate for GA (AGA) | 10th-90th centile |
| Large for GA (LGA) | Above the 90th centile (think maternal diabetes) |
| **Corrected (adjusted) age** | Chronological age minus weeks born early; use it for growth and development until **2 years** |
| **Postmenstrual age (PMA)** | Gestational age at birth + chronological age (used for ROP screening and caffeine decisions) |

**Two kinds of small baby** (they behave differently):

| Feature | Preterm AGA | Term SGA (growth-restricted) |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | Thin, shiny, red skin, lanugo, soft ear cartilage, few sole creases, little breast tissue | Wasted, dry peeling skin, loose skin folds, "old man" face, alert, good sole creases |
| Main problems | RDS, apnoea, poor suck, hypothermia, NEC, IVH, jaundice, ROP | **Hypoglycaemia** (low glycogen), **polycythaemia**, hypothermia, asphyxia, meconium aspiration |
| Feeding | Often cannot suck; NGT/cup | Usually sucks well; hungry |

> [!EXAM]
> "Is every LBW baby preterm?" No. A 2.1 kg baby at 39 weeks is LBW but **term and SGA**. Always state both **gestation** and **weight-for-gestation** (e.g. "32 weeks, 1.45 kg, AGA").

## Epidemiology & Uganda context

- About **1 in 10 babies worldwide is born preterm**; sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia carry most of the burden and most of the deaths.
- In Uganda, prematurity, intrapartum-related events (asphyxia) and infection are the three leading causes of neonatal death. Small babies who survive the first week remain at risk of infection, poor growth and neurodevelopmental problems.
- **Causes of preterm birth** you will see at Mengo/Mulago/Kawempe: spontaneous preterm labour (often with infection: UTI, malaria, chorioamnionitis), **PPROM**, **multiple pregnancy**, **pre-eclampsia** (iatrogenic delivery), APH, maternal HIV, severe anaemia, teenage pregnancy, poor ANC.
- **Facilities**: most regional referral hospitals and many HC IVs now have a **KMC unit** and a newborn unit with oxygen; **bubble CPAP** is available at national and many regional referral hospitals and some private-not-for-profit hospitals (Mengo, Nsambya, Lubaga); **surfactant**, mechanical ventilation and ROP laser are available only in a few centres (mainly Kampala).
- The biggest gains come from things you can do anywhere: keeping the baby warm, starting breast milk on day 1, hand-washing, and referring very small babies **before** they are cold and hypoglycaemic.

> [!UGANDA]
> UCG 2023 asks HC III/IV to look after babies **1.5-2.5 kg** or "up to 1 month early" (extra care, KMC, cup feeding) and to **refer urgently** babies **below 1.5 kg or below 32 weeks** to hospital, ensuring extra warmth (KMC) during transfer. In practice, Kampala hospitals receive many very small babies from district facilities; a well-warmed baby with a glucose check and a first dose of antibiotics travels much better.

## Pathophysiology: why small babies get into trouble

| Problem | Why it happens | What you do about it |
|---|---|---|
| **Hypothermia** | Large surface area for weight, thin skin (high evaporative loss), little brown fat and subcutaneous fat, cannot shiver, extended posture | Dry, skin-to-skin/KMC, hat, warm room (at least 25 C), delay bathing |
| **Hypoglycaemia** | Small glycogen and fat stores (laid down in the third trimester), high brain-to-body ratio, delayed feeds, cold and sepsis use glucose | Early feeds (within 1 h), 2-3-hourly feeds, glucose checks, IV 10% dextrose if needed |
| **Respiratory distress syndrome (RDS)** | **Surfactant deficiency** (type II pneumocytes mature from about 24 weeks, adequate from about 34-35 weeks); alveoli collapse at end-expiration, so the work of breathing rises and oxygenation falls | Antenatal steroids, CPAP, oxygen, surfactant where available |
| **Apnoea of prematurity** | Immature brainstem respiratory control; worse with cold, sepsis, hypoglycaemia, anaemia, reflux, feeding | Caffeine, gentle stimulation, monitor, treat the cause |
| **Feeding problems** | Suck-swallow-breathe coordination matures at about **32-34 weeks**; small stomach; slow gut motility | NGT/cup feeds of expressed breast milk, slow advancement |
| **Necrotising enterocolitis (NEC)** | Immature gut mucosa + formula + ischaemia + bacterial overgrowth | Breast milk, cautious advancement, stop feeds if distension |
| **Infection** | Low transplacental IgG (mostly crosses after 32 weeks), thin skin, invasive procedures, long admission | Hand hygiene, KMC, breast milk, minimal lines |
| **Jaundice** | Immature liver conjugation, shorter red cell life, slower gut transit | Lower phototherapy thresholds |
| **Intraventricular haemorrhage (IVH)** | Fragile germinal matrix vessels (below 32 weeks), fluctuating blood pressure | Gentle handling, avoid rapid boluses and hypercapnia |
| **Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP)** | Retinal vessels are incomplete; high or fluctuating oxygen drives abnormal vessel growth | Target SpO2 **90-95%**, screen at-risk babies |
| **Anaemia of prematurity** | Low iron stores, blood sampling, low erythropoietin | Iron from 2 weeks, minimise sampling, transfuse if symptomatic |
| **Metabolic bone disease** | Calcium and phosphate are mostly transferred in the third trimester | Breast milk, vitamin D, mineral supplements in VLBW |

> [!NOTE]
> Antenatal **dexamethasone** (6 mg IM 12-hourly × 4 doses to the mother) induces surfactant production and reduces RDS, IVH, NEC and death when given under the WHO 2022 conditions (24-34 weeks, accurate dating, birth likely within 7 days, no maternal infection, adequate newborn care). See the Preterm Labour, PROM & PPROM topic.

## Clinical acumen

### Focused history

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Gestation: LNMP, early scan, or Ballard score?** | Decides expected problems (below 32 weeks: RDS, apnoea, IVH, ROP; 34-36: feeding and jaundice) |
| **Birth weight and current weight** | Classifies LBW/VLBW; percentage weight loss guides feeds |
| **Antenatal steroids? How many doses, when?** | A full course more than 24 h before birth lowers RDS risk |
| **Why was the baby born early?** PPROM, fever, foul liquor, pre-eclampsia, APH, twins | PPROM > 18 h, maternal fever or foul liquor = **sepsis risk**; pre-eclampsia → SGA, hypoglycaemia, neutropenia |
| **Resuscitation at birth?** | Asphyxia adds HIE, hypoglycaemia, feeding problems |
| **Maternal HIV status, last viral load, syphilis, HBsAg** | Decides NVP or AZT + NVP prophylaxis, syphilis treatment, HepB birth dose |
| **Feeding**: breastfeeding, cup, NGT? Volume and frequency? Any other fluids? | Under-feeding causes hypoglycaemia; pre-lacteal feeds and formula raise NEC and infection risk |
| **Temperature**: how kept warm? KMC hours? | Hypothermia is the most common, most preventable problem |
| **Apnoeas, colour change, "stops breathing"?** | Apnoea of prematurity vs sepsis/hypoglycaemia/seizure |
| **Urine and stool passed?** | Hydration, gut function (NEC, obstruction) |
| **Immunisation**: BCG, OPV0 (HepB birth dose where available) | WHO: small babies get scheduled vaccines at birth/by discharge |
| **Mother's health and support** | KMC needs a well mother or a family member; who will help at home? |

### Examination

- **Weigh** (naked, same scale), **length**, **head circumference**; plot on a preterm chart (INTERGROWTH-21st or Fenton). Classify AGA/SGA.
- **Temperature** (axillary; low-reading thermometer), **respiratory rate** (normal 30-60/min; count a full minute), **heart rate** (100-160/min), **SpO2**, capillary refill.
- **Respiratory distress**: grunting, nasal flaring, intercostal/subcostal recession, tachypnoea, cyanosis; use the **Silverman-Andersen score** if your unit uses it.
- **Signs of sepsis**: lethargy, poor suck, temperature instability, apnoea, mottling, abdominal distension, jaundice, bulging fontanelle.
- **Abdomen**: distension, tenderness, visible loops, discoloration, bilious aspirates (NEC).
- **Skin**: jaundice (blanch over the nose/sternum), pallor, pustules, cord.
- **Neurology**: tone, posture, activity, suck, fontanelle (IVH, meningitis).
- **Maturity**: Ballard features (skin, lanugo, plantar creases, breast bud, ear, genitalia; posture, square window, scarf sign). See the Examination of the Newborn topic.

### Red flags

> [!REDFLAG]
> - Temperature **below 35.5 C** or above 37.5 C, or a baby who stays cold despite KMC
> - **Apnoea** (pause over 20 s, or shorter with bradycardia/cyanosis), especially recurrent or new after day 3 (think sepsis)
> - **Grunting, severe chest indrawing, SpO2 below 90%**, or rising oxygen need
> - **Not feeding**, vomiting, **abdominal distension, bilious aspirates, blood in stool** (NEC)
> - **Lethargy, floppy, convulsions**, bulging fontanelle
> - **Jaundice on day 1** or yellow palms/soles
> - **Glucose below 2.6 mmol/L**, especially if recurrent
> - Weight loss **above 10-15%** or no weight gain after day 7-10

### Thinking like a clinician

- **A sick preterm baby has sepsis until proven otherwise.** Apnoea, temperature instability, feed intolerance and lethargy are non-specific. Check glucose and temperature, then start **ampicillin + gentamicin** after a blood culture where possible.
- **New apnoea after the first few days, in a baby who was stable, is not "just prematurity".** Think sepsis, NEC, meningitis, hypoglycaemia, anaemia, IVH.
- **Respiratory distress in a preterm baby:** within 4 h of birth and worsening over 24-48 h = **RDS**; with maternal fever/PPROM = **pneumonia/sepsis** (cannot be separated clinically, so treat both); in a late preterm born by CS without labour = **transient tachypnoea**; sudden collapse on CPAP = **pneumothorax**.
- **An SGA term baby is a different animal**: feed early and check glucose; he is at risk of hypoglycaemia and polycythaemia, not RDS.
- **Do not chase weight before warmth.** A cold baby does not gain weight and does not tolerate feeds. Fix temperature first.
- **Count fluids as total fluids** (IV + feeds). Students often add full IV fluids on top of feeds and drown the baby.
- **Oxygen is a drug.** Use a pulse oximeter, target **90-95%**, wean as soon as possible.

## Clinical workup

### Step 0: First 5 minutes

1. **At birth**: dry, keep warm, assess breathing; resuscitate per HBB (size **0** mask for small babies; room air or 21-30% oxygen below 35 weeks). Delayed cord clamping (at least 1 min) if breathing.
2. **Temperature** now. If below 36.5 C, start **skin-to-skin/KMC** (or incubator/radiant warmer) and recheck in 30-60 min.
3. **Breathing**: count RR, look for grunting/recession, check **SpO2**. If distressed: oxygen by nasal prongs to SpO2 **90-95%**, consider **bubble CPAP**.
4. **Glucose**: check at **1-2 h of age** (or now if unwell). If below **2.6 mmol/L**: feed or give **10% dextrose 2 mL/kg IV** (see below).
5. **Danger signs** (not feeding, convulsions, lethargy, temp below 35.5 or above 37.5, RR above 60, severe indrawing, cyanosis, apnoea): admit, IV access, **antibiotics**, call for senior help.
6. **Below 1.5 kg or below 32 weeks at HC III/IV**: stabilise (warmth, glucose/feed, first antibiotic dose if indicated) and **refer in KMC** with a note.

### Step 1: Focused history

- [ ] **Gestation** (LNMP, early scan, fundal height, Ballard): decides expected problems and the caffeine/ROP plan.
- [ ] **Steroids, MgSO4** (neuroprotection below 32 weeks), antibiotics in labour: lowers RDS/IVH risk; changes sepsis risk.
- [ ] **Sepsis risk factors**: ROM > 18 h, maternal fever > 38 C, foul liquor, maternal UTI: start antibiotics for at least 48 h and reassess.
- [ ] **Resuscitation and Apgar**: HIE risk.
- [ ] **Maternal HIV (last VL), syphilis, HBsAg, blood group**: prophylaxis and jaundice risk.
- [ ] **Feeding so far**: when, what, how much: hypoglycaemia risk, NEC risk with formula.
- [ ] **Twin?** Other twin's condition; discharge both together (UCG).
- [ ] **Social**: mother's health, distance from facility, support for KMC at home.

### Step 2: Focused examination

- **General**: activity, colour (pink, pale, blue, yellow), posture, cry.
- **Vitals**: T, RR, HR, SpO2, CRT.
- **Growth**: weight, length, OFC on preterm chart; % weight loss from birth.
- **Respiratory**: grunting, flaring, recession, breath sounds; apnoea episodes on chart.
- **Abdomen**: distension, tenderness, bowel sounds, cord.
- **Neuro**: tone, suck, fontanelle.
- **Maturity**: Ballard score.
- **Document negatives**: "no grunting, no apnoeas in 24 h, abdomen soft, no jaundice".

### Step 3: Bedside tests

| Test | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **Glucose (glucometer)** | Below 2.6 mmol/L = treat; check before feeds 3-6-hourly until stable on feeds |
| **Temperature** (low-reading thermometer) | Hypothermia grade |
| **Pulse oximetry** | SpO2 target 90-95% on oxygen; below 90% in air = oxygen |
| **Weight** daily (same scale) | Losses up to 10-15% in VLBW in week 1; regain birth weight by 10-14 days (later in VLBW); then aim for about **15 g/kg/day** |
| **Gastric aspirate** before feeds (if NGT) | Bilious or more than half the previous feed = feed intolerance (think NEC/sepsis) |
| **Transcutaneous bilirubin** (if available) | Jaundice screening |
| **HemoCue Hb / PCV** | Anaemia (below 10-12 g/dL symptomatic) or polycythaemia in SGA (venous PCV above 65%) |

### Step 4: Laboratory & imaging

| Test | When | Expected / abnormal finding | How it changes management | Availability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Blood culture** | Before antibiotics in any suspected sepsis | Organism and sensitivities (Klebsiella, S. aureus, E. coli common) | Narrow or change antibiotics; duration | Regional/national referral, Mengo, Nsambya |
| **FBC** | Suspected sepsis, pallor, SGA | WBC below 5 or above 20 × 10^9/L, platelets below 100, Hb, PCV | Supports sepsis; transfusion; partial exchange for polycythaemia | HC IV and above |
| **CRP** | Sepsis work-up | Above 10 mg/L at 18-24 h supports infection | Two normal CRPs help stop antibiotics at 36-48 h | Referral hospitals, private labs |
| **Serum bilirubin** | Visible jaundice, especially day 1 or preterm | Compare with gestation-specific thresholds | Phototherapy / exchange | Referral hospitals |
| **Electrolytes, calcium** | VLBW on IV fluids, seizures | Hypo/hypernatraemia, hypocalcaemia | Adjust fluids; calcium gluconate | Referral hospitals |
| **Lumbar puncture** | Sepsis with seizures, bulging fontanelle, or positive culture | Raised WBC, low glucose | Meningitis = 21 days IV antibiotics | Hospital |
| **Chest X-ray** | Respiratory distress | RDS: ground-glass, air bronchograms, low volumes; pneumothorax; pneumonia | CPAP/surfactant; chest drain | Most hospitals |
| **Abdominal X-ray** | Suspected NEC | Pneumatosis intestinalis, portal gas, free air | Nil by mouth, antibiotics, surgical referral | Most hospitals |
| **Cranial ultrasound** | Below 32 weeks, seizures, falling Hb | IVH, PVL | Prognosis, follow-up | Kampala tertiary centres |
| **ROP eye exam** | At-risk babies (see below) | Stage, zone, plus disease | Laser/anti-VEGF | Few centres (Kampala) |

### Step 5: Putting it together

**Model summary line:** "Baby girl of Nakato, day 3 of life, born at **32 weeks** (by early scan) to a 19-year-old para 1 after preterm labour with **ROM for 20 hours**; mother received 2 doses of dexamethasone. Birth weight **1.45 kg** (VLBW, AGA), today **1.38 kg** (5% loss). She is in KMC, temperature 36.6 C, RR 58 with mild recession on bubble CPAP 5 cm H2O in 25% oxygen, SpO2 93%, had two apnoeas yesterday, and is on EBM 4 mL 2-hourly by NGT plus IV 10% dextrose. Mother HIV-negative at ANC, syphilis negative; BCG and OPV0 given."

**Problem list:** (1) VLBW very preterm infant; (2) RDS on CPAP; (3) apnoea of prematurity vs sepsis; (4) risk of early-onset sepsis (ROM 20 h) on ampicillin + gentamicin, culture pending; (5) establishing enteral feeds; (6) thermal care in KMC; (7) jaundice/ROP screening plans; (8) family support.

**Worked example (feeds):** a 1.6 kg baby on day 3 needs **120 mL/kg/day × 1.6 = 192 mL/day**. Given 2-hourly (12 feeds) that is **16 mL per feed**; 3-hourly (8 feeds) **24 mL per feed**. If she is also on IV fluids, subtract the feed volume from the total: e.g. feeds 10 mL × 12 = 120 mL, so IV = 192 − 120 = **72 mL/day = 3 mL/h**.

## Differential diagnosis

For the small baby with **respiratory distress**:

| Condition | Distinguishing features | Key investigation |
|---|---|---|
| **RDS** | Preterm, onset within 4 h, worsening over 24-48 h, grunting | CXR: ground-glass, air bronchograms |
| **Transient tachypnoea of the newborn** | Late preterm/term, CS without labour, mild, improves in 24-72 h | CXR: fluid in fissures, hyperinflation |
| **Pneumonia/sepsis** | PPROM, maternal fever, foul liquor; temperature instability | Culture, CRP, FBC; CXR patchy |
| **Pneumothorax** | Sudden deterioration (often on CPAP), asymmetric chest | Transillumination, CXR |
| **Congenital heart disease** | Cyanosis out of proportion to distress, murmur, poor pulses | Pre/post-ductal SpO2, echo |
| **Hypothermia / hypoglycaemia** | Cold, jittery, tachypnoea that settles with warmth/glucose | Temperature, glucose |

For the small baby with **apnoea**: apnoea of prematurity (diagnosis of exclusion, below 34 weeks, starts day 1-3), sepsis/meningitis, hypoglycaemia, hypothermia/hyperthermia, seizures, IVH, NEC, anaemia, reflux/feeding, maternal drugs (MgSO4, opioids).

## Investigations

| Test | What you are looking for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Glucose | Below 2.6 mmol/L | Commonest treatable cause of lethargy, apnoea, seizures |
| Temperature | Hypothermia | Drives apnoea, hypoglycaemia, acidosis |
| Blood culture, FBC, CRP | Sepsis | Guides antibiotic choice and duration |
| Bilirubin | Hyperbilirubinaemia | Lower thresholds in preterm |
| CXR | RDS, pneumonia, pneumothorax | Respiratory support decisions |
| AXR | NEC | Surgical vs medical |
| Cranial US | IVH/PVL | Prognosis and follow-up |
| ROP screen | Retinopathy | Treat before retinal detachment |

## Management

### 1. Emergency flow for the small baby on admission

1. **Warm**: KMC or incubator/radiant warmer; hat, socks; room at least 25 C.
2. **Airway/breathing**: position, gentle suction only if needed; oxygen by nasal prongs (0.5-1 L/min) to SpO2 90-95%; **bubble CPAP** for RDS (start 5-6 cm H2O, up to 7-8).
3. **Circulation**: CRT, HR; IV access (peripheral or umbilical vein).
4. **Glucose**: if below 2.6 mmol/L give **10% dextrose 2 mL/kg IV** over 5 min, then maintenance; recheck in 30 min.
5. **Antibiotics** if any sepsis risk or danger sign: **ampicillin + gentamicin** (doses below).
6. **Vitamin K** if not given; **chlorhexidine** cord care; **tetracycline eye ointment** if not given.
7. **Feeds**: start EBM as early as possible (day 1), even small volumes.
8. **Caffeine** for babies below 34 weeks with apnoea (and to consider for prevention).
9. **Document and communicate**: weight, plan, family counselling.

### 2. Thermal care and Kangaroo Mother Care

| Temperature (axillary) | Classification (WHO) | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 36.5-37.5 C | Normal | Continue KMC/warm care |
| 36.0-36.4 C | Cold stress (mild) | Skin-to-skin/KMC, warm room, cover head, recheck in 1 h |
| 32.0-35.9 C | Moderate hypothermia | KMC or incubator/radiant warmer, check glucose, feed, look for sepsis |
| Below 32 C | Severe hypothermia | Rapid rewarming (incubator/radiant warmer), IV 10% dextrose, antibiotics, oxygen, monitor closely |

**How to do KMC** (WHO 2022):
1. Baby naked except nappy, hat and socks, placed **upright, skin-to-skin between the mother's breasts**, head turned to one side, slightly extended.
2. Secure with a binder/wrap; cover mother and baby with her clothes or a blanket.
3. Continue **day and night** (at least **8-24 h/day**, as many hours as possible); fathers and relatives can take turns.
4. Start **immediately after birth** (iKMC) once the baby can tolerate handling; WHO 2022 now recommends this even for babies still needing oxygen or CPAP where the unit can support it.
5. Breastfeed in the KMC position; monitor temperature 4-6-hourly at first.
6. Mother sleeps propped up at 15-30 degrees.

> [!PEARL]
> KMC reduces mortality, hypothermia, sepsis and length of stay and improves breastfeeding and bonding. It is not a "second-best incubator"; for stable small babies it is **better** than an incubator.

### 3. Feeding the small baby

**Method by maturity (approximate):**

| Gestation / weight | Usual feeding method |
|---|---|
| 34 weeks or more / usually 2.0 kg or more | **Breastfeed** directly; support positioning |
| 32-34 weeks / about 1.5-2.0 kg | Breast + **cup or paladai** with expressed breast milk (EBM) |
| Below 32 weeks / below about 1.5 kg | **Nasogastric/orogastric tube** EBM ± IV fluids; move to cup/breast as suck matures |
| Very sick, distended abdomen, shocked, frequent seizures | **Nil by mouth**, IV fluids; trophic feeds once stable |

**Fluid and feed volumes (total = IV + enteral) (WHO Pocket Book 2013):**

| Day of life | Total fluids (mL/kg/day) |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | **60** |
| Day 2 | **90** |
| Day 3 | **120** |
| Day 4 onwards | **150** (up to **180** after some days if tolerating feeds) |

- Give feeds **every 2-3 hours** (8-12 feeds/day). Example: **1 kg baby on 150-180 mL/kg/day = 19-23 mL 3-hourly; 1.5 kg = 28-34 mL 3-hourly** (WHO).
- **Below 1.5 kg**: day 1 start **10 mL/kg/day** EBM (or 2-4 mL every 2 h by NGT if well) with the remaining ~50 mL/kg/day as IV 10% dextrose. Increase feeds by **1-2 mL per feed per day** (Pocket Book) or **up to 30 mL/kg/day** (WHO 2022). Aim for full enteral feeds within **5-7 days** so the drip can come out.
- **IV fluid**: 10% dextrose for the first 2 days, then a sodium-containing solution (e.g. 10% dextrose with 0.18% saline, or half-strength Darrow's per unit protocol). **Do not exceed 100 mL/kg/day IV** unless dehydrated or under phototherapy/radiant heater (Pocket Book). Use a burette; check hourly.
- Add **10-20%** to fluids under phototherapy or radiant warmers.
- **Feed intolerance**: vomiting, distension, aspirates more than half of the feed or bilious → reduce/stop feeds, examine for NEC/sepsis.
- **Milk**: **mother's own milk** first; **donor human milk** if not available (where a milk bank exists); **preterm formula** only if neither (WHO 2022). Help the mother express by hand within 1 h of birth and every 2-3 h (8 times a day) to build supply.
- Weight gain target after regaining birth weight: about **15-20 g/kg/day**.

> [!UGANDA]
> Uganda has few human milk banks (the first opened at Nsambya in 2021), so in most units **mother's own EBM** is the norm, with formula used reluctantly when the mother is very ill or absent. Expressing support (privacy, a chair, a clean cup, praise) is a real clinical intervention.

### 4. Apnoea of prematurity and caffeine

- **Apnoea** = cessation of breathing for **20 seconds or more**, or shorter with **bradycardia (HR below 100)** or desaturation/cyanosis.
- Common below **34 weeks**, especially below 30 weeks; typically starts day 1-3 and resolves by **34-37 weeks PMA**.
- Immediate response: **gentle stimulation** (rub the back/soles); if no breathing, **bag-mask ventilation**; check glucose and temperature; consider sepsis.
- Monitor with a pulse oximeter with the alarm on, or an apnoea monitor.

| Drug | Dose | Route | Frequency | Duration / notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Caffeine citrate** (first choice) | Loading **20 mg/kg**; maintenance **5 mg/kg** (may increase by 5 mg/kg steps to max **20 mg/kg/day**; many units use 5-10 mg/kg/day) | Oral or IV over 30 min | Maintenance **once daily**, starting 24 h after the load | Continue until **34-35 weeks PMA** and **5-7 days apnoea-free** (WHO: 4-5 days after apnoea stops). Watch tachycardia (above 180), jitteriness, feed intolerance. Note: 20 mg caffeine citrate = 10 mg caffeine base |
| **Aminophylline** (if caffeine unavailable) | Loading **6 mg/kg** IV over 20 min; maintenance **2.5 mg/kg** (week 1), 4 mg/kg (weeks 2-4) | IV/oral | **12-hourly** | Narrower safety margin: tachycardia, vomiting, seizures |

WHO 2022: caffeine is **recommended for treatment** of apnoea in preterm infants, **for extubation** below 34 weeks, and **may be considered for prevention** of apnoea below 34 weeks.

### 5. Respiratory distress syndrome and CPAP

1. **Minimal handling**, thermal care, nil by mouth or trophic feeds initially; IV fluids.
2. **Oxygen** by nasal prongs to keep SpO2 **90-95%** (not higher).
3. **Bubble CPAP** (WHO 2022 strong recommendation for RDS; may be considered immediately after birth for babies below 32 weeks): start **5-6 cm H2O**, FiO2 to target SpO2, nasal prongs of the right size, NGT on free drainage for air, protect the nasal septum.
4. **Antibiotics** (ampicillin + gentamicin) because pneumonia cannot be excluded; stop at 48 h if culture negative and baby well.
5. **Surfactant** (via ETT, or less-invasive methods) where available, usually if FiO2 needs to exceed about 0.30-0.40 on CPAP.
6. **Escalate** if apnoea, rising FiO2 above 0.4-0.6, worsening work of breathing, or pneumothorax: refer for ventilation where possible.
7. Expect improvement after **48-72 h** as the baby makes surfactant.

> [!UGANDA]
> Bubble CPAP made from locally available materials is used in many Ugandan units. The main dangers are **unblended 100% oxygen** (ROP, lung injury) and **nasal trauma**. Use an oxygen blender or air-oxygen mixing and a pulse oximeter.

### 6. Infection prevention and treatment

- **Hand hygiene** before and after every touch; mothers and visitors too. KMC and breast milk reduce infection.
- **Chlorhexidine 7.1% digluconate** to the cord (Uganda policy) and nothing else.
- Antibiotics for risk factors or signs (doses per kg, see Common Neonatal Disorders topic):

| Drug | Dose | Route | Frequency | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ampicillin** | **50 mg/kg** | IV/IM | **12-hourly** in week 1; **8-hourly** weeks 2-4 | 100 mg/kg/dose if meningitis |
| **Gentamicin** | **3 mg/kg** (LBW, WHO; UCG 4 mg/kg preterm); **5 mg/kg** (2.5 kg or more) in week 1; **7.5 mg/kg** weeks 2-4 | IV/IM | **Once daily** (every 24 h; some units 36-48-hourly below 30 weeks) | Stop at 48 h if well and cultures negative; 7-10 days for sepsis |

### 7. Jaundice in the preterm

Preterm babies get jaundice more often, later (peak day 5-7) and at lower bilirubin levels become dangerous. Use **gestation-specific thresholds** (NICE CG98 graphs; WHO Pocket Book: lower thresholds "for preterm below 35 weeks or any risk factor"). Phototherapy any visible jaundice on day 1. See Common Neonatal Disorders.

### 8. Supplements and routine care

| Supplement | Dose | Route | When / duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vitamin K** | **0.5 mg** (Uganda/local); WHO **0.4 mg/kg** (max 1 mg) for preterm; 1 mg term | IM | Once at birth |
| **Vitamin D** | **400 IU** | Oral | Daily once on full feeds (WHO 2022: may be considered) |
| **Iron (ferrous sulphate/elemental iron)** | **2-4 mg/kg/day elemental iron** | Oral | From **2 weeks** (or when on full feeds) until **6 months** (WHO) |
| **Multivitamins / calcium and phosphate** | Per unit protocol (Pocket Book: Ca 120-140 mg/kg, P 60-90 mg/kg/day for VLBW) | Oral | Once on full feeds |
| **Folic acid** | Per unit protocol | Oral | Commonly given in Ugandan units |
| **Emollient/topical oil** | Thin layer (WHO 2022: may be considered) | Skin | Daily |
| **Probiotics** | WHO 2022: may be considered for human-milk-fed babies below 32 weeks | Oral | Where a quality product is available |

**Immunisation:** give **BCG and OPV0 at birth/before discharge** regardless of weight (WHO: small babies receive scheduled vaccines on time by chronological age), then the UNEPI schedule from **6 weeks chronological age** (not corrected age).

### 9. Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP)

- **Who to screen**: international criteria are **below 31-32 weeks or below 1.5 kg**. In LMICs (including Uganda) babies up to **34 weeks or 2.0 kg** develop severe ROP when oxygen is unmonitored, so many programmes use **wider criteria**, plus any baby who had **unmonitored oxygen**.
- **When**: first exam at **4 weeks after birth or 31 weeks PMA, whichever is later**, then 1-2-weekly until vessels mature.
- **Prevention**: **SpO2 90-95%**, blenders, avoid fluctuations, good nutrition, treat sepsis.
- **Treatment**: laser photocoagulation or intravitreal anti-VEGF for treatment-requiring ROP, ideally within 48-72 h of diagnosis.

> [!UGANDA]
> ROP screening in Uganda is limited to a few Kampala centres and outreach programmes. If you are in a unit without screening, **write the plan in the discharge summary** ("ROP screen due at 4 weeks") and refer the baby to the nearest eye unit.

### 10. Discharge and follow-up

**Discharge criteria (WHO/UCG):**
- No danger signs or signs of infection
- **Feeding well** on breast (or cup), gaining weight on **3 consecutive days**
- Temperature normal in KMC/cot for **3 consecutive days**
- **Mother confident** to care for the baby (KMC, feeding, danger signs)
- Vaccines due given; HIV prophylaxis plan clear

**Follow-up:**
- **Weekly** weight and feeding review until **3 kg** (WHO); continue KMC at home until the baby wriggles out (usually around 2.5 kg or 40 weeks PMA).
- **Growth** on preterm charts then WHO charts using **corrected age** to 2 years; weight-for-age, length-for-age, weight-for-length Z-scores.
- **Development** (corrected age): CP, hearing (especially after jaundice, gentamicin, meningitis), vision (ROP, squint).
- **Anaemia**: Hb at 6-8 weeks; continue iron.
- **Immunisation** on chronological age.
- Teach **danger signs**: not feeding, cold, fast/difficult breathing, apnoea, convulsions, yellow palms/soles, fever.

## Complications

| Short-term | Long-term |
|---|---|
| Hypothermia, hypoglycaemia | Cerebral palsy, cognitive and learning difficulties |
| RDS, pneumothorax, apnoea | **Bronchopulmonary dysplasia** (oxygen need at 36 weeks PMA) |
| Sepsis, meningitis | **ROP** → blindness |
| NEC, feeding intolerance | Hearing loss |
| Jaundice, kernicterus | Growth faltering, metabolic bone disease |
| IVH, PVL | Anaemia of prematurity |
| PDA | Behavioural problems, re-admissions |
| Electrolyte imbalance, fluid overload | Family stress, maternal depression |

## Ward-round & exam pearls

### Model presentation

"This is **Baby Nakato**, a **9-day-old** female born at **31+2 weeks** by early scan, now **32+4 weeks PMA**. Birth weight **1.30 kg (VLBW, AGA)**, today **1.27 kg**, having regained 40 g in the last 3 days. She was born after preterm labour with **ROM for 22 hours**; mother had **one dose of dexamethasone**. She needed **bag-mask ventilation for 1 minute**, Apgar 5 and 8.

She had **RDS**, treated with **bubble CPAP for 4 days**, now in **room air** with SpO2 95%. She is on **caffeine citrate 5 mg/kg daily**, last apnoea 3 days ago. Blood culture grew nothing; she completed **5 days of ampicillin and gentamicin**. She is on **EBM 150 mL/kg/day by NGT**, 24 mL 3-hourly, with some cup feeds, no aspirates. She is in **KMC 20 h/day**, temperature 36.6-37.2. Phototherapy for 2 days for jaundice, now stopped.

Mother is **HIV-negative** (retested in labour), RPR negative. **BCG and OPV0 given.** Weight-for-age on the INTERGROWTH chart is on the 25th centile.

**Assessment:** a stable, growing VLBW very preterm infant, problems: (1) feeding progression; (2) apnoea of prematurity on caffeine; (3) needs ROP screening at 4 weeks; (4) anaemia and bone health supplementation; (5) discharge planning with KMC at home.

**Plan:** advance to cup feeds, start iron at 2 weeks and vitamin D, continue caffeine to 34-35 weeks PMA, ROP referral, check Hb weekly, discharge when criteria met."

### Viva questions (with answers)

1. **Define LBW, VLBW and ELBW.** Below 2,500 g, below 1,500 g, below 1,000 g at birth.
2. **Why do preterm babies get cold?** Large surface-area-to-weight ratio, thin skin with high evaporative loss, little brown and subcutaneous fat, extended posture, cannot shiver.
3. **What is KMC and who should get it?** Continuous skin-to-skin contact plus exclusive breastfeeding plus early discharge with follow-up. WHO 2022: all preterm/LBW babies, started as soon as possible after birth, 8-24 h/day.
4. **How much milk does a 1.6 kg baby need on day 4?** 150 mL/kg/day = 240 mL/day = 30 mL 3-hourly.
5. **What is the caffeine regimen?** Caffeine citrate 20 mg/kg load, then 5 mg/kg daily (up to 20 mg/kg/day) from 24 h later, until about 34-35 weeks PMA and 5-7 days apnoea-free.
6. **What SpO2 do you target in a preterm baby?** 90-95%.
7. **Name four signs of NEC.** Abdominal distension, feed intolerance/bilious aspirates, blood in stool, abdominal wall discoloration; plus apnoea, lethargy, temperature instability.
8. **When can a small baby go home?** Feeding well, gaining weight 3 days, temperature stable 3 days, no danger signs, mother confident.

### OSCE tips

- **Counselling a mother on KMC** is a common OSCE station: introduce, explain why (warmth, breastfeeding, infection, bonding), demonstrate position and binder, how long, sleeping position, danger signs, hygiene, who can help, check understanding.
- **Calculating feeds** is a favourite: show your working (weight × mL/kg/day ÷ number of feeds).

- **I**nfection
- **R**espiratory (RDS, apnoea, BPD)
- **E**yes (ROP)
- **D**igestive (feeding, NEC)
- **B**rain (IVH, PVL)
- **A**naemia
- **B**ilirubin (jaundice)

### Classic exam traps

- **"Put the cold small baby in a bath of warm water / under a bulb."** → Wrong. Use **KMC** or an incubator/radiant warmer; do not bathe small babies.
- **"A 1.4 kg baby should go to the incubator, KMC is for when he is bigger."** → Wrong. WHO 2022: **immediate KMC** for all stable small babies; it reduces death.
- **"Give IV fluids at 150 mL/kg/day plus full feeds."** → Wrong. Count **total** fluids; IV = total − feeds.
- **"Keep SpO2 at 100% to be safe."** → Wrong. Target **90-95%**; hyperoxia causes **ROP** and lung injury.
- **"Apnoea on day 6 in a stable 30-weeker is just prematurity, increase caffeine."** → Wrong. **Rule out sepsis** (and NEC, hypoglycaemia, anaemia) first.
- **"Give formula because the mother's milk has not come in."** → Wrong. Colostrum is enough in small volumes; help her **express**; formula only when no mother's or donor milk.
- **"Vaccinate preterm babies at their corrected age."** → Wrong. Use **chronological age**; give BCG/OPV0 before discharge.
- **"SGA term baby: watch for RDS."** → The bigger risk is **hypoglycaemia** (and polycythaemia).

### Questions seniors ask

1. **"Is this baby preterm, SGA, or both?"** Plot weight against gestation (INTERGROWTH-21st/Fenton); below 10th centile = SGA.
2. **"What is his temperature and how are we keeping him warm?"** Give the number and the method (KMC hours/incubator).
3. **"What are his total fluids in mL/kg/day, and how are they split?"** E.g. "120 mL/kg/day: 60 enteral, 60 IV".
4. **"When will you stop caffeine?"** About 34-35 weeks PMA and 5-7 days apnoea-free.
5. **"Why is he on antibiotics, and when will you stop?"** Risk factor/sign; stop at 48 h if well and culture negative; 7-10 days if sepsis.
6. **"Does he need ROP screening?"** Yes if below 32 weeks or below 1.5 kg (wider in LMICs) or unmonitored oxygen; first exam at 4 weeks or 31 weeks PMA.
7. **"What will you tell the mother before discharge?"** KMC, exclusive breastfeeding, danger signs, weekly weighing, immunisation, follow-up for eyes, hearing, development.
