## In a nutshell

The **puerperium** is the period from the **delivery of the placenta until 6 weeks (42 days)** after birth. During it the genital tract **involutes**, pregnancy's physiological changes reverse, and **lactation** is established.

It matters because **most maternal deaths occur after birth**, and most of those in the **first 24–48 hours**: PPH, then sepsis, hypertensive disorders, VTE and, later, **suicide**. It is also the time when **newborn deaths** cluster, when breastfeeding succeeds or fails, and when a woman who doesn't want another pregnancy soon needs **contraception** (ovulation can return from about **3–4 weeks** in a woman who isn't breastfeeding).

> [!PEARL]
> The one thing never to forget: **"the woman is still a patient after the baby is born."** Check her vital signs, uterus, lochia, perineum, bladder, breasts, legs and mood, not just the baby.

## Definitions & classification

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Puerperium** | From delivery of the placenta to **6 weeks (42 days)** postpartum. **Immediate**: first 24 h; **early**: up to day 7; **remote/late**: up to 6 weeks |
| **Involution** | Return of the uterus (and genital tract) to its non-pregnant size and state |
| **Lochia** | Vaginal discharge after birth: blood, decidua, mucus, bacteria |
| **Puerperal pyrexia** (classic UK definition) | Temperature **≥38 °C** during the first **14 days** after birth (older definition: on any 2 of the first 10 days, excluding the first 24 h) |
| **Puerperal sepsis** (WHO) | Infection of the genital tract occurring between rupture of membranes or labour and the **42nd day postpartum**, with fever plus ≥1 of: **pelvic pain, abnormal vaginal discharge, abnormal smell of discharge, delayed uterine involution** |
| **Maternal sepsis** (WHO 2017) | **Life-threatening organ dysfunction** from infection during pregnancy, childbirth, post-abortion or the postpartum period |
| **Lactational amenorrhoea method (LAM)** | Temporary contraception from breastfeeding (see Management) |
| **Exclusive breastfeeding** | Only breast milk (no water, other liquids or foods) except medicines, vitamins, ORS, for **6 months** |

## Epidemiology & Uganda context

- Globally, **most maternal deaths and a large share of newborn deaths happen after birth**, clustered in the **first day and the first week**.
- **Postnatal care (PNC) coverage in Uganda lags behind ANC and facility delivery**: many women leave within hours of a normal birth and never return until the baby's first immunisation.
- **Puerperal sepsis** is a leading cause of maternal death in some Ugandan tertiary hospitals (**about 31% of deaths at Mulago** in one series). Risk factors are common locally: prolonged labour, multiple VEs, PROM, home births, CS, anaemia, HIV and poor hygiene.
- **Postpartum depression** is common in Uganda (studies report about 10–40% depending on the setting and tool), and usually undiagnosed.
- **Breastfeeding** is almost universal in Uganda, but **exclusive breastfeeding to 6 months** is achieved by only about two-thirds of infants, and **early initiation (within 1 hour)** is often missed after CS.
- **Unmet need for family planning** is highest in the postpartum year, and short birth intervals raise maternal and child mortality.

> [!UGANDA]
> Ugandan health workers use the **"6 hours, 6 days, 6 weeks"** postnatal schedule, aligned with WHO's four contacts (within 24 h, day 3, days 7–14, 6 weeks). The **Essential Maternal and Newborn Clinical Care Guidelines (MoH 2022)** and **UCG 2023** recommend that women stay in the facility for **at least 24 hours** after a normal delivery. In practice, beds are scarce and many are discharged at 6–12 hours. That is why **danger-sign counselling at discharge** is critical.

## Physiology

### The genital tract

| Structure | Change |
|---|---|
| **Uterus** | Weighs ~1000 g at delivery → ~500 g at 1 week → ~300 g at 2 weeks → **~50–100 g by 6 weeks**. The fundus is at the **umbilicus** just after delivery (firm, "cricket ball"), descends about **1 cm (one finger-breadth) per day**, and is **no longer palpable abdominally by day 10–14**. Mechanism: **autolysis** of myometrial cells (proteolytic enzymes reduce cell size, not number) and **ischaemia** from contraction |
| **Placental site** | Thrombosed vessels are gradually excluded by endometrial regrowth from the margins, without scarring. It is the last area to heal (about 6 weeks). Failure = **subinvolution** of the placental site (secondary PPH) |
| **Endometrium** | Regenerates from the basal layer by about day 10 (except the placental site, about 6 weeks) |
| **Cervix** | Soft and patulous after delivery; closes to admit one finger by about **the end of week 1**; the external os becomes a transverse slit (parous os) |
| **Vagina / perineum** | Oedema and bruising settle; rugae return from week 3; tears heal in about 1–2 weeks. Hypo-oestrogenic dryness while breastfeeding |
| **Afterpains** | Painful contractions during breastfeeding (oxytocin), worse in multiparas; they help involution |

**Lochia:**

| Type | When | Appearance |
|---|---|---|
| **Rubra** | Days 1–4 | Red (blood, decidua) |
| **Serosa** | About days 4–10 | Pink-brown (less blood, more serum and leucocytes) |
| **Alba** | About day 10 to 4–6 weeks | Yellow-white (leucocytes, mucus) |

> [!REDFLAG]
> **Offensive lochia**, **persistent red lochia beyond about 2 weeks**, a return of heavy bleeding, or a **uterus that is bigger, softer or more tender than expected** (subinvolution) point to **infection or retained products**.

### Other systems

| System | Change and clinical relevance |
|---|---|
| **Cardiovascular** | Cardiac output **rises immediately after delivery** (autotransfusion of about 500 mL from the contracted uterus and relief of caval compression), then falls towards normal over **2–6 weeks** (up to 12 weeks for full remodelling). **This is when women with heart disease (e.g. mitral stenosis) decompensate into pulmonary oedema.** |
| **Blood** | Physiological **diuresis on days 2–5** removes the extra plasma volume. Clotting factors and fibrinogen stay high, so the woman is **hypercoagulable for about 6 weeks**, and **VTE risk is highest in the first weeks** (especially after CS). Leucocytosis up to 20–25 × 10⁹/L is normal in the first days |
| **Urinary** | Bladder oedema and reduced sensation after labour/epidural lead to **urinary retention** (overflow). Dilated ureters return to normal by 6–8 weeks. **Always check that she has passed urine within 6 h of birth** |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Constipation (progesterone effect, fear of pain from the perineum, opioids, dehydration) |
| **Endocrine** | Placental hormones (hPL, oestrogen, progesterone, hCG) fall rapidly (hCG is undetectable in about 2–4 weeks). **Prolactin stays high while breastfeeding**. **Ovulation** returns in non-breastfeeding women as early as **day 25** (mean about 45 days), and is suppressed by frequent breastfeeding |
| **BP** | Often rises on **days 3–6** postpartum; pre-eclampsia (and eclampsia) can **first appear postpartum** (up to 6 weeks) |
| **Abdominal wall / skin** | Rectus diastasis, striae fade, linea nigra fades |
| **Weight** | About 5–6 kg is lost at delivery and a further 2–4 kg with diuresis |
| **Psychological** | Rapid hormonal shifts, sleep deprivation and the new role make this a vulnerable period for mood disorders |

### Lactation physiology

1. **Mammogenesis** (pregnancy): **oestrogen** → duct growth; **progesterone** → lobulo-alveolar growth; plus **prolactin, hPL**, cortisol and insulin.
2. **Lactogenesis I** (from mid-pregnancy): the alveoli can make **colostrum**, but high **progesterone blocks full milk secretion**.
3. **Lactogenesis II** ("milk coming in", about **day 2–4**): triggered by the **fall in progesterone after delivery of the placenta**, with prolactin already high. **Retained placental fragments** (progesterone) or **severe PPH (Sheehan's)** delay or prevent it; so can diabetes, obesity, stress and CS.
4. **Galactopoiesis** (maintenance): requires **prolactin** (from the anterior pituitary, released in response to **suckling** and higher at night) and **regular removal of milk**. A local protein, **feedback inhibitor of lactation (FIL)**, accumulates in a full breast and **reduces production**. So **"supply follows demand"**: the more the baby removes, the more is made.
5. **Milk ejection (let-down) reflex**: suckling → **oxytocin** (posterior pituitary) → contraction of **myoepithelial cells** → milk flows. It can be **conditioned** (the baby's cry) and is **inhibited by pain, anxiety, embarrassment**. Oxytocin also causes **afterpains** and helps involution.

| Milk | Features |
|---|---|
| **Colostrum** (days 1–3) | Small volume (a few mL per feed, matching the newborn's small stomach), yellow, **rich in IgA, lactoferrin, leucocytes, protein, vitamin A**; laxative (clears meconium); "the first immunisation" |
| **Transitional** (days 4–14) | Rising volume |
| **Mature milk** | **Foremilk** (watery, lactose-rich, quenches thirst) then **hindmilk** (fat-rich, calorie-dense). Let the baby finish one breast before switching |

**Why breastfeeding contraception (LAM) works:** frequent suckling → high prolactin and neural signals → **suppressed GnRH pulsatility** → low LH/FSH → no follicular development → **anovulation and amenorrhoea**. Long gaps between feeds (especially at night) or supplementary feeds weaken this.

## Clinical acumen

### Focused history: the postnatal check

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Date, mode and place of birth**; length of labour; ROM duration; number of VEs; perineal tear or episiotomy; CS indication; placenta complete? | Sepsis risk (prolonged labour, PROM, CS), retained products, wound problems |
| **Bleeding**: amount, colour and smell of lochia; clots | Secondary PPH, infection |
| **Fever, chills, abdominal/pelvic pain** | Endometritis, malaria, UTI, mastitis, wound infection |
| **Headache, visual disturbance, epigastric pain, swelling** | **Postpartum pre-eclampsia** |
| **Breathlessness, chest pain, calf pain or swelling** | **PE / DVT**, peripartum cardiomyopathy |
| **Passing urine?** Pain, frequency, **continuous leakage** | Retention, UTI, **obstetric fistula** (VVF after obstructed labour) |
| **Bowels**: constipation, faecal incontinence, flatus incontinence | Missed 3rd/4th-degree tear, RVF |
| **Perineal pain** | Haematoma, infection, wound breakdown |
| **Breastfeeding**: how often, attachment, nipple pain, breast lumps/redness | Breastfeeding problems, mastitis |
| **Mood**: sleep, anxiety, tearfulness, loss of interest, **thoughts of harming herself or the baby**, bonding | Blues, depression, psychosis |
| **Support at home**, domestic violence | Risk for depression and neglect |
| **Contraception plans**, return of sexual activity | Postpartum FP; ovulation before menses |
| **HIV status, ART adherence**, baby's prophylaxis | eMTCT |
| **Medical conditions**: hypertension, diabetes, heart disease, anaemia, TB | Follow-up and medication adjustments |

### Examination

1. **General**: pallor (anaemia), temperature, pulse, BP, RR, **mood and affect**.
2. **Breasts**: engorgement, nipple fissures, redness, a tender lump (mastitis/abscess). Watch a **breastfeed** (attachment and positioning).
3. **Abdomen**: **fundal height** (normal involution: at the umbilicus after birth, below it in the first days, impalpable by day 10–14), **tenderness**, bladder distension, **CS wound** (redness, discharge, dehiscence).
4. **Perineum** (with consent and a chaperone): swelling, haematoma, wound infection, breakdown, **lochia** (look and smell).
5. **Legs**: calf tenderness, swelling (DVT), oedema.
6. **Speculum/VE** only when indicated (retained products, suspected tear breakdown, fistula).

### Red flags (maternal danger signs after birth)

> [!REDFLAG]
> - **Heavy bleeding** (soaking more than 1 pad in an hour), passing large clots
> - **Fever ≥38 °C**, rigors, foul-smelling lochia, severe abdominal pain
> - **Severe headache, blurred vision, epigastric pain, convulsions**
> - **Difficulty breathing, chest pain**; **painful swollen calf**
> - **Tachycardia >100 with hypotension**, confusion (sepsis or bleeding)
> - Perineal or wound pain getting worse, discharge from the wound
> - **Thoughts of self-harm or harming the baby**, hallucinations, confusion, not sleeping for days
> - Continuous leakage of urine or faeces

### Thinking like a clinician

- **Fever in the puerperium: think by system.** Use the **"7 Ws"**: **W**omb (endometritis), **W**ind (chest infection/aspiration after GA), **W**ater (UTI/pyelonephritis), **W**ound (CS or perineum), **W**alking (DVT/PE), **W**eaning (breast engorgement/mastitis), **W**onder drugs (drug fever, transfusion reaction). **In Uganda add an eighth: "Where's the mosquito?" (malaria).** HIV-associated infections (TB, PCP) also belong here.
- **Timing clues**: day 1–2 fever with an ill mother and **no foul smell** → think **group A streptococcus** (can be fulminant) or aspiration; days 2–5 → endometritis, UTI; day 3–5 → engorgement (low-grade fever); days 5–10 → wound infection, mastitis; any time → malaria, DVT.
- **The uterus is the clue**: tender, bulky and soft with offensive lochia means endometritis ± retained products. A **normal uterus with sepsis** makes you look elsewhere.
- **Fever not settling after 48–72 h of antibiotics** → pelvic abscess, infected haematoma, **septic pelvic thrombophlebitis**, resistant organisms, retained products, necrotising fasciitis, malaria, or a wrong diagnosis.
- **Postpartum headache**: not always "just tiredness". Check BP and urine (pre-eclampsia), think of post-dural-puncture headache (after spinal), cerebral venous sinus thrombosis (severe, seizures, focal signs) and meningitis.
- **Mood**: blues is **normal and transient**; symptoms lasting **more than 2 weeks** or severe symptoms are **not** blues. **Psychosis is an emergency.**
- **Common student mistakes**: not checking the uterus and lochia in a febrile woman; forgetting malaria; assuming breast pain means stopping breastfeeding; missing urinary retention (a "palpable uterus" that is actually a full bladder); forgetting contraception; not asking about mood.

> [!EXAM]
> "A woman on day 4 has a temperature of 38.5 °C. How will you approach her?" Answer with a **systematic search for the source** (7 Ws + malaria), the **examination** of breasts, chest, abdomen/uterus, wound, perineum, lochia and calves, the **investigations** (FBC, blood culture, malaria test, urinalysis/MSU, HVS, wound swab, ultrasound), and **empirical treatment** of the most likely source.

## Differential diagnosis

**Puerperal fever:**

| Condition | Distinguishing features | Key investigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Endometritis** | Days 2–10; fever, lower abdominal pain, **tender bulky uterus, offensive lochia**; after CS, prolonged labour, PROM | FBC, blood culture, HVS; ultrasound for retained products |
| **Malaria** | Fever, chills, headache, joint pains; uterus and lochia normal | **RDT or blood smear** |
| **UTI / pyelonephritis** | Dysuria, frequency, loin pain, catheterisation | Urinalysis, MSU culture |
| **Mastitis / breast abscess** | Days 5–30; unilateral red, hot, tender wedge-shaped area; fluctuant lump = abscess | Clinical; ultrasound for abscess; milk culture if recurrent |
| **Breast engorgement** | Days 3–5; bilateral, hard, painful breasts; low-grade fever | Clinical |
| **Wound infection** (CS/episiotomy) | Days 4–10; redness, discharge, dehiscence | Wound swab |
| **DVT / PE** | Unilateral calf swelling or pain; tachycardia, dyspnoea, pleuritic chest pain | Doppler ultrasound; CTPA/V-Q (where available) |
| **Chest infection** | Cough, crackles, after GA (aspiration) | CXR |
| **Septic pelvic thrombophlebitis** | Spiking fever despite antibiotics, no clear source | Diagnosis of exclusion; CT |
| **Pelvic abscess / infected haematoma** | Persistent fever, pelvic mass, rectal fullness | Ultrasound |

**Postpartum mood disorders:**

| Condition | Onset / duration | Features | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Postpartum blues** | Days 3–5 (peak), resolves by about day 10–14; very common (up to 50–80%) | Tearfulness, irritability, mood swings, anxiety; functioning preserved | Reassure, support, rest; review if lasting more than 2 weeks |
| **Postpartum depression** | Usually within the first weeks to months (up to 1 year); about 10–15% (often more in Uganda) | Low mood, anhedonia, guilt, poor sleep even when the baby sleeps, poor bonding, **suicidal thoughts** | **EPDS screen**, psychological therapy, antidepressant (sertraline), follow-up |
| **Puerperal psychosis** | Usually within the **first 2 weeks** (often days 3–10); about 1–2 per 1000 | Confusion, agitation, **delusions, hallucinations**, mania, severe insomnia; **risk of suicide and infanticide** | **Emergency psychiatric admission**; do not leave her alone with the baby |
| **Organic causes** | Any | Delirium from sepsis, eclampsia, hypoglycaemia, thyroiditis, cerebral malaria, HIV/CNS infection | Exclude before labelling as psychiatric |

## Investigations

| Test | What you are looking for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **FBC** | Anaemia (Hb <11 g/dL), leucocytosis | Anaemia after PPH; infection (remember that the WBC is normally raised in the first days) |
| **Malaria RDT / blood smear** | Plasmodium | Common cause of puerperal fever in Uganda |
| **Blood cultures** (before antibiotics) | Organism and sensitivities | Sepsis (GAS, E. coli, anaerobes, Staph) |
| **High vaginal / endocervical swab** | Organisms (GAS, gonorrhoea, chlamydia) | Guides antibiotics |
| **Urinalysis, MSU** | Nitrites, leucocytes, culture | UTI |
| **Wound swab / pus culture** | Organism | Wound infection, abscess |
| **Pelvic ultrasound** | Retained products, collections, abscess | Guides evacuation or drainage |
| **Breast ultrasound** | Abscess | Needle aspiration vs incision |
| **Doppler ultrasound of legs** | DVT | Anticoagulation |
| **BP and urine protein; LFTs, creatinine, platelets** | Postpartum pre-eclampsia/HELLP | Treat with MgSO₄ and antihypertensives |
| **Lactate, creatinine** | Organ dysfunction | Severity of sepsis |
| **EPDS** (Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale) | Score ≥10 possible and **≥13 probable** depression; **any positive answer to item 10 (self-harm)** needs same-day assessment | Validated screening tool |
| **HIV test** (if not done or negative in pregnancy), **β-hCG** (persistent bleeding) | Seroconversion, GTN | eMTCT; molar disease |

## Management

### A. Routine postnatal care

**The WHO 2022 schedule** (Uganda: "6 hours, 6 days, 6 weeks"):

| Contact | Timing | Key tasks |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Within 24 h** (stay in the facility **at least 24 h** after vaginal birth) | Vital signs and bleeding **every 15 min for the first 2 hours**, then at intervals; uterus, lochia, bladder (**passed urine within 6 h?**), perineum; initiate breastfeeding **within 1 hour**, skin-to-skin; newborn exam, vitamin K, eye care, cord care (chlorhexidine where indicated), BCG/OPV0; danger signs |
| **2** | **Day 3 (48–72 h)** | Check BP (postpartum pre-eclampsia peaks days 3–6), temperature, breasts and feeding, jaundice in the baby, perineum, mood |
| **3** | **Days 7–14** | Involution, wound healing, breastfeeding, mood (blues should be resolving), weight of baby |
| **4** | **6 weeks** | Full check: BP, Hb if anaemic, pelvic floor/continence, **family planning**, cervical cancer screening (VIA) if due, HIV retesting, **mood (EPDS)**, baby's immunisation (DPT-HepB-Hib 1, PCV1, OPV1, rotavirus) and growth |

**Every contact**: counsel on **danger signs**, nutrition (extra meal a day while breastfeeding), rest, hygiene, **exclusive breastfeeding**, sexual health and **FP**, and **iron + folic acid** (continue for about 3 months after birth; ferrous sulphate 200 mg daily + folic acid).

**After CS**: early mobilisation, analgesia, wound care (remove dressing at 24–48 h, keep clean and dry), **thromboprophylaxis** if risk factors, breastfeeding support (positions that avoid the wound), avoid heavy lifting for about 6 weeks, and discuss interpregnancy interval (at least 18–24 months) and mode of next delivery.

**Rh-negative mother with Rh-positive baby**: **anti-D within 72 hours** of birth.

**HIV-positive mother**: continue **ART (TLD)** without interruption, check viral load, **infant prophylaxis** (per the Uganda consolidated HIV guideline), and **exclusive breastfeeding** for 6 months, continuing breastfeeding with complementary feeds while the mother is virally suppressed. Retest HIV-negative mothers during breastfeeding per the national schedule.

### B. Perineal care

1. **Hygiene**: wash with clean water 2–3 times a day and after toileting; change pads every 4–6 h; front-to-back wiping.
2. **Analgesia**: **paracetamol 1 g orally every 6–8 h** (max 4 g/day) ± **ibuprofen 400 mg every 8 h** with food (safe in breastfeeding; avoid in severe pre-eclampsia with renal impairment or PPH with AKI); cold packs in the first 24–48 h; warm sitz baths afterwards.
3. **Prevent constipation**: fluids, fibre; lactulose 15 mL twice daily (routine after 3rd/4th-degree tears).
4. **Review** for infection or breakdown: redness, pus, gaping. Treat infection with antibiotics (e.g. amoxicillin-clavulanate or amoxicillin + metronidazole). Open, clean and allow **healing by secondary intention**, with resuturing later once clean.
5. **3rd/4th-degree tears (OASIS)**: broad-spectrum antibiotics, laxatives, physiotherapy/pelvic floor exercises, and review at 6–12 weeks for continence.
6. **Haematoma**: small → analgesia and ice; large or expanding → evacuation in theatre.
7. **Continuous urine leakage** after obstructed labour: suspect **VVF**. Insert an **indwelling catheter for 2–4 weeks** (small early fistulae may heal) and refer to a fistula centre.

### C. Puerperal sepsis: step by step

> [!DRUG]
> **Puerperal sepsis / endometritis regimen (WHO MCPC, Uganda):**
>
> | Drug | Dose | Route | Frequency | Duration / notes |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | **Ampicillin** | **2 g** | IV | Every 6 h | Until afebrile for **48 h** |
> | **Gentamicin** | **5 mg/kg** | IV | **Once daily** | Monitor renal function; avoid prolonged use |
> | **Metronidazole** | **500 mg** | IV | Every 8 h | Anaerobic cover |
> | Then oral step-down | Amoxicillin 500 mg 8-hourly + metronidazole 400 mg 8-hourly | Oral | — | To complete **7 days** in total (if the bacteraemia or source warrants it) |
> | Alternative (common in Ugandan hospitals) | **Ceftriaxone 1–2 g IV daily + metronidazole 500 mg IV 8-hourly** | IV | — | Per local antibiogram |
> | Penicillin allergy | Clindamycin 600–900 mg IV 8-hourly + gentamicin | IV | — | |

**Algorithm (sepsis in a postnatal woman):**

1. **Recognise**: fever or hypothermia, tachycardia >100, RR ≥22, altered mentation, SBP ≤100 (qSOFA-style), plus a likely source.
2. **Resuscitate**: oxygen; IV access; **crystalloid bolus 500 mL–1 L** (reassess after each; avoid overload, especially with pre-eclampsia); catheterise for urine output.
3. **Take cultures** (blood, HVS, urine) and do a **malaria test** before or at the same time as antibiotics. **Don't delay antibiotics**: give them **within 1 hour**.
4. **Give IV antibiotics** (table above).
5. **Source control**: **evacuate retained products** (MVA, gentle, after antibiotics have started); **drain abscesses**; debride infected wounds; laparotomy for peritonitis or pelvic abscess; **hysterectomy** for a necrotic, gangrenous or perforated uterus that isn't responding.
6. Treat **malaria** if positive (IV/IM **artesunate** for severe malaria, 2.4 mg/kg at 0, 12 and 24 h, then daily; oral ACT for uncomplicated).
7. **Supportive**: paracetamol, thromboprophylaxis, nutrition, breastfeeding support (continue, or express if very ill; most antibiotics above are compatible).
8. **Escalate**: persistent hypotension despite fluids → vasopressors and HDU/ICU at a RRH/NRH.
9. **If no improvement in 48–72 h**: re-examine, ultrasound for collections, review cultures, consider septic pelvic thrombophlebitis (add heparin), necrotising fasciitis, or a non-genital source.

> [!UGANDA]
> At a **HC III**: give the **first dose of IV/IM antibiotics** (ampicillin + gentamicin + metronidazole; or IM ceftriaxone if that is what's available), IV fluids and paracetamol, test for malaria, and **refer** to a HC IV or hospital for continued care and source control (MVA, drainage, surgery). Remember that MVA for retained products is a **BEmONC** function the HC III can do.

### D. Breastfeeding support and problems

**Support (WHO/UNICEF Baby-Friendly):** skin-to-skin and **initiation within 1 hour**; **rooming-in**; **feed on demand** (8–12 times per 24 h); **exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months**, then continue with complementary foods to 2 years or more; no teats or pre-lacteal feeds.

**Good attachment (4 signs):** mouth **wide open**; **lower lip turned out**; **chin touching the breast**; **more areola visible above** the mouth than below. Effective suckling: slow deep sucks with pauses, audible swallowing. Positioning: baby's head and body in line, facing the breast, close to the mother, whole body supported.

| Problem | Cause | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **Sore / cracked nipples** | **Poor attachment** (most common) | Correct attachment; apply expressed hindmilk to the nipple and air-dry; continue feeding; start on the less sore side |
| **Engorgement** (days 3–5) | Milk comes in and isn't removed effectively | **Frequent effective feeding**, express to soften the areola before latching, warm compress before and **cold compress after** feeds, paracetamol/ibuprofen, supportive bra |
| **Blocked duct** | Localised milk stasis (tight clothing, missed feeds) | Tender lump, no fever: keep feeding (start with the affected side), gentle massage towards the nipple, vary positions |
| **Mastitis** | Milk stasis ± infection (*Staph. aureus*), days 5–30 | **Continue breastfeeding or expressing** (it is safe for the baby and essential for recovery); rest, fluids, analgesia; if symptoms last more than 12–24 h, there are fissures, or she is unwell: **cloxacillin/flucloxacillin 500 mg orally every 6 h for 10–14 days** (**erythromycin 250–500 mg 6-hourly** if penicillin-allergic). If no improvement in 48 h, suspect an **abscess** |
| **Breast abscess** | Undertreated mastitis | Fluctuant tender mass: **needle aspiration** (ultrasound-guided if possible) or **incision and drainage** (radial incision away from the areola), antibiotics; feed from the other breast and express the affected one; resume on the affected side once the drainage is away from the nipple |
| **Nipple/oral thrush** | Candida | Shiny red nipple, burning pain; white plaques in the baby's mouth. **Treat both**: miconazole cream to the nipple after feeds, nystatin oral suspension 1 mL four times daily for the baby, for 7 days |
| **"Not enough milk"** | Usually perceived; true low supply from infrequent feeding, poor attachment, supplements, retained placenta, Sheehan's | Check baby's weight gain and urine output (≥6 wet nappies/day after day 5); correct attachment; feed more often; avoid formula/water; skin-to-skin; treat the cause |
| **Inverted/flat nipples** | Anatomical | Attachment to the breast (not the nipple); syringe eversion; expressing |

**Lactation suppression** (stillbirth, neonatal death, mother's choice where appropriate): a firm bra, cold compresses, analgesia, avoid stimulation; **cabergoline 1 mg single dose** within 24 h of birth (avoid in hypertension/pre-eclampsia). **Bromocriptine is no longer recommended** (risk of stroke, MI, seizures).

**Drugs and breastfeeding:** most common drugs are compatible (paracetamol, ibuprofen, penicillins, cephalosporins, metronidazole, gentamicin, nifedipine, methyldopa, labetalol, enalapril, **sertraline**, **TLD**, heparin/LMWH, warfarin). Avoid or use with caution: **ergometrine** (suppresses lactation), **combined oestrogen pills** (reduce supply early), high-dose aspirin, tetracyclines (long courses), amiodarone, lithium (specialist), and chemotherapy.

### E. Postpartum family planning (PPFP)

**Why:** a **birth-to-pregnancy interval of at least 24 months** reduces maternal, perinatal and infant risks. Ovulation can come **before the first period**: from about **day 25** in non-breastfeeding women.

**LAM**: over **98% effective** only if **all 3** criteria are met:
1. **Amenorrhoea** (no vaginal bleeding after day 56)
2. **Fully or nearly fully breastfeeding**, day and night (no gap longer than about 4 h by day or 6 h at night; little or no supplementation)
3. Baby **less than 6 months** old

As soon as **any** criterion is lost, start another method.

**Method, timing and WHO MEC category for breastfeeding women:**

| Method | When it can start postpartum | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Copper IUD / LNG-IUD** (postpartum IUD) | **Within 48 h** (ideally within 10 min of the placenta, including at CS), **or from 4 weeks** | Between 48 h and 4 weeks = **category 3** (higher expulsion/perforation risk). Not with puerperal sepsis (**category 4**) |
| **Implant** (Jadelle, Implanon) | **Immediately** (before discharge) | MEC 2015: category 2 under 6 weeks if breastfeeding (benefits outweigh risks); widely offered immediately in Uganda |
| **Progestogen-only pill** | **Immediately** | Take at the same time daily (within 3 h) |
| **DMPA** (injectable, Depo-Provera / Sayana Press) | MEC 2015: from **6 weeks** if breastfeeding (category 3 before 6 weeks); **immediately** if not breastfeeding | Uganda commonly gives it at the 6-week visit |
| **Combined hormonal contraception (COC)** | Breastfeeding: **not before 6 months** ideally (**category 4 under 6 weeks**, category 3 at 6 weeks to 6 months). Not breastfeeding: **not before 21 days** (VTE risk; category 4 under 21 days) | Oestrogen reduces milk supply and increases VTE risk |
| **Tubal ligation** (bilateral tubal ligation, BTL) | **Within 7 days** (or at CS), or **after 6 weeks** | Delay in 7–42 days (category D) and with sepsis, severe pre-eclampsia or major PPH until resolved. Needs **informed voluntary consent**, never conditional on care |
| **Vasectomy** | Any time | Partner option; not effective for 3 months |
| **Condoms** | Any time | Dual protection (HIV/STI) |

> [!UGANDA]
> Uganda's FP guidelines strongly promote **immediate PPFP**: counselling during ANC, then an **implant or postpartum IUD before discharge**. **Consent must be voluntary and informed**; offering a method is part of respectful care, but it must never be a condition for discharge.

### F. Postpartum mood disorders

**Screening:** ask the **Whooley questions** ("During the past month have you often been bothered by feeling down, depressed or hopeless? … by having little interest or pleasure in doing things?") at every contact, and use the **EPDS** (10 items; ≥10 possible, ≥13 probable depression; any positive response on **item 10** needs urgent assessment).

**Postpartum blues**: reassurance, rest, practical support; review at 2 weeks.

**Postpartum depression**:
1. **Assess risk** (suicide, harm to the baby, neglect), severity, support and domestic violence.
2. **Mild–moderate**: psychosocial support and psychological therapy (e.g. **interpersonal therapy**, **problem-solving therapy**, WHO *Thinking Healthy*, group interpersonal therapy as delivered in Uganda by lay health workers), family involvement, sleep and practical help.
3. **Moderate–severe or not responding**: an antidepressant. **Sertraline 50 mg daily** (preferred in breastfeeding; titrate to 100–200 mg) or **fluoxetine 20 mg daily** (on the Uganda EML). Continue for at least **6–12 months** after remission. Amitriptyline (UCG) is an alternative, but it is dangerous in overdose.
4. **Follow up** every 2 weeks initially; refer to mental health services (e.g. the district mental health unit or **Butabika**) if severe or suicidal.

**Puerperal psychosis: a psychiatric emergency**
1. Ensure **safety**: never leave her alone with the baby; remove means of harm.
2. **Exclude organic causes**: sepsis, malaria, eclampsia, hypoglycaemia, electrolyte imbalance, drugs, HIV/CNS infection (check vital signs, glucose, malaria test, BP/urine).
3. **Urgent psychiatric admission** (ideally keeping mother and baby together with supervision).
4. **Antipsychotic**: e.g. **haloperidol** (UCG: 5 mg orally or IM, repeated as needed for acute agitation) or **olanzapine** 5–10 mg daily; benzodiazepine for agitation or sleep; a mood stabiliser for mania under specialist care (lithium or valproate have breastfeeding and teratogenic implications, so they need a specialist).
5. **Future pregnancies**: high recurrence risk (about 50%), especially with **bipolar disorder**; plan preventive care.

### G. Other postnatal conditions to manage

- **Postpartum pre-eclampsia**: BP ≥140/90 with symptoms or proteinuria; MgSO₄ if severe features; antihypertensives (**nifedipine**, **methyldopa** is less preferred postpartum because it can worsen depression, **labetalol**, **enalapril**); review BP at days 3–5 and at 6 weeks.
- **VTE prophylaxis / treatment**: early mobilisation and hydration for all; LMWH (e.g. **enoxaparin 40 mg SC daily**) for high-risk women (CS plus other risk factors) for 10 days to 6 weeks; treatment doses for confirmed DVT/PE (warfarin is compatible with breastfeeding).
- **Urinary retention**: if she hasn't voided within 6 h or has a palpable bladder, **catheterise**; if the residual is more than about 500 mL, leave an indwelling catheter for 24–48 h.
- **Anaemia**: Hb <11 g/dL → oral iron (ferrous sulphate 200 mg twice to three times daily) + folic acid for 3 months; Hb <7 g/dL or symptomatic → consider transfusion (or IV iron where available).

## Complications

| Complication | Key point |
|---|---|
| **Secondary PPH** | Endometritis, retained products, subinvolution (see the PPH notes) |
| **Puerperal sepsis → septic shock, death** | Leading killer in some Ugandan hospitals; group A strep can kill within hours |
| **Pelvic abscess, septic pelvic thrombophlebitis, necrotising fasciitis** | Suspect when fever persists despite antibiotics |
| **VTE / PE** | Leading direct cause of maternal death in high-income countries; underdiagnosed in Uganda |
| **Postpartum pre-eclampsia / eclampsia**, stroke | Up to 6 weeks postpartum |
| **Peripartum cardiomyopathy** | Heart failure from the last month of pregnancy to 5 months postpartum |
| **Breast abscess**, lactation failure | Poor attachment, delayed treatment |
| **Mood disorders, suicide** | Suicide is a leading cause of late maternal death |
| **Incontinence, prolapse, fistula** | Pelvic floor damage, obstructed labour |
| **Dyspareunia** | Perineal scarring, vaginal dryness during lactation |
| **Sheehan's syndrome** | Failure of lactation after major PPH |
| **Unintended short-interval pregnancy** | Missed PPFP |

## Ward-round & exam pearls

**Model summary line:**
> "Mrs P, a 24-year-old para 2, is day 4 after an emergency CS at Mengo for obstructed labour after 18 hours in labour with membranes ruptured for 20 hours. She has had fever up to 38.9 °C since yesterday, lower abdominal pain and foul-smelling lochia. Pulse 112, BP 104/66, RR 22. The uterus is tender and 2 cm above the umbilicus (subinvolution), and the wound is clean. Malaria RDT negative; WBC 19 × 10⁹/L. Impression: **puerperal sepsis from post-CS endometritis**, with retained products to be excluded. Plan: IV ampicillin, gentamicin and metronidazole (started 10:00), blood culture and HVS taken, pelvic ultrasound, fluids, catheter for urine output, continue breastfeeding with support, thromboprophylaxis, and review in 24 h. Offered an implant; she will decide after discussing with her husband."

**Likely viva questions**
1. *Define the puerperium.* Delivery of the placenta to 6 weeks (42 days).
2. *Describe the normal involution of the uterus.* Fundus at the umbilicus after birth, descending about 1 cm/day, not palpable by day 10–14; 1 kg to about 60 g by 6 weeks; by autolysis and ischaemia.
3. *Types of lochia?* Rubra, serosa, alba (with timings).
4. *Causes of puerperal pyrexia?* The 7 Ws plus malaria.
5. *Hormones of lactation?* Prolactin (production) and oxytocin (ejection); progesterone withdrawal triggers lactogenesis II.
6. *LAM criteria?* Amenorrhoea, full breastfeeding, baby under 6 months.
7. *When can a postpartum IUD be inserted?* Within 48 h or after 4 weeks.
8. *Why avoid COCs in early lactation?* They reduce milk supply and increase VTE risk (MEC 4 under 6 weeks if breastfeeding).
9. *Blues vs depression vs psychosis?* Timing, severity, duration, psychotic features, risk.
10. *WHO postnatal schedule?* Within 24 h, day 3, days 7–14, 6 weeks; stay at least 24 h.

**OSCE tips:** in a **breastfeeding counselling station**, observe a feed, name the **4 signs of good attachment**, and use open questions. In a **postnatal examination station**, verbalise consent, a chaperone and hand hygiene; examine **breasts, fundus, wound/perineum, lochia and calves**; and finish with **danger signs and FP**. In an **FP station**, explain **LAM criteria** and the **timing of each method** clearly.

## Mnemonics & memory aids

- **Puerperal fever: 7 Ws + M**: **W**omb, **W**ind, **W**ater, **W**ound, **W**alking (DVT), **W**eaning (breast), **W**onder drugs, and **M**alaria.
- **Lochia "R-S-A: Red, Serous, Absent colour"**: rubra (1–4), serosa (4–10), alba (10 to 6 weeks).
- **Uterus "a finger a day"**: the fundus descends one finger-breadth a day and disappears behind the symphysis by day 10–14.
- **LAM "3 As"**: **A**menorrhoea, **A**ll breastfeeding, **A**ge under 6 months.
- **Attachment "WOLF-A"**: **W**ide mouth, **O**utturned lower lip, **L**ess areola below (more above), **F**ace (chin) touching the breast, **A**udible swallowing.
- **Postnatal visits "1-3-7-42"**: day **1** (within 24 h), day **3**, days **7**–14, and day **42** (6 weeks).
- **PPIUD "48 hours or 4 weeks."**
