## In a nutshell

Each year about **260,000 women die** from pregnancy and childbirth worldwide, and about **70% of them in sub-Saharan Africa**. In Uganda roughly **1 in every 530 live births** is accompanied by a maternal death (MMR 189/100,000, UDHS 2022). Most deaths happen **around labour, delivery and the first 24 hours after birth**.

The key insight of the safe-motherhood movement is this: **we cannot predict which woman will develop a complication**, because most obstetric emergencies (PPH, eclampsia, obstructed labour, sepsis) occur in "low-risk" women. So the strategy is to make sure **every woman can reach, in time, a facility that can perform the life-saving "signal functions"**. That is **Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (EmONC)**.

> [!PEARL]
> The one thing never to forget: **"Every pregnancy faces risk."** Prevent what you can (family planning, ANC, skilled birth attendance), but mothers survive because the emergency is **recognised early, reached quickly and treated competently**. That is the three delays turned upside down.

## Definitions & classification

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Maternal death** (ICD) | Death of a woman **while pregnant or within 42 days of termination of pregnancy**, irrespective of duration or site of the pregnancy, from any cause related to or aggravated by the pregnancy or its management, but **not** from accidental or incidental causes |
| **Late maternal death** | Death from direct or indirect obstetric causes **more than 42 days but less than 1 year** after the end of pregnancy |
| **Pregnancy-related death** | Death while pregnant or within 42 days, **irrespective of cause** (includes accidents) |
| **Direct obstetric death** | From obstetric complications of pregnancy, labour or puerperium, or from interventions, omissions or incorrect treatment (e.g. PPH, eclampsia, sepsis, ruptured uterus, abortion complications, ectopic, anaesthetic death) |
| **Indirect obstetric death** | From a pre-existing disease or one that developed in pregnancy, aggravated by the physiological effects of pregnancy (e.g. **malaria, HIV/TB, anaemia, heart disease**, sickle cell) |
| **Maternal mortality ratio (MMR)** | Maternal deaths per **100,000 live births** (measures obstetric risk per pregnancy) |
| **Maternal mortality rate** | Maternal deaths per 100,000 **women of reproductive age** (15–49) (reflects both obstetric risk and fertility) |
| **Lifetime risk** | Probability that a 15-year-old girl will eventually die from a maternal cause |
| **Maternal near miss** | A woman who **nearly died but survived** a complication during pregnancy, childbirth or within 42 days (WHO organ-dysfunction criteria, e.g. shock, need for ≥5 units of blood, hysterectomy for bleeding, eclampsia with coma, ICU admission) |
| **Skilled birth attendant** | An accredited health professional (midwife, nurse, doctor, clinical officer) trained to manage normal pregnancy and birth and to identify, manage and refer complications |
| **EmONC** | The package of life-saving interventions (signal functions) for obstetric and newborn complications |
| **Signal function** | A key medical intervention that treats the major direct causes of maternal death, used as an **indicator** of a facility's capacity (it must have been performed at least once in the last 3 months) |

### EmONC signal functions (UN, 2009)

| # | Signal function | What it treats | BEmONC | CEmONC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Parenteral antibiotics** | Puerperal sepsis, septic abortion, PROM | ✔ | ✔ |
| 2 | **Parenteral uterotonic drugs** (oxytocin) | PPH | ✔ | ✔ |
| 3 | **Parenteral anticonvulsants** (MgSO₄) | Pre-eclampsia/eclampsia | ✔ | ✔ |
| 4 | **Manual removal of placenta** | Retained placenta, PPH | ✔ | ✔ |
| 5 | **Removal of retained products** (MVA, D&C) | Incomplete abortion, secondary PPH | ✔ | ✔ |
| 6 | **Assisted vaginal delivery** (vacuum, forceps) | Prolonged 2nd stage, fetal distress | ✔ | ✔ |
| 7 | **Basic neonatal resuscitation** (bag and mask) | Birth asphyxia | ✔ | ✔ |
| 8 | **Surgery (caesarean section)** | Obstructed labour, praevia, abruption, fetal distress, rupture | | ✔ |
| 9 | **Blood transfusion** | Haemorrhage, severe anaemia | | ✔ |

> [!NOTE]
> A 2025 Delphi consensus (the "Re-Visioning EmONC" project) proposed an **expanded set of 25 signal functions** (11 obstetric, 13 neonatal, 1 referral), adding for example tranexamic acid, antenatal corticosteroids, kangaroo care and referral capacity. For exams, **learn the classic 7 + 2**, and mention that the framework is being updated.

## Epidemiology & Uganda context

### Global picture

- About **260,000 maternal deaths in 2023** (global MMR about 197); **about 70% in sub-Saharan Africa**. Most would be preventable with timely, quality care.
- **SDG 3.1**: reduce the global MMR to **<70 per 100,000 live births by 2030**, with **no country above 140**. **SDG 3.1.2**: proportion of births attended by skilled personnel.
- The **Millennium Development Goal 5** (reduce MMR by 75% between 1990 and 2015) was missed by most of sub-Saharan Africa, including Uganda.

### Uganda: trends

| Survey | MMR (per 100,000 live births) |
|---|---|
| UDHS 2000–01 | 505 |
| UDHS 2006 | 435 |
| UDHS 2011 | 438 |
| UDHS 2016 | **336** |
| UDHS 2022 | **189** |

This is real progress, driven by more facility births (**about 9 in 10 births now occur in a health facility** in UDHS 2022), more CEmONC at HC IVs, MPDSR and results-based financing. But **189 is still far above the SDG target**, and the **quality of care** at facilities, not just access, is now the main battleground (the "**third delay**").

### Causes of maternal death in Uganda (MPDSR reports and facility studies)

| Cause | Comment |
|---|---|
| **Obstetric haemorrhage** (especially **PPH**) | Leading cause nationally, around a quarter to a third of deaths |
| **Hypertensive disorders** (pre-eclampsia/eclampsia) | Often second; linked to late or low-quality ANC and failure to give MgSO₄ |
| **Sepsis** (puerperal, post-abortion, post-CS) | Leading cause in some tertiary series (e.g. Mulago, about 31% in one study) |
| **Abortion complications** | Unsafe abortion; restrictive law and stigma delay care |
| **Obstructed labour / ruptured uterus** | Late referral, herbal uterotonics |
| **Indirect causes** | **Malaria, HIV/TB, anaemia**, cardiac disease, sickle cell disease |
| Anaesthetic complications | Spinal/GA without trained anaesthetists at lower levels |

### The Uganda health system for mothers

| Level | Catchment (approx.) | Maternity role |
|---|---|---|
| **VHT** (village health team) | Village | Health education, birth preparedness, identifying danger signs, linking to facilities; community misoprostol in some districts |
| **HC II** | Parish (~5,000) | ANC, FP, outpatient; **not meant for deliveries** (but many happen) |
| **HC III** | Sub-county (~20,000) | Normal deliveries, **BEmONC**, maternity ward, laboratory |
| **HC IV** | County / health sub-district (~100,000) | **CEmONC**: theatre (CS), blood transfusion, doctor(s) |
| **General (district) hospital** | District (~500,000) | CEmONC, more specialists |
| **Regional referral hospital (RRH)** | Region (~2 million) | Obstetricians, HDU, blood bank, internship training |
| **National referral hospitals** | National | e.g. **Kawempe NRH** (dedicated maternity), Mulago Specialised Women and Neonatal Hospital; ICU; subspecialists |
| **PNFP/private hospitals** | — | e.g. **Mengo** (Church of Uganda), Nsambya, Rubaga, Lubaga: about a third of services |

> [!UGANDA]
> Common gaps at HC IVs, which you will see on outreach and in referral letters: no anaesthetist or a non-functional theatre, **no blood**, oxytocin or MgSO₄ stock-outs, one midwife for the whole maternity at night, no ambulance fuel, and referral forms without vital signs. Knowing these realities helps you understand the "third delay" and write better referral and MPDSR reports.

## Principles

### The three delays model (Thaddeus & Maine, 1994, *"Too far to walk"*)

| Delay | What it is | Examples of causes (Uganda) | Interventions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Deciding to seek care** | Woman/family doesn't recognise danger or decides not to go | Not knowing danger signs; husband or mother-in-law decides; fear of costs, of CS, of abusive staff; belief in TBAs or herbalists; previous bad experience; stigma (abortion, HIV) | Health education, **birth preparedness & complication readiness**, male involvement, VHTs, **respectful care**, community dialogues, free services |
| **2. Reaching a facility** | Physically getting to care | Distance, rough roads, no transport at night, cost of boda-boda, floods, insecurity, being referred between facilities | Maternity waiting homes, community transport schemes, ambulance networks, vouchers, **HC IV CEmONC** closer to people, mobile phone referral |
| **3. Receiving adequate care** | Care is delayed or poor at the facility | Staff shortage, lack of drugs/blood/theatre, poor triage, delayed decisions, poor skills, **referral without stabilisation**, demand for payment or supplies ("buy gloves") | Staffing, supplies, **EmONC training** (e.g. drills), protocols, triage, MPDSR, supportive supervision, quality improvement |

> [!EXAM]
> Examiners frequently ask you to **analyse a maternal death using the three delays**. Structure your answer delay by delay, and within each delay name **patient/family, community, and health-system** factors. Then propose **one action per delay**.

### Pillars of Safe Motherhood

The **Safe Motherhood Initiative** was launched in **Nairobi in 1987** (WHO, UNFPA, UNICEF, World Bank). The classic **four pillars**, resting on a base of **primary health care and equity for women** (education, status, empowerment):

1. **Family planning**: fewer unwanted, high-risk, and closely spaced pregnancies; fewer unsafe abortions.
2. **Antenatal care**: screening and prevention (iron/folate, IPTp, TT, HIV/syphilis testing, BP), and birth preparedness.
3. **Clean and safe delivery**: a skilled attendant at every birth.
4. **Essential (emergency) obstetric care**: EmONC for complications.

Later frameworks add **postnatal care**, **post-abortion care**, prevention of STIs/HIV, and **respectful maternity care**.

### Why skilled attendance plus EmONC, not "risk screening"?

- Risk scoring at ANC (age, parity, height) **misses most women who develop complications**: "low-risk" women still bleed and convulse.
- About **15% of all pregnant women develop a potentially life-threatening complication**, and about 5–15% of births need a CS. So we plan services for that 15%, for everyone.
- **CS rate as a population indicator**: <5% suggests unmet need (women dying from obstructed labour); >15% suggests possible over-use. Uganda's national CS rate is below this band in rural areas but much higher in urban private hospitals: both an access and an equity problem.

### UN EmONC process indicators (2009 handbook)

1. **Availability**: ≥5 EmONC facilities (≥1 comprehensive) per 500,000 population.
2. **Geographic distribution**: all sub-national areas meet indicator 1.
3. **Proportion of all births in EmONC facilities**.
4. **Met need for EmONC**: proportion of women with major direct obstetric complications treated in EmONC facilities (estimated as 15% of expected births); target **100%**.
5. **CS as a proportion of all births**: 5–15%.
6. **Direct obstetric case fatality rate**: **<1%**.
7. **Intrapartum and very early neonatal death rate**.
8. **Proportion of maternal deaths due to indirect causes** in EmONC facilities.

### MPDSR: Maternal and Perinatal Death Surveillance and Response

- Uganda made **maternal and perinatal deaths notifiable** (2008), with national **MPDSR guidelines (2017)**.
- **Cycle**: **identify and notify** (within 24 h, via the facility and DHIS2/weekly surveillance) → **review** (facility MPDSR committee, ideally within 7 days; case notes, staff interviews, **three-delays analysis**, cause of death using ICD-MM) → **analyse** (avoidable factors, patterns) → **respond** (action plan with responsible person and deadline) → **monitor and evaluate** the response.
- **Principles**: **no name, no blame**; confidentiality; the goal is learning, not punishment; the review isn't complete without a **response**.
- **Community**: **verbal autopsy** for deaths outside facilities.
- **Perinatal deaths** (stillbirths and early neonatal deaths) are reviewed in the same system.
- Uganda produces a **National Annual Maternal and Perinatal Death Report**. Common findings: late referral, delayed CS, lack of blood, and failure to monitor after delivery.

### Respectful maternity care (RMC)

The WHO **"positive childbirth experience"** (2018 intrapartum recommendations) and the White Ribbon Alliance **Respectful Maternity Care Charter** state women's rights to:

- freedom from harm and ill-treatment; information, **informed consent** and refusal; **privacy and confidentiality**; **dignity and respect**; equality and non-discrimination (HIV status, age, poverty, disability); timely care; **liberty** (no detention for unpaid bills); a **companion of choice** during labour.

Seven categories of **disrespect and abuse** (Bowser & Hill): physical abuse, non-consented care, non-confidential care, non-dignified care (shouting, scolding), discrimination, **abandonment or neglect** (delivering alone), and **detention in facilities**.

**Why it matters clinically:** disrespect drives the **first delay**. Women who were slapped or shouted at last time deliver at home or with a TBA next time.

## Clinical acumen

This topic is about systems, but your acumen is still tested on the ward: **recognising the emergency, triaging it and referring it correctly**.

### Focused history: danger signs to ask every pregnant or postnatal woman

| Danger sign | Think of |
|---|---|
| **Vaginal bleeding** (any amount in pregnancy; heavy after birth) | Abortion, ectopic, APH, PPH |
| **Convulsions / fits**, severe headache, blurred vision, epigastric pain | Pre-eclampsia/eclampsia (also malaria, epilepsy, meningitis) |
| **Fever**, foul-smelling discharge, abdominal pain | Sepsis, malaria, chorioamnionitis |
| **Severe abdominal pain** | Abruption, rupture, ectopic, sepsis, surgical causes |
| **Labour >12 hours**, or contractions without progress | Obstructed/prolonged labour |
| **Draining liquor** before labour | PROM/PPROM |
| **Reduced or absent fetal movements** | Fetal compromise/IUFD |
| **Difficulty breathing**, fast heartbeat, extreme weakness | Anaemia, heart failure, PE, sepsis |
| **Swelling of face and hands** | Pre-eclampsia |

For a woman who has been referred, **also ask the three delays questions**: *When did the problem start? When did you decide to come? How did you travel, and how long did it take? Where did you go first, and what was done there?* This is both clinically useful (duration of labour, doses already given) and good MPDSR data.

### Examination: the rapid initial assessment (RIA)

On arrival, **before the history**, triage every woman in under a minute:

1. **Airway, Breathing**: very difficult breathing, cyanosis.
2. **Circulation (shock)**: cold clammy skin, pulse >110, SBP <90, confusion.
3. **Vaginal bleeding**: heavy bleeding, pads soaked.
4. **Unconscious or convulsing**.
5. **Dangerous fever** (≥38 °C) with weakness or a stiff neck.
6. **Abdominal pain** (severe).
7. **Labour**: imminent delivery, prolonged labour.

Anyone with a "yes" gets **emergency care immediately**. Use **obstetric early warning scores (MEOWS)** on the wards to catch deterioration.

### Red flags in the system

> [!REDFLAG]
> - A **referral note without vital signs, drugs given or times**: re-assess everything
> - A woman **referred in shock without an IV line** or without MgSO₄ for eclampsia
> - **Decision-to-delivery interval** for an emergency CS of more than 30–60 minutes
> - **No monitoring after birth** (first 2 hours unobserved), where most PPH deaths happen
> - **Stock-outs** of oxytocin, MgSO₄, antibiotics, blood; theatre closed at night
> - A mother "waiting for relatives to buy supplies" before surgery

### Thinking like a clinician (and like a public-health doctor)

- **Every maternal death has a story**: analyse it by delays and by missed opportunities (ANC, labour monitoring, postpartum observation).
- **"Stabilise before transfer"**: pre-referral treatment saves lives. A woman with eclampsia should get her **MgSO₄ loading dose** before the ambulance; PPH needs **oxytocin, TXA, fluids and NASG**; sepsis needs the **first antibiotic dose**.
- **The first 24 hours postpartum** is the most dangerous period. Keep women at the facility for **at least 24 hours** after birth.
- **Indirect causes** kill Ugandan mothers too: test for **malaria and HIV**, look for **anaemia**, and ask about heart disease.
- **Pitfall**: thinking of EmONC as "buildings and equipment". The signal function must actually have been **performed** in the last 3 months to count; a vacuum extractor in a cupboard is not assisted vaginal delivery.

## Differential diagnosis

When a woman collapses or dies around childbirth, the differential of the **cause** guides both care and the MPDSR review.

| Condition | Distinguishing features | Key investigation |
|---|---|---|
| PPH / APH (hypovolaemic shock) | Visible or concealed bleeding, soft uterus or known praevia/abruption | Clinical; Hb; clotting test |
| Eclampsia / stroke | Seizures, BP ≥160/110, proteinuria, headache | BP, urine protein, (CT if focal signs) |
| Sepsis | Fever or hypothermia, tachycardia, foul discharge, post-abortion or post-CS | FBC, cultures, malaria test, lactate |
| Ruptured uterus | Obstructed labour or scar, shock, fetal parts palpable | Clinical, laparotomy |
| Amniotic fluid embolism | Sudden collapse in labour or just after, hypoxia, DIC | Clinical diagnosis |
| Pulmonary embolism | Sudden dyspnoea, chest pain, often postpartum or post-CS | Clinical ± CTPA/echo where available |
| Severe malaria | Fever, coma, hypoglycaemia, anaemia | RDT/smear, glucose |
| Peripartum cardiomyopathy / cardiac disease | Orthopnoea, oedema, murmur, late pregnancy to 5 months postpartum | Echo, CXR, ECG |
| Anaesthetic complication | High spinal, failed intubation, aspiration | Clinical history |

## Investigations

For EmONC and safe motherhood, "investigations" are both **clinical** (what every emergency facility must be able to test) and **programmatic** (data you use to evaluate a service).

| Test / data source | What you are looking for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Hb** (HemoCue or lab), blood group and cross-match | Anaemia, ability to transfuse | Basic to every EmONC facility |
| **Urine protein** (dipstick) | Pre-eclampsia | Cheap and life-saving |
| **Malaria RDT, HIV test, syphilis test** | Indirect causes, eMTCT | Uganda burden |
| **Bedside clotting test** | DIC | Needs no lab |
| **Partograph / Labour Care Guide** | Prolonged or obstructed labour | Prevents rupture and fistula |
| **HMIS/DHIS2 data**: births, complications, CS rate, case fatality | EmONC indicators | Monitor met need and quality |
| **EmONC needs assessment** (signal functions in last 3 months) | Whether facilities are truly functional | Plan where to invest |
| **MPDSR reviews, verbal autopsies, near-miss audits** | Causes and avoidable factors | Drive the response |
| **Criterion-based audit** (e.g. % eclampsia given MgSO₄; % women with AMTSL) | Quality of care against standards | Quality improvement |

## Management

### A. At the individual level: managing an obstetric emergency (the EmONC approach)

1. **Rapid initial assessment and triage** on arrival (see above). Shout for help.
2. **Resuscitate** (ABC): airway, oxygen, **IV access with two large-bore cannulae**, fluids, left lateral tilt if pregnant, keep warm, catheter.
3. **Identify the problem** quickly: bleeding, fits, fever, prolonged labour, collapse.
4. **Give the signal-function treatment** without delay:

| Emergency | First-line EmONC treatment (dose) |
|---|---|
| **PPH** | Massage; **oxytocin 10 IU IV/IM** + infusion (20 IU in 1 L at 60 drops/min); **TXA 1 g IV over 10 min**; fluids; examine; **misoprostol 800 µg SL** if no oxytocin; MRP if retained placenta; bimanual compression; NASG |
| **Severe pre-eclampsia / eclampsia** | **MgSO₄**: loading **4 g IV** (20% solution over 5–10 min) **+ 10 g IM** (5 g deep IM in each buttock, 50% solution with 1 mL of 2% lidocaine), then **5 g IM every 4 h** in alternate buttocks for **24 h after delivery or the last fit** (Pritchard, used in Uganda); check reflexes, RR ≥16, urine ≥30 mL/h. Antidote: **calcium gluconate 1 g IV** (10 mL of 10%). BP ≥160/110: **hydralazine 5 mg IV** slowly, repeat every 20–30 min (max 20 mg), or **nifedipine 10 mg orally** (repeat after 30 min), or labetalol |
| **Sepsis** (puerperal, septic abortion, chorioamnionitis) | **Ampicillin 2 g IV 6-hourly + gentamicin 5 mg/kg IV daily + metronidazole 500 mg IV 8-hourly**; fluids; find and remove the source (evacuate retained products, drain pus) |
| **Incomplete abortion** | **MVA** (or misoprostol 400–600 µg as per protocol) + antibiotics if infected; post-abortion family planning |
| **Prolonged 2nd stage / fetal distress** | **Vacuum extraction** if criteria are met; otherwise CS |
| **Obstructed labour / praevia / rupture** | **Caesarean section or laparotomy** (CEmONC); catheter; antibiotics; blood |
| **Birth asphyxia** | **Helping Babies Breathe**: dry, stimulate, clear airway if needed, **bag-and-mask ventilation within the Golden Minute** |

5. **Decide: treat here or refer?** If the needed signal function isn't available (e.g. CS or blood at a HC III), **stabilise and refer**.
6. **Monitor and document** (time-stamped), and **communicate** with the family.

### B. Referral: step by step

> [!UGANDA]
> Uganda's referral chain is **VHT → HC II → HC III → HC IV → General hospital → RRH → NRH**, but **emergencies should go directly to the nearest facility that can perform the needed signal function**, skipping levels where possible. Phone ahead, and use the district ambulance system where available.

1. **Decide early.** Refer when it's indicated, not when the woman is moribund.
2. **Explain** to the woman and family: why, where, the cost, and who should come (a potential **blood donor**).
3. **Stabilise before and during transfer:**
   - IV line with fluids running (two lines if bleeding)
   - **Eclampsia**: MgSO₄ loading dose given (4 g IV + 10 g IM), BP controlled
   - **PPH**: oxytocin infusion, TXA, NASG, bimanual/aortic compression during transfer if needed
   - **Sepsis**: first dose of IV antibiotics
   - **Preterm labour**: first dose of dexamethasone; baby in **kangaroo** position if born
   - **Obstructed labour**: IV fluids, **catheter**, antibiotics, no oxytocin
4. **Referral note**: identity; history; findings **with vital signs and times**; **drugs given (dose and time)**; reason for referral; name of the referring worker and a phone number. Include the partograph.
5. **Transport**: ambulance where possible; **accompanied by a health worker** who can manage complications en route, with an emergency kit (oxytocin, MgSO₄, gloves, delivery kit, bag and mask).
6. **Communicate** with the receiving facility (phone) so they can prepare theatre and blood.
7. **Counter-referral / feedback** from the receiving facility, which closes the loop and improves care.

### C. At the facility level: making EmONC work

- **Staffing**: a skilled birth attendant for every birth, 24/7; an anaesthetic officer and a doctor at every HC IV.
- **Essential supplies**: oxytocin (cold chain), misoprostol, MgSO₄, calcium gluconate, antibiotics, TXA, IV fluids, MVA kits, vacuum extractors, NASG, blood (UBTS supply chain).
- **Protocols and drills**: PPH, eclampsia and neonatal resuscitation drills (e.g. **ALSO, PRONTO, HBB, Emergency Obstetric Care skills training**).
- **Triage** and **early warning scores**; 24-hour postnatal stay; functional theatre.
- **Quality improvement**: MPDSR, near-miss reviews, criterion-based audits, clinical mentorship, supportive supervision.
- **Respectful care**: privacy screens, companions of choice, informed consent, no informal payments.

### D. At the community and population level: Safe Motherhood interventions

| Stage | Interventions (Uganda examples) |
|---|---|
| **Before pregnancy** | Girls' education, delaying marriage, **family planning** (unmet need about 1 in 5 married women), nutrition, HIV prevention |
| **Pregnancy** | **At least 8 ANC contacts** (WHO 2016; Uganda adopting), first contact in the **first trimester**; iron/folate; IPTp-SP; LLIN; TT/Td; HIV/syphilis/hepatitis B testing (eMTCT); BP and urine; **birth preparedness** (plan place of birth, transport, money, blood donor, companion, **mama kit**) |
| **Labour and birth** | **Skilled birth attendance** in a facility, **partograph/Labour Care Guide**, AMTSL, clean birth, EmONC |
| **Postnatal** | Stay ≥24 h; postnatal checks (within 24 h, day 3, days 7–14, 6 weeks); FP; danger-sign education |
| **Post-abortion care** | Emergency treatment (MVA), counselling, FP, links to other services |
| **Health system** | Maternity waiting homes; free delivery at public facilities; results-based financing and vouchers; community transport; MPDSR; data use (DHIS2) |

### E. Follow-up: MPDSR in practice (what you do after a maternal death)

1. **Notify** within **24 hours** (phone and form) to the district and MoH.
2. **Collect** the file, partograph, drug charts, referral notes; interview staff and relatives (with sensitivity).
3. **Convene the facility MPDSR committee** (ideally within 7 days): present a **summary of events** chronologically; agree the **cause of death** (underlying cause, contributing factors); identify **delays and avoidable factors** at patient, community and facility levels.
4. **Recommend actions** (SMART: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound), each with a responsible person.
5. **Implement and follow up**; report to the district and the national committee.
6. **Keep it no-name, no-blame**: focus on systems, not individuals.

## Complications

"Complications" here means the consequences of failure of safe-motherhood systems:

| For the woman | For the baby and family | For the system |
|---|---|---|
| Death; **near miss** with long-term morbidity | **Stillbirth** (intrapartum stillbirths are largely preventable) | Loss of trust leading to home births (a vicious cycle of the first delay) |
| **Obstetric fistula** (VVF/RVF) after neglected obstructed labour | Neonatal death, asphyxia, cerebral palsy | Cost of catastrophic care |
| Hysterectomy, infertility | **Orphaned children**: babies whose mothers die have much higher mortality | Health-worker burnout, moral injury |
| Sheehan's syndrome, renal failure | Household poverty, school dropout of older girls | Litigation, poor morale |
| Post-traumatic stress, depression | | |

## Ward-round & exam pearls

**Model presentation of a referred emergency:**
> "Mrs L, a 19-year-old primigravida at 38 weeks, referred from a HC III 40 km away with eclampsia. She fitted twice at home, and the family delayed 6 hours before seeking care (first delay: they consulted a herbalist first). Transport took 3 hours on a boda-boda then an ambulance (second delay). She received MgSO₄ 4 g IV and 10 g IM at the HC III at 14:00, but no antihypertensive, and no BP is recorded on the referral note. On arrival: BP 172/114, GCS 13, reflexes present, RR 18, urine protein 3+, cephalic, 4 cm, FHR 150. Plan: continue MgSO₄ maintenance, hydralazine 5 mg IV, bloods (FBC, LFTs, creatinine, clotting), catheter, deliver within 12 hours (augment or CS), and note the delays for the MPDSR/near-miss audit."

**Viva questions with answers**
1. *Define maternal death.* See definitions: while pregnant or within 42 days, related to or aggravated by pregnancy, not accidental.
2. *List the BEmONC signal functions.* Antibiotics, uterotonics, anticonvulsants (all parenteral); MRP; removal of retained products; assisted vaginal delivery; neonatal resuscitation. **CEmONC adds CS and blood transfusion.**
3. *What are the three delays? Give an intervention for each.* Deciding (education and birth preparedness), reaching (transport, waiting homes), receiving care (staffing, supplies, drills, MPDSR).
4. *What is the UN recommendation on EmONC availability?* ≥5 EmONC facilities, including ≥1 CEmONC, per 500,000 population.
5. *What is MPDSR and its principles?* Continuous identification, notification and review of deaths **with a response**; no name, no blame; confidentiality.
6. *What is SDG 3.1?* Global MMR <70 per 100,000 live births by 2030; no country >140.
7. *Why is MMR a better measure than maternal mortality rate for obstetric care?* MMR measures risk **per pregnancy/birth** (quality of obstetric care); the rate also reflects fertility levels.
8. *What is the acceptable direct obstetric case fatality rate in EmONC facilities?* **<1%**.

**OSCE tips**: in a referral station, verbalise **stabilisation before transfer**, write the **referral note with vital signs, drugs and times**, and **call the receiving facility**. In a counselling station on birth preparedness, cover **place of birth, skilled attendant, transport, money, blood donor, companion, danger signs, and items (mama kit)**.

## Mnemonics & memory aids

- **BEmONC signal functions, "AAA + MRP + RR + AVD + NR"**: **A**ntibiotics, **A**nticonvulsants, **A**nti-haemorrhagic uterotonics (all parenteral); **M**anual **R**emoval of **P**lacenta; **R**emoval of **R**etained products; **A**ssisted **V**aginal **D**elivery; **N**eonatal **R**esuscitation. **CEmONC = + "Cut and Carry blood"** (CS and transfusion).
- **Three delays: "Decide, Drive, Deliver"**: decide to seek care, drive (reach) the facility, deliver (receive) adequate care.
- **5 per 500,000, at least 1 comprehensive** ("five-for-half-a-million").
- **42 days** (maternal death) vs **1 year** (late maternal death).
- **MPDSR: "Notify in 1 day, review in 1 week, respond always."**
- **SDG 3.1: "70 by 2030."**
