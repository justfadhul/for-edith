## In a nutshell

Examining a child's **nervous system and musculoskeletal system** is a skill that rewards **patience and play** more than a reflex hammer. In infants and toddlers, most of the examination is done by **watching**: how the child lies, moves, reaches, sits, crawls, walks, talks and interacts. Hands-on and unpleasant parts (fundoscopy, reflexes, painful joints, meningism) come **last**.

On a Ugandan paediatric ward, the CNS examination is used every day for **coma** (cerebral malaria, meningitis, hypoglycaemia, poisoning), **convulsions**, **weakness** (polio-like acute flaccid paralysis, Guillain–Barré syndrome, sickle cell stroke, injection injury), **developmental delay** and **cerebral palsy**. The MSK examination catches **septic arthritis, osteomyelitis, pyomyositis, rickets, TB spine, sickle cell crises and rheumatic fever**. The examination must end in a **summary and classification** (e.g. "Blantyre 2, no meningism, no focal signs" or "UMN pattern, spastic diplegia") that tells you what to do next.

> [!PEARL]
> The one thing never to forget: **in a sick child, the neurological examination begins with ABCD and the conscious level, not with the reflex hammer.** A child who responds only to pain (P) or not at all (U) on AVPU is in coma: check glucose, stop fits, protect the airway and start treatment before you complete the rest.

## Definitions & classification

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **AVPU** | Alert, responds to Voice, responds to Pain, Unresponsive. P or U = coma (WHO ETAT) |
| **Blantyre coma scale (BCS)** | Coma scale for pre-verbal children (developed in Malawi for cerebral malaria). Score 0–5 (eyes 0–1, motor 0–2, verbal 0–2) |
| **Glasgow coma scale (GCS)**, modified for children | 3–15 (eyes 1–4, verbal 1–5, motor 1–6); verbal component adapted for infants |
| **Upper motor neurone (UMN) lesion** | Lesion of brain or spinal cord: spasticity, hyperreflexia, clonus, upgoing plantar, weakness in a pyramidal pattern |
| **Lower motor neurone (LMN) lesion** | Anterior horn cell, root, plexus or nerve: flaccid weakness, wasting, fasciculation, reduced or absent reflexes |
| **Spasticity** | Velocity-dependent increase in tone ("catch" on fast stretch; clasp-knife) |
| **Rigidity** | Increased tone throughout the range regardless of speed (lead-pipe); extrapyramidal/dystonia |
| **Hypotonia** | Reduced tone ("floppy"): central (brain: with preserved or brisk reflexes, often alert-poor) or peripheral (neuromuscular: weakness, absent reflexes, alert face) |
| **Ataxia** | Incoordination (cerebellar, sensory) |
| **Meningism** | Signs of meningeal irritation: neck stiffness, Kernig, Brudzinski, photophobia |
| **pGALS** | Paediatric Gait, Arms, Legs, Spine: a validated MSK screening examination for school-age children (Foster and Jandial) |

### Age-appropriate approach

| Age | Approach |
|---|---|
| **Newborn and young infant (<6 months)** | Observe posture (flexed in a term baby), spontaneous movements, cry, suck; tone (pull-to-sit, ventral and vertical suspension); primitive reflexes; HC, fontanelles, sutures; spine and hips |
| **Older infant and toddler (6 months–3 years)** | Observe on the parent's lap; play to test reaching, pincer, transfer, sitting balance, standing, walking; tone and reflexes once the child is settled; parachute reflex |
| **Preschool (3–5 years)** | Make it a game: "walk like a soldier, on tiptoes, on heels, hop"; "touch my finger then your nose"; jump; draw shapes |
| **School-age and adolescent** | Near-adult examination (formal cranial nerves, MRC power, sensation, coordination), pGALS, Tanner staging where relevant; respect privacy |

## Indications & contraindications

**Indications**: any child with seizures, altered consciousness, headache, fever with irritability or drowsiness, weakness or abnormal gait, developmental delay or regression, abnormal head size, suspected CP, limp, joint or bone pain or swelling, back pain or deformity, and as part of the routine newborn check.

**Precautions (not contraindications)**:
- **Suspected cervical spine injury** (trauma): do not test neck stiffness or move the neck; immobilise.
- **Raised ICP**: do not perform an LP; minimise distress.
- **Unstable child**: stabilise first (ETAT), do the full examination later.
- **Painful joints**: examine the painful joint last and gently; never force movement.
- **Suspected septic joint or fracture**: avoid weight-bearing tests; immobilise.

## Procedure step by step

### A. Introduction and observation (every child)

1. **WIPER**: wash hands, introduce yourself to the parent and child (by name), explain, get **consent** from the parent (and **assent** from the child), adequate **privacy**, expose appropriately (vest and nappy/shorts), position the child on the parent's lap or bed, **parent present**.
2. **General inspection** from the end of the bed: **ABCD**, conscious level, distress, posture, spontaneous movements (symmetry), involuntary movements (seizures, chorea, dystonia, tremor), **dysmorphic features**, **neurocutaneous stigmata** (≥6 café-au-lait macules >5 mm prepubertal or >15 mm postpubertal: neurofibromatosis type 1; ash-leaf macules and facial angiofibromas: tuberous sclerosis; port-wine stain on the face: Sturge–Weber), skin (petechiae, purpura, bruises), nutrition (wasting, oedema), aids (wheelchair, splints, AFOs, shunt reservoir behind the ear, gastrostomy).
3. **Vital signs and glucose** in an ill child (HR, RR, BP: Cushing response; temperature; SpO₂; glucose).

### B. Conscious level

| Blantyre coma scale (pre-verbal children) | Score |
|---|---|
| **Best motor response**: localises painful stimulus (firm pressure with the blunt end of a pencil on the sternum or supraorbital ridge) | 2 |
| Withdraws limb from pain (pressure on a nail bed with a pencil held horizontally) | 1 |
| No response or inappropriate response | 0 |
| **Best verbal response**: cries appropriately with pain (or speaks, if verbal) | 2 |
| Moan or abnormal cry with pain | 1 |
| No vocal response to pain | 0 |
| **Eye movements**: watches or follows (e.g. the mother's face) | 1 |
| Fails to watch or follow | 0 |
| **Total** | **0–5** (≤2 = unrousable coma; 5 = normal) |

| Modified GCS (child <5 years) | Score |
|---|---|
| **Eyes**: spontaneously 4; to voice 3; to pain 2; none 1 | 1–4 |
| **Verbal**: alert, babbles, coos or words as usual 5; fewer words than usual or irritable cry 4; cries only to pain 3; moans to pain 2; none 1 | 1–5 |
| **Motor**: normal spontaneous movements or obeys 6; localises pain (or withdraws to touch in infants) 5; withdraws from pain 4; abnormal flexion (decorticate) 3; abnormal extension (decerebrate) 2; none 1 | 1–6 |
| **Total** | 3–15; **≤8 = protect the airway** |

### C. Head and meningism

1. **Head circumference**: maximal occipitofrontal; plot; compare with previous measurements.
2. **Shape**: plagiocephaly, scaphocephaly (craniosynostosis), frontal bossing (rickets, hydrocephalus).
3. **Fontanelles and sutures** (child upright and calm): size, tension (bulging, flat, sunken), split sutures, overriding sutures (microcephaly).
4. **Signs of hydrocephalus**: scalp veins, sunsetting eyes; **shunt** (palpate along its course; press the reservoir only if trained).
5. **Neck stiffness**: with the child lying supine, gently flex the neck (or ask an older child to "kiss your knees" or look at a toy on the belly); resistance or pain = positive.
6. **Kernig's sign**: hip and knee flexed to 90°, then extend the knee: pain and resistance in the hamstrings/back = positive.
7. **Brudzinski's sign**: passive neck flexion causes involuntary flexion of the hips and knees.
8. **Infants**: bulging fontanelle, irritability when handled ("paradoxical irritability": settles when left still, cries when picked up), high-pitched cry, opisthotonus.

### D. Cranial nerves (adapted to age)

| Nerve | Infant / toddler | Older child |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Not tested | Smell (rarely needed) |
| **II** | Fixes and follows a face or bright toy (by 3 months); **red reflex**; pupils | Acuity (picture or letter chart), fields by confrontation with a toy, pupils, **fundoscopy** |
| **III, IV, VI** | Follows a toy in all directions; corneal light reflex and **cover test** for squint; nystagmus; ptosis | Eye movements in "H" pattern, diplopia, nystagmus; **sixth-nerve palsy** = false localising sign of raised ICP |
| **V** | Corneal reflex (only if comatose), rooting, suck | Facial sensation, jaw clench |
| **VII** | Facial symmetry when crying or smiling (UMN spares the forehead; LMN/Bell's affects the whole half) | Raise eyebrows, close eyes tight, show teeth, puff cheeks |
| **VIII** | Startles or turns to sound (distraction test from behind, out of sight) | Whisper test, finger rub; Rinne and Weber in older children |
| **IX, X** | Suck, swallow, cry, gag (if indicated), drooling, nasal regurgitation | Palate elevation ("say aah"), voice, swallow |
| **XI** | Head control | Shrug shoulders, turn head against resistance |
| **XII** | Tongue at rest (fasciculation in SMA), sucking | Protrude tongue (deviates towards the weak side), move side to side |

> [!UGANDA]
> In a comatose child with malaria, look at the **fundi** after dilating the pupils if possible: **malarial retinopathy** (patchy retinal whitening, vessel colour changes, white-centred haemorrhages, papilloedema) strongly supports **cerebral malaria** as the cause of coma; its absence should make you think harder about meningitis, encephalitis, hypoglycaemia or poisoning. Retinal haemorrhages without malaria in an infant with an unexplained head injury raise concern for **abusive head trauma**.

### E. Motor system

1. **Inspection**: posture (frog-leg hypotonia, scissoring, fisting with adducted thumb, hemiplegic posture), **muscle bulk** (wasting; calf pseudohypertrophy in Duchenne), **fasciculation** (tongue, limbs: SMA), involuntary movements, asymmetry, contractures.
2. **Tone**:
   - **Infants**: **pull-to-sit** (head lag beyond 4 months is abnormal), **ventral suspension** (hold prone over your hand: a floppy baby drapes like an inverted "U"), **vertical suspension** (hold under the arms: a hypotonic baby slips through your hands; a spastic baby **scissors** the legs and points the toes), "scarf sign", passive limb movement.
   - **Older children**: relaxed limbs, roll the leg, lift the knee quickly (catch), flex and extend elbow and knee at different speeds (spasticity is velocity-dependent; rigidity is not). Grade with the **Modified Ashworth Scale** (0 no increase; 1/1+ slight catch; 2 marked increase but easily moved; 3 considerable increase, passive movement difficult; 4 rigid).
   - **Ankle clonus**: brisk dorsiflexion and hold; >3–5 beats sustained = abnormal (a few beats can be normal in newborns).
3. **Power**:
   - **Infants and toddlers**: functional: kicks against gravity, reaches, bears weight, sits, stands, climbs; **Gowers sign** (rising from the floor by "climbing up" the thighs: proximal weakness, e.g. Duchenne).
   - **Older children**: **MRC scale** 0 (no contraction), 1 (flicker), 2 (movement with gravity eliminated), 3 (against gravity), 4 (against some resistance), 5 (normal). Test shoulder abduction (C5), elbow flexion (C5–6) and extension (C7), wrist extension (C6–7), finger abduction (T1), hip flexion (L1–2), knee extension (L3–4), ankle dorsiflexion (L4–5), plantar flexion (S1–2). Pronator drift.
4. **Reflexes**: child relaxed, compare sides, use reinforcement (clench teeth, Jendrassik). **Biceps C5–6, supinator C5–6, triceps C7, knee L3–4, ankle S1.** Grade 0–4+. **Plantar response**: stroke the lateral sole with a blunt object; upgoing (extensor) is normal in infants until about **12–18 months**, abnormal after walking is established.
5. **Primitive and protective reflexes** (infants): Moro, rooting, palmar and plantar grasp, ATNR, stepping, **parachute** (from 6–9 months). Note persistence and asymmetry.
6. **Coordination**: infants: reaching accuracy, tremor; older: finger-nose, heel-shin, rapid alternating movements (dysdiadochokinesia), **tandem (heel-to-toe) walking**, Romberg (sensory ataxia).
7. **Sensation** (older children, or if a spinal level is suspected): light touch, pain (blunt/sharp with care), vibration, proprioception; look for a **sensory level** (cord lesion) and saddle anaesthesia.

### F. Gait

Watch the child walk in shorts and bare feet, then on tiptoes, on heels, run, hop, and (older) heel-to-toe.

| Gait | Features | Causes (Uganda examples) |
|---|---|---|
| **Antalgic** | Short stance on the painful leg | Septic arthritis, osteomyelitis, pyomyositis, fracture, sickle crisis |
| **Trendelenburg / waddling** | Pelvis drops on the opposite side when standing on the affected leg; bilateral = waddling | DDH, Perthes disease, slipped capital femoral epiphysis, proximal myopathy (Duchenne), rickets |
| **Hemiplegic** | Arm flexed, leg extended and circumducted, toe-walking on one side | Hemiplegic CP, **sickle cell stroke**, post-meningitis |
| **Diplegic / scissoring** | Both legs stiff, adducted, toe-walking | Spastic diplegia (preterm PVL), HIV encephalopathy |
| **Ataxic** | Broad-based, unsteady, cannot tandem walk | Cerebellar lesion, post-malaria cerebellar ataxia, drug toxicity (phenytoin), posterior fossa tumour |
| **High-stepping (foot drop)** | Lifts the knee high, foot slaps | **Sciatic or common peroneal nerve injury from IM injection** in the buttock, Guillain–Barré syndrome, polio sequelae |
| **Toe walking** | Persistent tiptoe | CP, Duchenne, tight Achilles tendons, habit (idiopathic), autism |

### G. Spine and back

Inspect from behind: midline skin markers (**sacral dimple above the natal cleft, hair tuft, lipoma, haemangioma, sinus**: occult spinal dysraphism), **scoliosis** (shoulder and hip asymmetry; **Adam's forward-bend test** shows a rib hump), **kyphosis/gibbus** (**TB spine/Pott's disease**), tenderness, range of movement.

### H. Musculoskeletal: pGALS screen (school-age child), then look–feel–move

**Three screening questions**: (1) Do you (or your child) have any pain or stiffness in your joints, muscles or back? (2) Can you (or your child) dress yourself completely without any difficulty? (3) Can you (or your child) walk up and down stairs without any difficulty?

| Manoeuvre | What it tests |
|---|---|
| Observe standing (front, back, side) | Posture, alignment (genu varum/valgum), swelling, muscle wasting, leg-length discrepancy, scoliosis |
| Walk; walk on tiptoes; walk on heels | Gait, ankles, subtalar and foot joints, foot drop |
| "Hold your hands out straight in front of you" | Forward flexion of shoulders, elbow extension, wrist extension, finger extension |
| "Turn your hands over and make a fist" | Supination, small joints of fingers |
| "Pinch your index finger and thumb together" | Fine motor, index finger and thumb joints |
| "Touch the tips of your fingers with your thumb" | Manual dexterity, small finger joints |
| Squeeze the metacarpophalangeal joints | Tenderness (inflammatory arthritis) |
| "Put your hands together palm to palm, then back to back" | Wrist and finger extension and flexion |
| "Reach up and touch the sky; look at the ceiling" | Shoulders, elbows, wrists, neck extension |
| "Put your hands behind your neck" | Shoulder abduction and external rotation |
| "Try to touch your shoulder with your ear" | Cervical lateral flexion |
| "Open your mouth and put 3 of your own fingers in" | Temporomandibular joints (often affected in JIA) |
| Feel for a knee effusion (patellar tap, bulge test); active knee flexion and extension | Knee swelling and range |
| Passive hip flexion and internal rotation (lying) | Hip (loss of internal rotation is an early sign of hip disease) |
| "Bend forward and touch your toes" | Thoracolumbar flexion, scoliosis |

For any abnormal joint: **Look** (swelling, redness, deformity, wasting, scars), **Feel** (warmth, tenderness, effusion, bony tenderness at metaphyses in osteomyelitis), **Move** (active, then passive range; pain; crepitus), then examine the joint above and below and do a **neurovascular** check. In infants examine the **hips** (Ortolani and Barlow up to about 3 months; then limited abduction, leg-length difference, asymmetric thigh creases, Galeazzi sign).

### I. Completing the examination

"To complete my examination I would like to: plot the head circumference and growth, examine the other systems (abdomen for organomegaly, heart for murmurs, skin), perform fundoscopy, check the glucose, assess development, and review the history and investigations."

## Clinical acumen

### Focused history (what to ask before examining)

- **Onset and course**: sudden (stroke, trauma, haemorrhage), acute over hours to days (infection, Guillain–Barré, poisoning), subacute (TB meningitis, tumour), chronic static (CP), progressive (neurodegenerative, HIV, tumour, muscular dystrophy).
- **Fever**, headache, vomiting, neck pain, photophobia, rash → infection, raised ICP.
- **Seizures** (description, duration, focal features), **drugs** given.
- **Weakness**: where (one side, both legs, proximal, distal), ascending, bladder and bowel involvement, preceding diarrhoea or respiratory illness (Guillain–Barré), **injections** into the buttock or thigh (injection neuritis), **polio immunisation** status.
- **Joint/bone**: pain, swelling, redness, limp, refusal to walk or move a limb, trauma, fever, migratory pattern (rheumatic fever), morning stiffness (JIA), night pain (tumour), **sickle cell disease**.
- **Development** and regression; **birth history**; **head injury**.
- **Immunisation** (UNEPI: OPV, IPV, measles-rubella, Hib, PCV), **HIV status**, **TB contact**, **feeding and nutrition**.
- **Travel and context**: northern Uganda (nodding syndrome), poisoning (organophosphates, paraffin, traditional herbs).

### Examination: what experienced clinicians look for first

- **Is the child sick?** (ETAT, coma score, glucose).
- **Is there meningism or raised ICP?** (determines LP and urgency).
- **Is there a focal sign?** (pupils, eye movements, facial weakness, hemiparesis) → structural lesion, stroke, abscess, TB meningitis, focal seizure with Todd's paresis.
- **Is it UMN, LMN or muscle?** (tone, reflexes, plantar, wasting, fasciculation, Gowers).
- **Is there a spinal level?** (sensory level, bladder, saddle anaesthesia, back pain, gibbus) → cord compression is an emergency.
- **Is the head size normal and the fontanelle normal?**
- **Is there a hot joint?** → septic arthritis until proven otherwise.

### Red flags

> [!REDFLAG]
> Act now if you find:
> - **AVPU P or U, Blantyre ≤2 or GCS ≤8**: airway, glucose, treat fits, ETAT coma pathway
> - **Signs of raised ICP**: unequal/fixed pupils, posturing, irregular breathing, bradycardia with hypertension, papilloedema → no LP, head-up 30°, senior/ICU
> - **Meningism with fever or a petechial/purpuric rash** → antibiotics within the hour
> - **Acute flaccid weakness** (especially ascending, with respiratory or bulbar involvement) → AFP notification; monitor breathing (Guillain–Barré, polio)
> - **Spinal cord signs** (sensory level, urinary retention, back pain, gibbus) → urgent imaging (TB spine, tumour, Burkitt lymphoma)
> - **Hot, swollen, painful joint with fever**, or a child refusing to bear weight → septic arthritis/osteomyelitis
> - **Developmental regression** or a rapidly enlarging head
> - **Unexplained bruises, retinal haemorrhages, or fractures in a young child** → safeguarding

### Thinking like a clinician

- **Floppy infant**: alert and weak with absent reflexes and tongue fasciculation = **peripheral** (SMA); poor responsiveness with preserved reflexes and dysmorphism = **central** (HIE, Down syndrome, hypothyroidism, sepsis).
- **Ascending symmetrical weakness with areflexia** days after a diarrhoeal or respiratory illness = **Guillain–Barré syndrome**; **asymmetric flaccid paralysis with fever** in an under-immunised child = **polio** (or non-polio enterovirus): both are AFP and must be notified.
- **Unilateral foot drop after an injection in the buttock** = **sciatic nerve injection injury** (common in Uganda after IM quinine or other injections); UCG advises IM injections into the anterolateral **thigh**, never the buttock, in children.
- **Child with sickle cell disease and sudden hemiparesis** = **stroke** until proven otherwise; with **dactylitis** (painful swollen hands/feet) in infants, or **bone pain and fever** (osteomyelitis vs vaso-occlusive crisis; Salmonella).
- **Child with a limp and fever**: septic arthritis of the hip (holds hip flexed, abducted and externally rotated, refuses to move) vs transient synovitis (well, can bear some weight) vs osteomyelitis vs pyomyositis (Uganda: very common, thigh or buttock swelling).
- **Bowed legs**: physiological genu varum is normal up to about **2 years** (and genu valgum at 3–5 years); persisting, progressive, asymmetric bowing or wide wrists and a rachitic rosary = **rickets** (or Blount disease).
- **Gibbus with back pain, weight loss and leg weakness** = **Pott's disease** (spinal TB): start TB treatment and refer to protect the cord.
- **Nodding syndrome** (northern Uganda, ages 5–15): repeated head-nodding atonic seizures triggered by food or cold, cognitive decline, stunting; associated with *Onchocerca volvulus*; UCG 2023 section 9.1.2 covers it.
- **Cerebellar ataxia after cerebral malaria** is usually transient; persistent ataxia needs imaging.

## Clinical workup

For a skill topic, the workup is the **pre-examination assessment** and the **post-examination checks**.

### Step 0: First 5 minutes

1. **Is it safe to do a full examination now?** ETAT: airway, breathing (SpO₂, RR), circulation (CRT, pulse), **coma** (AVPU), **convulsions**, dehydration.
2. **Glucose** in any child with altered consciousness or seizures; treat <3.0 mmol/L (UCG; WHO <2.5, <3.0 in SAM) with 10% dextrose 5 mL/kg (neonate 2 mL/kg).
3. **If convulsing**: treat first (diazepam 0.5 mg/kg PR, max 10 mg; see Febrile Convulsions).
4. **If trauma**: immobilise the cervical spine before any neck examination.
5. **If unstable**: do a **focused** neuro exam (coma score, pupils, posture, limb movements, fontanelle, neck stiffness unless trauma) and complete the rest later.

### Step 1: Focused history

- [ ] **Onset and tempo** → acute infection vs static vs progressive.
- [ ] **Fever, headache, vomiting, rash** → meningitis, malaria, raised ICP.
- [ ] **Seizures and drugs given** → post-ictal state, drug sedation, number of benzodiazepine doses.
- [ ] **Weakness pattern, injections, immunisation** → AFP, injection injury, polio.
- [ ] **Joint/bone pain, limp, sickle cell, trauma** → septic arthritis, osteomyelitis, crisis, fracture.
- [ ] **Development, birth history, HIV, TB contact, nutrition** → CP, HIV encephalopathy, TB meningitis/spine, rickets.

### Step 2: Focused examination

Use the procedure above, prioritised by the presenting problem:

| Presentation | Must-do elements |
|---|---|
| Coma / convulsions | ABCD, Blantyre/GCS, pupils, posture, fontanelle, meningism, **fundi**, focal signs, glucose |
| Weakness | Pattern (UMN vs LMN), reflexes, sensation and level, bladder, cranial nerves (bulbar), breathing |
| Developmental delay | Observation, tone, reflexes, primitive reflexes, HC, dysmorphism, vision and hearing |
| Limp / joint pain | Gait, pGALS, look–feel–move, hips, spine, neurovascular |

| Document these positives | And these negatives |
|---|---|
| AVPU and Blantyre/GCS with components (e.g. "BCS 2 = M1 V1 E0") | "Pupils equal and reactive, no papilloedema" |
| Tone, power, reflexes per limb; plantar responses | "No neck stiffness, fontanelle soft" |
| Head circumference with centile | "No sensory level; bladder not palpable" |
| Joint findings (swelling, warmth, range) | "No spinal deformity or midline skin markers" |

### Step 3: Bedside tests

| Test | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Glucose** | Hypoglycaemia (coma, seizures) |
| **Malaria RDT/smear** | Cerebral malaria, malaria with seizures |
| **SpO₂, RR, peak cough/vital capacity (older child), single-breath count** | Respiratory muscle weakness in Guillain–Barré |
| **Hb (HemoCue), sickle test** | Severe anaemia; sickle cell disease in stroke/dactylitis |
| **Urine dipstick, bladder scan or palpation** | Retention in cord compression |
| **Fundoscopy** | Papilloedema, malarial retinopathy, retinal haemorrhages |
| **Head circumference plotted** | Micro- or macrocephaly, crossing centiles |
| **Joint aspiration (by a trained clinician)** | Pus in septic arthritis (Gram stain, culture) |

### Step 4: Laboratory & imaging

| Test | When | Expected / abnormal finding | How it changes management |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lumbar puncture (CSF)** | Meningism, coma without contraindication, suspected GBS (after a week) | Bacterial: neutrophils, low glucose, high protein; TB: lymphocytes, very high protein, low glucose; GBS: high protein with normal cells | Antibiotics/TB treatment/IVIG. HC IV (if trained) and hospitals; culture at RRH/NRH |
| **FBC, CRP/ESR, blood culture** | Fever, joint/bone infection | Leucocytosis, raised CRP/ESR | Antibiotics; monitoring response |
| **Electrolytes, calcium, magnesium** | Seizures, weakness, tetany | Hyponatraemia, hypocalcaemia (rickets), hypokalaemia (periodic paralysis) | Correction |
| **CK** | Proximal weakness, Gowers sign, calf hypertrophy | Very high in Duchenne; high in myositis | Diagnosis, genetic counselling |
| **Stool samples ×2** (24 h apart, within 14 days) | **Acute flaccid paralysis** | Poliovirus or non-polio enterovirus | Public health response; sent via the district surveillance officer to the national polio lab (UVRI) |
| **Joint/bone X-ray** | Limp, swelling, deformity, rickets | Effusion, periosteal reaction (osteomyelitis after ~10–14 days), metaphyseal cupping and fraying (rickets), fracture | Surgical drainage, rickets treatment. All hospitals |
| **Ultrasound of joint (hip)** | Painful hip | Effusion | Guided aspiration |
| **Spine X-ray** | Gibbus, back pain, scoliosis | Vertebral collapse, disc space loss (TB), Cobb angle | TB treatment, orthopaedic/neurosurgical referral |
| **CT / MRI brain or spine** | Focal signs, raised ICP, suspected abscess, stroke, cord compression | Infarct, abscess, tumour, hydrocephalus, cord compression | Neurosurgery, stroke management. CT at RRH/NRH/Mengo; MRI in Kampala |
| **EEG** | Seizures of unclear type, encephalopathy, nodding syndrome | Epileptiform activity | Antiepileptic choice. Mulago, CoRSU, private |
| **Nerve conduction studies** | GBS, neuropathy | Demyelination/axonal loss | Diagnosis. Very limited (Mulago, private) |

### Step 5: Putting it together

**Model summary of findings**
> "On examination, a 3-year-old boy, Blantyre 2 (motor 1, verbal 1, eyes 0) one hour after his last seizure, with equal reactive pupils, no papilloedema but bilateral malarial retinopathy (retinal whitening and haemorrhages), no neck stiffness, normal tone with symmetrical brisk reflexes and flexor plantars, no focal deficit and no evidence of raised ICP; glucose 5.2 mmol/L; malaria smear positive. Summary: unrousable coma with malarial retinopathy, consistent with cerebral malaria; meningitis not excluded."

> [!EXAM]
> **Worked example: localising weakness.** A 6-year-old has had 4 days of progressive weakness starting in the legs and now the arms, after a diarrhoeal illness 2 weeks ago. Findings: symmetrical weakness (legs MRC 2/5, arms 4/5), **absent reflexes**, flexor plantars, no sensory level, bladder normal, single-breath count 12. **Interpretation:** LMN pattern, ascending, areflexic, no cord level = **Guillain–Barré syndrome** (an AFP case: notify and send stool samples). Falling breath count signals impending respiratory failure: monitor 4-hourly, refer to HDU/ICU, specialist IVIG if available. Compare: a **sensory level at T10 with brisk reflexes and urinary retention** = cord compression (TB spine, tumour): urgent imaging.

## Differential diagnosis

| Finding | Differentials to consider |
|---|---|
| **Coma with fever** | Cerebral malaria, bacterial meningitis, TB meningitis, viral encephalitis, sepsis, post-ictal state, hypoglycaemia |
| **Coma without fever** | Poisoning (organophosphates, alcohol, benzodiazepines), hypoglycaemia, head injury (including abuse), DKA, uraemia, intracranial bleed, status epilepticus |
| **Acute flaccid weakness** | Guillain–Barré syndrome, poliomyelitis/non-polio enterovirus, transverse myelitis, injection neuritis, hypokalaemia, botulism, tick paralysis, spinal cord compression (early) |
| **Spastic weakness** | CP, HIV encephalopathy, stroke (sickle cell), spinal cord lesion (TB spine, tumour), hereditary spastic paraplegia, konzo (cassava-related, regional) |
| **Floppy infant** | Central (HIE, Down syndrome, sepsis, hypothyroidism, metabolic) vs peripheral (SMA, congenital myopathy, myasthenia) |
| **Ataxia** | Post-infectious cerebellitis (varicella), drug toxicity (phenytoin, carbamazepine), posterior fossa tumour, cerebral malaria sequelae |
| **Painful swollen joint** | Septic arthritis, osteomyelitis (adjacent), sickle crisis, rheumatic fever (migratory), JIA, haemophilia, trauma, leukaemia |
| **Limp** | Septic arthritis, transient synovitis, osteomyelitis, pyomyositis, Perthes, SCFE, DDH, fracture, injection injury, leukaemia, Burkitt lymphoma |

## Investigations

| Test | What you are looking for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Glucose, malaria test | Hypoglycaemia, malaria | Commonest treatable causes of coma and fits in Uganda |
| CSF analysis | Meningitis/encephalitis/GBS | Specific treatment |
| Imaging (CT/MRI, X-ray, USS) | Structural lesions, effusions, bone changes | Surgery or specific therapy |
| CK, electrolytes | Myopathy, metabolic causes | Diagnosis |
| Stool for poliovirus (AFP) | Polio | Eradication surveillance |
| Joint aspirate | Pus, organisms | Septic arthritis treatment |

## Management

The examination itself has no complications if done gently, but **what you find must lead to action**. "Management" here means acting on abnormal findings and caring for the child during the examination.

### A. Acting on what you find

| Finding | Immediate action |
|---|---|
| **Coma (AVPU P/U, BCS ≤2, GCS ≤8)** | Airway (recovery position, suction, oxygen; airway adjunct if needed), **glucose** (10% dextrose 5 mL/kg if <3.0 mmol/L), treat seizures, **IV artesunate** (3 mg/kg if <20 kg; 2.4 mg/kg if ≥20 kg) if malaria positive, **ceftriaxone 100 mg/kg/day** if meningitis not excluded, maintenance fluids (avoid overload), 2-hourly neuro-observations (BCS, pupils), turn 2-hourly, NG feeds, eye care |
| **Signs of raised ICP** | **Do not do an LP**; head-up 30°, head midline; avoid hypotonic fluids and fluid overload; treat fever and seizures; oxygen; senior and anaesthetic/ICU review; CT when stable; specialist mannitol or hypertonic saline only on senior advice (WHO advises against routine mannitol/steroids in cerebral malaria) |
| **Meningism** | LP if no contraindication; start **ceftriaxone 100 mg/kg/day** (max 4 g) without delay (UCG 2023) |
| **Acute flaccid paralysis** (<15 years) | Notify the district surveillance focal person; **2 stool samples 24 h apart within 14 days** (kept cold); **no IM injections** (UCG); monitor breathing and swallowing; physiotherapy and positioning; GBS → HDU/ICU monitoring, IVIG where available |
| **Spinal cord compression** | Lie flat, catheterise if retention, urgent imaging and neurosurgical/orthopaedic referral; start TB treatment if Pott's disease is likely; dexamethasone for tumour only on specialist advice |
| **Focal deficit in sickle cell disease** | Oxygen, hydration, urgent **exchange (or simple) transfusion** per protocol, CT, then long-term transfusion/hydroxyurea programme |
| **Septic arthritis / osteomyelitis** | Analgesia, splint, blood culture, **joint aspiration** or surgical drainage (hip = surgical emergency), **cloxacillin 50 mg/kg IV 6-hourly** (UCG 2023), adding **ceftriaxone** in children with a negative Gram stain or sickle cell disease (Salmonella); IV for 2 weeks then oral to complete 4 weeks (longer for osteomyelitis) |
| **Rickets** | Vitamin D and calcium, diet and sunlight advice, follow-up X-ray and ALP |
| **Developmental delay / CP signs** | HIV test, hearing and vision, MDT referral (see Cerebral Palsy and Growth & Development topics) |
| **Unexplained injuries or retinal haemorrhages** | Safeguarding: admit, document carefully, skeletal survey, inform the senior and the social worker/probation officer |

### B. Drugs you may start after the examination

| Drug | Dose | Route | Frequency | Duration / max | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **10% dextrose** | **5 mL/kg** | IV | Bolus; recheck in 30 min | | For glucose <3.0 mmol/L (UCG; WHO <2.5, <3.0 in SAM) |
| **Diazepam** | **0.5 mg/kg** (rectal) or **0.2–0.3 mg/kg** (IV) | PR / IV | Once; repeat once after 10 min | **Max 10 mg/dose**; 2 doses total | For a seizure ≥5 min |

| **Cloxacillin** | **50 mg/kg/dose** | IV | **6-hourly** | 2 weeks IV, then oral to 4 weeks total (adult max 2 g/dose) | Septic arthritis, osteomyelitis, pyomyositis (UCG 2023) |

| **Ibuprofen** | **5–10 mg/kg** | Oral | 6–8-hourly | Max 30–40 mg/kg/day | Musculoskeletal pain (avoid if dehydrated, renal impairment, bleeding) |

### C. Care during the examination

1. Keep the child **warm** and comfortable; the parent holds or stays with the child.
2. Do the **unpleasant parts last** (fundoscopy, reflexes, meningism, painful joints, pain stimulus for coma scoring).
3. Use the **least painful stimulus** that gives the answer (pencil on the nail bed or sternum; never needles or pinching that bruises).
4. **Explain findings** to the parents in simple words and **document** carefully, including the time (coma scores change).

## Ward-round & exam pearls

### Model presentation

> "Akello is a 3-year-old girl from Masaka weighing 12 kg (WAZ −1.0, HAZ −0.8, WHZ −0.9, MUAC 14.5 cm), fully immunised per the UNEPI card, HIV-negative, on a family diet, admitted 6 hours ago with 2 days of fever and 3 generalised seizures. On neurological examination 1 hour after the last seizure, she is responding to pain only; Blantyre coma scale 2 (withdraws from pain, M1; moans, V1; not following, E0). Pupils are 3 mm, equal and reactive; fundoscopy shows patchy retinal whitening and white-centred haemorrhages. There is no neck stiffness, Kernig's sign is negative, and there are no signs of raised ICP. Tone is normal, reflexes are symmetrical and brisk, plantars are flexor, with no focal deficit. Glucose 4.6 mmol/L; malaria smear 4+ (180,000/µL); Hb 7.2 g/dL. **Impression: cerebral malaria (BCS 2 with malarial retinopathy) with repeated convulsions; bacterial meningitis not excluded.** Plan: IV artesunate 36 mg (3 mg/kg) at 0, 12 and 24 h, LP when stable (no contraindication now) or ceftriaxone 1.2 g/day empirically, seizure management, 2-hourly BCS and glucose, maintenance fluids, NG feeds after 24 h if still unconscious."

> [!NOTE]
> In the model presentation above the motor score is 1 (withdraws), so BCS = M1 + V1 + E0 = 2. Always state the **components**, not just the total; it shows you know the scale and allows others to track change.

### Viva questions (with answers)

1. **Describe the Blantyre coma scale.** Motor 0–2 (localises, withdraws, none), verbal 0–2 (appropriate cry, moan, none), eyes 0–1 (watches/follows or not); total 5; ≤2 = coma.
2. **How do you distinguish UMN from LMN weakness?** UMN: increased tone, brisk reflexes, clonus, upgoing plantars, little wasting. LMN: decreased tone, reduced/absent reflexes, wasting, fasciculation.
3. **When is an upgoing plantar normal?** In infants until about 12–18 months.
4. **What is Gowers sign?** Using the hands to "climb up" the legs to stand from the floor: proximal (pelvic girdle) weakness, classically Duchenne.
5. **Signs of raised ICP in a child?** Bulging fontanelle, unequal pupils, posturing, focal deficit, irregular breathing, bradycardia with hypertension, papilloedema, sixth-nerve palsy, falling coma score.
6. **What do you do with a case of AFP?** Notify, collect 2 stool samples 24 h apart within 14 days, avoid IM injections, monitor breathing.
7. **Name the pGALS screening questions.** Pain or stiffness in joints, muscles or back; difficulty dressing; difficulty with stairs.
8. **Why is the hip held flexed, abducted and externally rotated in septic arthritis?** This position maximises the joint capsule volume and reduces pain from the effusion.

### OSCE tips

- **Narrate**: "I am now assessing tone by..." helps the examiner follow you.
- In an infant, **start with observation and play**; a crying baby gives you nothing. Ask the parent to help.
- Always mention **head circumference, fundoscopy and glucose** even if you are not asked to perform them.
- Finish with a **localising summary**: level (cerebral, cerebellar, spinal, nerve, muscle), side, likely cause, and the next investigation.

## Mnemonics & memory aids

- **Reflex roots "1-2, buckle my shoe (S1–2 ankle); 3-4, kick the door (L3–4 knee); 5-6, pick up sticks (C5–6 biceps/supinator); 7-8, lay them straight (C7–8 triceps)."**
- **Blantyre "2-2-1"**: Motor 2, Verbal 2, Eyes 1 = maximum 5.
- **UMN = "everything UP"**: tone up, reflexes up, plantars up. **LMN = "everything DOWN"**: tone down, reflexes down, muscle bulk down (plus fasciculations).
- **Raised ICP in infants "FIBS"**: **F**ontanelle bulging, **I**rritability/high-pitched cry, **B**ig head (crossing centiles), **S**unsetting eyes and split sutures.
- **Septic hip, Kocher criteria "FEW-W"**: **F**ever >38.5°C, **E**SR >40 mm/h, **W**hite cells >12 × 10⁹/L, **W**on't bear weight. The more present, the more likely septic arthritis (all 4: very likely).
- **AFP "2-24-14"**: **2** stool samples, **24** h apart, within **14** days.

### Classic exam traps

- **"Neck stiffness is absent, so this 8-month-old does not have meningitis."** Wrong: meningism is unreliable under 12–18 months; look at the fontanelle and behaviour.
- **"Upgoing plantar in a 9-month-old means UMN disease."** Wrong: normal until about 12–18 months.
- **"Do an LP to diagnose meningitis in a child with unequal pupils."** Wrong: raised ICP; treat empirically, LP later.
- **"Test neck stiffness in a child after a fall from a tree."** Wrong: immobilise the cervical spine first.
- **"Report only total coma scores."** Wrong: report components.
- **"Give an IM injection to a child with acute flaccid paralysis."** Wrong: IM injections can worsen paralysis (provocation polio); UCG says avoid.
- **"Bowed legs at 18 months = rickets."** Not necessarily: physiological genu varum is normal until about 2 years; look for other rickets signs.
- **"Septic hip can wait for an X-ray to show changes."** Wrong: X-rays are normal early; ultrasound and aspiration urgently.

### Questions seniors ask

**How would you assess the conscious level of a 2-year-old with cerebral malaria?**
AVPU first, then the Blantyre coma scale with components (motor 0–2 with pencil pressure on the sternum and nail bed, verbal 0–2, eyes 0–1); repeat 2-hourly; ≤2 = coma.

**This child has brisk reflexes and upgoing plantars on one side. Where is the lesion?**
Upper motor neurone, contralateral cerebral hemisphere or brainstem (or ipsilateral spinal cord if there is a sensory level); look for a cause such as stroke (sickle cell), abscess, TB meningitis or a post-ictal Todd's paresis.

**How do you examine tone in a 6-month-old?**
Pull-to-sit (head control), ventral suspension (posture over the hand), vertical suspension (slipping through or scissoring), passive limb movements, and check for persistent primitive reflexes.

**What would make you think a floppy infant has a neuromuscular rather than central cause?**
Alert face with good social interaction, marked weakness, absent reflexes, tongue fasciculation, frog-leg posture (SMA); central causes have reduced alertness, dysmorphism or seizures with preserved or brisk reflexes.

**A 7-year-old walks with a high-stepping gait on the right after a "quinine injection" last month. What happened and what do you do?**
Sciatic (or common peroneal) nerve injection injury from an IM buttock injection; examine power and sensation, refer for physiotherapy and an AFO, consider nerve studies, and report it; prevent by giving IM injections in the anterolateral thigh.

**What would you do for a 10-year-old with a hot, swollen knee and fever?**
Analgesia, splint, FBC/CRP/blood culture, urgent aspiration (Gram stain, culture), IV cloxacillin 50 mg/kg 6-hourly (plus ceftriaxone if sickle cell disease or Gram-negative), orthopaedic review for washout.

**Why examine the fundi in a comatose child in Uganda?**
Malarial retinopathy supports cerebral malaria; papilloedema suggests raised ICP (no LP); retinal haemorrhages without malaria in an infant raise concern for abusive head trauma.
