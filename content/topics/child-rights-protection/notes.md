## In a nutshell

**Child rights** are the entitlements every person under 18 has to **survive, develop, be protected and participate**. **Child maltreatment** (abuse and neglect) is any act or failure to act by a parent, carer or person in authority that results in actual or potential harm to the child's health, survival, development or dignity.

Maltreatment is **common in Uganda** and mostly **hidden**: in the home, in school, in the community, by people the child knows and trusts. It presents to health workers disguised as an injury, a burn, failure to thrive, a vaginal discharge, a teenage pregnancy, a behavioural problem or a child brought "late".

Health workers have three jobs:
1. **Recognise** it: think of abuse when the story doesn't fit the injury.
2. **Respond medically**: treat injuries, prevent HIV, pregnancy and STIs, and document as legal evidence.
3. **Protect**: keep the child safe and **report** to the police, the Probation and Social Welfare Officer (PSWO) or the **Sauti 116** helpline, working with social workers.

> [!PEARL]
> The one thing never to forget: **the child is your patient, and the child's safety comes first.** If you suspect abuse and the child would go home to the abuser, admit the child (hospital as a place of safety) while you involve the PSWO and police. Never confront a suspected abuser in a way that puts the child at more risk.

## Definitions & classification

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Child** | Any person under **18 years** (UNCRC Art. 1; African Charter Art. 2; Uganda Constitution; Children Act) |
| **Child maltreatment** (WHO) | All forms of physical and/or emotional ill-treatment, sexual abuse, neglect or negligence and commercial or other exploitation that harm, or could harm, the child's health, survival, development or dignity, in a relationship of responsibility, trust or power |
| **Physical abuse** | Intentional use of force: hitting, kicking, shaking, burning, scalding, poisoning, suffocating, excessive corporal punishment |
| **Sexual abuse** | Involving a child in sexual activity they cannot understand or consent to, or that breaks the law: contact (touching, penetration) and non-contact (exposure, pornography, grooming). In Uganda any sexual act with a person under 18 is **defilement** |
| **Emotional abuse** | Persistent rejection, threats, humiliation, terrorising, isolating, exposure to domestic violence |
| **Neglect** | Persistent failure to meet basic needs (food, shelter, hygiene, supervision, health care, education, emotional care), when the carer could reasonably do so |
| **Exploitation** | Child labour (hazardous work), commercial sexual exploitation, trafficking, use in armed conflict, begging |
| **Harmful traditional practices** | Child marriage, female genital mutilation (FGM), "ebiino" (extraction of infants' canine tooth buds), child sacrifice, witchcraft accusations, scarification |
| **Defilement** (Penal Code s.129) | Unlawful sexual act with a person under 18 |
| **Aggravated defilement** (s.129(3)–(4)) | Victim under 14; offender HIV-positive; offender is a parent, guardian or person in authority; offender a serial offender; or victim has a disability |

### The four categories of rights (UNCRC)

| Category | Examples | Health worker's role |
|---|---|---|
| **Survival** | Life, health care, nutrition, clean water (Art. 6, 24) | Treat, immunise, feed |
| **Development** | Education, play, rest, freedom of thought (Art. 28, 31) | Growth and development monitoring, ECD |
| **Protection** | From violence, abuse, neglect, exploitation, harmful practices, child labour (Art. 19, 32, 34–39) | Recognise, treat, report |
| **Participation** | To be heard in decisions affecting them (Art. 12), freedom of expression | Listen to children; assent; confidentiality for adolescents |

## Epidemiology & Uganda context

- **WHO (global):** about **6 in 10** children under 5 regularly suffer physical punishment and/or psychological violence from caregivers; **1 in 5 women and 1 in 7 men** report being sexually abused as a child; about 40,000 child homicides a year, many misattributed to falls, burns or drowning.
- **Uganda Violence Against Children Survey (VACS, 2015):** about **1 in 3 girls (35%)** and **1 in 6 boys (17%)** experienced sexual violence in childhood; around **6 in 10 girls and 7 in 10 boys** experienced physical violence, most often by parents, caregivers and **teachers**; few children told anyone and fewer received services (VERIFY figures against the published report).
- **Child marriage and adolescent pregnancy:** about one-third of Ugandan women aged 20–24 married before 18, and about **1 in 4 girls aged 15–19** have begun childbearing (UDHS). Every pregnancy in a girl under 18 involves an offence (defilement), though prosecution depends on the circumstances.
- **Defilement** is among the most reported crimes in Uganda's annual police crime reports. Most offenders are known to the child: neighbours, relatives, teachers, boda-boda riders, "boyfriends" and peers.
- **Other Ugandan concerns:** child labour (agriculture, domestic work, mining, stone quarrying, fishing), street children (Kampala, from Karamoja), trafficking, **FGM** in Sebei and Pokot communities (banned by the Prohibition of FGM Act 2010), **child sacrifice** (rare but reported), witchcraft accusations against children with disabilities, and violence in **refugee** settings.

### Ugandan legal and policy framework

| Instrument | Key provisions for health workers |
|---|---|
| **UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989)** | Ratified by Uganda **1990**. Four principles; Art. 19 (protection from violence), Art. 24 (health), Art. 34 (sexual exploitation) |
| **African Charter on the Rights and Welfare of the Child (1990)** | Ratified **1994**. Child = under 18 without exception; Art. 21 bans **child marriage** and harmful practices; children have duties too |
| **Constitution of Uganda (1995)** | **Art. 34**: rights of children (to know and be cared for by parents, education, health, protection from exploitation and hazardous work); **Art. 31**: marriage only from 18 |
| **Children Act (Cap 59), amended 2016** | Defines child as under 18; **welfare principle** (best interests); parental responsibility; care and protection orders; Family and Children Court; role of the **PSWO**; 2016 amendment strengthened protection from **violence, harmful practices, child labour and trafficking**, and banned **corporal punishment in schools** (VERIFY section numbers; the Act was renumbered in the 2023 revised laws) |
| **Penal Code Act (as amended 2007)** | **s.129 defilement** (under 18), **aggravated defilement** (death penalty maximum); rape; indecent assault; age of criminal responsibility **12** (Children Act). An offender under 18 is dealt with under the Children Act |
| **Prohibition of FGM Act (2010)** | Criminalises FGM, including procuring or failing to report it |
| **Domestic Violence Act (2010)** | Protection orders; health workers record and refer |
| **Prevention of Trafficking in Persons Act (2009)** | Trafficking of children is aggravated |
| **Employment Act (2006) and Employment of Children Regulations (2012)** | Restricts child labour; no hazardous work under 18 |
| **National Child Policy (2020)** and **National Plan of Action on VAC** | Coordinated prevention and response |

> [!UGANDA]
> The people in the protection system you will work with: the **Probation and Social Welfare Officer (PSWO)** at the district (statutory lead for child protection), the police **Child and Family Protection Unit (CFPU)**, the **LC1 chairperson**, the hospital **medical social worker**, community development officers, NGOs (e.g. ANPPCAN, Raising Voices, Plan, World Vision) and the **Sauti 116** national child helpline (free, 24 hours). Courts: the **Family and Children Court** (for care orders and children in conflict with the law) and the High Court for aggravated defilement.

## Pathophysiology: why abuse happens and how it harms

### Risk factors (ecological model)

| Level | Risk factors |
|---|---|
| **Child** | Age under 4 (physical abuse) or adolescence (sexual abuse), disability, prematurity, chronic illness, crying infant, unwanted child, orphan, girl (sexual abuse, marriage), living away from parents |
| **Caregiver** | Young age, substance (alcohol) misuse, mental illness, own history of abuse, unrealistic expectations, step-parent or non-relative in the home |
| **Family** | Domestic violence, poverty, overcrowding, social isolation, family breakdown, polygamous conflicts |
| **Community/society** | Social acceptance of corporal punishment, gender inequality, conflict and displacement, weak law enforcement, "settling" defilement cases between families for money |

### How maltreatment harms the child

- **Physical**: injuries, disability, death; abusive head trauma kills or disables about one-third of victims.
- **Toxic stress**: chronic activation of the stress response damages the developing brain (memory, emotional regulation), immune function and growth. Adverse childhood experiences (**ACEs**) increase lifelong risk of depression, substance misuse, HIV, chronic disease and becoming a perpetrator (the **intergenerational cycle**).
- **Sexual**: STIs including HIV, pregnancy (high risk of obstructed labour, fistula, unsafe abortion and death in young adolescents), genital injury, school drop-out, PTSD.

## Clinical acumen

### Focused history

Take the history from the carer, then, **if the child is old enough and willing, from the child alone** (with a chaperone of the child's choosing).

| Ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Exactly how did the injury happen?** Who saw it? When? | A vague, changing or implausible story is the key warning sign |
| **Delay** in seeking care | Delay without good reason suggests concealment |
| Is the mechanism compatible with the child's **developmental stage**? | "He rolled off the bed and broke his femur" in a 2-month-old who cannot roll: inconsistent |
| **Previous injuries**, admissions, burns | Pattern of repeated harm |
| **Who lives at home** and who looks after the child | Identifies possible perpetrators and protective adults |
| **Domestic violence**, alcohol, mental illness | Risk factors |
| **Growth, feeding, immunisation, school attendance** | Neglect |
| For sexual abuse: **disclosure in the child's own words** (record verbatim), timing (within 72 hours?), type of contact, pain, bleeding, discharge, menarche, contraception | Medical needs (PEP, EC), forensic sampling, legal evidence |
| **Behaviour changes**: withdrawal, aggression, sexualised behaviour, bed-wetting, school refusal, self-harm | Emotional and sexual abuse |

> [!NOTE]
> Let a child **disclose freely**. Use open questions ("Tell me what happened"), avoid leading questions ("Did your uncle touch you?"), do not promise to keep secrets, tell the child they did the right thing by telling you, and write down their exact words. Repeated interviews re-traumatise the child and weaken the court case: one good interview is better than many.

### Examination

- Do a **full top-to-toe examination** with the child undressed in stages (keep warm, respect privacy), with a chaperone.
- **Growth**: weight, height, head circumference, Z-scores (neglect).
- **Skin**: bruises (site, size, colour, shape), burns, bites, scars of different ages. Use a **body map**; measure with a ruler.
- **Head**: scalp (bald patches from hair pulling, swellings), **fontanelle**, ears (bruising inside/behind), eyes (subconjunctival haemorrhage; **fundoscopy** for retinal haemorrhages in infants).
- **Mouth**: torn **frenulum** in an infant (force-feeding, blow), dental injuries, palatal petechiae (oral sex).
- **Skeleton**: tenderness, swelling, deformity, reduced movement.
- **Abdomen**: bruising, tenderness (visceral injury, duodenal haematoma).
- **Anogenital examination**: only after consent/assent, ideally **once**, by the most experienced person available, in the **frog-leg** or knee-chest position; look for bruising, lacerations, bleeding, discharge, warts, hymenal transections (a normal hymen does **not** exclude abuse: most sexually abused children have normal genital findings).
- **Development and behaviour** (frozen watchfulness in toddlers).

### Red flags

> [!REDFLAG]
> - **Any bruise in a baby who is not yet mobile** (under about 6 months, not cruising).
> - **TEN-4-FACESp** bruising (see High-yield).
> - **Patterned injuries**: hand marks, loop marks (electric cable, belt), bite marks, ligature marks on wrists/ankles or neck.
> - **Burns**: immersion scalds with clear "glove and stocking" margins or sparing of the buttocks/flexures; cigarette burns (round, 5–10 mm, uniform depth); contact burns shaped like an iron or hot plate.
> - **Fractures in non-mobile infants**, posterior rib fractures, metaphyseal fractures, multiple fractures of different ages.
> - **Infant with unexplained encephalopathy**, seizures or apnoea (abusive head trauma).
> - **STI in a prepubertal child** (gonorrhoea, syphilis beyond the neonatal period, trichomonas, HIV not explained by vertical transmission), **pregnancy under 14**.
> - **A child who says they were abused.** Believe them and act.

### Thinking like a clinician

- **The history is the main diagnostic tool**: an injury that is **unexplained, inconsistent with development, or explained differently each time** is abuse until proven otherwise.
- **Consider mimics, but don't let them stop you**: dermal melanocytosis (Mongolian blue spots: present since birth, slate-grey, don't change), bleeding disorders (check FBC and clotting), osteogenesis imperfecta (blue sclerae, family history), rickets, impetigo (vs cigarette burns), cultural practices (scarification, cupping, herbal burns), accidental straddle injuries (anterior, unilateral), lichen sclerosus and urethral prolapse (vs sexual abuse).
- **Neglect vs poverty**: a family that is poor but uses available resources (brings the child for care, accepts food support) is struggling, not neglectful. A family that repeatedly fails to give prescribed ART, misses every review and leaves a toddler alone all day is neglecting the child.
- **Adolescent "relationships"**: a 15-year-old with a 25-year-old "boyfriend" is a defilement case and possibly exploitation (transactional sex, "sugar daddy"). A consensual relationship between two 16-year-olds is still legally defilement, but the Children Act diverts child offenders; handle with judgement and counselling, involving the PSWO.
- **Don't be the investigator.** Your job is medical care, careful documentation and referral; the police and PSWO investigate.

## Clinical workup

### Step 0: First 5 minutes

1. **ABC and life-threatening injuries first** (head injury, abdominal bleeding, burns with shock, poisoning). Use ETAT.
2. **Safety**: is the child safe right now? Who is with them? If the suspected abuser is present and the child is frightened, separate gently (e.g. for an "X-ray" or "weighing").
3. **Time since sexual assault**: if within **72 hours**, prioritise **PEP** and **emergency contraception** (start within the hour if possible) and **forensic samples** before washing.
4. Call for help: the senior on call, the medical social worker; don't manage suspected abuse alone.

### Step 1: Focused history

- [ ] Mechanism, timing, witnesses, delay. *Plausibility.*
- [ ] Developmental stage. *Could the child have done this?*
- [ ] Previous injuries/admissions. *Pattern.*
- [ ] Household members and carers. *Risk and protection.*
- [ ] Child's own account (verbatim). *Disclosure and evidence.*
- [ ] For sexual assault: time, type of contact, menarche, symptoms. *PEP, EC, samples.*
- [ ] HIV status, immunisation (HepB, tetanus). *Prophylaxis.*

### Step 2: Focused examination

- General: growth (Z-scores), hygiene, demeanour.
- Skin and body map; head, eyes (fundi in infants), mouth, ears; skeleton; abdomen.
- Anogenital (once, by the most experienced examiner, with consent/assent; colposcope or good light).
- Document **positive and negative** findings.

### Step 3: Bedside tests

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| **Urine pregnancy test** | Every post-menarchal girl before EC/PEP and as evidence |
| **HIV rapid test** | Baseline before PEP (do not delay PEP for the result) |
| **RBS, Hb** | Neglect, malnutrition, bleeding |
| **Urine dipstick** | Haematuria (abdominal/renal trauma) |
| **Photography** (with consent, scale ruler in the picture) | Legal documentation |

### Step 4: Laboratory & imaging

| Test | When | Expected / abnormal finding | How it changes management | Availability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FBC, platelets, PT/INR, APTT** | Unexplained bruising or bleeding | Normal results support inflicted injury; abnormal suggests a bleeding disorder | Excludes mimic (ITP, haemophilia, vitamin K deficiency, leukaemia) | HC IV (FBC); clotting at hospitals |
| **Skeletal survey** | All children **under 2 years** with suspected physical abuse; consider to 3 years | Occult fractures (ribs, metaphyses), fractures of different ages | Strong evidence of abuse | Regional/national referral hospitals |
| **Repeat skeletal survey (11–14 days)** | If first survey is equivocal | Healing fractures become visible | Confirms injury | As above |
| **CT head** | Infant with encephalopathy, seizures, scalp swelling, or under 1 year with any suspected abuse | Subdural haemorrhage (different ages), skull fractures, hypoxic injury | Abusive head trauma; neurosurgery | Mulago, some regional referral hospitals, private |
| **Fundoscopy (by ophthalmologist)** | Suspected abusive head trauma | Extensive multilayered retinal haemorrhages | Supports AHT | Regional/national referral hospitals |
| **Liver enzymes, amylase, urinalysis** | Abdominal injury | Raised (occult visceral injury) | Imaging (ultrasound/CT) | Hospitals |
| **STI tests**: HIV, syphilis (RPR/TPHA), HBsAg; vaginal/urethral swabs for gonorrhoea, chlamydia, trichomonas (NAAT/culture where available) | Sexual abuse | Positive | Treatment; legal significance in prepubertal children | HIV, RPR, HBsAg at HC III–IV; swabs at hospitals |
| **Forensic swabs** (vaginal, anal, oral, skin; clothing in paper bags) | Sexual assault within 72 h (up to 7 days for some samples) | Semen/DNA | Prosecution | Collected at facility; analysed at the Government Analytical Laboratory (Wandegeya) via police |
| **Age assessment** (dental/X-ray) | Age disputed (legal relevance: under 14 = aggravated) | Estimated age range | Charge category | Hospitals, police surgeon |

### Step 5: Putting it together

**Model one-liner:** "A 4-month-old boy (5.2 kg, WAZ −1.4), non-mobile, brought with a 'swollen thigh after falling from the bed 3 days ago', has a spiral femoral fracture, two bruises on the cheek and a torn upper frenulum; the history does not explain the findings and care was delayed."

**Problem list:** (1) femoral fracture; (2) suspected physical abuse (bruising in a non-mobile infant, delay, implausible mechanism); (3) safety of the infant and siblings; (4) rule out a bleeding disorder or bone disease.

**Worked interpretation:** "fell from the bed" (a short fall of under 1 m) rarely causes serious injury in an infant and cannot explain facial bruises plus a frenulum tear; a non-mobile infant cannot generate the twisting force of a spiral femoral fracture. Plan: admit (place of safety), analgesia and orthopaedic care, skeletal survey, CT head (age <1 year), fundoscopy, FBC and clotting, body map and photographs, inform the PSWO and police (CFPU), and screen siblings.

## Differential diagnosis

| Finding | Abuse | Mimic / accidental explanation | How to tell apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blue-grey patches** | Bruises (change colour over days, tender, swollen) | **Dermal melanocytosis** (Mongolian blue spots): present since birth, sacrum/buttocks/back, non-tender, unchanged | Document at birth; review in a few days |
| **Multiple bruises** | Inflicted injury | **ITP, haemophilia, vitamin K deficiency, leukaemia, von Willebrand** | FBC, film, clotting, family history |
| **Fractures** | Inflicted injury | **Osteogenesis imperfecta, rickets, osteopenia of prematurity**, birth injury (clavicle), accidental fall (toddler's spiral tibial fracture) | Blue sclerae, dentinogenesis, family history; rickets signs and alkaline phosphatase; developmental stage |
| **Round burns** | Cigarette burns (uniform, deep, 5–10 mm) | **Impetigo** (crusted, varying size, spreads), insect bites | Culture, response to antibiotics, pattern |
| **Scald** | Immersion (sharp margins, symmetrical, buttock sparing) | Spill/splash (irregular, arrow-shaped, asymmetrical, on the front) | Pattern and history |
| **Linear marks** | Beating with a stick or cable | **Traditional scarification**, cupping, herbal burns | History, cultural context (still harmful if painful/dangerous) |
| **Genital bleeding or bruising** | Sexual abuse | **Accidental straddle injury** (anterior, unilateral labial), **urethral prolapse**, **lichen sclerosus**, vulvovaginitis, foreign body, precocious puberty | Examination pattern, history consistency |
| **Encephalopathy in an infant** | Abusive head trauma | Meningitis, cerebral malaria, metabolic disease, accidental major trauma (road traffic) | CT, LP, fundoscopy, history |
| **Failure to thrive** | Neglect | Organic disease: HIV, TB, cardiac, renal, malabsorption, SAM from food insecurity | Growth on admission (gains in hospital = neglect/feeding), tests |

| Test | What you are looking for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| FBC, clotting | Bleeding disorder | Excludes mimic |

## Management

### A. Immediate medical care

1. Treat life-threatening injuries (ETAT, surgical care, burns care).
2. **Analgesia**: paracetamol **15 mg/kg** 6-hourly (max 60 mg/kg/day); ibuprofen **10 mg/kg** 8-hourly (max 40 mg/kg/day; avoid if bleeding risk or dehydration); morphine for severe pain (0.1 mg/kg IV/SC, titrated).
3. Admit if the child needs treatment **or is unsafe at home**.

### B. Sexual abuse/defilement: medical management (step by step)

1. **Consent/assent**: explain each step; the child can say "stop". Consent for examination and forensic sampling from the parent/guardian (if not the suspect) or, if needed, proceed in the child's best interests with the PSWO/police.
2. **Forensic evidence first** (if within 72 hours, before washing, ideally before medication that requires undressing): swabs, clothing in **paper** bags, chain-of-custody form, labelled and sealed, handed to the police.
3. **HIV PEP** if within **72 hours** (sooner is better; ideally within 2 hours).
4. **Emergency contraception** for post-menarchal girls (or Tanner stage 3+), within 72 hours, up to 120 hours.
5. **STI prophylaxis.**
6. **Hepatitis B vaccination** (if not fully immunised) and **tetanus** if wounds.
7. **Baseline tests**: HIV, syphilis, HBsAg, pregnancy.
8. **Psychosocial first aid** and referral for counselling.
9. **Report** (police CFPU, PSWO) and complete **Police Form 3A**.
10. **Follow-up**: PEP adherence and side-effects (2 weeks), HIV test at **6 weeks and 3 months** (and 6 months per some guidance), pregnancy test at 2–3 weeks if EC failed, syphilis repeat, psychological support, school, legal process.

Use whatever your ART clinic stocks; **starting promptly matters more than the exact regimen**.

### C. Physical abuse and neglect

1. Treat injuries and pain; admit the child (and consider siblings' safety).
2. Investigations above (skeletal survey, CT head, clotting).
3. **Document**: history verbatim, body map, measured injuries, photos.
4. **Inform** the PSWO and police CFPU the same day; involve the medical social worker.
5. **Multidisciplinary meeting** (paediatrician, social worker, PSWO, police) to plan safety and discharge: back home with support, to a relative (kinship care), or to a place of safety by **care order** (Family and Children Court).
6. For neglect: nutrition rehabilitation, catch-up immunisation, treat illness, link the family to social protection (e.g. cash transfers, OVC programmes), parenting support, and follow-up home visits.

### D. Documentation and the Police Form

- **Police Form 3 (PF3)**: medical examination of a person with injuries (assault). **Police Form 3A (PF3A)**: medical examination of victims of **sexual offences**. The police issue the form; the examining medical officer completes it and may be summoned to court.
- Write **legibly**, factually and in plain language: describe injuries (site, size in cm, shape, colour, pattern), state whether findings are **consistent** with the history, record what the child said **in their own words**, and date, time and sign.
- **Never** write "no evidence of rape" when the examination is normal: write "normal examination; a normal examination does not exclude sexual abuse".
- **Do not delay treatment** waiting for the police form; treat first, then complete the form when it arrives.

```
       ├─► Police Child & Family Protection Unit  →  PF3 / PF3A, investigation, court
       ├─► District Probation & Social Welfare Officer  →  safety plan, care order, follow-up
       ├─► LC1 chairperson (community)   ·   Sauti 116 helpline (toll-free, 24 h)
       └─► Follow-up: medical (PEP, HIV tests), psychosocial, school, legal
```

> [!UGANDA]
> **"Settling" defilement out of court** (the family accepts money or a marriage arrangement from the offender) is illegal and leaves the child unprotected. Explain this kindly to families, and report anyway. Health workers have a duty to act in the child's best interests; withholding information that a child is being abused is not protected by confidentiality (see *Professionalism & Ethics*).

### F. Prevention (what clinicians can do)

- Ask about **discipline and violence** at routine visits; teach **positive parenting** (e.g. Parenting for Lifelong Health, Raising Voices' Good School toolkit).
- Recognise and support **at-risk families** (young mothers, disability, alcohol, domestic violence).
- **Comprehensive sexuality education**, adolescent-friendly services, keeping girls in school.
- **Birth registration** (proof of age protects against child marriage and child labour).
- Advocate against corporal punishment and harmful practices (FGM, ebiino).

## Complications

- **Death** (abusive head trauma, abdominal injury, burns, starvation).
- **Disability**: cerebral palsy, blindness, epilepsy after head trauma; contractures after burns.
- **Sexual and reproductive**: HIV and other STIs, pregnancy (obstructed labour, fistula, unsafe abortion), genital injury.
- **Psychological**: PTSD, depression, anxiety, self-harm and suicide, substance misuse, conduct disorder, poor school performance.
- **Social**: school drop-out, early marriage, homelessness, revictimisation and intergenerational violence.
- **Iatrogenic**: secondary trauma from repeated interviews and examinations, stigma when confidentiality is breached.

## Ward-round & exam pearls

**Model case presentation (child):**
> "Atim is a 7-year-old girl from Wakiso, weight 19 kg (WAZ −0.9), height 117 cm (HAZ −0.8), fully immunised for age per her card, HIV status negative (tested today), brought by her mother with vaginal bleeding and pain on passing urine since last evening. She told me, in her words, 'the neighbour's son took me inside and hurt me down there' yesterday at about 4 pm, about 20 hours ago. On examination she is distressed but stable; there is a fresh posterior fourchette laceration and bruising of the labia minora. Summary: sexual assault of a 7-year-old within 72 hours, with genital injury, which constitutes aggravated defilement (victim under 14). Plan: forensic swabs collected and handed to police with chain of custody, HIV PEP (ABC/3TC + pDTG for 28 days by weight band), STI prophylaxis, hepatitis B vaccine catch-up, analgesia, PF3A completed, PSWO and police CFPU informed, psychosocial support, and follow-up for HIV testing at 6 weeks and 3 months."

**Likely viva questions (with answers)**

1. *Who is a child in Uganda?* Anyone under 18.
2. *What are the four general principles of the UNCRC?* Non-discrimination, best interests, survival and development, participation.
3. *Define aggravated defilement.* Defilement where the victim is under 14, has a disability, or the offender is HIV-positive, a parent/guardian/person in authority, or a serial offender.
4. *What is TEN-4-FACESp?* A bruising clinical decision rule: Torso, Ears, Neck bruising under 4 years, any bruise under 4 months, or bruising of the Frenulum, Angle of jaw, Cheek, Eyelid, Subconjunctiva, or Patterned bruising.
5. *What do you give a 15-year-old girl seen 30 hours after rape?* PEP (TLD 28 days if ≥30 kg), levonorgestrel 1.5 mg, STI prophylaxis, HepB vaccine, pregnancy and HIV tests, counselling, PF3A, report.
6. *Which fractures are most specific for abuse?* Posterior rib fractures, metaphyseal corner fractures, scapular, spinous process and sternal fractures, multiple fractures of different ages.
7. *What is the role of the PSWO?* Statutory child-protection officer at district level: investigates welfare, arranges safe placement and care orders, coordinates services.

**OSCE tips:** stations include **taking a history from a parent where the story changes**, **counselling a mother after her child's defilement**, and **completing a body map/PF3**. Stay non-judgemental, keep the child's safety central, explain what you must report and why, and never promise confidentiality you cannot keep.

## Mnemonics & memory aids

- **UNCRC principles "N-B-S-P"**: **N**on-discrimination (Art. 2), **B**est interests (Art. 3), **S**urvival and development (Art. 6), **P**articipation (Art. 12). Articles **2-3-6-12**.
- **Rights categories "SDPP"**: **S**urvival, **D**evelopment, **P**rotection, **P**articipation.
- **TEN-4-FACESp**: **T**orso, **E**ars, **N**eck (under **4** years); any bruise under **4** months; **F**renulum, **A**ngle of jaw, **C**heek, **E**yelid, **S**ubconjunctival; **p**atterned.
- **Aggravated defilement "14-HIV-PAD"**: under **14**, offender **HIV**+, **P**arent/guardian/person in authority, **A**gain (serial), victim with **D**isability.
- **After sexual assault "PEP-PEP-SHOT"**: **P**EP, **E**mergency contraception, **P**regnancy test; **P**rophylaxis for STIs, **E**vidence (forensic), **P**olice form 3A; **S**upport (psychosocial), **H**epatitis B vaccine, **O**ther injuries, **T**etanus.
- **Neglect "FAIL"**: **F**ailure to thrive, **A**bandonment/absent supervision, **I**mmunisations missed, **L**ack of medical care.

### Classic exam traps

- **"She consented, so it isn't defilement."** Wrong: under 18 consent is legally irrelevant.
- **"A normal hymen excludes sexual abuse."** Wrong: most abused children have **normal** genital findings.
- **"Wait for the HIV test result before starting PEP."** Wrong: start PEP at once; adjust if the baseline test is positive (then it becomes ART).
- **"Ask the child leading questions to get the full story."** Wrong: use open questions; record verbatim; avoid repeated interviews.
- **"The family has settled the matter with the neighbour; no need to report."** Wrong: defilement is a criminal offence; report and protect the child.
- **"Blue spots on the buttocks of a baby = abuse."** Could be **dermal melanocytosis**; check the history and whether they change.
- **"Confront the father on the ward."** Wrong: keep the child safe, involve the PSWO/police and the senior team.
- **"Fill the PF3 before treating."** Wrong: treat first; documentation follows.

### Questions seniors ask

**What makes you suspect that an injury was not accidental?**
No or changing explanation, a mechanism incompatible with the child's development, delay in seeking care, injuries of different ages, patterned or high-risk sites (TEN-4-FACESp), and the child's or a witness's disclosure.

**Which investigations do you request for a 6-month-old with an unexplained fracture?**
Skeletal survey (repeat in 2 weeks), CT head, fundoscopy, FBC and clotting, bone profile (calcium, phosphate, ALP, vitamin D), and screen siblings.

**A 13-year-old is pregnant and says the father is her 19-year-old boyfriend. What do you do?**
This is aggravated defilement (under 14). Provide antenatal care and counselling, assess safety, test for HIV/syphilis, report to the police CFPU and PSWO, and involve the social worker; her pregnancy is high risk (anaemia, pre-eclampsia, obstructed labour).

**What PEP would you give a 22 kg child?**
ABC/3TC 120/60 mg 3 tablets + pDTG 10 mg 3 tablets (or DTG 50 mg one tablet) once daily for 28 days (20–24.9 kg band), or ABC + 3TC + LPV/r per UCG 2023 if DTG is unavailable.

**Who do you report to, and does confidentiality stop you?**
Police CFPU and the district PSWO (and the hospital social worker); Sauti 116 for advice. Confidentiality does not cover concealing abuse of a child; share what is needed for protection, with the people who need to know.

**How do you tell neglect from poverty?**
Ask whether the carer uses the resources available; poverty with good care-seeking and adherence is not neglect. Support poor families with social protection; act on persistent failure to meet the child's needs despite support.

**What is the role of the doctor in court?**
To present the medical findings factually: what was found, whether it is consistent with the account, and the limits of what medicine can say. You are a professional witness, not an advocate for either side.
