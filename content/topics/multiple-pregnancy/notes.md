## In a nutshell

- A **multiple pregnancy** is one with **two or more fetuses**. Twins are by far the commonest; triplets and higher-order multiples are rare and usually follow fertility treatment.
- **Sub-Saharan Africa has the highest twinning rates in the world** (mainly dizygotic twins), so you will see twins on every Ugandan labour ward.
- Twins are **high-risk for everyone**: the mother has more **anaemia, pre-eclampsia, preterm labour, malpresentation, operative delivery and PPH**; the babies have more **prematurity, growth restriction, anomalies and intrapartum hypoxia** (especially **twin 2**), and **monochorionic twins** share a placenta, which brings unique complications (TTTS, TAPS, co-twin death).
- **Chorionicity, not zygosity, determines risk.** Determine it by ultrasound early (ideally 11–14 weeks).
- The one thing never to forget: **after the first twin is born, do not give oxytocin/ergometrine for the third stage** (you would trap twin 2). **Check the lie of the second twin** and be ready to act.

## Definitions & classification

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Zygosity** | The number of **fertilised eggs** (genetics): **dizygotic (DZ)** = two eggs + two sperm ("non-identical", "fraternal"); **monozygotic (MZ)** = one egg splits ("identical") |
| **Chorionicity** | The number of **placentas (chorions)**: **dichorionic (DC)** or **monochorionic (MC)** |
| **Amnionicity** | The number of **amniotic sacs**: **diamniotic (DA)** or **monoamniotic (MA)** |
| **DCDA** | Two placentas (may be fused), two sacs. **All DZ twins** and about 25–30% of MZ twins. The lowest risk |
| **MCDA** | One placenta, two sacs. About 70–75% of MZ twins. At risk of **TTTS, TAPS, sFGR** |
| **MCMA** | One placenta, one sac. About 1–2% of MZ twins. At risk of **cord entanglement** and sudden death |
| **Conjoined twins** | Incomplete division of the embryonic disc (after day 13) |
| **Discordant growth** | EFW difference ≥20–25% of the larger twin's weight: (larger EFW − smaller EFW) ÷ larger EFW × 100 |
| **Selective FGR (sFGR)** | One twin's EFW <10th centile, usually with discordance ≥25% |
| **TTTS** | Twin-twin transfusion syndrome (see below) |
| **TAPS** | Twin anaemia-polycythaemia sequence: tiny anastomoses → one twin anaemic, the other polycythaemic, **without** liquor discordance |
| **TRAP** | Twin reversed arterial perfusion: an **acardiac twin** perfused by the "pump" twin |
| **Vanishing twin** | Early loss of one twin, usually in the first trimester |

### Timing of the split in monozygotic twins

```
Day of splitting after fertilisation  →  Resulting type          % of MZ twins
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Day 0–3   (morula, before chorion forms)   →  DCDA                  ~25–30%
Day 4–8   (blastocyst, chorion formed)     →  MCDA                  ~70–75%
Day 8–12  (after amnion forms)             →  MCMA                  ~1–2%
Day 13+   (embryonic disc)                 →  Conjoined twins       rare
```

**Memory aid:** "the later the split, the more they share". First they share nothing, then the chorion, then the amnion, then the body.

> [!EXAM]
> Examiners love this: **"Are all DCDA twins non-identical?"** No. All DZ twins are DCDA, but about a quarter of MZ twins are also DCDA (early split). **Different-sex twins are always DZ.** Same-sex DCDA twins may be either, and only DNA testing proves zygosity. **All MC twins are MZ.**

## Epidemiology & Uganda context

- **Monozygotic twinning is constant worldwide** at about **3.5–4 per 1,000 births**: it is a random event, not inherited, and not affected by race or age.
- **Dizygotic twinning varies widely**, driven by FSH levels (multiple ovulation). It is highest in **sub-Saharan Africa** (often around 18–28 per 1,000 births; the Yoruba of Nigeria and parts of Benin are the famous examples), intermediate in Europe/USA, and lowest in East Asia.
- **Risk factors for DZ twins**: **Black African ethnicity**, **maternal family history** (the mother's side matters, because it is about her ovulation), **older maternal age** (peaks at 35–39 years, as FSH rises), **high parity**, **tall/heavier women**, **ovulation induction** (clomiphene, gonadotropins) and **IVF** (multiple embryo transfer).
- Because DZ twins predominate in Africa, the **proportion of MC twins is lower** in Uganda than in Europe (where about 1 in 3 twins are MC). But MC twins still occur and carry most of the fetal risk.
- Twins account for a disproportionate share of **perinatal deaths** (perinatal mortality several times higher than for singletons) and **preterm births** (about half of twins deliver before 37 weeks).

> [!UGANDA]
> **Cultural context**: among the Baganda, twins (*abalongo*) are special. There are traditional names (e.g. **Babirye/Nakato** for girls, **Waswa/Kato** for boys) and ceremonies, and the mother is called *Nalongo* and the father *Salongo*. Families are usually delighted, but twins bring real **financial strain** (feeding, transport for ANC, hospital delivery costs). Uganda MoH policy is that **twins should be delivered at a facility with capacity for caesarean section and newborn care (HC IV or hospital)**, not at HC II/III or at home. Undiagnosed twins (no scan) still present in labour at lower-level facilities, and a "retained second twin" is a classic referral.

## Pathophysiology

### Why twins cause more maternal problems

| Physiological change (exaggerated in twins) | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Higher hCG** | More **hyperemesis gravidarum** |
| **Greater plasma volume expansion** (about 500 mL more than singletons) + **higher iron and folate demand** | **Anaemia** (dilutional and iron/folate deficiency) |
| **Larger placental mass** → more sFlt-1 | **Pre-eclampsia** 2–3 times more common, earlier and more severe |
| **Larger placental mass** → more human placental lactogen | **Gestational diabetes** |
| **Uterine overdistension** → gap junctions, oxytocin receptors | **Preterm labour, PPROM** |
| Large placental surface area | **Placenta praevia** |
| Sudden decompression after twin 1 | **Placental abruption** before twin 2 |
| **Overdistended uterus contracts poorly** + large placental bed | **PPH** (atonic) |
| Increased cardiac output and pressure on organs | Breathlessness, back pain, oedema, varicose veins, reflux |
| **Two fetuses competing for space** | **Malpresentation** (especially twin 2), cord prolapse, locked twins |

### Why monochorionic twins are special: the shared placenta

In **every MC placenta** the two fetal circulations are connected by **vascular anastomoses** on the chorionic plate:

- **Artery-to-artery (AA)** and **vein-to-vein (VV)** anastomoses: superficial and **bidirectional**. They balance blood flow (AA anastomoses are protective against TTTS).
- **Artery-to-vein (AV)** anastomoses: deep, through a shared cotyledon, and **unidirectional**: blood from twin A's artery drains into twin B's vein.

**TTTS** develops when **unbalanced AV anastomoses** send net blood from one twin (the **donor**) to the other (the **recipient**) without enough AA compensation:

| Donor twin | Recipient twin |
|---|---|
| **Hypovolaemia** → renin-angiotensin activation | **Hypervolaemia** → atrial natriuretic peptide release |
| **Oliguria** → **oligohydramnios** (deepest pocket <2 cm): the "**stuck twin**" plastered to the uterine wall | **Polyuria** → **polyhydramnios** (deepest pocket >8 cm) |
| Small or absent bladder | Large bladder |
| **Growth restriction**, anaemia | **Cardiac overload** → cardiomyopathy, tricuspid regurgitation, **hydrops** |
| Abnormal umbilical artery Doppler | Abnormal ductus venosus Doppler |

The polyhydramnios also causes **preterm labour and PPROM**. Without treatment, severe mid-trimester TTTS has a perinatal mortality of **80–90%**.

**Why co-twin death is so dangerous in MC twins:** when one MC twin dies, its circulation becomes a low-pressure sink. The surviving twin **exsanguinates acutely into the dead twin** through the anastomoses → hypotension, brain ischaemia. The survivor has about a **15% risk of death** and about a **25% risk of neurological injury**. In DC twins (no anastomoses) the risks are much lower (about 3% and 2%), and the main risk is preterm labour.

**MCMA twins** share one sac: **cord entanglement** is almost universal, so there is a risk of sudden unpredictable death. Hence early planned **caesarean** at 32–34 weeks.

**Locked twins** happen when twin 1 is **breech** and twin 2 is **cephalic**: as twin 1's body delivers, its chin locks against twin 2's chin above the pelvic brim.

## Clinical acumen

### Focused history

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| LNMP, dating and **first scan**: was chorionicity reported? | Chorionicity determines the whole care pathway |
| **Family history of twins** (on the mother's side) | DZ twinning is inherited through the mother |
| **Fertility treatment** (clomiphene, IVF)? | Increases DZ twins and triplets |
| **Excessive vomiting** in early pregnancy? | Hyperemesis (↑ hCG) |
| **Breathlessness, tiredness, dizziness**? | Anaemia; pulmonary oedema |
| **Headache, visual disturbance, epigastric pain, swelling**? | Pre-eclampsia (commoner and earlier in twins) |
| **Rapidly increasing abdominal size, tightness, breathlessness** in mid-pregnancy | **Polyhydramnios**: think **TTTS** in MC twins |
| **Reduced fetal movements** (hard to judge with two), bleeding, leaking, contractions | Fetal compromise, praevia/abruption, PPROM, preterm labour |
| Previous CS, previous PPH, parity | Affects mode of delivery; PPH risk |
| HIV status, Hb at booking | Anaemia, eMTCT plan |
| Social: distance to hospital, finances, support | Plan admission near term; birth preparedness |

### Examination

1. **General**: pallor (anaemia), BP (pre-eclampsia), oedema, weight.
2. **Abdominal inspection**: abdomen larger than dates, often globular.
3. **Fundal height larger than dates** (by ≥3–4 cm). Differential: wrong dates, polyhydramnios, macrosomia, fibroids, molar pregnancy (early).
4. **Palpation**: **multiple fetal poles** (two heads, or three or more poles), **many fetal parts**, a **small head relative to the uterine size**.
5. **Auscultation**: two fetal hearts heard by two examiners simultaneously at different points, with rates differing by **≥10 bpm**. Unreliable on its own; **ultrasound confirms**.
6. **In labour**: after twin 1 delivers, the **uterus is still large** and another fetus is palpable. Never be surprised by the second twin: palpate the abdomen after **every** delivery before giving oxytocin.

### Ultrasound: chorionicity

| Sign | Appearance | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Lambda (λ) / twin-peak sign** | Triangular tongue of placental (chorionic) tissue extending into the base of the inter-twin membrane | **Dichorionic** |
| **T sign** | Thin membrane meeting the placenta at a right angle with no tissue between | **Monochorionic diamniotic** |
| No dividing membrane | Single sac; cords may be entangled | **Monoamniotic** |
| Two separate placentas | Two distinct placental masses | Dichorionic |
| Different sexes | | Dizygotic → dichorionic |
| Membrane thickness | Thick (>2 mm, 4 layers: amnion-chorion-chorion-amnion) vs thin (<2 mm, 2 layers: amnion-amnion) | DC vs MC (less reliable later) |

> [!PEARL]
> Chorionicity is **most accurate at 11–14 weeks** (the lambda sign fades as the pregnancy advances, and after 20 weeks it can be impossible to be sure). If chorionicity is uncertain, **manage as monochorionic** (the higher-risk assumption).

**Labelling**: label the twins (e.g. **twin 1 = the lower/presenting twin**, or "left/right" with a description) consistently on every scan, so that growth trends are tracked for the right baby.

### Red flags

> [!REDFLAG]
> - **Rapidly enlarging abdomen, breathlessness, tense uterus** in an MC twin pregnancy → **TTTS** (urgent scan)
> - **Hypertension, proteinuria, headache, epigastric pain** → pre-eclampsia (earlier and more severe in twins)
> - **Contractions, leaking liquor, pelvic pressure** before 37 weeks → preterm labour/PPROM
> - **Hb <10 g/dL** (and worse, <7) → anaemia needing treatment
> - **Reduced fetal movements**, **discordant growth ≥25%**, abnormal Doppler, single fetal death
> - In labour: **transverse lie of twin 2**, **cord prolapse**, **fetal bradycardia of twin 2**, **bleeding after twin 1** (abruption), **undiagnosed second twin** after a delivery at a lower-level facility
> - After delivery: **heavy bleeding** (atonic PPH)

### Thinking like a clinician

- **"Large for dates" is twins until proven otherwise** in Uganda, if there has been no scan. Get an ultrasound.
- **Ask one question about any twin pregnancy: "What is the chorionicity?"** It determines scan frequency, the delivery date, and what can go wrong.
- **Anticipate the complications in order**: early (hyperemesis, miscarriage, vanishing twin, anomalies), middle (TTTS, anaemia, preterm labour), late (pre-eclampsia, growth discordance, malpresentation), labour (twin 2 problems), postpartum (PPH, breastfeeding difficulty, depression).
- **Twin 2 is the vulnerable one in labour**: after twin 1 is born, the uterus shrinks and the placenta may partly separate, the cord can prolapse and the lie can change. Twin 2 has more hypoxia, operative delivery and death, especially if the **inter-twin interval** is long.
- **Differential for "polyhydramnios in twins"**: TTTS (MC only, with an oligohydramnios co-twin), diabetes, anomalies (oesophageal atresia), infections. A **big difference in liquor between MC twins is TTTS until proven otherwise.**
- **Discordant growth in DC twins** is usually placental insufficiency of one placenta; in MC twins it is sFGR (unequal placental sharing) or TTTS.
- **Common student mistakes**: forgetting to palpate for a second baby before giving oxytocin; giving ergometrine or oxytocin after twin 1; calling "same sex = identical"; forgetting that pre-eclampsia risk means **aspirin** may be indicated; not preparing **two neonatal resuscitation sets**; forgetting iron and folate.


## Clinical workup

Twins present to you in two ways: at **ANC** (large for dates, or a scan report saying "twins") and on the **labour ward** (in labour, bleeding, hypertensive, or after one baby has already been born somewhere else). The workup always answers: **How many babies? What chorionicity? Is the mother safe (BP, Hb, bleeding)? Are both babies well and growing equally? Where, when and how will they be born?**

### Step 0: First 5 minutes

1. **Vital signs and shock index** (HR ÷ SBP; **≥0.9** abnormal): twins mean more **APH, PPH and anaemia**. BP for **pre-eclampsia** (commoner and earlier in twins); temperature (PPROM, chorioamnionitis).
2. **Is she bleeding?** Antepartum: think **praevia** (no VE until scan) or **abruption**. Postpartum: **atonic PPH** → call for help, massage, oxytocin (only once all babies are out), TXA, the E-MOTIVE bundle.
3. **Has a baby already been born?** A woman arriving after delivering one baby at home or at an HC III with a large uterus = **retained second twin**: **no oxytocic**, check lie and FHR of twin 2, prepare to deliver (see the red flag box in Management).
4. **Is she in labour, and how far?** Contractions, bearing down, visible presenting part. Late preterm or term twins in advanced labour are delivered **where you are**, with help called: two resuscitation sets, oxytocin drawn up but **not** given.
5. **Both fetal hearts**: two examiners or a Doppler/ultrasound, identifying which heart belongs to which twin. **Twin 2 bradycardia after twin 1** = expedite.
6. **Quick bedside scan** (if available) for **number, presentation of twin 1, lie of twin 2, placental site**: this decides vaginal birth vs caesarean.
7. **Call**: the most experienced obstetrician available, midwife, anaesthetist (theatre may be needed for twin 2), and the neonatal team. **IV access (16–18 G)**, Hb and **group and cross-match 2 units**.
8. At HC II/III with undiagnosed twins in early labour: **refer** (twins should be born where there is theatre). If delivery is imminent, deliver twin 1, **no oxytocic**, and refer at once if twin 2 is transverse or delayed.

> [!REDFLAG]
> Before giving the third-stage uterotonic after any birth, **palpate the abdomen**. A uterus that is still large after the baby is out is a second twin until proven otherwise.

### Step 1: Focused history

| Ask | Why it matters (what answer changes the plan) |
|---|---|
| **Dating**: LNMP, earliest scan (use the **larger twin's CRL** for dating) | Delivery dates depend on GA (DCDA 37, MCDA 36 weeks); steroids if preterm |
| **Has any scan reported chorionicity?** At what gestation? | The whole care pathway depends on it; a report after 20 weeks is less reliable. **Unknown = manage as MC** |
| **Family history of twins** (mother's side), **fertility treatment** (clomiphene, IVF) | DZ likelihood; triplets after treatment |
| **Hyperemesis** in the first trimester | Supports multiple pregnancy (higher hCG) |
| **Rapidly increasing abdominal size, breathlessness, tightness** (16–26 weeks) | **Polyhydramnios** → in MC twins, **TTTS** (urgent specialist scan) |
| **Headache, visual symptoms, epigastric pain, swelling** | **Pre-eclampsia** (earlier and more severe) |
| **Tiredness, dizziness, breathlessness**; iron/folate adherence | **Anaemia** (greater demand) |
| **Contractions, leaking, pelvic pressure** before 37 weeks | **Preterm labour/PPROM** → steroids, MgSO₄ <32 weeks, transfer |
| **Bleeding** (painless or painful) | Praevia (large placental area) or abruption |
| **Fetal movements** | Hard to judge with two, but a change matters (fetal death, sFGR) |
| **Previous CS, previous PPH, parity** | Mode of delivery; PPH preparedness |
| **HIV status, syphilis, Rh group** | eMTCT plan; anti-D |
| **Aspirin, calcium, IPTp** taken? | Pre-eclampsia and malaria prevention |
| **Distance from hospital, money, support** | Admission near term (e.g. 36 weeks); birth preparedness |

### Step 2: Focused examination

**General**
- Pallor (Hb), **BP**, oedema, weight; signs of heart failure or breathlessness (polyhydramnios, anaemia).

**Abdomen**
- **Symphysis-fundal height larger than dates** by ≥3–4 cm; a globular abdomen.
- **Palpation**: count the **poles** (two heads, or three or more poles), **multiple small parts**, a head that seems **small for the size of the uterus**. State the **lie and presentation of each twin** and which one is lower (twin 1 = the presenting twin).
- **Liquor**: tense, shiny uterus with a fluid thrill = polyhydramnios.
- **Auscultation**: **two fetal hearts** heard at the same time by two people at different points, differing by **≥10 bpm**, supports twins but is **not reliable alone**: confirm by ultrasound.
- Uterine tenderness (abruption, chorioamnionitis), contractions.

**Vaginal examination** (in labour, after praevia is excluded)
- Dilatation, membranes, presenting part of **twin 1**, station, cord.

**In the second stage after twin 1 is born**
- Palpate again: **lie and presentation of twin 2**, stabilise it longitudinally, **FHR of twin 2**, then a **VE** (membranes, presenting part, cord).

**Document the key negatives**: "BP 118/72, no pallor, SFH 38 cm at 32 weeks, twin 1 cephalic 3/5, twin 2 breech, two FHRs 140 and 128, uterus soft and non-tender, no bleeding".

### Step 3: Bedside tests

| Test | How / what to look for | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Point-of-care ultrasound** | Number of fetuses, **presentation of twin 1**, **lie of twin 2**, viability, placental site, gross liquor difference | Mode of delivery (**twin 1 non-cephalic → CS**); praevia → no VE; big liquor difference in MC twins → TTTS |
| **FHR of both twins** (Pinard/Doppler; dual-channel CTG where available) | Two different rates, each identified (label twin 1 and twin 2) | Fetal well-being; make sure you are not counting the same heart twice (check the maternal pulse too) |
| **Hb (HemoCue)** | **<11 g/dL** anaemia; **<7** severe | Iron, transfusion planning, PPH reserve |
| **Urine dipstick** | Protein (pre-eclampsia), nitrites/leucocytes (UTI), glucose | PE work-up; treat UTI (preterm labour trigger) |
| **BP** at every visit | ≥140/90 | Pre-eclampsia pathway |
| **RBS** | Raised | GDM screen (commoner in twins) |
| **mRDT** | Positive | Treat malaria (preterm labour, anaemia) |
| **Bedside clotting test** | No clot by 7 min | Abruption, retained dead twin, pre-eclampsia with bleeding |
| **Partograph** | As for singletons, from active labour | Labour progress for twin 1; the inter-twin interval is recorded separately |

### Step 4: Laboratory & imaging

| Test | When | Expected / abnormal finding | How it changes management |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First-trimester scan** (number, viability, **chorionicity/amnionicity**, CRL dating, NT) | **11–14 weeks** (ideally; any first scan). | **Anomaly scan** | **18–22 weeks**. Regional/national referral, private | Cardiac and neural tube anomalies (commoner in MZ twins) | Counselling; place of birth |
| **Growth scans**: EFW each twin, **deepest vertical pocket (DVP)**, bladders, umbilical artery Doppler | **DC: every 4 weeks from 20 weeks. | **MCA peak systolic velocity** (MCA-PSV) Doppler | MC twins from about 20 weeks; any single fetal death in MC twins. Specialist centres only | Classic criteria: **>1.5 MoM** in one twin (anaemia) and **<1.0 MoM** in the other (polycythaemia) = **TAPS** (newer criteria use the difference between the twins) | Fetal medicine referral; delivery planning |
| **Full blood count / Hb** | **Booking, 20–24 weeks and 28 weeks** (more often if low), and before delivery. All HC IVs and hospitals | Hb <11 (twin demand for iron and folate) | Treat with 120 mg elemental iron daily; plan blood for delivery |
| **Blood group, Rh, antibody screen** | Booking; **group and cross-match 2 units** for delivery. HC IV with blood bank upward | Rh negative | Anti-D; blood ready for PPH |
| **HIV, syphilis, HBsAg** | Booking and retest per protocol. All levels (rapid tests) | Positive | eMTCT; infant prophylaxis for **both** babies |

| **Pre-eclampsia bloods** (FBC, LFT, creatinine) | If hypertensive or proteinuric. Hospitals | Platelets <100, AST >70, creatinine >90 | Severe PE pathway; delivery |

| **Placental examination** (± histology) | After every twin birth. Histology at referral hospitals | Number of placentas; **thin translucent membrane** (MC) vs thick opaque (DC) | Confirms chorionicity; audit; counselling for future |

> [!UGANDA]
> Most Ugandan women with twins are first diagnosed on a **third-trimester scan** or in labour, when chorionicity can no longer be read reliably. Ask for an **early scan (before 14 weeks)** when you suspect twins at booking; if a woman arrives later with uncertain chorionicity, **treat as MC** (2-weekly scans at a referral hospital, delivery at 36 weeks) unless the twins are clearly of **different sexes** or there are **two separate placentas**.

### Step 5: Putting it together

**Model one-line summary**

> "A 30-year-old G4P3 at 32+2 weeks with a **DCDA twin pregnancy** (lambda sign at 12 weeks), twin 1 cephalic, twin 2 breech, **growth discordance of 12%**, normal liquor and Dopplers, Hb 9.8 g/dL, BP normal, for iron optimisation, 4-weekly growth scans and planned vaginal birth at 37+0 weeks."

**Problem list**
1. Multiple pregnancy: number, **chorionicity/amnionicity** (and how reliably known), GA.
2. Fetal: growth of each twin, discordance, liquor (DVP each sac), Dopplers, presentations.
3. Maternal: anaemia, BP/pre-eclampsia, GDM, preterm labour/PPROM risk.
4. Delivery plan: date (by chorionicity), place (theatre, blood, two neonatal teams), mode (presentation of twin 1), plan for twin 2.
5. Postpartum: PPH prevention, breastfeeding two babies, KMC, family planning.

**Working diagnosis**: always state number + chorionicity + amnionicity + GA + complication, e.g. "MCDA twins at 22 weeks with Quintero stage II TTTS" or "DCDA twins at 35 weeks, twin 1 cephalic, in early labour".

#### Worked example 1: reading a chorionicity scan

| Scan finding at 12 weeks | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Two gestational sacs, thick membrane with a **triangular tongue of placenta at its base** | **Lambda sign → DCDA** |
| One placental mass, **thin membrane meeting it at a right angle** | **T sign → MCDA** |
| One placental mass, **no dividing membrane**, cords close together | **MCMA** (confirm; plan CS at 32–33+6 weeks) |
| Scan at 26 weeks: "single placenta, thin membrane, same sex" | **Cannot be sure** (two placentas can fuse and the lambda sign fades): **manage as MC** |

#### Worked example 2: calculating growth discordance

EFW twin 1 = **2,400 g**; EFW twin 2 = **1,800 g**.

**Discordance = (larger − smaller) ÷ larger × 100 = (2,400 − 1,800) ÷ 2,400 × 100 = 600 ÷ 2,400 × 100 = 25%.**

**Reading it:** 25% is **significant** (the threshold used in these notes is ≥25%, with some units acting at ≥20%). Next: is the smaller twin **<10th centile** (sFGR)? What is the **umbilical artery Doppler** of the small twin? What is the **chorionicity**? In DC twins, increase surveillance (Doppler every 1–2 weeks) and deliver when the small twin's Doppler deteriorates or at the planned date, balancing prematurity for the healthy twin; give steroids if delivery before 34 weeks is likely. In MC twins, refer to fetal medicine (sFGR or TTTS). **Remember to divide by the LARGER twin's weight**, not the smaller one (600 ÷ 1,800 = 33% is a common exam error).

#### Worked example 3: MC twins with unequal liquor

MCDA twins at 20 weeks: twin A DVP **1.5 cm**, bladder visible; twin B DVP **9 cm**, large bladder; Dopplers normal; no hydrops.

**Reading it:** DVP **<2 cm** in one sac and **>8 cm** in the other in MC twins = **TTTS, Quintero stage I** (donor bladder still visible). (Some fetal medicine centres use **>10 cm after 20 weeks** for the recipient; follow the referral centre.) **Action:** urgent referral to a fetal medicine specialist, weekly (or more frequent) scans to detect progression to stage II (donor bladder not visible), counselling on laser ablation (16–26 weeks; not routinely available in Uganda) or amnioreduction for symptomatic polyhydramnios, and steroids when viability approaches.

## Differential diagnosis

### Of "uterus large for dates"

| Condition | Distinguishing features | Key investigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Multiple pregnancy** | Many fetal parts, ≥2 poles, two FHRs | **Ultrasound** |
| Wrong dates | Uncertain LNMP, size consistent with a later GA | Dating/biometry scan |
| **Polyhydramnios** | Tense, fluid thrill, fetal parts hard to feel | Ultrasound: AFI >24–25 cm or deepest pocket >8 cm |
| Fetal macrosomia | Diabetes, large single fetus | Ultrasound EFW, glucose |
| **Fibroids** complicating pregnancy | Irregular firm masses, pain (red degeneration) | Ultrasound |
| **Molar pregnancy** (first/second trimester) | Bleeding, hyperemesis, early pre-eclampsia, no fetal parts | Ultrasound, β-hCG |
| Full bladder, ovarian mass | Mass separate from uterus | Ultrasound |

### Of complications specific to MC twins

| Condition | Distinguishing features | Key investigation |
|---|---|---|
| **TTTS** | Oligohydramnios (deepest pocket <2 cm) in donor + polyhydramnios (>8 cm) in recipient | Ultrasound (deepest pockets, bladders, Doppler) |
| **sFGR** | One twin small (<10th centile), liquor normal or mildly low in both | EFW, umbilical artery Doppler |
| **TAPS** | Large difference in Hb without liquor discordance | **Middle cerebral artery peak systolic velocity** (MCA-PSV) Doppler |
| **TRAP** | One normal "pump" twin + an acardiac mass with retrograde flow | Ultrasound, colour Doppler |

## Management

### 1. Antenatal care (enhanced ANC)

1. **Confirm chorionicity** on a first-trimester scan (11–14 weeks). If unavailable or uncertain, **treat as MC** and refer for a specialist scan.
2. **Refer to a hospital-based ANC clinic** (obstetrician-led; HC IV at a minimum) and plan delivery there.
3. **More frequent visits**: at least every 2–4 weeks from 20 weeks, and every 1–2 weeks from 32 weeks (more for MC twins).
4. **Nutrition and supplements**:

| Supplement | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Iron** (ferrous sulphate 200 mg = 60 mg elemental) + **folic acid 400 mcg** | 1 tablet daily throughout pregnancy (many clinicians give **twice daily** in twins or if Hb <11) | Twins have a greater iron and folate requirement. Treat anaemia with **120 mg elemental iron daily** |
| **Calcium** | 1.5–2 g elemental daily | Pre-eclampsia prevention in low-calcium diets |
| **Low-dose aspirin** | **75–150 mg at night from 12 weeks until 36 weeks** (NICE NG133 continues it until birth; many LMIC protocols stop at 36 weeks) | Multiple pregnancy is a **moderate risk factor**; give if there is **one more** moderate risk factor (e.g. first pregnancy, age ≥40, BMI ≥35). Many clinicians give aspirin to all twin pregnancies in high-burden settings |
| IPTp-SP | Monthly from 13 weeks | As for all pregnant women in Uganda (not with cotrimoxazole) |
| Td vaccine, ITN, deworming (mebendazole after first trimester) | Routine | |

5. **Screen** at each visit: BP, urine protein, symptoms of preterm labour, Hb at 20–24 and 28 weeks.
6. **Ultrasound surveillance** as above (DC 4-weekly from 20 weeks; MC 2-weekly from 16 weeks).
7. **Counsel** about danger signs (preterm labour, bleeding, pre-eclampsia symptoms, reduced movements) and **birth preparedness** (money, transport, blood donor, where to deliver). Consider **admission near term** (e.g. 36 weeks) for women who live far from the hospital.
8. **Preterm birth prevention**: routine cervical cerclage and routine progesterone **do not** help in twins; bed rest does not help. Treat UTIs and malaria. Give **dexamethasone** if preterm birth is likely between 24 (28) and 34 weeks.

### 2. Managing specific complications

#### TTTS

**Quintero staging:**

| Stage | Findings |
|---|---|
| I | Oligohydramnios in donor (deepest pocket <2 cm) + polyhydramnios in recipient (>8 cm); **donor bladder still visible** |
| II | **Donor bladder not visible** |
| III | **Critically abnormal Doppler** in either twin (absent/reversed end-diastolic flow in the umbilical artery, reversed a-wave in the ductus venosus, pulsatile umbilical vein) |
| IV | **Hydrops** in either twin |
| V | **Death** of one or both twins |

**Treatment:**
- **Fetoscopic laser ablation** of the placental anastomoses (Solomon technique) at **16–26 weeks** is the treatment of choice for stage II and above (and some stage I). It improves survival (at least one survivor in about 85–90%) and reduces neurological damage compared with amnioreduction.
- **Serial amnioreduction** (draining the recipient's polyhydramnios) relieves maternal symptoms and reduces preterm labour; it is an option where laser is unavailable.
- **Selective reduction** (cord occlusion) in selected severe cases.
- Stage I may be monitored closely (it can remain stable or regress).
- After 26 weeks: steroids and delivery if worsening.

> [!UGANDA]
> Fetoscopic laser surgery is **not routinely available in Uganda**. In practice, suspected TTTS at a Ugandan hospital means: urgent review by an obstetrician with good ultrasound skills, counselling, **amnioreduction** for symptomatic polyhydramnios where expertise exists, **steroids** when viability approaches, close surveillance and timely delivery. Some families seek referral abroad. This reality is why **early chorionicity scanning and 2-weekly MC surveillance** matter: they allow early counselling.

#### Discordant growth / sFGR

- **DC twins** with discordance ≥25% or one twin <10th centile: Doppler surveillance of the small twin; deliver when the small twin's Doppler deteriorates, balancing the risk of prematurity for the healthy twin.
- **MC sFGR**: classified by umbilical artery Doppler (Gratacós types I–III); referral to fetal medicine; the risk is sudden death of the small twin, with harm to the co-twin through the anastomoses.

#### Single fetal death

| | DC twins | MC twins |
|---|---|---|
| Risk to the survivor | Mainly **preterm labour** | **Death (~15%)** and **neurological damage (~25%)** from acute exsanguination into the dead twin |
| Management | Expectant; aim for term or at least 37 weeks; watch for preterm labour | Referral to fetal medicine; **immediate delivery does NOT prevent the damage** (it happens at the moment of death); MRI of the survivor's brain 3–4 weeks later where available; plan delivery at 34–36 weeks |
| Maternal DIC | Rare (theoretical with retained dead fetus >4 weeks); check clotting | Same |

### 3. Timing of delivery

| Type | Planned birth (uncomplicated, NICE NG137) | Mode |
|---|---|---|
| **DCDA** | **37+0 weeks** | Vaginal if twin 1 is cephalic |
| **MCDA** | **36+0 weeks** (after steroids per senior decision) | Vaginal if twin 1 is cephalic |
| **MCMA** | **32+0 to 33+6 weeks** (after steroids) | **Caesarean section** (cord entanglement) |
| **Triplets** | **35+0 weeks** | Usually **caesarean section** |

Continuing beyond these dates increases the risk of **stillbirth**. Earlier delivery may be needed for pre-eclampsia, FGR, TTTS, PPROM, etc.

### 4. Mode of delivery

| Presentation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Cephalic / cephalic** (about 40%) | **Vaginal delivery** |
| **Cephalic / non-cephalic** (about 35–40%) | **Vaginal delivery** is reasonable with an experienced obstetrician (twin 2 can be turned or delivered by breech extraction). The **Twin Birth Study** (2013) showed planned CS gave **no benefit** over planned vaginal birth when twin 1 is cephalic (32–38 weeks) |
| **Non-cephalic twin 1** (breech or transverse, about 20%) | **Caesarean section** (risk of **locked twins** if breech/cephalic) |
| **MCMA, conjoined twins, triplets** | **Caesarean section** |
| Other indications | Same as singletons (placenta praevia, previous classical CS, two or more previous CS, severe fetal compromise); **one previous CS** is not an absolute indication but most Ugandan units favour repeat CS |

### 5. Intrapartum management of twins: step by step

**Before delivery (preparation):**

1. **Confirm the presentation of both twins** by ultrasound on admission.
2. Deliver in a **facility with theatre** and blood (HC IV/hospital), in a room close to theatre.
3. **Senior obstetrician** (or the most experienced person available), a **midwife**, the **anaesthetist informed**, and **two neonatal resuscitation teams/sets** (two resuscitaires, two sets of towels, cord clamps labelled "Twin 1" and "Twin 2").
4. **Wide-bore IV access** (16–18 G), FBC, **group and cross-match 2 units**.
5. **Monitor both fetal hearts** (continuous CTG with two channels where available, otherwise intermittent auscultation of both, identifying each).
6. **Epidural analgesia** (where available) is helpful: it allows manipulations for twin 2 and rapid CS.
7. Have an **oxytocin infusion prepared** (but not running) for augmentation after twin 1, and ready for PPH prevention after twin 2.
8. Partograph as usual; **oxytocin augmentation** in the first stage can be used with caution if contractions are inadequate.

**Delivery of twin 1:**

9. Deliver **twin 1** as for a singleton cephalic delivery.
10. **Clamp and cut the cord**; label **"Twin 1"** (one clamp on the maternal end; some units tie one ligature). Hand the baby to the neonatal team.
11. **DO NOT give oxytocin, ergometrine or misoprostol** at this point.

**Delivery of twin 2 (the critical phase):**

12. **Palpate the abdomen** (and/or use ultrasound) to determine the **lie and presentation of twin 2**. **Listen to the fetal heart** of twin 2.
13. Have an assistant **stabilise the lie** from the abdomen (hands on either side of the uterus, holding twin 2 in a longitudinal lie).
14. **Do a vaginal examination**: check membranes (intact?), presenting part, and exclude **cord prolapse**.

| Lie of twin 2 | Action |
|---|---|
| **Longitudinal, cephalic** | Wait for the head to **descend into the pelvis** (with contractions). When the head is engaged/in the pelvis, **ARM with a contraction** (not before, to avoid cord prolapse). Encourage pushing. If contractions are weak after about **10 minutes**, start **oxytocin infusion** (e.g. 2.5–5 IU in 500 mL, titrated). Vacuum if the head is low and there is delay or fetal distress |
| **Longitudinal, breech** | Allow descent; ARM when the breech is in the pelvis; **assisted breech delivery**. If fetal distress: **breech extraction** |
| **Transverse / oblique** | 1) **External cephalic version (ECV)** to cephalic (or to breech) with membranes intact, if the FHR is normal. 2) If ECV fails or is not possible, an experienced obstetrician performs **internal podalic version** (grasp a foot through intact membranes, bring it down) followed by **breech extraction**. 3) Otherwise **caesarean section** |

15. **Fetal distress in twin 2** or **cord prolapse**: expedite (vacuum/forceps if cephalic and low; breech extraction; otherwise **emergency caesarean**).
16. **Inter-twin interval**: there is no absolute limit if twin 2's FHR is monitored and normal, but fetal acidosis increases with time; historically **aim to deliver within 30 minutes**. At a facility without continuous monitoring, don't let it drift.
17. Deliver twin 2; clamp the cord with **two clamps** (labelled **"Twin 2"**).

**Third stage:**

18. After the **last** baby: **oxytocin 10 IU IM/IV** (active management of the third stage), controlled cord traction.
19. **Prophylactic oxytocin infusion** (e.g. 20–40 IU in 1 L normal saline over 4 hours) is common practice because of the high PPH risk.
20. **Examine the placenta(s)** and membranes: count, completeness, and **chorionicity** (a thin, translucent dividing membrane suggests MC; confirm by histology where available). Label and record.
21. **Monitor closely for PPH** for at least the next 1–2 hours (fundus, lochia, pulse, BP).

> [!REDFLAG]
> **"Retained second twin"**: a woman who delivered one baby at home or a HC III and still has a large uterus with a second fetus. **Do not give oxytocics before delivery of twin 2.** Assess the lie and FHR; if longitudinal and the cervix is open, deliver vaginally (ARM, oxytocin infusion if needed). If transverse, if the cervix has clamped down, if there is fetal distress or sepsis, or if **ruptured uterus** is suspected → caesarean section. If twin 2 is dead and transverse with a retraction ring, CS is still usually safest in modern practice.

### 6. Locked twins

- **What**: twin 1 **breech**, twin 2 **cephalic**; as twin 1's trunk delivers, its chin locks under twin 2's chin (chin-to-chin) above the pelvic inlet.
- **How rare**: roughly **1 in 1,000 twin deliveries** (much rarer overall), but with a very high perinatal mortality for twin 1.
- **Prevention**: **caesarean section when twin 1 is not cephalic** (the main reason for this rule).
- **Management if it happens**: call for senior help; under general anaesthesia (deep relaxation), an assistant pushes twin 2's head upward abdominally while the operator tries to disengage the chins and deliver twin 1 (rotating the body and flexing twin 1's head). If this fails and twin 1 is **alive**: **emergency caesarean section** (twin 2's head is dislodged from above; twin 1 may be delivered through the abdomen). If twin 1 is **dead**: historically, **decapitation** of twin 1 to deliver twin 2, but in modern practice **caesarean section** is usually preferred.

### 7. Postpartum care

1. **PPH vigilance**: fundus and lochia every 15 minutes for the first hour, then half-hourly; oxytocin infusion; TXA 1 g IV if bleeding.
2. **Check Hb** before discharge (anaemia is common); continue iron and folate for 3 months.
3. **Breastfeeding support**: both twins can be breastfed (simultaneously in the "rugby/football hold"); milk supply responds to demand. Early and exclusive breastfeeding.
4. **Newborn care**: low birth weight/preterm: **KMC** (one twin on each side of the chest is possible), thermal care, feeding support, and early review of any growth-restricted or anaemic/polycythaemic twin (TAPS).
5. **Screen for postpartum depression** and fatigue; mobilise family support.
6. **Family planning**: counsel before discharge; offer LARC; interpregnancy interval ≥24 months. DZ twinning tends to recur (the woman has a higher chance of twins again).
7. **Thromboprophylaxis** after CS or with other risk factors.

### 8. What to do where (Uganda levels of care)

| Level | Actions |
|---|---|
| **HC II/III** | Suspect twins (large for dates, two FHRs); **refer for ultrasound** and hospital ANC; health education; iron/folate, IPTp. In labour with undiagnosed twins: deliver twin 1 if imminent, **no oxytocic until twin 2 is delivered**; if twin 2 is longitudinal and descending, deliver; if transverse or delayed, **refer urgently** with IV access and an escort |
| **HC IV / general hospital** | Enhanced ANC, basic ultrasound, planned delivery with theatre and blood; manage twin 2 (ECV, breech delivery, CS); PPH management; KMC. Refer MC twins with complications (TTTS, sFGR), MCMA, triplets and very preterm twins to a referral hospital |
| **Regional / national referral** | Specialist ultrasound (chorionicity, Doppler, MCA-PSV), amnioreduction, planned CS for MCMA/triplets, NICU, fetal medicine referral |

|---|---|
| Hyperemesis gravidarum | Miscarriage, vanishing twin |
| **Anaemia** | **Congenital anomalies** (2–3× in MZ twins) |

## Ward-round & exam pearls

### Model case presentation

> "Mrs N.R., a 33-year-old para 4+0 (all alive), at 35+1 weeks by an 11-week scan, with a **DCDA twin pregnancy** confirmed by the lambda sign at 12 weeks, admitted at 34 weeks for birth preparedness because she lives 50 km away. There is a family history of twins on her mother's side. She is on iron/folate twice daily and aspirin 150 mg until 36 weeks. Latest Hb 10.4 g/dL, BP 124/78, urine protein negative. Scan at 34 weeks: twin 1 cephalic, EFW 2.3 kg; twin 2 transverse, EFW 2.1 kg (discordance 9%), normal liquor and Dopplers. **Plan**: induction of labour at 37+0 weeks in the main labour ward with an experienced obstetrician, two neonatal teams, cross-matched blood and an oxytocin infusion ready; plan ECV or internal podalic version for twin 2 if still transverse after twin 1 delivers; prophylactic oxytocin infusion after delivery; recheck Hb."

### Viva questions (with answers)

1. **How do you determine chorionicity?** Ultrasound at 11–14 weeks: lambda sign = DC; T sign = MC; also separate placentas, different sexes (DC), membrane thickness.
2. **What decides whether MZ twins are DC, MC or conjoined?** The day of splitting: 0–3 days DCDA, 4–8 days MCDA, 8–12 days MCMA, >13 days conjoined.
3. **Explain TTTS.** In MC placentas, unbalanced arteriovenous anastomoses shunt blood from donor to recipient: the donor becomes hypovolaemic, oliguric, oligohydramniotic and growth restricted; the recipient becomes hypervolaemic, polyuric, polyhydramniotic, in heart failure and hydropic. Treated by fetoscopic laser at 16–26 weeks.
4. **What do you do immediately after twin 1 is delivered?** No oxytocic; clamp and label the cord; palpate for the lie of twin 2 and stabilise it; check the FHR; VE for presentation and cord; ARM when the presenting part is in the pelvis; oxytocin infusion if contractions are inadequate; manage malpresentation (ECV, internal podalic version + breech extraction, or CS).
5. **When should twins be delivered?** DCDA 37 weeks; MCDA 36 weeks; MCMA 32–34 weeks by CS; triplets 35 weeks.
6. **What are locked twins, and how are they prevented?** Twin 1 breech and twin 2 cephalic with interlocking chins; prevented by CS when twin 1 is non-cephalic.
7. **Why is the risk of PPH increased?** Overdistended uterus contracts poorly, large placental bed, prolonged labour, operative delivery, anaemia.
8. **Why is the co-twin of an MC twin that dies at risk?** Acute exsanguination through placental anastomoses into the low-pressure circulation of the dead twin → death or brain injury.

### OSCE tips

- **Abdominal examination station** (large for dates): inspect, measure the SFH, palpate the poles systematically, note "multiple fetal parts", auscultate two fetal hearts, and state that you would confirm with an ultrasound and determine chorionicity.
- **Counselling station (newly diagnosed MCDA twins)**: congratulate, explain chorionicity in simple words ("they share one placenta"), extra scans every 2 weeks and why (TTTS), symptoms to report (rapid abdominal growth, breathlessness), nutrition/iron, delivery plan at a hospital around 36 weeks.
- **Delivery of twin 2** (mannequin/viva): say "**no oxytocin**", stabilise the lie, check the FHR, VE, ARM with a contraction once the presenting part is engaged, start oxytocin infusion if contractions stop, and describe the manoeuvres for a transverse lie.

> **"Lambda = Lots (two chorions); T = Together (one chorion)."**

- **Split timing: "3–8–12–13"**: split by day **3** = DCDA; by day **8** = MCDA; by day **12** = MCMA; after day **13** = conjoined.

### Classic exam traps

- **"Same-sex twins are identical."** Wrong: same-sex DCDA twins may be DZ or MZ; only **different-sex** proves DZ, and only **MC** proves MZ.
- **"DCDA means non-identical."** Wrong: about a quarter of MZ twins are DCDA (split in days 0–3).
- **"Give oxytocin 10 IU IM after twin 1 as part of AMTSL."** Wrong: this can trap twin 2. **No uterotonic until the last baby is born.**
- **"Discordance = difference ÷ smaller twin."** Wrong: divide by the **larger** twin's EFW.
- **"Twins = elective caesarean."** Wrong: if twin 1 is **cephalic**, planned vaginal birth is reasonable (Twin Birth Study); CS for non-cephalic twin 1, MCMA and triplets.
- **"Chorionicity can be read on a 30-week scan."** Unreliable: the lambda sign fades; if in doubt **manage as MC**.
- **"Deliver immediately after an MC co-twin death to protect the survivor."** Wrong: the damage happens at the moment of death; immediate delivery does not prevent it and adds prematurity.
- **"Cerclage or progesterone prevents preterm birth in twins."** Wrong: routine cerclage, progesterone and bed rest do **not** help.

### Questions seniors ask

1. **How do you diagnose twins clinically, and how do you confirm them?** Large for dates, multiple poles and parts, a small head for the uterine size, two FHRs differing by ≥10 bpm; **confirm by ultrasound**, with chorionicity at 11–14 weeks.
2. **What is the chorionicity, and why does it matter?** DC vs MC decides scan frequency, delivery date and specific risks (TTTS, TAPS, sFGR and co-twin death in MC; cord entanglement in MCMA).
3. **Calculate the discordance for 2,400 g and 1,800 g.** (2,400 − 1,800) ÷ 2,400 = **25%**: significant; check for sFGR and Doppler.
4. **What do you prepare before a twin delivery?** Experienced obstetrician, theatre ready, anaesthetist aware, IV access, 2 units cross-matched, both FHRs monitored, **two neonatal resuscitation sets**, labelled cord clamps, oxytocin infusion ready but not running, ultrasound to hand.
5. **Twin 1 delivered; twin 2 is transverse with intact membranes and a normal FHR. What next?** Stabilise, **ECV** to a longitudinal lie; if it fails, an experienced obstetrician performs **internal podalic version and breech extraction**; otherwise **caesarean section**.
6. **Why are twins at risk of PPH, and how do you prevent it?** Overdistended uterus, large placental bed, anaemia, long labour, operative delivery. Oxytocin 10 IU after the last baby, **prophylactic oxytocin infusion**, close monitoring for 1–2 h, TXA if bleeding.
7. **MCDA twins at 20 weeks with DVP 1.5 cm and 9 cm: what is it?** **TTTS** (Quintero I if the donor bladder is visible): urgent fetal medicine referral, close scans, laser (16–26 weeks) or amnioreduction where laser is unavailable.
8. **When should uncomplicated twins be delivered?** DCDA 37+0, MCDA 36+0, MCMA 32+0–33+6 by CS, triplets 35+0 (NICE NG137).
