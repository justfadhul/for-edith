## In a nutshell

**Birth asphyxia** means the baby's oxygen supply was interrupted before, during or just after birth so that he fails to start or sustain breathing. The resulting brain damage is **hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy (HIE)**. "Neonatal encephalopathy" is the broader clinical term for a term baby with abnormal consciousness, tone, feeding or seizures; HIE is its commonest cause.

Why it matters in Uganda: intrapartum-related events (asphyxia) are one of the **three leading causes of neonatal death** (with prematurity and infection), a major cause of **fresh stillbirth**, and the commonest preventable cause of **cerebral palsy**. Most cases follow prolonged or obstructed labour, poorly monitored labour, cord prolapse, abruption, ruptured uterus or delayed resuscitation.

There is no antidote. In Ugandan units, the treatment is **meticulous supportive care**: keep the baby normothermic (and **never overheated**), keep glucose normal, give the right fluids, support breathing, **stop seizures with phenobarbital**, treat infection, feed carefully and follow up.

> [!PEARL]
> The one thing never to forget: **prevention is the treatment.** A well-monitored labour (partograph, fetal heart every 30 min in active labour, timely action) and a trained person with a working bag and mask at every birth prevent most HIE.

## Definitions & classification

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Birth asphyxia** (WHO) | Failure to initiate and sustain breathing at birth |
| **Perinatal asphyxia** (physiological) | Impaired gas exchange → **hypoxaemia, hypercapnia and metabolic acidosis** |
| **Neonatal encephalopathy (NE)** | Clinical syndrome in a baby of 35 weeks or more: reduced consciousness or seizures, abnormal tone and reflexes, poor feeding, often breathing difficulty |
| **HIE** | NE caused by intrapartum hypoxia-ischaemia |
| **Apgar score** | 0-10 at 1 and 5 min; describes the baby's state, **does not diagnose asphyxia** and is never used to decide whether to resuscitate |

**Criteria that support an intrapartum hypoxic-ischaemic cause** (ACOG/AAP "Neonatal encephalopathy and neurologic outcome"; used in research and medicolegal work):
1. **Cord (or early) arterial pH below 7.0** and/or **base deficit 12 mmol/L or more**
2. **Apgar 0-3 beyond 5 minutes** (or below 5 at 5 and 10 minutes)
3. Early-onset **moderate or severe encephalopathy**
4. **Multi-organ dysfunction** (kidney, liver, heart, gut, blood)
5. A **sentinel event** (cord prolapse, abruption, uterine rupture, shoulder dystocia) and fetal heart abnormalities
6. Neuroimaging consistent with acute hypoxic injury (basal ganglia/thalamus, watershed)

> [!UGANDA]
> Cord blood gases are rarely available in Ugandan public hospitals. On the ward, asphyxia is diagnosed **clinically**: a history of a difficult or prolonged labour or fetal distress, a baby who needed resuscitation (low Apgar), and **encephalopathy** in the first hours to days. Write all three in your notes.

### Sarnat staging (modified): the ward version

| Feature | Stage 1 (mild) | Stage 2 (moderate) | Stage 3 (severe) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Level of consciousness** | **Hyperalert**, irritable, jittery | **Lethargic** or obtunded | **Stupor or coma** |
| **Tone** | Normal or increased | **Mild hypotonia** | **Flaccid** |
| **Posture** | Mild distal flexion | Strong distal flexion | Intermittent decerebration |
| **Stretch reflexes** | Brisk | Brisk | Decreased or absent |
| **Moro** | Strong, low threshold | Weak, incomplete | Absent |
| **Suck** | Weak | Weak or absent | Absent |
| **Autonomic** | Sympathetic: **dilated pupils**, tachycardia | Parasympathetic: **constricted pupils**, bradycardia, secretions | Both depressed: pupils variable/poor light reflex, apnoea |
| **Seizures** | **None** | **Common** (often subtle) | Uncommon clinically (EEG suppressed) or frequent early |
| **Duration** | Under 24 h | 2-14 days | Hours to weeks |
| **Outcome** | **Usually normal** | **Variable**: about 20-40% die or have disability (lower with cooling in HICs) | **Death in about half or more; nearly all survivors disabled** |

> [!EXAM]
> Examiners love: "Mild = hyperalert + no seizures + normal outcome; moderate = lethargy + hypotonia + seizures; severe = coma + flaccid." Also: **pupils dilated in mild (sympathetic), constricted in moderate (parasympathetic)**.

The **Thompson score** (0-22; tone, consciousness, fits, posture, Moro, grasp, suck, respiration, fontanelle) is a simple bedside alternative, used in African and Asian research and some Ugandan units. A higher score means worse encephalopathy; scores of about 15 or more on days 1-7 predict poor outcome.

## Epidemiology & Uganda context

- Worldwide, intrapartum-related events cause roughly a quarter of neonatal deaths and over a million stillbirths a year, almost all in LMICs.
- In Uganda, many asphyxiated babies come from **prolonged or obstructed labour**, delayed referral from lower-level facilities, home births, inadequate fetal monitoring and a lack of skilled resuscitation.
- **Who is at risk?** Prolonged second stage, obstructed labour, malpresentation, cord prolapse, APH (abruption), ruptured uterus, eclampsia, maternal shock, breech and twin delivery, shoulder dystocia, thick meconium, oxytocin overuse (hyperstimulation), preterm and post-term babies.
- **Facilities**: HC III/IV should resuscitate (HBB) and refer; regional and national referral hospitals (Mulago/Kawempe, Mengo, Nsambya, Lubaga) have newborn units with oxygen, phenobarbital and IV fluids. **Therapeutic hypothermia** is not standard in public Ugandan units; aEEG/EEG and MRI are available in only a few Kampala centres.

## Pathophysiology

### The two phases of brain injury

```
Insult (hypoxia + ischaemia)
   |
   v
PRIMARY energy failure  -> ATP falls, Na/K pumps fail, cytotoxic oedema,
   |                         glutamate release, calcium influx, cell necrosis
   v
Resuscitation -> partial recovery of brain energy
   |
   v
LATENT phase (~ 1-6 hours)  <- the "therapeutic window" (cooling must start here)
   |
   v
SECONDARY energy failure (6-72 h) -> mitochondrial failure, free radicals,
                                     inflammation, apoptosis, seizures
   |
   v
TERTIARY phase (weeks-months) -> ongoing injury, impaired repair
```

Everything that adds to the energy deficit in the secondary phase makes the injury worse: **hyperthermia, hypoglycaemia, hyperglycaemia, seizures, hypotension, hypoxaemia, hyperoxia, hypocapnia (over-ventilation reduces cerebral blood flow), infection and fluid overload**.

### The "diving reflex" and multi-organ injury

When oxygen falls, the fetus redistributes blood to the **brain, heart and adrenals** at the expense of the **kidneys, gut, liver, skin and lungs**. So asphyxia damages several organs:

| Organ | Consequence | What to monitor |
|---|---|---|
| **Brain** | HIE, seizures, cerebral oedema | Neuro exam, seizures |
| **Kidneys** | Acute tubular necrosis → **oliguria, AKI**, hyponatraemia | Urine output, creatinine, sodium |
| **Heart** | Myocardial dysfunction, tricuspid regurgitation, hypotension | HR, CRT, BP |
| **Lungs** | Meconium aspiration, persistent pulmonary hypertension (PPHN), haemorrhage | SpO2 (pre/post-ductal) |
| **Liver** | Raised transaminases, coagulopathy, hypoglycaemia | Glucose, bleeding |
| **Gut** | Feed intolerance, NEC | Abdomen, aspirates |
| **Blood** | Thrombocytopenia, **DIC** | Bleeding, platelets |
| **Endocrine/metabolic** | **Hypoglycaemia**, hypocalcaemia, SIADH | Glucose, calcium, sodium |

### Patterns of brain injury

- **Acute profound ("near total")** insult (e.g. cord prolapse, uterine rupture): deep grey matter (**basal ganglia, thalami**) → dyskinetic/spastic quadriplegic CP.
- **Prolonged partial** insult (e.g. long obstructed labour): **watershed** cortex and white matter → cognitive problems, spastic CP, cortical visual impairment.

## Clinical acumen

### Focused history

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Labour**: duration, augmentation, partograph, obstructed, malpresentation | Mechanism and severity of the insult |
| **Fetal heart**: bradycardia, decelerations, meconium | Evidence of fetal hypoxia |
| **Sentinel event**: cord prolapse, abruption, rupture, eclamptic fit, shoulder dystocia | Supports intrapartum cause, predicts pattern |
| **Resuscitation**: time to first breath/cry, bag-mask duration, HR response, compressions/adrenaline, Apgar 1/5/10 | Longer resuscitation = more severe HIE |
| **Gestation and birth weight** | Cooling eligibility (36 weeks or more); preterm encephalopathy has different causes |
| **Maternal fever, ROM duration, foul liquor** | **Sepsis/meningitis** can mimic or add to HIE |
| **Maternal drugs** (opioids, MgSO4, diazepam) | Can cause depression without HIE |
| **Seizures noticed**: when, what they looked like | Timing (HIE seizures typically start within 12-24 h) |
| **Feeding, urine output since birth** | Suck = neuro status; oliguria = AKI |
| **Maternal HIV (last VL), syphilis, blood group** | Prophylaxis and other causes |

### Examination

- **Vitals**: temperature (avoid above 37.5), HR, RR/apnoea, SpO2, CRT, BP if available.
- **Neurological exam (Sarnat)**: level of consciousness, spontaneous activity, tone (pull-to-sit, ventral suspension), posture, Moro, suck, grasp, pupils, breathing pattern, fontanelle.
- **Seizures**: subtle (lip smacking, eye deviation, cycling, apnoea), clonic, tonic, myoclonic.
- **Other organs**: chest (meconium aspiration, PPHN), heart (murmur of TR, poor perfusion), liver size, bleeding, abdomen, urine output.
- **Look for other causes**: dysmorphism, skin lesions, bulging fontanelle (meningitis), cephalhaematoma or subgaleal swelling (blood loss), jaundice.
- **Repeat the Sarnat exam** at least daily (and at 6 h if cooling were possible): encephalopathy evolves.

### Red flags

> [!REDFLAG]
> - **Apnoea** or irregular breathing, SpO2 falling
> - **Seizures**, especially repeated or prolonged; seizure in the first hours
> - **Coma, flaccidity, absent suck and Moro** (severe HIE)
> - **Hypotension**, poor perfusion, bradycardia
> - **No urine for 24 h**, rising weight, oedema (AKI, fluid overload)
> - **Bleeding** from puncture sites, gut or cord (DIC)
> - **Hypoglycaemia** (below 2.6 mmol/L) or hyperthermia (above 37.5 C)
> - **Bulging fontanelle** or fever (think meningitis)

### Thinking like a clinician

- **Not every floppy baby after a hard labour has HIE.** Always consider **hypoglycaemia, sepsis/meningitis**, maternal drugs, intracranial haemorrhage (subgaleal, subdural after instrumental delivery), metabolic disease, congenital neuromuscular disorders.
- **Encephalopathy without a hypoxic history** (normal labour, good Apgar) points away from HIE: look for infection, metabolic disease, stroke, or malformation.
- **Seizures in HIE are often subtle**: a baby with apnoea and eye deviation is seizing until proven otherwise.
- **Over-treatment harms too**: hyperthermia from radiant heaters, hyperoxia from 100% oxygen, fluid overload from "routine" 60-90 mL/kg/day in an oliguric baby, and stacked anticonvulsants causing apnoea.
- **Mild HIE today can be moderate tomorrow.** Re-examine; write the stage and the time.
- **Prognosis needs time.** Avoid firm predictions on day 1; recovery of suck and tone over the first week is the best clinical guide in Uganda.

## Clinical workup

### Step 0: First 5 minutes

1. **Resuscitation** as per HBB/neonatal resuscitation (see that topic): dry, airway, **bag-mask ventilation by 60 s**, HR checks, compressions/adrenaline in hospital.
2. Once breathing, **admit** if: prolonged ventilation (more than 1-2 min), abnormal tone, poor suck, seizures, or ongoing respiratory distress.
3. **Temperature**: aim **36.5-37.5 C**. Turn the radiant warmer down if above 37.5; do not overwrap. **Do not actively cool** without a cooling protocol and NICU.
4. **Airway and breathing**: position, suction if secretions; oxygen only to keep SpO2 **90-95%** (avoid hyperoxia); bag-mask for apnoea.
5. **Glucose** within the first hour and every 3-6 h for 24-48 h; treat below **2.6 mmol/L**.
6. **IV access** (peripheral or umbilical venous catheter); start **10% dextrose** at **60 mL/kg/day**.
7. **Seizure?** → **phenobarbital 20 mg/kg IV**.
8. **Sepsis risk** → blood culture, **ampicillin + gentamicin**.
9. **Vitamin K** if not given.
10. Call the senior; inform and support the mother.

### Step 1: Focused history

- [ ] Labour length, partograph, augmentation, obstruction: severity.
- [ ] FHR abnormalities, meconium, sentinel event: timing and pattern.
- [ ] Resuscitation details and Apgar 1/5/10 min: severity.
- [ ] Gestation and birth weight: other diagnoses; cooling eligibility where available.
- [ ] Infection risks: maternal fever, ROM over 18 h, foul liquor.
- [ ] Maternal medicines: opioids, MgSO4.
- [ ] Seizure description and timing.
- [ ] Urine and meconium passed.
- [ ] Maternal HIV/VL, syphilis, blood group; immunisation (BCG/OPV0).

### Step 2: Focused examination

- General: consciousness, activity, colour, posture, seizures.
- Vitals and perfusion (CRT below 3 s), urine output.
- **Sarnat stage** with each item recorded (consciousness, tone, posture, reflexes, Moro, suck, pupils, autonomic, seizures).
- Head: fontanelle, sutures, scalp swelling (caput vs cephalhaematoma vs **subgaleal haemorrhage**).
- Chest, heart, abdomen, liver; bleeding; skin.
- Document key negatives: "no bulging fontanelle, no dysmorphism, no subgaleal swelling".

### Step 3: Bedside tests

| Test | What you are looking for |
|---|---|
| **Glucose** | Below 2.6 mmol/L (treat); above 10 mmol/L (reduce glucose infusion rate) |
| **Temperature** | Keep 36.5-37.5 C; avoid above 37.5 |
| **SpO2** | 90-95%; check pre- and post-ductal if PPHN suspected (difference above 3-5%) |
| **Urine output** (weigh nappies: 1 g = 1 mL) | Below **1 mL/kg/h** after the first 24 h = oliguria |
| **Daily weight** | Weight gain in the first week = fluid retention |
| **HemoCue Hb / PCV** | Blood loss (subgaleal), polycythaemia |
| **Bedside clotting (20-minute whole-blood clotting test)** | DIC |

### Step 4: Laboratory & imaging

| Test | When | Expected / abnormal finding | How it changes management | Availability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cord/arterial blood gas** | At birth/first hour | pH below 7.0, BE 12 or more | Supports intrapartum asphyxia; cooling eligibility in HICs | Few Kampala centres |
| **Electrolytes, urea, creatinine** | Day 1-3 | Hyponatraemia (SIADH), rising creatinine/potassium (AKI) | Fluid restriction; stop potassium; adjust gentamicin | Regional/national referral |
| **Calcium, magnesium** | Seizures | Hypocalcaemia (below 1.75-2.0 mmol/L total) | 10% calcium gluconate 2 mL/kg slow IV | Referral hospitals |
| **FBC, platelets, coagulation** | Bleeding, sepsis | Thrombocytopenia, DIC | FFP, platelets, vitamin K | Referral hospitals |
| **Blood culture, CRP** | Risk factors or signs of sepsis | Positive culture | Antibiotic duration | Referral hospitals |
| **Lumbar puncture** | Fever, bulging fontanelle, seizures without clear HIE, positive culture | Meningitis | 21 days IV antibiotics | Hospital, once stable |
| **LFTs** | Severe HIE | Raised transaminases | Drug dosing, coagulopathy | Referral hospitals |
| **Cranial ultrasound** | Day 1-3 | Oedema, haemorrhage; excludes other lesions | Prognosis | Kampala tertiary |
| **aEEG/EEG** | Seizures, grading | Background suppression, electrographic seizures | Seizure treatment, prognosis | Very limited |
| **MRI brain** | Day 5-14 | Basal ganglia/thalamus or watershed injury | Best prognostic test | Few private/tertiary centres |

### Step 5: Putting it together

**Model summary line:** "A 14-hour-old term male, birth weight 3.4 kg, born by vacuum after **obstructed labour for 6 hours** with thick meconium and FHR 90/min, needing **4 minutes of bag-mask ventilation** (Apgar 2, 5, 7), now with **moderate HIE (Sarnat 2)**: lethargic, hypotonic, weak suck, constricted pupils, with **two subtle seizures** (lip smacking and eye deviation) treated with phenobarbital. Temperature 37.0 C, glucose 3.8 mmol/L, SpO2 95% in air, no urine yet."

**Problem list:** (1) moderate HIE with seizures; (2) risk of AKI/SIADH (no urine at 14 h); (3) possible meconium aspiration; (4) sepsis to be excluded; (5) feeding (unable to suck); (6) family counselling.

**Worked example: fluids in an oliguric baby.** A 3.0 kg baby with moderate HIE has passed 10 mL of urine in 24 h (0.14 mL/kg/h) and his weight has risen from 3.0 to 3.15 kg. Sodium is 128 mmol/L. Interpretation: **oliguria + weight gain + hyponatraemia = fluid retention (AKI/SIADH)**. Plan: restrict to **insensible losses (about 30-40 mL/kg/day) plus urine output**, no potassium, recheck electrolytes and creatinine, review gentamicin (prolong interval/stop if culture negative), weigh twice daily.

## Differential diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing features | Key investigation |
|---|---|---|
| **HIE** | Hypoxic labour, low Apgar, resuscitation, encephalopathy from birth, multi-organ dysfunction | Clinical; gas, imaging |
| **Neonatal sepsis/meningitis** | Maternal fever, ROM over 18 h, fever/hypothermia, bulging fontanelle, onset after a normal period | Culture, CRP, LP |
| **Hypoglycaemia** | Jittery, lethargic, seizures; SGA/IDM; responds to glucose | Glucose |
| **Intracranial/subgaleal haemorrhage** | Instrumental delivery, boggy scalp crossing sutures, falling Hb, shock | Hb, head circumference, cranial US |
| **Maternal drug effect** | Opioid or MgSO4 before delivery; depression improves over hours; no seizures | History; naloxone not routine |
| **Inborn error of metabolism** | Normal at birth, then lethargy, vomiting, seizures after feeds; consanguinity | Glucose, ammonia, lactate, ketones |
| **Neonatal stroke** | Focal seizures in a well-looking term baby, often day 1-3 | Cranial US/MRI |
| **Congenital neuromuscular disease / malformation** | Hypotonia from birth without encephalopathy; dysmorphism, contractures | Genetics, imaging |
| **Hypocalcaemia / hypomagnesaemia** | Jitteriness, seizures, IDM, preterm | Calcium, magnesium |
| **Kernicterus** | Day 3-7, deep jaundice, opisthotonus, high-pitched cry | Bilirubin |

## Management

### 1. Emergency algorithm (first 24 hours)

1. **Resuscitate effectively** (HBB/NRP). Ventilation is the key intervention.
2. **Admit** to the newborn unit/SCBU every baby with encephalopathy, prolonged resuscitation, or abnormal exam.
3. **Thermoregulation**: **36.5-37.5 C**; avoid hyperthermia; no ad-hoc cooling with ice or fans.
4. **Oxygenation and ventilation**: SpO2 **90-95%**; avoid hyperoxia and over-ventilation; bag-mask/CPAP/ventilation for apnoea or respiratory failure.
5. **Circulation**: CRT and HR; normal saline **10 mL/kg** over 10-20 min only if clearly hypovolaemic (e.g. blood loss, subgaleal haemorrhage); dopamine where available for hypotension due to myocardial dysfunction.
6. **Glucose**: keep **2.6 mmol/L or more** (many units target 2.6-8); **10% dextrose 2 mL/kg IV** for hypoglycaemia, then infusion.
7. **Fluids**: **10% dextrose 60 mL/kg/day** day 1; **restrict** (e.g. 40 mL/kg/day or insensible losses + urine output) if oliguric or hyponatraemic.
8. **Seizures**: **phenobarbital** (table below); exclude hypoglycaemia, hypocalcaemia and meningitis.
9. **Antibiotics**: ampicillin + gentamicin if sepsis cannot be excluded; take care with gentamicin in AKI (extend interval, check levels if available).
10. **Feeding**: nil by mouth or small EBM feeds by NGT while unstable; start/advance as the baby improves.
11. **Monitor**: temperature, HR, RR, SpO2, glucose, urine output, weight, Sarnat stage, seizures.
12. **Communicate** with the family; document carefully.

### 2. Seizure management

| Drug | Dose | Route | Frequency | Duration / notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Phenobarbital** (first line, WHO/UCG) | **Loading 20 mg/kg** | IV slowly over **15-20 min** (IM if no IV) | Once; if seizures persist give **further 10 mg/kg** doses up to **40 mg/kg total** | **Maintenance 5 mg/kg/day** once daily (start 12-24 h after load). Watch for **apnoea and hypotension**; have a bag and mask ready. Stop when seizure-free (often before discharge; senior decision) |
| **Phenytoin** (second line) | **Loading 20 mg/kg** | IV in normal saline over **20-30 min** (max 1 mg/kg/min), with ECG/HR monitoring | Once; maintenance 3-5 mg/kg/day in 2 doses | Not in dextrose (precipitates); arrhythmias |
| **Levetiracetam** (second line where available) | **40-60 mg/kg** loading | IV/oral | Then 10-30 mg/kg 12-hourly per local protocol | Fewer side effects; less effective than phenobarbital as first line (NEOLEV2 trial) |
| **Midazolam** (refractory, NICU) | 0.05-0.15 mg/kg bolus then infusion | IV | Infusion | Respiratory depression; needs ventilation support |
| **10% calcium gluconate** (if hypocalcaemic) | **2 mL/kg** | **Slow IV** over 10 min with HR monitoring | Then oral/IV maintenance | Extravasation burns; bradycardia if fast |
| **10% dextrose** (if hypoglycaemic) | **2 mL/kg** | IV | Then infusion | Recheck in 30 min |

> [!DRUG]
> **Phenobarbital example**: 3.2 kg baby → loading **64 mg** IV over 20 min; if still seizing after 15-30 min, **32 mg** (10 mg/kg), repeat once if needed (total 40 mg/kg = 128 mg); maintenance **16 mg once daily**. Diazepam is **not** recommended as a routine neonatal anticonvulsant (respiratory depression, sodium benzoate displaces bilirubin).

### 3. Fluids and nutrition

| Day | Typical total fluids in HIE | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | **60 mL/kg/day** 10% dextrose (some units 40-60 if oliguric) | Glucose infusion rate ≈ 4 mg/kg/min at 60 mL/kg/day |
| Day 2-3 | Increase by 20-30 mL/kg/day **only if** passing urine and sodium normal | Add sodium after 48 h (e.g. 10% dextrose with 0.18% saline) |
| Oliguria/AKI | **Insensible losses (30-40 mL/kg/day) + urine output** | No potassium; daily weight and electrolytes |

- **Glucose infusion rate (GIR)** = (% dextrose × mL/kg/day) ÷ 144. Example: 10% at 60 mL/kg/day = **4.2 mg/kg/min**.
- **Feeding**: start **EBM by NGT** (10-20 mL/kg/day) once the baby is stable (no seizures for 24 h, abdomen soft, bowel sounds); advance by 20-30 mL/kg/day; move to cup/breast as suck returns. Severe HIE babies may need long-term NGT feeding.

### 4. Temperature and therapeutic hypothermia

- **Therapeutic hypothermia (TH)** = whole-body cooling to **33.5 C for 72 hours**, started **within 6 hours** of birth, followed by slow rewarming (0.5 C per hour), for babies **36 weeks or more** with **moderate or severe HIE**, in a **NICU** with ventilation, monitoring, and blood gases. In high-income settings it reduces death or disability (number needed to treat about 6-9).
- **HELIX trial** (Lancet Global Health 2021; India, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh): TH did **not** reduce death or disability and **significantly increased death** in LMIC tertiary units. Many babies in LMICs have longer, partial insults, more infection, and different timing, so the benefit from HIC trials does not transfer.

> [!UGANDA]
> Therapeutic hypothermia is **not standard care** in Ugandan public hospitals and should be offered only within a research protocol or a fully equipped NICU. On the ward, the priority is the opposite mistake: **avoid hyperthermia** (radiant warmers set too high, heavy wrapping, fever). Keep the baby at **36.5-37.5 C**.

### 5. Other organ support

- **Respiratory**: meconium aspiration → oxygen, CPAP, antibiotics; PPHN (labile SpO2, pre/post-ductal difference) → oxygen, avoid acidosis and hypothermia, refer.
- **Cardiovascular**: hypotension → cautious saline (only if hypovolaemic), inotropes (dopamine 5-10 microgram/kg/min) where available.
- **Renal**: fluid restriction, avoid nephrotoxins, adjust gentamicin; monitor potassium.
- **Liver/blood**: vitamin K, FFP and platelets for bleeding.
- **Gut**: delay or slow feeds; watch for NEC.

### 6. Level-of-care plan in Uganda

| Level | What to do |
|---|---|
| **HC III/HC IV** | HBB resuscitation; keep warm (not hot); breastfeed or EBM by cup if able; check glucose or give EBM/sugar water by NGT; **first dose of phenobarbital 20 mg/kg IM** if convulsing; first dose of ampicillin + gentamicin if risk factors; **refer** with the mother and a referral note |
| **General/regional referral** | SCBU admission, oxygen, IV fluids, glucose monitoring, phenobarbital, antibiotics, NGT feeding, daily Sarnat |
| **National referral/tertiary (Kampala)** | CPAP/ventilation, blood gases, electrolytes, cranial US, EEG/MRI in some centres, multidisciplinary follow-up |

### 7. Prognosis and follow-up

- **Mild HIE**: usually normal outcome; still review development.
- **Moderate HIE**: significant risk of death or disability (roughly a third or more without cooling); outcome depends on recovery.
- **Severe HIE**: high mortality; most survivors have **cerebral palsy** (often spastic quadriplegia or dyskinetic), microcephaly, epilepsy, intellectual disability, cortical visual impairment, hearing loss, feeding problems.
- **Good signs**: normal tone, alertness and **sucking by day 7** (WHO: "an infant who is normally active will usually do well"); seizures controlled early; normal neurological exam at discharge.
- **Bad signs**: severe (Sarnat 3) encephalopathy, seizures that are hard to control, abnormal exam beyond 1 week, **inability to suck at 1 week**, microcephaly at follow-up.
- **Follow-up plan**: weight and head circumference, development at 6 weeks, 3, 6, 9, 12 and 18-24 months; hearing and vision screening; physiotherapy early; nutrition support; epilepsy care; refer to paediatric neurology and cerebral palsy/rehabilitation services (e.g. Mulago, CoRSU, Katalemwa Cheshire Home) and parent support groups.
- **Breaking news**: be honest and gentle; avoid firm predictions on day 1; review with the family daily; involve the father; screen the mother for depression.

## Complications

| Acute | Long-term |
|---|---|
| Seizures, cerebral oedema | **Cerebral palsy** |
| Apnoea, respiratory failure, PPHN, meconium aspiration | Epilepsy |

| Myocardial dysfunction, hypotension | Visual impairment (cortical) |
| Hypoglycaemia, hypocalcaemia | Hearing loss |

## Ward-round & exam pearls

### Model presentation

"**Baby Okello**, a **2-day-old** term male, birth weight **3.5 kg**, born at Mengo by emergency CS for **obstructed labour** (referred from a HC IV after 14 hours of labour) with **thick meconium** and **FHR 80/min**. He was **not breathing at birth**, needed **5 minutes of bag-mask ventilation**, HR rose above 100 at 3 minutes; Apgar 2, 4 and 7.

On day 1 he had **moderate HIE (Sarnat 2)**: lethargic, hypotonic, weak suck, constricted pupils, with **three seizures** (cycling movements and eye deviation) controlled with **phenobarbital 20 mg/kg** then a further **10 mg/kg**; now on **5 mg/kg/day**. Temperature 36.8-37.3. He has had no seizures for 20 hours. Glucose 3.5-6.0 mmol/L. On **10% dextrose 60 mL/kg/day**; passed **1.2 mL/kg/h** urine in the last 12 hours. Sodium 132. Blood culture pending; on **ampicillin and gentamicin** (ROM 20 h). Started **EBM 10 mL/kg/day by NGT** today.

Mother **HIV-negative** in labour, RPR negative. **BCG and OPV0 not yet given** (will give before discharge). Weight today **3.4 kg** (−3%).

**Assessment:** moderate HIE, improving, seizures controlled; problems: feeding, fluid balance, sepsis exclusion, family counselling. **Plan:** daily Sarnat, advance feeds as suck returns, stop antibiotics at 48 h if culture negative and well, continue phenobarbital and review, development follow-up at 6 weeks."

### Viva questions (with answers)

1. **What is the difference between birth asphyxia and HIE?** Asphyxia is the event (hypoxia, hypercapnia, acidosis/failure to breathe); HIE is the brain injury that results.
2. **Describe Sarnat stage 2.** Lethargy, hypotonia, weak suck and Moro, constricted pupils, bradycardia, seizures common.
3. **Why avoid hyperthermia?** It increases cerebral metabolic demand and worsens secondary energy failure; even 1 C rise increases the odds of death/disability.
4. **First-line anticonvulsant and dose?** Phenobarbital 20 mg/kg IV over 15-20 min; further 10 mg/kg to 40 mg/kg; maintenance 5 mg/kg/day.
5. **Why restrict fluids in HIE?** Risk of AKI and SIADH → fluid overload, hyponatraemia, cerebral oedema.
6. **Is cooling recommended in Uganda?** Not as routine care; HELIX showed increased mortality in LMICs; use only within protocols in fully equipped NICUs.
7. **What predicts a poor outcome?** Severe encephalopathy, prolonged resuscitation (no HR at 10 min), seizures hard to control, abnormal exam and inability to suck at 1 week, abnormal EEG/MRI.

### OSCE tips

- Expect a station on **Sarnat staging** from a video or description, or on **calculating phenobarbital doses**.
- When counselling parents, use plain words ("the baby's brain was short of oxygen during labour"), avoid blame, explain uncertainty, describe the plan and follow-up.

## Mnemonics & memory aids

**Avoid secondary injury: "Keep it in the middle"**: not too hot, not too cold; not too much glucose, not too little; not too much oxygen, not too little; not too much fluid, not too little.

**Multi-organ injury: "Brain Kills Heart, Lungs, Liver, Gut, Blood"** (brain, kidneys, heart, lungs, liver, gut, blood/DIC).

**Sarnat: "1 = Hyper, 2 = Hypo + fits, 3 = Coma + flat"**.

## High-yield summary

### Numbers & doses to know

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Asphyxia supporting criteria | **pH below 7.0**, **BE 12 or more**, Apgar **0-3 beyond 5 min**, encephalopathy, multi-organ failure |
| Latent phase / therapeutic window | About **6 hours** |
| Cooling (where offered) | **33.5 C for 72 h**, start within **6 h**, **36 weeks or more**, moderate/severe HIE |
| HELIX trial (LMIC, 2021) | No benefit; **more deaths** with cooling |
| Temperature target | **36.5-37.5 C**; avoid above 37.5 |
| SpO2 target | **90-95%** |
| Glucose | Treat **below 2.6 mmol/L** with **10% dextrose 2 mL/kg** |
| Day-1 fluids | **10% dextrose 60 mL/kg/day** (restrict if oliguric) |
| Oliguria | **Below 1 mL/kg/h** |
| GIR formula | **(% dextrose × mL/kg/day) ÷ 144** |
| Phenobarbital | **20 mg/kg** load (+10 mg/kg to **40 mg/kg**); maintenance **5 mg/kg/day** |
| Phenytoin | **20 mg/kg** IV over 20-30 min in saline |
| Calcium gluconate 10% | **2 mL/kg** slow IV |
| Saline bolus (if hypovolaemic) | **10 mL/kg** |
| Prognostic checkpoint | Suck and tone at **1 week** |

### Classic exam traps

- **"Apgar of 3 at 1 minute = birth asphyxia."** → Apgar alone does not diagnose asphyxia; it is affected by prematurity, drugs and resuscitation.
- **"Cool all asphyxiated babies with a fan and wet towels."** → Wrong. Uncontrolled cooling is dangerous; **avoid hyperthermia** and offer TH only within NICU protocols.
- **"Give diazepam for neonatal seizures."** → Phenobarbital is first line; diazepam causes apnoea and is not recommended routinely.
- **"Give full maintenance fluids from day 1 and increase daily."** → Restrict if oliguric; AKI and SIADH are common.
- **"No seizures = mild HIE."** → Severe HIE may have few clinical seizures; grade on **consciousness and tone**.
- **"Floppy baby after a long labour: it's HIE; no need for antibiotics."** → Always exclude **sepsis, meningitis and hypoglycaemia**.
- **"Mild HIE needs no follow-up."** → Review development; mild cases can still have subtle problems.

### Questions seniors ask

1. **"What Sarnat stage is he, and why?"** Name each feature (consciousness, tone, suck, pupils, seizures).
2. **"How long was he ventilated and when did the heart rate come up?"** Reflects the depth of the insult.
3. **"Why is he on antibiotics?"** Infection risk (ROM, fever) and sepsis cannot be excluded; stop at 48 h if well and culture negative.
4. **"What is his urine output and fluid plan?"** mL/kg/h and total fluids mL/kg/day.
5. **"What anticonvulsant and how much?"** Phenobarbital in mg/kg and total mg.
6. **"Would you cool this baby?"** Not in our setting outside a NICU protocol (HELIX); keep normothermic.
7. **"What will you tell the parents about the future?"** Honest uncertainty; review suck and tone over the week; follow-up for development, hearing and vision.
