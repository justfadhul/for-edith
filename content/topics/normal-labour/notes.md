## In a nutshell

**Labour** is the process by which the fetus, placenta and membranes are expelled from the uterus after 28 weeks (Uganda's threshold of viability; WHO uses 22 weeks for statistics). **Normal labour** is spontaneous in onset, at term (37 to 42 weeks), with a **single fetus in vertex (occiput) presentation**, progressing without intervention to a spontaneous vaginal birth, with a healthy mother and baby afterwards.

Why it matters in Uganda: most maternal deaths (haemorrhage, obstructed labour, sepsis, ruptured uterus) and a large share of newborn deaths (birth asphyxia) happen **during labour and the first 24 hours after birth**. Almost every one of these starts as a normal labour that was not watched properly, or a problem that was not recognised on time. A junior clerk who can *diagnose labour, follow its progress on a partograph/Labour Care Guide, deliver the baby safely, give oxytocin in the first minute, and dry and assess the newborn* is directly preventing deaths.

> [!PEARL]
> The one thing never to forget: **every birth gets a uterotonic within one minute** (after you have palpated the abdomen to exclude another baby). Postpartum haemorrhage is the leading cause of maternal death in Uganda, and active management of the third stage (AMTSL) roughly halves it.

## Definitions & classification

| Term | Definition (WHO 2018 unless stated) |
|---|---|
| **Labour** | Regular, painful uterine contractions leading to progressive effacement and dilatation of the cervix |
| **False labour (Braxton Hicks)** | Irregular contractions, usually painless or felt in the lower abdomen, relieved by rest/walking, **no cervical change** |
| **Latent first stage** | Painful contractions with variable cervical change, including some effacement and slow dilatation **up to 5 cm** |
| **Active first stage** | Regular painful contractions, substantial effacement, faster dilatation **from 5 cm to full dilatation (10 cm)** |
| **Second stage** | From full dilatation to birth of the baby; the woman has an involuntary urge to bear down. Divided into *passive* (before bearing down) and *active* (expulsive) phases |
| **Third stage** | From birth of the baby to delivery of the placenta and membranes |
| **"Fourth stage"** | The first 1 to 2 hours after placental delivery, when PPH risk is highest (not an official stage, but taught and examined) |
| **Term** | 37+0 to 41+6 weeks |
| **Engagement** | Widest transverse diameter of the presenting part (biparietal, 9.5 cm) has passed through the pelvic inlet. Clinically: **2/5 or less palpable abdominally** |
| **Station** | Level of the lowest bony part of the head relative to the ischial spines (0 = at the spines; -3 to +3 cm) |
| **Effacement** | Shortening/thinning of the cervix as it is taken up into the lower segment |

> [!UGANDA]
> The **modified WHO partograph** (used in many Ugandan units and in the Uganda Clinical Guidelines) defines the active phase from **4 cm**. The **WHO Labour Care Guide (LCG)**, adopted by the Uganda Ministry of Health with the Essential Maternal and Newborn Clinical Care guidelines (2022), starts at **5 cm**. Know both; use whichever form your ward uses, and say which one you are using when you present.

### Duration of normal labour

| Stage | First labour (nullipara) | Subsequent labours (multipara) |
|---|---|---|
| Latent first stage | Variable; no standard length (can be 20 h or more) | Variable (can be 14 h or more) |
| Active first stage (from 5 cm) | Median about 4 h; usually not longer than 12 h | Median about 3 h; usually not longer than 10 h |
| Second stage | Usually up to 3 h | Usually up to 2 h |
| Third stage | 5-15 min with AMTSL; over 30 min = prolonged (retained placenta) | Same |

The old rule "cervix must dilate at 1 cm per hour" is the basis of the partograph alert line. WHO 2018 says a slower rate **alone** is not an indication to intervene if mother and baby are well, because many normal women dilate more slowly, especially before 5 cm.

## Epidemiology & Uganda context

- Uganda records about **1.5 million births a year**. Maternal mortality has fallen but remains high (UDHS 2022 estimated roughly 189 per 100,000 live births), and neonatal mortality is about 22 per 1,000 live births.
- Most births now happen in health facilities, but many are in **HC III and HC IV** units staffed by one or two midwives at night, with intermittent electricity, few Dopplers, and limited blood.
- The **"three delays"** model explains many deaths: delay in deciding to seek care, delay in reaching care (transport, money, night), and delay in receiving adequate care at the facility (no staff, no partograph filled, no oxytocin, no theatre).
- **Obstructed labour** remains a major cause of ruptured uterus, obstetric fistula (VVF/RVF) and fresh stillbirth in Uganda, and it is **preventable by careful monitoring and timely referral**.
- **Level of care:** HC II has no delivery service in principle; **HC III** provides basic emergency obstetric and newborn care (BEmONC: uterotonics, MgSO4, antibiotics, manual removal of placenta, MVA, assisted vaginal delivery, newborn resuscitation). **HC IV** and hospitals provide **comprehensive EmONC** (add caesarean section and blood transfusion).

> [!UGANDA]
> A woman labouring at an HC III with a slow partograph needs **early referral** (when the alert line is crossed), because transport to the HC IV/hospital can take hours. Waiting for the action line at an HC III is too late.

## Physiology: why labour happens and what makes it progress

### The 3 Ps (plus 2)

Progress in labour depends on the **Powers** (contractions), the **Passenger** (fetus: size, lie, presentation, position, attitude) and the **Passage** (bony pelvis and soft tissues). Add the **Psyche** (fear increases catecholamines, which slows labour; a companion helps) and the **Provider** (good support vs unnecessary interventions).

### Powers: uterine contractions

- The uterus is smooth muscle. Near term, **oxytocin receptors, gap junctions (connexin-43) and prostaglandin receptors** increase sharply, so the myometrium changes from quiet to coordinated.
- **Progesterone withdrawal** (functional, in humans) and a rise in **oestrogen**, **CRH from the placenta**, fetal cortisol and **prostaglandins (PGE2, PGF2alpha)** from membranes and decidua all push the uterus into labour.
- **Ferguson reflex**: stretching of the cervix and upper vagina by the presenting part triggers oxytocin release from the posterior pituitary, which strengthens contractions. This is a positive feedback loop, and it explains why labour accelerates once the head is well applied to the cervix, and why a poorly applied head (malpresentation, high head, big CPD) often gives poor contractions.
- **Fundal dominance**: contractions start near the cornua and spread downwards; they are strongest and longest in the fundus. The upper segment **contracts and retracts** (the fibres become permanently shorter after each contraction), and the passive lower segment stretches and thins. This pulls the cervix up (effacement) and open (dilatation).
- **Pathological retraction ring (Bandl's ring)**: in obstructed labour the upper segment keeps retracting against an immovable fetus, the lower segment becomes dangerously thin, and a ridge becomes visible across the abdomen. This is a warning of **imminent uterine rupture**.
- **Adequate contractions** in the active phase: 3-5 in 10 minutes, each lasting 40-60 seconds. More than 5 in 10 minutes (**tachysystole**) reduces placental blood flow (the placental bed is only perfused between contractions) and causes fetal distress.

### The passenger: fetal skull

```
     Anterior fontanelle (bregma) - diamond, 4 sutures meet
           \
   frontal  \   parietal        parietal
   bone ----[ ]--------------------------  occiput
             ^ sagittal suture           ^
                                          Posterior fontanelle (lambda)
                                          - Y/triangle, 3 sutures meet

  Diameters (anteroposterior) and the attitude that presents them:
  Suboccipitobregmatic   9.5 cm   well-flexed vertex  -> normal labour
  Suboccipitofrontal    10.0 cm   partly flexed vertex
  Occipitofrontal       11.5 cm   deflexed (military) -> OP positions
  Mentovertical         13.0 cm   brow  -> will NOT deliver vaginally at term
  Submentobregmatic      9.5 cm   face (mento-anterior can deliver)
  Transverse: biparietal 9.5 cm, bitemporal 8.0 cm
```

The **denominator** of a vertex is the **occiput**. Position describes where the occiput points: left occipito-anterior (LOA), direct OA, right OA, left/right occipito-transverse (LOT/ROT), and occipito-posterior (LOP/ROP/direct OP).

**Moulding**: the parietal bones override each other and the occipital/frontal bones slide under them, reducing the diameters a little. **Graded 0 (bones separate), + (touching), ++ (overlapping but reducible), +++ (overlapping, not reducible)**. **Caput succedaneum** is scalp oedema over the presenting part. Severe moulding plus big caput plus a head still high = **cephalopelvic disproportion (CPD)**.

### The passage: the pelvis

| Plane | Widest diameter | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Inlet (brim) | **Transverse** (about 13 cm); AP (obstetric conjugate) about 11 cm | Oval side-to-side |
| Mid-cavity | Round (about 12 cm) | Round |
| Outlet | **Anteroposterior** (about 12.5-13 cm with coccyx moved back); transverse (intertuberous) about 11 cm | Oval front-to-back |

Because the inlet is widest **side to side** and the outlet is widest **front to back**, the head must **enter transverse and rotate to anterior** to fit. That is the whole logic of the mechanism of labour. The **gynaecoid** pelvis is ideal; **android** pelvis (narrow forepelvis, prominent spines) favours deep transverse arrest and OP positions.

### The mechanism of labour (cardinal movements) for LOA

1. **Engagement**: head enters the brim in the **transverse or oblique** diameter, usually by 36 weeks in primigravidae (often only in labour in multiparas). Clinically, 2/5 or less palpable above the brim.
2. **Descent**: continues throughout labour, driven by contractions, and in the second stage by maternal bearing down.
3. **Flexion**: as the head meets resistance from the cervix, pelvic walls and pelvic floor, the chin tucks onto the chest, changing the presenting diameter from occipitofrontal (11.5 cm) to **suboccipitobregmatic (9.5 cm)**. The occiput becomes the leading part.
4. **Internal rotation**: the leading occiput hits the gutter-shaped **pelvic floor (levator ani)**, which directs it forwards and medially; the occiput rotates **one-eighth of a circle (45 degrees)** from LOA to OA, so the sagittal suture lies in the AP diameter of the outlet. The shoulders are still oblique, so the neck twists slightly. (Rule: "whatever part reaches the pelvic floor first rotates forwards.")
5. **Extension (crowning and birth of the head)**: the nape of the neck pivots under the pubic symphysis (suboccipital region) and the head extends: occiput, then bregma, forehead, face and chin sweep over the perineum. **Crowning** = the head no longer recedes between contractions (the biparietal diameter has passed the vulval ring).
6. **Restitution**: once the head is free, the neck untwists and the occiput turns back 45 degrees to the side it came from (LOA baby: occiput turns to the mother's left).
7. **External rotation**: the anterior shoulder reaches the pelvic floor and rotates forwards to lie under the symphysis; the head turns a further 45 degrees, so the face looks at the mother's thigh. The bisacromial diameter (12 cm) is now in the AP of the outlet.
8. **Expulsion**: the anterior shoulder is born under the symphysis by gentle downward (posterior) traction, then the posterior shoulder by upward (anterior) traction, then the trunk by lateral flexion.

> [!EXAM]
> Viva favourite: "Why does the head rotate?" Answer: **the shape of the pelvic floor** (a forward-sloping gutter formed by the levator ani) and the **shape of the pelvis** (outlet widest AP) make the leading, well-flexed occiput rotate anteriorly. A lax pelvic floor (grand multipara) or a deflexed head (OP) is why rotation fails.

### Third stage physiology

After the baby is born, the uterus contracts down sharply. The placental site shrinks but the placenta cannot, so it **shears off** the decidua spongiosa; a retroplacental clot forms. Signs of separation: (1) **the uterus becomes firm, globular and rises** in the abdomen, (2) **a gush of blood**, (3) **lengthening of the cord** at the introitus. Haemostasis depends on the **"living ligatures"**: the criss-cross myometrial fibres kink the spiral arteries shut when the uterus contracts. This is why **atony = bleeding**, and why an oxytocic is the core of PPH prevention.

## Clinical acumen

### Focused history (when a woman arrives "in labour")

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Age, gravida, para, **gestational age** (LNMP, early scan) | Term vs preterm (preterm labour changes management: steroids, tocolysis, neonatal team). Grand multiparity (P5+) = PPH and uterine rupture risk |
| **When did pains start? How often? How long? Are they getting stronger?** | Distinguish true vs false labour; estimate how long she has been labouring (prolonged labour at home is common in Uganda) |
| **Has there been a show or water breaking? When? Colour of the liquor?** | Rupture of membranes over 18 h = infection risk; **meconium** = possible fetal compromise; **blood** = abruption or placenta praevia |
| **Is there vaginal bleeding (more than a show)?** | Antepartum haemorrhage: never do a digital VE until praevia is excluded |
| **Is the baby moving normally?** | Reduced movements may mean fetal compromise or IUFD |
| **Headache, blurred vision, epigastric pain, fits?** | Pre-eclampsia/eclampsia |
| **Fever, foul discharge, burning urine?** | Chorioamnionitis, UTI (needs antibiotics) |
| **Previous deliveries**: mode, **previous caesarean or uterine surgery**, birth weights, PPH, perineal tears, stillbirths, duration | Previous CS = trial of labour after CS (TOLAC) must be at a facility with theatre; past big baby/shoulder dystocia/PPH predicts recurrence |
| **ANC**: number of visits, **HIV status** (and ART/viral load), syphilis, Hb, blood group, sickle cell, malaria prophylaxis (IPTp) | HIV-positive: continue ART, avoid unnecessary ARM/episiotomy, give infant prophylaxis. Anaemia lowers tolerance to blood loss |
| Chronic illness (hypertension, diabetes, cardiac disease, sickle cell, asthma) | Affects choice of uterotonic, fluids, analgesia, monitoring |
| **Birth companion, birth plan, family planning intentions** | Respectful maternity care; PPFP (e.g. IUD within 48 h, implant) is offered in labour ward |

### Examination

**General**: pallor, oedema, hydration (dry tongue, ketones), temperature, **pulse, BP**, respiratory rate. Tachycardia above 100 with fever suggests infection or dehydration; above 120 is an alert.

**Abdominal examination** (between contractions):
1. Inspect: scars (previous CS), shape (longitudinal ovoid), fetal movements, **Bandl's ring**, full bladder.
2. **Symphysis-fundal height** and fundal height relative to dates.
3. **Lie** (longitudinal/transverse/oblique), **presentation** (cephalic/breech), **position** (where is the back?).
4. **Descent in fifths** of the head palpable above the brim (5/5 = floating; 0/5 = fully in the pelvis). **This is more useful than station** because caput can make station look low when the head is actually high.
5. **Contractions**: palpate for 10 minutes; count how many and how long each lasts (from when you feel the uterus harden until it relaxes).
6. **Fetal heart rate** with Pinard or Doppler for a full minute, **immediately after a contraction** (normal 110-160 bpm).

**Vaginal examination** (aseptic, after consent, with a chaperone; **not if there is APH** until praevia is excluded):
1. Vulva: discharge, liquor colour, bleeding, warts/ulcers, FGM scarring, oedema.
2. **Cervix**: position, consistency, **effacement** (length in cm or %), **dilatation** (cm), application to the presenting part.
3. **Membranes**: intact or ruptured; **liquor** colour (clear/meconium/blood-stained).
4. **Presenting part**: vertex, face, brow, breech; **exclude cord prolapse**.
5. **Position** by sutures and fontanelles (feel the posterior fontanelle: small Y-shape, anterior = OA).
6. **Station** relative to the ischial spines, **caput** and **moulding**.
7. **Pelvis** (clinical pelvimetry): sacral promontory reachable? spines prominent? subpubic angle (admits 2 fingers normally)? intertuberous diameter (4 knuckles)?

> [!PEARL]
> The most common student error is calling a big caput "head at +2". Always **correlate station on VE with descent in fifths on abdominal palpation**. If the abdomen says 3/5 but the VE says +2, you are feeling caput, and the head is not engaged. That combination (high head + big caput + severe moulding) is obstruction until proved otherwise.

### Red flags in labour (act and call for help)

> [!REDFLAG]
> - Vaginal bleeding more than a show (abruption, praevia, ruptured uterus)
> - FHR below 110 or above 160, or decelerations after contractions (late decelerations)
> - Thick meconium-stained liquor
> - Cord prolapse or cord presentation
> - Maternal fever of 38 degrees C or more, maternal pulse above 120, BP 140/90 or more (or diastolic 110 or more), convulsions
> - Partograph crossing the **action line**, or an LCG cervix alert (time spent at one dilatation reaches the LCG threshold: 6 h at 5 cm, 5 h at 6 cm, 3 h at 7 cm, 2.5 h at 8 cm, 2 h at 9 cm)
> - Signs of obstruction: head not descending, severe moulding (+++), large caput, oedematous cervix/vulva, haematuria, **Bandl's ring**, maternal exhaustion and dehydration
> - Signs of rupture: sudden severe pain, contractions stop, fetal parts easily felt, shock, FHR lost
> - Malpresentation discovered in labour (breech, face, brow, transverse lie, compound)

### Thinking like a clinician

- **"Is she in labour?"** Only two things make the diagnosis: *regular painful contractions* and *cervical change on two examinations (or a cervix that is already 4-5 cm and effaced)*. A show and ruptured membranes support it but do not define it.
- **"Is it normal?"** Ask the four questions every time you review a labouring woman: *Is the mother well? Is the baby well? Is labour progressing? Is the partograph/LCG up to date?*
- **If progress is slow, think the 3 Ps in order**: *Powers* (count contractions; inadequate? dehydrated? full bladder?), *Passenger* (malposition like OP, deflexion, big baby, malpresentation), *Passage* (CPD, contracted pelvis). Poor contractions in a multipara are rare; in a multipara slow progress is **obstruction** until proved otherwise, and oxytocin can rupture her uterus.
- **If X then think Y**:
  - Contractions strong, head not descending, moulding +++ in a primigravida -> CPD/obstruction -> caesarean, not oxytocin.
  - Maternal tachycardia + fever + tender uterus + foul liquor -> chorioamnionitis -> antibiotics, expedite delivery.
  - Suprapubic swelling that dulls on percussion -> full bladder -> catheterise, then reassess (a full bladder can stop descent).
  - Sudden fetal bradycardia after ARM -> cord prolapse -> VE immediately.
  - Lower abdominal ridge + tender lower segment + haematuria -> impending rupture -> stop oxytocin, emergency CS.
- **Common student mistakes**: doing VEs too often (infection) or not at all; plotting the first VE in the latent phase on the alert line; forgetting the FHR after a contraction; forgetting to palpate for a second twin before giving oxytocin; pulling on the cord before the uterus has contracted (uterine inversion); leaving the mother unobserved in the first hour after birth.

## Differential diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing features | Key investigation / action |
|---|---|---|
| **True labour** | Regular, increasing contractions; cervical dilatation/effacement progress; show | Serial VE 4 h apart; partograph/LCG |
| **False labour / Braxton Hicks** | Irregular, not increasing, relieved by rest/analgesia, cervix unchanged | Reassess after 2-4 h; discharge with advice if cervix unchanged and all well |
| **Prolonged latent phase** | Painful contractions, cervix less than 5 cm, slow change over many hours | Supportive care, analgesia, hydration; do not augment before 5 cm if mother/baby well |
| **Preterm labour** | Labour before 37 weeks | Gestational age dating; steroids (dexamethasone 6 mg IM 12-hourly x 4 doses if 28-34 weeks), tocolysis per protocol |
| **Placental abruption** | Continuous pain, tense tender "woody" uterus, dark bleeding, shock out of proportion, FHR abnormal or absent | Clinical; ultrasound may miss it; clotting (bedside clotting test) |
| **Uterine rupture** | Previous CS/grand multipara/obstructed labour; sudden pain, loss of contractions, shock, easily palpable fetal parts | Clinical; emergency laparotomy |
| **Chorioamnionitis** | Fever, maternal and fetal tachycardia, uterine tenderness, foul liquor | FBC, clinical; antibiotics |
| **UTI/pyelonephritis, acute abdomen (appendicitis, torsion)** | Pain not rhythmic with uterine hardening, dysuria, loin pain, localised tenderness | Urinalysis, ultrasound |
| **Severe pre-eclampsia (epigastric pain)** | BP 160/110, proteinuria, headache, RUQ pain | Urine protein, BP, platelets, LFTs |

## Investigations

Normal labour is a **clinical** diagnosis. These are what you check on admission.

| Test | What you are looking for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Haemoglobin** (HemoCue or FBC) | Anaemia (Hb below 11 g/dL; severe below 7) | Tolerance of blood loss; need for group and crossmatch |
| **Blood group and Rh** (if not in ANC card) | Rh negative | Anti-D after delivery if baby Rh positive; crossmatch if at risk |
| **HIV test** (if unknown or last test over 3 months) | Positive result | Start ART immediately, infant prophylaxis, feeding counselling |
| **Syphilis (RPR/SD Bioline dual test)** | Reactive | Treat mother and baby |
| **Urinalysis** | Protein (pre-eclampsia), ketones (dehydration/starvation), glucose, nitrites | Hydration and hypertension screening |
| **Malaria RDT** (if febrile) | Positive | Treat; fever in labour is not always chorioamnionitis |
| **Random blood glucose** | Diabetes/hypoglycaemia | Known diabetes or prolonged fasting |
| **Ultrasound** (if available and indicated) | Presentation, number of fetuses, placenta site, FHR | Confirm uncertain presentation, exclude twins, placenta praevia |

## Management

### Setting: who does what where

| Level | What can be done |
|---|---|
| **HC III** | Normal delivery, partograph/LCG, AMTSL, newborn resuscitation with bag and mask, loading dose MgSO4, first dose antibiotics, manual removal of placenta, **early referral** |
| **HC IV / general hospital** | All of the above plus caesarean section, blood transfusion, oxytocin augmentation with close monitoring, vacuum delivery, SCBU (some) |
| **Regional/national referral (e.g. Mengo, Mulago, Kawempe)** | Specialist obstetric and neonatal care, ICU, high-risk cases |

### 1. Admission and first-stage care

1. **Greet, introduce yourself, explain, get consent.** Respectful maternity care is a WHO standard, and a relaxed woman labours better (less adrenaline).
2. **Take the focused history and examine** (above). Check the ANC card.
3. **Decide**: false labour, latent phase, or active labour? Normal or high risk?
4. **Start the partograph/LCG** once she is in the active phase (4 cm on the modified WHO partograph; 5 cm on the LCG). Record latent-phase findings in the notes.
5. **Supportive care**:
   - **Birth companion** of her choice.
   - **Oral fluids and light food** (do not starve low-risk women; dehydration and ketosis impair contractions). If vomiting or dehydrated: IV Ringer's lactate or normal saline.
   - **Mobility and upright positions**; avoid lying flat on the back (aortocaval compression reduces placental blood flow).
   - **Empty the bladder** every 2 hours (a full bladder impedes descent and causes atony later).
   - **Pain relief** on request: non-drug methods (massage, warm compress, breathing, positions); pethidine 50-100 mg IM (with an antiemetic) in the early active phase if needed, **avoid within about 2-3 hours of expected birth** because it depresses the newborn's breathing. Epidural is available only in a few Ugandan hospitals.
6. **Monitoring** (modified WHO partograph schedule):

| Parameter | Frequency in active first stage | Frequency in second stage |
|---|---|---|
| Fetal heart rate (after a contraction, 1 full minute) | Every 30 min (WHO 2018: every 15-30 min) | Every 5 min (or after every contraction) |
| Contractions (number in 10 min, duration) | Every 30 min | Continuously |
| Maternal pulse | Every 30 min | Every 5-15 min |
| BP | Every 4 h (more often if abnormal) | Every 15-30 min |
| Temperature | Every 2 h (LCG: every 4 h) | |
| Urine (volume, protein, ketones) | Every time passed | |
| Vaginal examination (cervix, descent, liquor, moulding) | Every 4 h, or sooner if a clinical indication | When the urge to push begins or to confirm full dilatation |

7. **Avoid routine interventions** in a normal labour: no routine enema, pubic shaving, routine amniotomy, routine IV fluids, routine oxytocin, or routine CTG (WHO 2018).
8. **Infection prevention**: hand hygiene, sterile gloves for each VE, minimise VEs, especially after rupture of membranes.

> [!UGANDA]
> Women living with HIV: continue their ART in labour (TLD), avoid artificial rupture of membranes and unnecessary episiotomy or instrumental delivery where possible (they increase the baby's exposure to maternal blood/secretions), and start infant prophylaxis (nevirapine +/- zidovudine per the current consolidated guidelines) within 6-12 hours of birth.

### 2. Second stage: conducting the delivery

**Diagnose full dilatation** by VE (or visible head at the introitus). Signs: urge to push, anal pouting, perineal bulging, the head visible.

1. **Prepare**: delivery pack open on a clean trolley, **oxytocin 10 IU drawn up** (check for a second twin before giving), newborn corner ready (warm room, 2 dry towels, cap, bag and mask size 1 and 0 checked, suction bulb, clock), gloves, apron, goggles.
2. **Position**: the position she prefers (semi-sitting, left lateral, squatting, kneeling). WHO encourages upright positions.
3. **Pushing**: let her push **with her own urge** rather than coached prolonged Valsalva. In a primigravida with an epidural, allow a passive phase of 1-2 hours before pushing if the FHR is fine.
4. **Monitor FHR every 5 minutes** or after each contraction; pulse every 15 minutes.
5. **Perineal protection**: warm compresses, perineal massage in second stage, and "hands-on" control of the head at crowning. Ask her to **pant or give short gentle pushes** as the head crowns, so it is born slowly between contractions (sudden expulsion tears the perineum).
6. **Episiotomy only if indicated** (fetal distress needing rapid delivery, instrumental delivery, rigid perineum causing delay, shoulder dystocia manoeuvres). Not routine (WHO 2018). Mediolateral, at about 60 degrees from the midline at crowning, after infiltrating 1% lignocaine.
7. **Delivery of the head**: keep the head flexed with the left hand on the occiput; the right hand supports the perineum with a pad. Let the head extend slowly.
8. **Wipe the face** (do not routinely suction). **Feel for cord around the neck**: if loose, slip it over the head; if tight, clamp twice and cut between (rarely needed; do this only if it prevents delivery).
9. **Wait for restitution and external rotation** (the next contraction).
10. **Deliver the anterior shoulder** by gentle downward traction on the head (hands on the sides of the head, over the parietal bones, never pulling on the neck), then the **posterior shoulder** by lifting upwards, then the body by lateral flexion towards the mother's abdomen.
11. **Note the time of birth** and sex, place the baby on the mother's abdomen.

> [!REDFLAG]
> **Shoulder dystocia**: the head is born and then retracts against the perineum ("turtle sign"), and the shoulders do not deliver with gentle traction. Call for help, stop pushing, do not pull hard, **McRoberts manoeuvre + suprapubic pressure** (HELPERR). See the vaginal delivery skills topic.

### 3. Immediate newborn care (the first minute, then the first hour)

1. **Dry thoroughly** (head, body, limbs) with a warm dry towel within the first 30 seconds while assessing breathing; remove the wet towel.
2. **Crying or breathing well?**
   - **Yes**: keep **skin-to-skin** on the mother's chest, cover with a dry cloth and cap. **Delay cord clamping for 1-3 minutes** (not earlier than 1 minute).
   - **No**: clear the airway **only if** secretions are blocking it, stimulate by rubbing the back, and if still not breathing **clamp and cut the cord and start bag-and-mask ventilation within the Golden Minute** (see Neonatal resuscitation topic).
3. **Give the mother oxytocin 10 IU IM** within 1 minute of birth (after palpating the abdomen to exclude a second baby).
4. **Cord**: clamp/tie at about 2 cm and 5 cm from the umbilicus and cut between with sterile scissors. Apply **chlorhexidine 7.1% digluconate gel** to the stump (Uganda policy since 2016), keep it clean and dry; nothing else on the cord.
5. **Breastfeeding within the first hour**, uninterrupted skin-to-skin for at least an hour.
6. **After the first hour**: weigh, full newborn examination, **vitamin K (phytomenadione) 1 mg IM** into the anterolateral thigh (**0.5 mg for preterm/low birth weight babies** per local protocol), **tetracycline 1% eye ointment** to both eyes once, identification band.
7. **Immunisation**: BCG and OPV-0 at birth (and hepatitis B birth dose where it has been introduced).
8. **Do not bathe for at least 24 hours** (hypothermia risk). Keep mother and baby together (rooming-in).

### 4. Third stage: active management (AMTSL)

The three components, in order:

1. **Uterotonic within 1 minute of birth** (after excluding another fetus).
2. **Controlled cord traction (CCT)** when the uterus contracts, **with counter-traction** on the uterus (Brandt-Andrews manoeuvre). CCT is recommended where skilled attendants are present; if the provider is not skilled in CCT, wait for signs of separation and let the mother push the placenta out.
3. **Uterine tone assessment** (palpation) after the placenta, and fundal massage if soft; check every 15 minutes for 2 hours. Sustained continuous massage is no longer recommended when oxytocin has been given, but tone must be checked.

**How to do CCT**
1. Clamp the cord close to the perineum and hold it with one hand.
2. Place the other hand just above the pubic bone and **push the uterus upwards (towards the umbilicus)** during a contraction. This counter-traction prevents **uterine inversion**.
3. Apply **steady, gentle downward traction** on the cord during the contraction.
4. If the placenta does not descend within 30-40 seconds, stop, wait for the next contraction and repeat.
5. As the placenta appears, hold it with both hands and turn it gently to twist the membranes into a rope; tease them out slowly (sponge forceps if needed).
6. **Examine the placenta and membranes** for completeness (missing cotyledons, succenturiate lobe vessels running to the edge of the membranes), count the cord vessels (2 arteries + 1 vein).

| Drug (PPH prevention) | Dose | Route | Frequency | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Oxytocin** (first choice) | **10 IU** | **IM** (or slow IV) | Once, within 1 min of birth | Store at 2-8 degrees C (cold chain). Acts in 2-3 min IM. Not a bolus IV push in shocked or cardiac patients |
| **Misoprostol** (if no oxytocin) | **600 micrograms** | **Oral** | Once | Uganda Clinical Guidelines dose; WHO allows 400 or 600 micrograms. Side effects: shivering, fever |
| **Heat-stable carbetocin** (where supplied) | 100 micrograms | IM or IV | Once | WHO alternative; does not need cold chain; **not** for induction/augmentation |
| **Ergometrine** (or oxytocin-ergometrine) | 0.2 mg (ergometrine) | IM | Once | **Contraindicated in hypertension, pre-eclampsia, cardiac disease**; causes sustained contraction; may retain placenta |

> [!DRUG]
> Always palpate the abdomen for a second twin before giving the oxytocin. Oxytocin given with an undiagnosed second twin in utero can make the uterus contract tonically around the second baby and asphyxiate it.

> [!REDFLAG]
> **Retained placenta**: placenta not delivered 30 minutes after birth despite AMTSL. Empty the bladder, repeat oxytocin 10 IU IM, try CCT again; if still retained, **manual removal of placenta** under analgesia/anaesthesia with IV antibiotics (single dose ampicillin 2 g IV + metronidazole 500 mg IV, or cefazolin 1 g IV + metronidazole 500 mg IV). If bleeding, manage as PPH.

### 5. The fourth stage (first 1-2 hours) and early postpartum care

1. **Inspect the perineum, vagina and cervix** for tears; repair (see Vaginal delivery skills).
2. **Estimate blood loss** (normal under 500 mL). Weigh soaked pads and drapes if needed.
3. **Monitor every 15 minutes for 2 hours**: uterine tone (and massage if soft), vaginal bleeding, pulse, BP. Then every 30 min for the next hour, then 4-hourly.
4. **Empty the bladder**: encourage voiding within 2-6 hours (a full bladder pushes up the uterus and causes atony).
5. **Keep mother and baby together**, observe the baby (breathing, colour, warmth, feeding).
6. **Counsel**: danger signs (heavy bleeding, fever, headache/fits, foul lochia, baby not feeding), exclusive breastfeeding, **postpartum family planning** (IUD within 48 h, implant, POPs; LAM), hygiene.
7. **Discharge** after at least **24 hours** for facility births (WHO), with postnatal visits at day 3, day 7-14 and 6 weeks.

### Emergency flow: something goes wrong during a "normal" labour

1. **Call for help** (senior midwife/doctor).
2. **ABC for the mother**: left lateral position, oxygen if hypoxic, IV access with two large-bore cannulas, send blood for Hb/grouping/crossmatch.
3. **Assess the baby**: FHR; if abnormal, stop oxytocin, IV fluids, left lateral, VE to exclude cord prolapse and assess progress.
4. **Decide the route and timing of delivery**: fully dilated with head low (0/5, station +2 or more) = assisted vaginal delivery (vacuum); not fully dilated or head high = emergency caesarean.
5. **If at HC III**: stabilise, refer with a completed referral note and the partograph, accompanying health worker, IV line running, and (if relevant) MgSO4 loading dose or first-dose antibiotics already given.

## Complications

| Complication | Cause/mechanism | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| **Prolonged/obstructed labour** | CPD, malposition, malpresentation | Partograph/LCG, timely referral, CS |
| **Uterine rupture** | Obstruction, oxytocin misuse in multiparas, previous CS | Never augment a multipara without excluding obstruction; TOLAC only in hospital |
| **Postpartum haemorrhage** | Atony (tone), trauma, retained tissue, coagulopathy (the 4 Ts) | AMTSL, monitoring in the fourth stage, early detection |
| **Perineal tears/OASI** | Uncontrolled delivery of the head, instrumental delivery, big baby | Controlled crowning, warm compress, restrictive episiotomy |
| **Infection (chorioamnionitis, puerperal sepsis)** | Prolonged ROM, many VEs, poor asepsis | Hand hygiene, limit VEs, antibiotics when indicated |
| **Fetal hypoxia/birth asphyxia** | Tachysystole, cord compression, prolonged labour | FHR monitoring, avoid over-augmentation, prompt delivery |
| **Obstetric fistula (VVF/RVF)** | Prolonged pressure necrosis of the bladder/rectum by the impacted head | Prevent obstructed labour; catheterise for 10-14 days after prolonged obstructed labour |
| **Uterine inversion** | Cord traction without counter-traction on a relaxed uterus | Always counter-traction during CCT |
| **Shoulder dystocia** | Macrosomia, diabetes, prolonged second stage | Anticipate, HELPERR drill |

## Ward-round & exam pearls

### Model presentation (active labour)

"This is **Mrs N.A., a 24-year-old G2P1+0** at **39 weeks by dates consistent with an early scan**, admitted at 06:00 with **regular painful contractions since 01:00** and a show. Her ANC was attended four times; she is **HIV-negative**, VDRL non-reactive, Hb 11.8 g/dL, blood group O positive. Her previous delivery was a spontaneous vaginal delivery of a 3.2 kg baby two years ago.

On examination she is in good general condition, afebrile, **pulse 88, BP 118/72**. The fetus is in **longitudinal lie, cephalic presentation, LOA, 2/5 palpable**, with **3 contractions in 10 minutes lasting 40 seconds**, FHR 140 bpm regular. On VE at 06:30 the **cervix was 5 cm, 80% effaced**, membranes intact, vertex at station -1, **no caput, no moulding**, adequate pelvis.

**Summary**: a multigravida at term in the **active first stage of labour**, mother and fetus in good condition. **Plan**: partograph/LCG started, supportive care with companion and oral fluids, FHR and contractions every 30 minutes, VE at 10:30 or earlier if indicated, prepare for AMTSL and newborn care."

### Viva questions (with answers)

1. **What are the signs of placental separation?** Uterus becomes firm, globular and rises; gush of blood; lengthening of the cord at the vulva.
2. **Why is the suboccipitobregmatic diameter important?** It is the smallest AP diameter (9.5 cm), presented by a well-flexed vertex, so normal labour requires flexion.
3. **What is AMTSL and why does it work?** Uterotonic within 1 min, CCT with counter-traction, uterine tone checks. The oxytocin makes the uterus contract, which shears the placenta off and clamps the spiral arteries ("living ligatures").
4. **When would you not give ergometrine?** Hypertension/pre-eclampsia, cardiac disease, retained second twin; it causes vasoconstriction and a sustained tetanic contraction.
5. **Define engagement and how you assess it.** Passage of the biparietal diameter through the inlet; 2/5 or less palpable abdominally.
6. **What is the Ferguson reflex?** Stretch of the cervix/upper vagina by the presenting part leads to oxytocin release, reinforcing contractions.
7. **Name the components of the partograph.** Fetal condition (FHR, liquor, moulding), labour progress (cervix, descent, contractions), drugs and fluids, maternal condition (pulse, BP, temperature, urine).
8. **What is restitution?** The return of the head to its natural alignment with the shoulders after delivery of the head (a 45-degree turn back towards the side where the occiput started).

### OSCE tips

- Mechanism of labour on a mannequin: narrate each movement *and* say what the pelvis is doing (for example, "the head flexes as it meets resistance, presenting the 9.5 cm diameter").
- AMTSL station: say "I palpate the abdomen to exclude a second twin" *before* you give oxytocin. That single sentence often carries marks.
- Always greet, consent, maintain dignity and cover the woman.

## Mnemonics & memory aids

- **Cardinal movements**: "**E**very **D**ecent **F**etus **I**s **E**xceptionally **R**otated **E**very **E**xpulsion" = Engagement, Descent, Flexion, Internal rotation, Extension, Restitution, External rotation, Expulsion.
- **Signs of placental separation**: "**G**ush, **L**engthening, **R**ise" (GLR) of the uterus.
- **AMTSL**: "**O**-**C**-**M**": Oxytocin in 1 minute, Controlled cord traction, Massage/tone checks.
- **Progress problems**: the "3 Ps" (Powers, Passenger, Passage) plus Psyche.
- **Newborn care**: "**D**ry, **S**kin-to-skin, **D**elay cord clamp, **B**reastfeed, **V**itamin K, **E**yes, **C**ord care" = "Do Some Deliveries, Babies Very Easily Cry".
- **Pelvis**: "**In**let **T**ransverse, **Out**let **A**P": the head enters sideways and leaves face-down.
