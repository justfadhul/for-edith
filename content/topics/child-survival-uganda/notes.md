## In a nutshell

**Child survival** is about preventing the deaths of children under 5. Ugandan children are far more likely to survive now than in 1990, but about **1 in 19** still dies before the fifth birthday, and **progress has slowed**, especially for **newborns**.

The causes are not mysterious. Most deaths come from a **few conditions**: preterm birth, birth asphyxia, neonatal sepsis, **malaria**, **pneumonia** and **diarrhoea**, made deadlier by **malnutrition** and **HIV**. Each has a **cheap, proven intervention**. Children die because those interventions **don't reach them**, or reach them **too late**: the family delays seeking care, the child can't get to a facility, or the facility can't give quality care.

Uganda's main strategies are:
- the **continuum of care** (adolescence → pregnancy → birth → newborn → childhood);
- **IMCI** at facilities, **iCCM** by Village Health Teams (VHTs), and **ETAT** in hospitals;
- **prevention**: immunisation (now including **malaria vaccine**), ITNs, breastfeeding, nutrition, WASH;
- **accountability**: maternal and perinatal death surveillance and response (**MPDSR**).

> [!PEARL]
> The one thing never to forget: **a child who dies of pneumonia, diarrhoea or malaria almost always had a treatable illness for which care arrived too late.** On every ward round, ask "When did it start? When did they seek care? What happened on the way?" (the **three delays**).

## Definitions & classification

| Indicator | Definition | Uganda (UDHS 2022, approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| **Neonatal mortality rate (NMR)** | Deaths in the first **28 days** per 1,000 live births | **22** |
| **Post-neonatal mortality** | Deaths from 1 to 11 months per 1,000 live births | About 14 (IMR − NMR) |
| **Infant mortality rate (IMR)** | Deaths before the **first birthday** per 1,000 live births | **36** |
| **Child mortality rate** | Deaths between 1 and 4 years per 1,000 children surviving to age 1 | About 17 |
| **Under-five mortality rate (U5MR)** | Probability of dying before age **5** per 1,000 live births | **52** |
| **Perinatal mortality** | Stillbirths (≥28 weeks) + early neonatal deaths (first 7 days) per 1,000 total births | Around 30–40 (VERIFY) |
| **Stillbirth rate** | Babies born with no signs of life at ≥28 weeks per 1,000 total births | |
| **Stunting** | Height-for-age < −2 Z (chronic undernutrition) | About **24%** of under-5s (UDHS 2022; 29% in 2016) |
| **Wasting** | Weight-for-height < −2 Z or MUAC <12.5 cm (acute) | Low nationally (about 3–4%) but much higher in **Karamoja** |
| **Zero-dose child** | No DTP1 | 36,452 in 2025 (MoH) |

**SDG 3.2 (by 2030):** end preventable deaths of newborns and under-fives; every country to reduce **NMR to ≤12** and **U5MR to ≤25** per 1,000 live births.

> [!NOTE]
> UDHS figures are averages over the 5 years before the survey; check the UDHS 2022 final report for exact values and confidence intervals. Different sources (UN IGME modelled estimates, UDHS) give slightly different numbers. In an exam, quote one source and its year.

## Epidemiology & Uganda context

### Global picture (WHO fact sheet, data for 2024)

- **4.9 million** children died before age 5 in 2024: one every 6 seconds.
- **2.3 million (47%)** were **neonatal** deaths.
- Global **U5MR 37.4** per 1,000 (less than half the 2000 level), but progress has **slowed since 2015** and the number of deaths has stagnated since 2022.
- **Sub-Saharan Africa: U5MR 71.6** per 1,000, nearly 14 times higher than Europe/North America, and **58%** of all under-five deaths.
- Leading causes: **preterm complications, intrapartum events, pneumonia, diarrhoea, malaria**.

### Uganda

- **Trend:** U5MR fell from about 90 (UDHS 2011) to **64 (2016)** to **52 (2022)**. Neonatal mortality has fallen much more slowly (27 → **22**), so the **newborn share** of under-five deaths keeps rising (now about 40%).
- **Causes:**
  - Newborns: **prematurity**, **birth asphyxia**, **sepsis/pneumonia**, congenital anomalies.
  - 1–59 months: **malaria** (MoH: "the leading cause of illness and death of young children in Uganda"), **pneumonia**, **diarrhoea**, plus **severe anaemia**, **HIV**, **measles** (outbreaks in 66 districts in 2025), **injuries** (burns, drowning, road traffic), and **sickle cell disease**.
  - **Undernutrition** (stunting, wasting, micronutrient deficiency) is an underlying cause in nearly half of deaths.
- **Inequity:** mortality is higher in **rural** areas, in the **poorest** quintile, among children of mothers with **no education**, in **Karamoja**, West Nile and refugee-hosting districts, among children born **<24 months** after a sibling, and to **adolescent mothers**.
- **Where children die:** many at **home** or **in transit**; in hospitals, most deaths happen within **24–48 hours of admission**, a sign of late arrival and of triage delays.

> [!UGANDA]
> The health-system ladder: **VHT (iCCM)** → **HC II** (parish; outpatient) → **HC III** (sub-county; maternity, lab) → **HC IV** (county; theatre, blood) → **general hospital** → **regional referral hospital** → **national referral hospital** (Mulago, and specialised units). Mission hospitals such as **Mengo** and **Nsambya** (Uganda Protestant/Catholic Medical Bureaus) provide a large share of care. Common gaps: stock-outs (amoxicillin DT, zinc, ACTs), **oxygen** and pulse oximeters, blood, transport for referral, and staffing at night.

## Principles: why children die, and the frameworks

### The causal chain

```
Distal (basic)       Poverty, maternal education, gender, conflict, health financing
        │
Intermediate         Household food insecurity, poor WASH, crowding, no ITN,
(underlying)         short birth spacing, low vaccine coverage, care-seeking practices
        │
Proximate            Low birth weight, malnutrition, HIV, exposure to pathogens
        │
Immediate            Pneumonia · diarrhoea · malaria · neonatal sepsis · asphyxia · measles
        │
Death  ◄─ the three delays: (1) deciding to seek care (2) reaching care (3) receiving quality care
```

(Adapted from the Mosley–Chen framework and the UNICEF conceptual framework of malnutrition.)

### Why malnutrition multiplies the risk
Undernutrition weakens the gut barrier, cell-mediated immunity and the response to infection, and infection in turn worsens malnutrition (the **infection–malnutrition cycle**). A wasted child with pneumonia or diarrhoea is many times more likely to die than a well-nourished child with the same illness.

## Clinical acumen

Child survival is public health, but it is practised **one child at a time**. The clinical skill is **IMCI-style assessment**: picking out the child who will die without urgent action.

### Focused history (for every sick child under 5)

| Ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Age, and **how long ill** | Infants and long illnesses carry higher risk |
| **General danger signs**: drinking/breastfeeding? vomiting everything? convulsions? | Any one = urgent referral |
| Cough or difficult breathing; diarrhoea (how long, blood?); fever (how long, malaria test?); ear problem | The four IMCI main symptoms |
| **Where did they go first?** (drug shop, herbalist, VHT, clinic) and what was given | First delay, prior treatment, partial antibiotics |
| **Feeding** (breastfeeding, complementary foods, appetite) | Malnutrition risk |
| **Immunisation** (card) and **vitamin A**, deworming | Missed opportunities |
| **HIV** status of mother and child | HIV changes thresholds and treatment |
| **ITN** use last night | Malaria prevention |
| **Birth history** for infants (preterm, LBW, asphyxia) | Newborn risk |
| **Social**: who cares for the child, distance to facility, money for transport | Second delay; follow-up feasibility |

### Examination

- **Look, listen, feel** for danger signs: lethargy, unconsciousness, convulsing.
- **Breathing**: count RR for a full minute (fast: ≥60 if <2 months; ≥50 at 2–11 months; ≥40 at 12–59 months), chest indrawing, stridor, **SpO₂** (hypoxaemia <90%).
- **Dehydration**: general condition, sunken eyes, drinking, skin pinch.
- **Fever/malaria**: stiff neck, measles signs, RDT.
- **Malnutrition**: weight-for-height/length Z-score, **MUAC** (<11.5 cm severe, 11.5–12.4 moderate), bilateral **pitting oedema**.
- **Anaemia**: palmar pallor (severe/some).
- **Ear**: discharge, mastoid swelling.

### Red flags

> [!REDFLAG]
> - **ETAT emergency signs**: obstructed breathing, central cyanosis, severe respiratory distress; shock (cold hands + CRT >3 s + weak fast pulse); coma (AVPU P or U); convulsions; severe dehydration with lethargy, sunken eyes and very slow skin pinch (in diarrhoea).
> - **IMCI general danger signs**: unable to drink/breastfeed, vomits everything, convulsions, lethargic/unconscious.
> - **Young infant (0–2 months) danger signs**: not feeding well, convulsions, RR ≥60, severe chest indrawing, temperature ≥37.5°C or <35.5°C, movement only when stimulated or none.
> - **Severe acute malnutrition with medical complications.**
> - **Hb <5 g/dL**, or <7 g/dL with respiratory distress.
> - **A child returning** to the facility for the same illness.

### Thinking like a clinician

- **Classify, don't diagnose only.** IMCI asks "How sick?" before "What disease?" A pink classification means **pre-referral treatment and referral now**, whatever the diagnosis.
- **One illness rarely comes alone.** Malaria + pneumonia + malnutrition is common. Assess every child for **all** main symptoms, nutrition, immunisation and HIV.
- **Hypoxaemia is invisible without a pulse oximeter.** Cyanosis is a late sign. Measure SpO₂ in every child with cough or difficult breathing.
- **Most hospital deaths happen in the first 24 hours.** Triage on arrival (ETAT), not after the queue.
- **"Treat and send home" includes counselling.** The mother must know **when to return immediately** (cannot drink, becomes sicker, fever, fast or difficult breathing, blood in stool) and when to come for **follow-up** (2 days for pneumonia/malaria, 5 days for persistent diarrhoea, 14 days for nutrition).

## Clinical workup

### Step 0: First 5 minutes (ETAT triage at the door)

1. **E**mergency signs (ABCD: Airway/Breathing, Circulation, Coma/Convulsions, Dehydration) → treat immediately in the emergency area.
2. **P**riority signs ("3 TPR-MOB": **T**iny baby <2 months, **T**emperature very high, **T**rauma; **P**allor (severe), **P**oisoning, **P**ain (severe); **R**estless/irritable/lethargic, **R**espiratory distress, **R**eferral (urgent); **M**alnutrition (visible severe wasting), **O**edema of both feet, **B**urns (major)) → assess and treat next.
3. **Q**ueue (non-urgent) → routine IMCI.
4. Check **RBS** and **SpO₂** in every sick child; give oxygen if SpO₂ <90%; treat hypoglycaemia (glucose <2.5 mmol/L, or <3 mmol/L in malnourished) with **10% dextrose 5 mL/kg IV** (or sugar water by NGT).

### Step 1: Focused history

- [ ] Danger signs. *Pink classification = urgent.*
- [ ] Main symptoms and duration. *Classification and treatment.*
- [ ] Care already sought and drugs given. *Delays; partial treatment.*
- [ ] Feeding, immunisation, vitamin A, ITN. *Prevention gaps to close today.*
- [ ] HIV status. *Changes management.*
- [ ] Social circumstances. *Can this child be treated at home and come back?*

### Step 2: Focused examination

- RR, indrawing, SpO₂; dehydration signs; temperature; neck stiffness; pallor; **weight, height, Z-scores, MUAC, oedema**; ears; skin (measles rash, burns, signs of abuse).

### Step 3: Bedside tests

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| **Malaria RDT** | Every febrile child in Uganda (test, then treat) |
| **SpO₂** | Hypoxaemia → oxygen and referral |
| **RBS** | Hypoglycaemia in the very sick, malnourished, convulsing |
| **Hb (HemoCue)** | Severe anaemia |
| **MUAC tape** | Acute malnutrition (6–59 months) |
| **Respiratory timer** (ARI timer) | Counting RR accurately (VHTs and HCs) |
| **HIV rapid test** | Unknown status in a sick or malnourished child |

### Step 4: Laboratory & imaging

| Test | When | Expected / abnormal finding | How it changes management | Availability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Blood smear for malaria** | Severe malaria suspected; RDT negative but clinical suspicion | Parasitaemia, species | Confirms; follow parasite clearance | HC III and above |
| **FBC** | Pallor, sepsis, severe illness | Severe anaemia, leucocytosis | Transfusion; antibiotics | HC IV and above |
| **Blood culture** | Sepsis, severe pneumonia | Organism and sensitivities | Targeted antibiotics | Regional/national referral; few HC IVs |
| **Lumbar puncture** | Convulsions with fever, stiff neck, bulging fontanelle | Meningitis | Antibiotics for 10–14 days | Hospitals |
| **Stool microscopy** | Persistent diarrhoea, dysentery | Parasites (e.g. *Giardia*, amoeba) | Specific treatment | HC III and above |
| **Electrolytes, creatinine** | Severe dehydration, SAM, AKI | Hypo-/hypernatraemia, hypokalaemia | Fluid choice | Hospitals |
| **Chest X-ray** | Severe pneumonia not responding, TB suspected | Consolidation, effusion, hilar nodes | Change antibiotics, drain, TB treatment | General hospital and above |
| **GeneXpert** | TB symptoms or contact, SAM not responding | MTB detected | TB treatment | HC IV and above |

### Step 5: Putting it together

**Model one-liner:** "A 14-month-old girl from Luweero, weight 7.2 kg (WAZ −2.8, MUAC 11.9 cm), partially immunised (no MR), sleeps under a net 'sometimes', brought on day 4 of fever and cough after treatment at a drug shop, now with chest indrawing, SpO₂ 88% and a positive malaria RDT."

**IMCI classification (worked example):**
- Cough + chest indrawing (no danger sign) + SpO₂ 88% → **severe pneumonia** (WHO 2024: hypoxaemia counts as severe) → pink: oxygen, injectable antibiotics, admit/refer.
- Fever + RDT positive in a high-malaria area → **malaria**; with severe pneumonia, treat as **severe febrile illness** with artesunate if any danger sign, otherwise ACT.
- MUAC 11.9 cm, no oedema → **moderate acute malnutrition** → feeding counselling, supplementary feeding, follow up.
- Missed MR → **vaccinate before discharge**.
- Three delays: **first** (drug shop for 3 days), **second** (distance?), **third** (no oxygen at the first facility?).

## Differential diagnosis

In child survival the "differential" is the **cause of death or severe illness** behind a presenting syndrome, and they often overlap.

| Presenting syndrome | Main causes to distinguish | Distinguishing features | Key test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fever without localising signs** | Malaria, sepsis, UTI, typhoid, meningitis, HIV-related infection | RDT result, neck stiffness, urine, toxicity | RDT, urine dipstick, LP, blood culture |
| **Fast breathing** | Pneumonia, malaria with acidosis, severe anaemia, heart failure, sepsis | Crackles/indrawing vs deep (acidotic) breathing vs pallor, hepatomegaly | SpO₂, RDT, Hb, CXR |
| **Diarrhoea** | Acute watery (rotavirus, cholera), dysentery (Shigella), persistent (malnutrition, HIV, Giardia) | Duration, blood, dehydration, nutrition | Stool microscopy/culture |
| **Convulsions with fever** | Febrile seizure, cerebral malaria, meningitis, hypoglycaemia | Age, consciousness after the fit, RDT, glucose | RBS, RDT, LP |
| **Newborn not feeding** | Sepsis, hypothermia, hypoglycaemia, asphyxia/HIE, jaundice, prematurity | Temperature, glucose, birth history | RBS, temperature, bilirubin |
| **Severe pallor** | Malaria, sickle cell disease, iron deficiency, hookworm, HIV, malignancy | Spleen, jaundice, ethnicity/family history | Hb, smear, sickling test |

## Investigations

| Test | What you are looking for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Malaria RDT/smear | Plasmodium | Test-and-treat; avoid treating every fever as malaria |
| SpO₂ | Hypoxaemia | The single strongest predictor of death in pneumonia |
| RBS | Hypoglycaemia | Common, deadly and easily treated |
| Hb | Severe anaemia | Transfusion decision |
| Weight, height, MUAC, oedema | Malnutrition | Changes fluid, antibiotic and feeding management |
| HIV test | Infection | ART, CTX, different thresholds |
| Immunisation card | Missed doses | Catch up before discharge |

## Management

### A. High-impact interventions along the continuum of care

| Stage | Interventions (Uganda) |
|---|---|
| **Adolescence and pre-pregnancy** | Girls' education, family planning (birth spacing ≥24 months), HPV vaccine, nutrition, prevention of adolescent pregnancy, PMTCT readiness |
| **Pregnancy** | **ANC (WHO: 8 contacts)**, IPTp-SP, ITN, iron–folic acid, Td, syphilis and HIV testing (eMTCT), pre-eclampsia screening, birth preparedness |
| **Birth** | **Skilled attendance** at a facility, partograph/LCG, **Helping Babies Breathe** (Golden Minute), clean birth, **delayed cord clamping** (≥1 min), **antenatal corticosteroids** for preterm labour (24–34 weeks), management of complications |
| **Newborn (first week)** | **Immediate drying and skin-to-skin**, **breastfeeding within 1 hour**, **vitamin K**, tetracycline **eye ointment**, **chlorhexidine 7.1% cord gel**, BCG/OPV0/HepB-BD, **KMC** (immediate KMC for all babies <2 kg, WHO 2022), postnatal checks (within 24 h, day 3, days 7–14, 6 weeks), management of sepsis, jaundice and hypothermia |
| **Infancy and childhood** | **Exclusive breastfeeding to 6 months**, complementary feeding from 6 months, **immunisation** (incl. PCV, rota, malaria vaccine), **vitamin A** and **deworming**, **ITNs**, growth monitoring and **CMAM** for wasting, **ORS + zinc**, **amoxicillin** for pneumonia, **RDT + ACT** for malaria, **rectal artesunate** pre-referral, **oxygen**, HIV testing and treatment, sickle cell screening, early childhood development, injury prevention |
| **Environment** | **Safe water, sanitation and handwashing (WASH)**, clean cooking fuel (less indoor smoke), housing |

### B. Delivering the interventions: platforms

1. **Community (VHTs/CHEWs, iCCM)**: health promotion, newborn home visits, case management of malaria, pneumonia and diarrhoea in children 2–59 months, referral, follow-up of immunisation defaulters.
2. **Health centres (IMCI/IMNCI)**: assess–classify–treat–counsel–follow up; immunisation; growth monitoring; ANC/PNC.
3. **Hospitals (ETAT+)**: triage, emergency care (oxygen, fluids, glucose, anticonvulsants), inpatient management of severe illness, newborn units (KMC, CPAP), blood.
4. **Campaigns**: measles–rubella, polio, vitamin A and deworming in **Child Health Days** (April and October in Uganda).
5. **Accountability**: **MPDSR** (every maternal and perinatal death reviewed), HMIS/DHIS2 data, verbal autopsy.

### D. Emergency algorithm: the sick child arriving at a facility (ETAT)

1. **Triage** within minutes of arrival: emergency, priority or queue.
2. **Airway/Breathing**: position, suction, **oxygen** if SpO₂ <90% or central cyanosis, bag-mask ventilation if not breathing.
3. **Circulation**: shock (cold hands + CRT >3 s + weak fast pulse): **10–20 mL/kg Ringer's/saline** over 30–60 min (WHO; avoid large boluses in severe malaria/febrile illness without shock); in **SAM** 15 mL/kg over 1 h of a dextrose-containing solution; transfuse if Hb <5 g/dL.
4. **Coma/Convulsions**: recovery position, **glucose** (10% dextrose 5 mL/kg), **diazepam 0.5 mg/kg rectal** or 0.3 mg/kg IV (or midazolam 0.3–0.5 mg/kg buccal, max 10 mg), phenobarbital 20 mg/kg if fits continue.
5. **Dehydration (severe)**: Plan C.
6. **Then**: full assessment, specific treatment, monitoring chart, nutrition, immunisation check.

### E. Prevention at every encounter ("no missed opportunity")

1. Check and update **immunisation**.
2. **Vitamin A** and **deworming** if due.
3. **Growth** (weight, height, MUAC) and feeding counselling.
4. **HIV** status known?
5. **ITN** at home? (distribution through ANC, EPI, mass campaigns).
6. **Counsel** on danger signs and when to return.

## Complications

At population level, the "complications" of failed child survival are:
- **Deaths** (the indicator), and **survivors with disability**: cerebral palsy after asphyxia or meningitis, epilepsy and cognitive impairment after cerebral malaria, blindness after measles/vitamin A deficiency, deafness after meningitis.
- **Stunting** with lifelong lower cognitive and economic potential.
- **Chronic lung disease** after severe pneumonia, measles, TB.
- **Family impact**: poverty from care costs, more pregnancies to replace lost children (high fertility).
- **Antimicrobial resistance** from untested fevers treated with antibiotics at drug shops.

## Ward-round & exam pearls

**Model case presentation (child):**
> "Nakato is a 10-month-old girl from Kayunga, weight 6.4 kg (WAZ −2.7), length 69 cm (LAZ −1.5), WLZ −2.6, MUAC 12.0 cm, no oedema. She was born at home, is HIV-unexposed and is still breastfed, but complementary feeds are thin porridge twice a day. Her card shows vaccines to 14 weeks only; she has never had vitamin A or the malaria vaccine. She presented on day 5 of fever and day 2 of diarrhoea, after two visits to a drug shop where she was given 'syrups'. On arrival she had some dehydration and a positive malaria RDT. Summary: uncomplicated malaria with diarrhoea and some dehydration in a child with moderate wasting and missed immunisations, illustrating a first delay and several missed preventive opportunities. Plan: ACT 1 tablet twice daily for 3 days, ORS Plan B 480 mL over 4 hours, zinc 20 mg daily for 10 days, nutrition support, catch-up vaccines and vitamin A before discharge, ITN, and counselling on danger signs."

**Likely viva questions (with answers)**

1. *Define U5MR and give Uganda's current value.* Probability of dying before age 5 per 1,000 live births; about 52 (UDHS 2022).
2. *What are the commonest causes of under-five death in Uganda?* Neonatal causes (prematurity, asphyxia, sepsis), malaria, pneumonia, diarrhoea, with malnutrition as an underlying cause.
3. *What are the three components of IMCI?* Health-worker skills, health systems, family and community practices.
4. *What does iCCM treat?* Malaria, pneumonia and diarrhoea in children 2–59 months, plus newborn home visits and referral.
5. *Explain the three delays.* Deciding to seek care, reaching care, receiving adequate care.
6. *What is GOBI-FFF?* Growth monitoring, ORS, Breastfeeding, Immunisation; Female education, Family spacing, Food supplementation.
7. *Name five newborn interventions that save lives.* Skilled birth care with HBB, early breastfeeding, thermal care/KMC, chlorhexidine cord care, prompt treatment of sepsis (also vitamin K, steroids for preterm labour).

**OSCE tips:** IMCI stations test **counting RR for 60 seconds**, **classifying by colour**, and **counselling** (how to give ORS, when to return). Say the classification aloud ("This is **severe pneumonia**, a pink classification, so I will give the first dose of antibiotic and refer urgently").

### Classic exam traps

- **"Most under-five deaths in Uganda are from malaria."** Malaria is the biggest single killer after the neonatal period, but **neonatal deaths** together make up about **40%**; do not forget the newborn.
- **"U5MR is deaths per 1,000 children under 5."** Wrong: it is per **1,000 live births** (a probability of dying before age 5).
- **"IMCI is a treatment protocol for health workers."** Only one of its **three** components; the others are health systems and family/community practices.
- **"Treat every fever as malaria."** Wrong: **test first** (RDT); treat negatives for other causes.
- **"Give antibiotics for all diarrhoea."** Wrong: only for **dysentery** (and cholera/severe cases); otherwise ORS + zinc.
- **"Cyanosis means hypoxaemia; no cyanosis means OK."** Wrong: use the **pulse oximeter**; cyanosis is late.
- **"Big IV boluses for every febrile child with poor perfusion."** The FEAST trial (East Africa, including Uganda) showed boluses increased deaths in febrile children **without** shock; give boluses only for true shock, and cautiously.

### Questions seniors ask

**Why has neonatal mortality fallen more slowly than post-neonatal mortality?**
Post-neonatal deaths respond to vaccines, nets, ORS and antibiotics delivered at community level; newborn deaths need **quality care around birth** (skilled attendants, resuscitation, thermal care, sepsis treatment, preterm care), which is harder to deliver.

**What single intervention would you scale up first in Uganda, and why?**
Defensible answers: **quality care at birth and in the first week** (largest share of deaths); **oxygen and pulse oximetry**; **malaria prevention and prompt treatment** (largest single cause after the newborn period). Justify with burden, effectiveness and cost.

**What is iCCM and what are its limits?**
VHTs test and treat malaria, pneumonia and diarrhoea in children 2–59 months with RDTs, ACTs, amoxicillin, ORS and zinc, and refer danger signs. Limits: stock-outs, supervision, VHT motivation (often unpaid), young infants (<2 months) must be referred.

**How do you use the three delays to analyse a child's death?**
Ask when the illness started and when care was sought (delay 1), how long it took to reach a facility and why (delay 2), and what happened at the facility (triage, drugs, oxygen, staff) (delay 3); then plan actions at each level (MPDSR).

**How does malnutrition change your management of a child with diarrhoea?**
Use **ReSoMal** and slower rehydration (not Plan C boluses unless shocked), give antibiotics routinely in SAM, treat hypoglycaemia and hypothermia, and start F-75 early.

**Name the newborn interventions you would ensure in the first hour of life.**
Dry and stimulate, HBB if not breathing, skin-to-skin, delayed cord clamping, breastfeeding within 1 hour, vitamin K, eye prophylaxis, chlorhexidine cord care, and weigh (KMC if <2 kg).
