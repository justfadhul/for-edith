## In a nutshell

**Growth** is the increase in size (weight, length/height, head circumference); **development** is the acquisition of skills (moving, using hands and eyes, communicating, relating to people and looking after oneself). Both follow predictable patterns, and both are **sensitive markers of a child's health**: a child who is ill, hungry, unloved, infected with HIV or TB, or has a brain or genetic problem will often show it first as a **flattening growth curve** or **delayed milestones**.

In Uganda, about **1 in 4 children under five is stunted**, and developmental delay is common because of birth asphyxia, prematurity, malnutrition, malaria, meningitis, HIV and limited stimulation. Measuring and plotting every child, asking every mother about milestones, and acting early are among the most powerful things a clinician can do.

> [!PEARL]
> The one thing never to forget: **a single measurement means little; the trend is everything.** Weigh and measure accurately, plot on the right WHO chart (sex, age, measurement), and ask "is this child following their curve?" Likewise for development, the most worrying sign is not slowness but **regression**: a child who **loses** a skill needs urgent assessment.

## Definitions & classification

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Growth** | Increase in physical size; measured as weight, length (lying, <2 years or <87 cm) or height (standing, ≥2 years), head circumference (HC), MUAC |
| **Development** | Acquisition of function across domains: **gross motor**, **fine motor and vision**, **speech, language and hearing**, **social, emotional and self-care** (and cognition) |
| **Z-score (SD score)** | How many standard deviations a measurement lies from the WHO median for age and sex. Z = (observed − median) ÷ SD. 0 = median; −2 ≈ 2nd–3rd centile; +2 ≈ 97th–98th centile |
| **Centile** | The percentage of the reference population below the measurement (e.g. 50th = median) |
| **Underweight** | Weight-for-age (WAZ) < −2 (severe < −3) |
| **Stunting** | Length/height-for-age (LAZ/HAZ) < −2 (severe < −3): **chronic** undernutrition and illness |
| **Wasting** | Weight-for-length/height (WLZ/WHZ) < −2 (severe < −3) or MUAC < 12.5 cm: **acute** undernutrition |
| **SAM** (WHO 2023 guideline on wasting and nutritional oedema; UCG 2023) | WHZ < −3, **or** MUAC < 11.5 cm (6–59 months), **or** bilateral pitting oedema |
| **MAM** | WHZ −3 to < −2, or MUAC 11.5–12.4 cm |
| **Overweight / obesity** (under 5) | WHZ > +2 / > +3 (BMI-for-age above 5 years: > +1 overweight, > +2 obese) |
| **Microcephaly / macrocephaly** | HC < −2 SD (severe < −3 SD) / HC > +2 SD |
| **Faltering growth** (NICE NG75) | Fall across ≥1 weight centile space if birthweight < 9th centile; ≥2 spaces if 9th–91st; ≥3 spaces if > 91st; or weight < 2nd centile at any time |
| **Developmental delay** | Milestones achieved significantly later than expected (beyond the limit age) in one domain |
| **Global developmental delay** | Significant delay in **two or more domains** in a child under 5 years |
| **Developmental quotient (DQ)** | Developmental age ÷ chronological (corrected) age × 100. DQ < 70 = significant delay |
| **Regression** | Loss of previously acquired skills: always abnormal |
| **Corrected age** | For preterm infants (until 2 years): chronological age minus the number of weeks born before 40 weeks |

## Epidemiology & Uganda context

- **Undernutrition**: the Uganda Demographic and Health Survey 2022 reported roughly **a quarter of under-fives stunted**, about **3–4% wasted** and about **1 in 10 underweight**, with higher rates in Karamoja, the West Nile and South-Western regions (stunting in the South-West despite food production), and among children of poorer, less-educated mothers.
- **Low birthweight** (<2.5 kg) affects about 1 in 10 births; prematurity and fetal growth restriction start many children on a lower curve.
- **Developmental delay**: global estimates suggest about **250 million children under 5** in low- and middle-income countries are at risk of not reaching their developmental potential because of poverty, stunting and lack of stimulation (Lancet series). In Uganda, birth asphyxia, kernicterus, cerebral malaria, meningitis, HIV and severe malnutrition add neurological injury.
- **Services**: growth monitoring is meant to happen at every immunisation and sick-child visit using the **Child Health Card** (weight-for-age plotted; length and MUAC increasingly recorded). In practice, **length is often not measured**, MUAC tapes run out, and milestones are rarely asked about. **Early Childhood Development (ECD)** policy and **Nurturing Care** (WHO/UNICEF 2018: good health, adequate nutrition, responsive caregiving, safety and security, opportunities for early learning) frame national programmes.

> [!UGANDA]
> UCG 2023 (HIV chapter) says: monitor growth with **weight, length/height and MUAC at every encounter** and record it on the growth card; MUAC only from **6 months**; **failure to gain weight or height may be the first sign of HIV** (failure to thrive affects up to 50% of HIV-infected children); assess **age-specific milestones** at each visit; **test children with developmental delay for HIV** and start ART; measure head circumference. Apply this to every child, not only HIV-exposed children.

## Physiology & principles

### Phases of growth (the infancy–childhood–puberty model)

1. **Fetal and infancy phase (0–2 years)**: fastest growth; driven mainly by **nutrition** (and insulin, IGF-2). Infants often **cross centiles** in the first 6–18 months to reach their genetic channel ("catch-up" or "catch-down"). Stunting that develops here (the **first 1000 days**, conception to 2 years) is largely irreversible.
2. **Childhood phase (2 years–puberty)**: steady **5–6 cm/year**; driven by **growth hormone** and **thyroid hormone**. A child should follow their centile; crossing down suggests disease.
3. **Pubertal phase**: **sex steroids** plus GH → the **pubertal growth spurt** (peak height velocity about 8–9 cm/year in girls, 9–10 cm/year in boys), then fusion of the epiphyses.

### Why measurements differ in what they tell you

- **Weight** reflects recent nutrition and illness (and fluid: oedema can hide wasting).
- **Length/height** reflects long-term nutrition, chronic disease, endocrine and genetic factors. It falls more slowly than weight.
- **Weight-for-height** separates the thin child (wasted) from the short child (stunted but proportionate).
- **Head circumference** reflects **brain growth** in the first 2 years. A falling HC centile is a sign of brain injury or disease (HIV encephalopathy, Rett syndrome, severe malnutrition); a rising one suggests hydrocephalus or a mass.
- **MUAC** reflects muscle and fat mass; a MUAC below 11.5 cm independently predicts death in children 6–59 months, which is why it is used for community screening.

### Principles of development

- **Cephalocaudal and proximodistal**: control develops from **head to toe** (head control → sitting → standing) and from **trunk to fingertips** (whole-hand palmar grasp → pincer grip).
- **Primitive reflexes must disappear** (cortical inhibition) before voluntary skills emerge; **protective reflexes** (parachute) appear.
- **Sequence is fixed, rate varies**: some children bottom-shuffle and walk late (often familial) but the sequence is the same.
- **Domains interact**: a deaf child's language is delayed; a blind child's motor skills may be delayed; a child with CP may have normal cognition hidden behind motor and speech problems.
- **Environment matters**: responsive caregiving, talking, play and good nutrition improve outcomes, especially in the first 3 years when brain plasticity is greatest.

Median ages are approximate; the **limit age** is the age by which about 95–97% of children have achieved the skill (refer if not achieved).

> [!EXAM]
> The **CDC (2022 revision)** "Learn the Signs. Act Early." checklists list skills that **most (about 75%) children** have by each age, e.g. at 9 months: sits without support, uses fingers to rake food, looks for objects dropped out of sight, babbles "mamamama"; at 12 months: pulls to stand, walks holding furniture, pincer grip, waves bye-bye, says "mama/dada" with meaning; at 18 months: walks without holding on, scribbles, tries to say 3 or more words, follows one-step directions without gestures, points to show something interesting. ### Primitive and protective reflexes

| Reflex | How to elicit | Appears | Disappears |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Moro** | Sudden head drop (supported) → arms abduct and extend, then adduct | Birth (from 28–32 weeks) | **3–6 months** (asymmetry: brachial plexus palsy, fractured clavicle, hemiplegia) |
| **Rooting / sucking** | Stroke cheek → turns to that side and sucks | Birth | 3–4 months |
| **Palmar grasp** | Finger in palm → grasps | Birth | **3–4 months** (must go before voluntary reach) |
| **Plantar grasp** | Pressure on sole → toes curl | Birth | 9–12 months |
| **Asymmetric tonic neck reflex (ATNR)** | Turn head → "fencing posture" | 2 weeks–1 month | **4–6 months**; obligatory or persistent = CP |
| **Stepping / placing** | Hold upright, feet touch surface | Birth | 2 months |
| **Parachute (protective)** | Tip child forward towards a surface → arms extend | **6–9 months** | **Persists for life** (absent/asymmetric = CP, hemiplegia) |

## Puberty

### Tanner (sexual maturity) stages

| Stage | Girls: breasts (B) | Boys: genitalia (G) | Both: pubic hair (PH) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Prepubertal (papilla only) | Prepubertal; testes **< 4 mL** | None |
| **2** | **Breast bud**: small mound, areola widens | Testes **≥ 4 mL**, scrotum enlarges and reddens | Sparse, long, slightly pigmented, at base of penis/labia |
| **3** | Breast and areola enlarge further, no separation of contours | Penis lengthens; testes 6–12 mL | Darker, coarser, curly, spreads over pubis |
| **4** | **Areola and papilla form a secondary mound** | Penis broadens, glans develops; testes 12–15 mL; scrotum darkens | Adult type, not on thighs |
| **5** | Adult contour; areola recedes to breast contour | Adult; testes 15–25 mL | Adult, spreads to medial thighs |

Testicular volume is measured with a **Prader orchidometer** (beads 1–25 mL).

### Sequence and timing

| | Girls | Boys |
|---|---|---|
| First sign | **Thelarche** (breast bud, B2): mean ~10–11 years (range 8–13) | **Testicular enlargement ≥ 4 mL** (G2): mean ~11–12 years (range 9–14) |
| Then | Pubic hair (pubarche), growth spurt | Pubic hair, penile growth |
| Peak height velocity | **Early** (B2–B3), ~11.5–12 years, ~8–9 cm/year | **Late** (G4), ~13.5–14 years, ~9–10 cm/year (why men are taller) |
| Late | **Menarche** at B4, ~**2–2.5 years after thelarche** (mean ~12.5–13 y); height gain after menarche only ~5–7 cm | Voice breaks, facial and axillary hair, adult body habitus |
| **Precocious** | Signs before **8 years** (some US guidance uses 7 in White and 6 in Black girls) | Before **9 years** |
| **Delayed** | No breast development by **13 years**, or no menarche by **15 years** (or 3 years after thelarche) | Testicular volume < 4 mL at **14 years** |

> [!NOTE]
> Precocious puberty in **boys** is more often pathological (CNS tumour, hamartoma, hydrocephalus, congenital adrenal hyperplasia) than in girls (mostly idiopathic central). **Delayed puberty** is most often **constitutional delay of growth and puberty** (often familial, delayed bone age), but in Uganda think of **chronic disease and undernutrition** (HIV, sickle cell disease, TB, IBD, chronic kidney disease), hypothyroidism, and gonadal failure (Turner syndrome in a short girl, Klinefelter in a tall boy).

## Clinical acumen

### Focused history

**For growth**
- **Birth**: gestation, birthweight (LBW, SGA), multiple birth.
- **Feeding history** (the single most useful part): **exclusive breastfeeding to 6 months?** How often? Formula or cow's milk (how prepared, dilution)? **Complementary foods** from 6 months: what, how thick, how often, who feeds, animal-source foods? Appetite, feeding difficulties, food insecurity. Use a **24-hour dietary recall**.
- **Illness**: diarrhoea, recurrent infections, cough >2 weeks (TB), fevers (malaria), vomiting, polyuria, snoring, previous admissions.
- **HIV**: mother's status, PMTCT, child's test results (DNA PCR, antibody), ART and co-trimoxazole.
- **Family**: parents' heights (mid-parental height), siblings' growth, deaths of siblings, consanguinity, family history of delayed puberty.
- **Social**: who cares for the child, mother's health and mood, alcohol, poverty, recent separation, child abuse/neglect risk.
- **Immunisation** (UNEPI card), **vitamin A** and **deworming** in the last 6 months.

**For development**
- **Parental concern**: "What worries you?" Parents are usually right.
- Milestones **by domain**: when did the child smile, hold the head, sit, crawl, walk, say first words, put words together? What can the child do **now**? Use the Child Health Card if recorded.
- **Any loss of skills?**
- **Hearing and vision**: does the child respond to voices, look at faces, recognise people across the room?
- **Antenatal** (infections, alcohol, drugs, HIV/syphilis), **perinatal** (prematurity, asphyxia, jaundice, neonatal admission, seizures) and **postnatal** (meningitis, cerebral malaria, head injury, seizures) history.
- **Play and stimulation**: who plays and talks with the child, toys, books, screen time, day-care.
- **Behaviour**: eye contact, pointing, response to name, repetitive behaviours (autism); sleep; feeding.
- **Family history** of delay, learning difficulty, consanguinity.

### Examination

1. **Measure accurately** (see Clinical workup), plot, calculate Z-scores, compare with previous points.
2. **General**: wasting (buttocks, ribs), oedema (feet first: SAM), hair and skin changes, pallor, dysmorphic features (Down syndrome, fetal alcohol), signs of HIV (thrush, lymphadenopathy, parotid swelling), rickets (wrist widening, rosary, bowing), thyroid, bruises (abuse).
3. **Systemic**: chest (TB, recurrent pneumonia), heart murmurs (CHD), abdomen (organomegaly), genitalia and **Tanner staging** in older children.
4. **Developmental assessment**: observe play first, then test each domain with simple equipment (see Procedure below).
5. **Neurology**: tone, power, reflexes, primitive reflexes, head circumference, fontanelle, gait.
6. **Hearing and vision** screen.

### Red flags

> [!REDFLAG]
> **Developmental red flags** (refer for full assessment):
> - **Loss of skills** at any age
> - **Parental concern** about vision, hearing or development
> - No **social smile by 8 weeks**; not **fixing and following by 3 months**; no response to sound
> - **Asymmetry** of movement or **hand preference before 12 months**
> - **Persistent primitive reflexes** beyond 6 months; very floppy or stiff
> - Not **sitting by 9 months**, not **walking by 18 months**, no **words by 18 months**, no 2-word phrases by 2–2.5 years
> - No pointing, poor eye contact, no response to name by 12–18 months (autism)
> - **Head circumference** above +2 or below −2 SD, or crossing centiles
>
> **Growth red flags**: weight < −3 Z or MUAC < 11.5 cm or oedema (SAM: treat now); falling across centile spaces; **height crossing downwards** after age 2; short stature with disproportion, dysmorphism or delayed puberty; HC falling.

### Thinking like a clinician

- **Short but growing normally parallel to the 2nd centile, with short parents** = familial short stature (normal velocity, bone age = chronological age). **Short with delayed bone age and a parent who was a "late bloomer"** = constitutional delay. **Short and crossing centiles down** = pathology (hypothyroidism, GH deficiency, chronic disease, malnutrition, Turner syndrome).
- **Weight falls before height, then height follows** = undernutrition/illness. **Height falls first while weight is preserved (child looks chubby)** = endocrine (hypothyroidism, GH deficiency, Cushing's, including steroid use).
- **Oedema hides wasting**: a child with kwashiorkor may have a "normal" weight. Press the feet.
- **Plot against corrected age** for preterm babies until 2 years, or you will diagnose delay and faltering that are not there.
- **Isolated speech delay**: always check **hearing** first (chronic otitis media with effusion, meningitis sequelae), then consider autism, global delay, and lack of stimulation.
- **Motor delay with normal social and language skills** suggests CP, a neuromuscular disorder or benign late walking (bottom-shufflers). **Delay in all domains** suggests a global brain problem (genetic, HIE, infection, metabolic).
- **HIV**: growth faltering, developmental delay or regression, recurrent infections in a child of unknown status → **test**.
- **Measurement error** is the commonest cause of "abnormal" growth: check the scale (zeroed?), clothes removed, length measured lying with two people, correct chart for sex.

## Clinical workup

### Step 0: First 5 minutes

Usually a clinic task, but on the ward:

1. **ETAT**: emergency signs (airway, breathing, circulation, coma, convulsions, severe dehydration), then **priority signs** including **visible severe wasting** and **oedema of both feet**.
2. **SAM screen**: MUAC (6–59 months), oedema, WHZ. SAM with medical complications (danger signs, poor appetite, oedema +++, infants <6 months at risk) → **inpatient therapeutic care (ITC)**; check **glucose** (treat if < 3.0 mmol/L), **temperature** (hypothermia), dehydration (use ReSoMal per SAM protocol, not standard IV boluses).
3. **Measure properly** before anyone writes "normal growth".

### Step 1: Focused history

- [ ] **Birthweight and gestation** → expected curve; corrected age.
- [ ] **Feeding** (breastfeeding, complementary diet, 24-hour recall) → commonest cause of faltering.
- [ ] **Illnesses** (diarrhoea, cough, fevers) → infection-driven faltering, TB, HIV.
- [ ] **HIV status** of mother and child → test if unknown.
- [ ] **Milestones by domain and any regression** → delay pattern.
- [ ] **Hearing and vision** → isolated delays.
- [ ] **Parents' heights, puberty timing** → genetic target.
- [ ] **Immunisation, vitamin A, deworming** → catch up.
- [ ] **Social situation** → neglect, food insecurity, maternal depression.

### Step 2: Focused examination (with measurement technique)

| Measurement | Technique |
|---|---|
| **Weight** | Calibrated scale zeroed; infant **naked** (or minimal clothing), older child light clothes, no shoes. Salter hanging scale or electronic scale; record to 0.1 kg (0.01 kg in infants on a baby scale) |
| **Length** (< 2 years or < 87 cm) | **Lying** on a length board, **two people**: one holds the head against the headboard (Frankfort plane vertical), the other straightens the knees and brings the footboard to the heels. Record to 0.1 cm |
| **Height** (≥ 2 years) | **Standing**, no shoes, heels, buttocks, shoulders and head touching the board, looking straight ahead. If a child ≥ 2 years is measured lying, subtract 0.7 cm (WHO) |
| **Head circumference** | Non-stretch tape around the **largest occipitofrontal circumference** (just above the eyebrows, around the occiput); take the largest of 3 |
| **MUAC** (6–59 months) | **Left** arm, midpoint between acromion and olecranon (arm bent to find it), arm then hanging relaxed; tape snug but not compressing. Red < 11.5 cm, yellow 11.5–12.4, green ≥ 12.5 |
| **Oedema** | Thumb pressure on the dorsum of **both feet** for 3 seconds; pit remains = bilateral pitting oedema (+ feet, ++ feet and legs/hands/arms, +++ generalised including face) |

Then: systemic examination, Tanner stage (if > 8 years), developmental assessment, neurology, hearing and vision.

### Step 3: Bedside tests

| Test | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Plot on the WHO chart and calculate Z-scores** (WAZ, LAZ/HAZ, WLZ/WHZ, HCZ, BMI-for-age > 5 y) | Underweight, stunting, wasting, micro/macrocephaly; trend |
| **Growth velocity** (cm/year over ≥ 6 months) | < 4 cm/year in a pre-pubertal child is abnormal |
| **Mid-parental height** | Boys (father + mother + 13)/2; girls (father + mother − 13)/2; range ± 8.5 cm; is the child within the genetic target? |
| **Glucose** | Hypoglycaemia in sick or malnourished children |
| **HIV rapid test** (DNA PCR < 18 months if exposed) | HIV |
| **Hb (HemoCue)** | Anaemia (iron deficiency, malaria, hookworm) |
| **Urine dipstick** | UTI, glucose (diabetes), protein (renal disease) |
| **Simple hearing and vision tests** | Distraction test, response to voice; fixing, following, red reflex |
| **Developmental screening tool** | Milestones checklist; Ages and Stages Questionnaire (ASQ-3), **Malawi Developmental Assessment Tool (MDAT)**, Guide for Monitoring Child Development (GMCD), Denver II |

### Step 4: Laboratory & imaging

| Test | When | Expected / abnormal finding | How it changes management |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FBC, ferritin (if available), blood film** | Pallor, faltering, poor diet | Microcytic anaemia; malaria parasites | Iron, antimalarial, deworming. HC IV and above (ferritin at RRH/NRH) |
| **HIV test** (DNA PCR if < 18 months) | Faltering, delay, recurrent infections, unknown status | Positive | ART, co-trimoxazole (all levels; PCR via hub system) |
| **TB workup** (CXR, Xpert Ultra on sputum/stool/gastric aspirate) | Cough > 2 weeks, contact, weight loss, faltering despite feeding | Xpert positive, hilar nodes | TB treatment (WHO 2022: 4-month regimen for non-severe TB). Xpert at HC IV and above; stool Xpert increasingly available |
| **Urine culture, creatinine, electrolytes** | Faltering without cause, polyuria, abnormal dipstick | CKD, renal tubular acidosis, UTI | Specific treatment. RRH/NRH |
| **TSH, free T4** | Short stature with falling height velocity, delayed bone age, delay, constipation, prolonged jaundice | High TSH, low T4 | Levothyroxine. RRH/NRH/private |
| **Bone age** (left hand and wrist X-ray) | Short stature, delayed or precocious puberty | Delayed (constitutional delay, hypothyroidism, GH deficiency, chronic disease); advanced (precocious puberty, obesity, CAH) | Differentiates normal variants from pathology. Hospitals with X-ray |
| **Wrist/knee X-ray, ALP, calcium, phosphate** | Signs of rickets | Cupping, fraying, widened metaphyses; raised ALP | Vitamin D and calcium |
| **Karyotype** | Short girl (any unexplained short stature: Turner), dysmorphism, global delay | 45,X; trisomy 21 | Genetic counselling, specific care. Mulago/MakCHS genetics or private labs; costly |
| **Coeliac serology, stool microscopy** | Chronic diarrhoea, faltering | Positive tTG; parasites (Giardia, hookworm) | Diet; antiparasitic treatment |
| **Neuroimaging (USS, CT, MRI), EEG** | Developmental delay with abnormal head size, neurological signs, seizures, regression | Hydrocephalus, malformation, HIE pattern, atrophy | Neurosurgery, counselling, prognosis. CT at RRH/NRH; MRI Kampala |
| **Formal hearing test (OAE, ABR) and ophthalmology** | Any speech delay; at-risk babies (prematurity, meningitis, kernicterus, CMV) | Hearing loss; visual impairment | Hearing aids, glasses, communication plans. Mulago, CoRSU, some RRHs |
| **GH stimulation tests, LH/FSH, sex steroids** | Specialist evaluation of short stature/puberty | GH deficiency; central vs peripheral precocious puberty | Specialist endocrine care (Mulago) |

### Step 5: Putting it together

**Model one-line summary**
> "A 9-month-old girl, born at term weighing 3.1 kg, exclusively breastfed to 4 months then fed thin maize porridge twice a day, with recurrent diarrhoea, now weighing 6.6 kg (WAZ −2.0), length 66 cm (LAZ −1.7), WLZ about −1.2, MUAC 12.3 cm, HC 43 cm (normal), whose weight has crossed down two centile spaces since 4 months, with age-appropriate milestones (sits without support, transfers, babbles 'mamama', stranger anxiety): growth faltering with moderate underweight and MAM by MUAC due to inadequate complementary feeding and recurrent diarrhoea; development normal."

**Problem list**
1. Growth faltering, MAM by MUAC (12.3 cm), moderate underweight.
2. Inadequate complementary feeding (thin, infrequent, low-diversity diet).
3. Recurrent diarrhoea.
4. HIV status unknown (test).
5. Normal development.

> [!EXAM]
> **Worked example 1: plotting and interpreting.** A boy aged 2 years 6 months: weight 10.2 kg, height 84 cm. On WHO charts: WAZ ≈ −2.6 (underweight), HAZ ≈ −2.4 (**stunted**), WHZ ≈ −1.3 (not wasted). His weight-for-height is acceptable because he is short and light **in proportion**. Interpretation: **chronic undernutrition (stunting)** rather than acute wasting; look for long-standing poor diet, recurrent infections, HIV, TB, and family height; counsel on complementary feeding and follow growth velocity. He does not meet SAM or MAM criteria.

> [!EXAM]
> **Worked example 2: mid-parental height.** A 12-year-old girl is 136 cm (HAZ ≈ −2.1). Father 165 cm, mother 152 cm. MPH = (165 + 152 − 13) ÷ 2 = **152 cm**; target range **143.5–160.5 cm**. Her centile is consistent with her parents' (MPH ≈ −1.5 to −2 SD on adult charts), her growth velocity over a year is 5.5 cm, she is B2 and her bone age is 11 years. Interpretation: **familial short stature with mild constitutional delay**; reassure and follow velocity. If she were B1 at 13, crossing centiles, or had a velocity < 4 cm/year, investigate (TSH, karyotype for Turner syndrome, coeliac, chronic disease).

## Differential diagnosis

### Causes of growth faltering and short stature

| Group | Examples | Clues |
|---|---|---|
| **Inadequate intake** | Poor complementary feeding, food insecurity, breastfeeding problems, neglect | Diet history; catch-up with feeding |
| **Infection** | HIV, TB, recurrent diarrhoea, malaria, intestinal parasites | Tests; chronic symptoms |
| **Malabsorption** | Coeliac disease, persistent diarrhoea, cystic fibrosis (rare) | Chronic diarrhoea, bulky stools |
| **Chronic organ disease** | Congenital heart disease, CKD, sickle cell disease, chronic lung disease, liver disease | Murmur, failure to thrive with breathlessness, anaemia |
| **Endocrine** | Hypothyroidism, GH deficiency, Cushing's/steroid use, rickets | Height affected more than weight; bone age delayed |
| **Genetic / syndromic** | Down syndrome, Turner syndrome, skeletal dysplasias (achondroplasia), SGA without catch-up | Dysmorphism, disproportion, use syndrome-specific charts |
| **Normal variants** | Familial short stature, constitutional delay | Normal velocity; MPH; bone age |
| **Psychosocial** | Emotional deprivation, abuse | Improves in a caring environment |

### Causes of developmental delay

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Prenatal** | Genetic (Down syndrome, fragile X), congenital infections (CMV, rubella, toxoplasmosis, syphilis, HIV), fetal alcohol spectrum disorder, brain malformations, hypothyroidism |
| **Perinatal** | Prematurity, **birth asphyxia/HIE**, **kernicterus**, neonatal hypoglycaemia, neonatal sepsis/meningitis |
| **Postnatal** | **Cerebral malaria**, **meningitis**, encephalitis, head injury, severe malnutrition, iodine/iron deficiency, lead poisoning, epilepsy |
| **Sensory** | Hearing loss, visual impairment |
| **Neurodevelopmental** | Autism spectrum disorder, specific language disorder |
| **Neuromuscular** | Duchenne muscular dystrophy, spinal muscular atrophy |
| **Environmental** | Lack of stimulation, neglect, maternal depression |
| **Progressive (regression)** | HIV encephalopathy, metabolic and neurodegenerative disorders, Rett syndrome, SSPE after measles |

| Test | What you are looking for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Accurate measurement and plotting | Z-scores and trend | Diagnoses faltering, stunting, wasting, abnormal head growth |

## Management

### A. Growth monitoring and promotion (every child, every contact)

1. **Weigh, measure length/height and MUAC** (from 6 months); **HC** in under-2s and in any child with delay.
2. **Plot** on the WHO chart / Child Health Card; calculate Z-scores; **compare with the last point**.
3. **Classify**: normal, growth faltering, MAM, SAM, stunting, overweight.
4. **Counsel the caregiver on the trend**: praise good growth; explore causes of poor growth.
5. **Act** (see below) and book follow-up.

### B. Managing growth faltering and undernutrition step by step

1. **Exclude SAM** (WHZ < −3, MUAC < 11.5 cm or oedema) → SAM protocol (see the malnutrition topic): **outpatient therapeutic care (OTC)** with RUTF if appetite good and no complications; **inpatient (ITC)** with F-75 then F-100/RUTF if complicated. WHO 2023 guideline on wasting also covers infants < 6 months at risk of poor growth and development.
2. **MAM**: nutrition counselling and supplementary feeding where available (WHO 2023 suggests specially formulated foods for selected children), treat illness, review every 2 weeks.
3. **Treat underlying disease**: HIV (ART), TB, malaria, diarrhoea (ORS and zinc), worms, UTI, anaemia.
4. **Feeding counselling** (WHO 2023 guideline on complementary feeding of children 6–23 months; IMCI):
   - **Exclusive breastfeeding** for 6 months; continue breastfeeding to 2 years or beyond.
   - **Complementary foods from 6 months**: thick enough to stay on a spoon; **2–3 meals/day at 6–8 months**, **3–4 meals/day at 9–23 months** plus 1–2 snacks.
   - **Dietary diversity**: at least **5 of 8 food groups** daily; **animal-source foods daily** (eggs, milk, fish, meat, insects), legumes and nuts (beans, groundnut/simsim paste), vitamin A–rich fruits and vegetables (pumpkin, mango, dark-green leaves, orange-fleshed sweet potato).
   - **Responsive feeding**: feed slowly and patiently, encourage, use a separate plate; continue feeding during illness and give extra afterwards.
   - **Hygiene**: safe water, handwashing, clean utensils.
5. **Micronutrients and deworming** (UCG 2023; table below).
6. **Follow-up**: monthly weight; refer if no improvement after 2–3 months, if height crosses down, or if red flags appear.

| Drug / supplement | Dose | Route | Frequency | Duration / max | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vitamin A** | **< 6 months: 50,000 IU**; **6–11 months: 100,000 IU**; **12–59 months: 200,000 IU** | Oral | **Every 6 months** from 6 months (routine) | Treatment: 3 doses (day 0, 1 and 4 weeks) for measles, persistent diarrhoea or eye signs (UCG); in SAM follow the SAM protocol | Do not repeat within 1 month (toxicity) except as a treatment schedule |
| **Albendazole** | **1–2 years: 200 mg**; **> 2 years: 400 mg** | Oral | Single dose **every 6 months** | | Or mebendazole 250 mg (1–2 y) / 500 mg (> 2 y) single dose (UCG 2023) |
| **Iron** (ferrous sulphate, elemental iron) | Treatment of iron-deficiency anaemia: **3 mg/kg/day** elemental iron (range 3–6); UCG HC band: syrup 25 mg/mL, 1 mL daily < 1 year; ½ tablet (200 mg) daily 1–5 years | Oral, after meals | Once daily | 14 days in IMCI, then reassess; continue ~3 months after Hb normalises in confirmed deficiency | Treat malaria first in malarious areas; deworm; **do not give iron in the acute phase of SAM** |
| **Zinc** (for diarrhoea) | **2–6 months: 10 mg; > 6 months: 20 mg** | Oral | Once daily | **10 days** (UCG 2023) | With ORS; reduces duration and recurrence |
| **Vitamin D** (rickets) | Treatment 2,000–6,000 IU/day (or a single high dose per specialist) + calcium 500 mg/day; prevention 400 IU/day | Oral | Daily | 2–3 months then maintenance | Check ALP and X-ray response |

### C. Managing developmental concerns step by step

1. **Confirm** the delay with a structured assessment (use corrected age if preterm); classify: single domain vs **global**; delay vs **regression** (regression = urgent specialist referral).
2. **Check hearing and vision** in every child with delay (especially speech delay).
3. **Look for a cause**: history, examination (head size, dysmorphism, neurocutaneous signs, neurology), **HIV test** (UCG 2023), Hb, TSH; imaging, karyotype or EEG as indicated.
4. **Treat what is treatable**: HIV (ART), hypothyroidism (levothyroxine), malnutrition, anaemia, epilepsy, hearing loss (hearing aids, grommets), visual problems (glasses, cataract surgery).
5. **Early intervention** (do not wait for a diagnosis): physiotherapy, occupational therapy, speech and language therapy; **teach parents to play, talk, read and sing** with the child (WHO/UNICEF **Care for Child Development**), responsive caregiving; community-based rehabilitation.
6. **Support the family**: explain clearly, address stigma and beliefs, screen the mother for depression, link to parent groups, social support, **inclusive education** and later vocational training.
7. **Review** every 3 months; re-assess milestones; re-plot growth.

### D. Puberty problems (what to do on the ward or in clinic)

| Problem | First steps | Refer |
|---|---|---|
| **Precocious puberty** (girls < 8, boys < 9) | Tanner stage, growth velocity, bone age, neuro exam (headache, visual fields), abdominal/testicular exam, café-au-lait spots (McCune–Albright) | **All boys** and girls < 6–7 years or with rapid progression/neurological signs → paediatric endocrinology (Mulago) for LH/FSH, sex steroids, pelvic USS, MRI brain |
| **Delayed puberty** (girls no breasts at 13; boys testes < 4 mL at 14) | Growth chart, MPH, family history, chronic disease screen (HIV, sickle, TB, renal, IBD), nutrition, smell (Kallmann), bone age | Short girl → **karyotype** (Turner); persistent delay → LH/FSH, specialist |
| **Premature thelarche** (isolated breast buds, girls < 2 years, no growth spurt) | Reassure, follow-up | If progression or growth acceleration |

## Complications

| Of poor growth | Of untreated developmental delay |
|---|---|
| Increased risk of death from infection (wasting and stunting together multiply risk) | Poor school performance, exclusion |
| Impaired cognitive development, lower school attainment and adult earnings (stunting) | Behavioural problems, low self-esteem |
| Short adult stature; obstructed labour in short women (next generation) | Secondary problems (contractures, malnutrition in CP) |
| Later obesity and non-communicable disease after rapid catch-up | Family stress, maternal depression |

## Ward-round & exam pearls

### Model presentation

> "Nabukeera is a 9-month-old girl from Mpigi, born at term weighing 3.1 kg, HIV-exposed (mother on ART since pregnancy, virally suppressed), with a negative DNA PCR at 6 weeks and on co-trimoxazole; immunised up to date (BCG, OPV, IPV, DPT-HepB-Hib, PCV, rotavirus; MR due today). Vitamin A and deworming not yet given. She was exclusively breastfed to 4 months, then given thin maize porridge twice a day plus breast milk; no animal-source foods. She has had 3 episodes of diarrhoea in 2 months. Weight 6.6 kg (WAZ −2.0), length 66 cm (LAZ −1.7), WLZ about −1.2, MUAC 12.3 cm (MAM), HC 43 cm (normal), no oedema; weight has crossed down from about the 25th to below the 3rd centile since 4 months. Development: sits without support, transfers objects, rakes small items, babbles 'mamama', shows stranger anxiety: **normal for age**. **Impression: growth faltering with moderate acute malnutrition by MUAC, due to inadequate complementary feeding and recurrent diarrhoea; development normal.** Plan: repeat HIV test per PMTCT schedule (DNA PCR at 9 months per national algorithm), give MR vaccine, vitamin A 100,000 IU, counsel on 3–4 thick meals a day with eggs, beans and groundnut paste plus continued breastfeeding, handwashing, zinc and ORS for future diarrhoea, review in 2 weeks."

### Viva questions (with answers)

1. **What is a Z-score?** The number of SDs a measurement lies from the WHO median for age and sex; < −2 moderate, < −3 severe.
2. **Difference between stunting and wasting?** Stunting = low height-for-age (chronic); wasting = low weight-for-height or low MUAC (acute).
3. **SAM criteria?** WHZ < −3, MUAC < 11.5 cm (6–59 months), or bilateral pitting oedema.
4. **When should a child sit, walk and talk?** Sit by 9 months, walk by 18 months, 6 words by 18 months, two-word phrases by 2 years (limit ages).
5. **Name the domains of development.** Gross motor; fine motor and vision; speech, language and hearing; social, emotional and self-care.
6. **What is global developmental delay?** Significant delay in two or more domains in a child under 5.
7. **When does the anterior fontanelle close?** 12–18 months.
8. **Define precocious and delayed puberty.** Precocious: girls < 8, boys < 9 years. Delayed: no breast development by 13 (girls), testes < 4 mL by 14 (boys).
9. **How do you calculate mid-parental height?** Boys (F + M + 13)/2; girls (F + M − 13)/2, ± 8.5 cm.
10. **When do you correct for prematurity?** Until 2 years of age.

### OSCE tips

- **Developmental assessment station**: introduce yourself, explain to the parent, **observe the child playing first**, then go domain by domain (gross motor, fine motor/vision, language/hearing, social), using simple toys (cubes, small bean or raisin under supervision, ball, crayon and paper, picture book, rattle). State the **developmental age in each domain** and compare with the chronological (corrected) age; finish with "I would also check hearing and vision, plot growth and head circumference."
- **Growth chart station**: check the chart matches the child's **sex and age range** and the **measurement type**; plot accurately (age on x-axis, measurement on y-axis); comment on the **trend**, Z-score and proportionality; give an interpretation and a plan.

## Mnemonics & memory aids

- **Weight rule "2–1–2.5"**: **doubles** by 5 months, **triples** by **1** year, **quadruples** by **2.5** years.
- **Length "50–75–100"**: 50 cm at birth, 75 cm at 1 year, 100 cm at 4 years.
- **HC "35–46"**: 35 cm at birth, about 46 cm at 1 year (2–1–0.5 cm/month per quarter-year).
- **Limit ages "Smile 2, See 3, Head 4, Reach 6, Sit 9, Pinch 12, Walk & Words 18, Phrases 2"**: smile by 2 months, fix and follow by 3, head control by 4, reach by 6, sit by 9, pincer by 12 months, walk and 6 words by 18 months, two-word phrases by 2 years.
- **Drawing shapes "circle 3, cross 4, square 4.5, triangle 5"** (the number of years matches the age).
- **Cubes**: tower of 3 at 18 months, 6–7 at 2 years, 9 at 3 years (roughly age in years × 3).
- **Puberty order, girls "Buds, Bush, Growth, Gush"**: thelarche → pubarche → growth spurt → menarche. **Boys "Balls first"**: testes ≥ 4 mL.

### Classic exam traps

- **"Weight is normal, so nutrition is fine."** Wrong: check **oedema** and **height**; stunting and kwashiorkor can coexist with "normal" weight.
- **"Measure MUAC in a 3-month-old."** Wrong: MUAC cut-offs apply from **6 months** (UCG; WHO 6–59 months).
- **"A 10-month-old ex-28-week baby who cannot sit has motor delay."** Wrong: corrected age is ~7 months; sitting is not yet due.
- **"Hand preference at 9 months shows she will be right-handed."** Wrong: it is abnormal before 12 months; examine the other arm.
- **"Isolated speech delay is laziness; wait and see."** Wrong: test **hearing** first.
- **"Short child = growth hormone deficiency."** Wrong: commonest causes are familial short stature, constitutional delay and undernutrition; always plot the parents and the velocity.
- **"Precocious puberty in a boy is usually idiopathic."** Wrong: it is often pathological; refer all boys.
- **"Regression in a toddler is just a phase."** Wrong: loss of skills is always a red flag (HIV encephalopathy, metabolic, Rett syndrome, autism).

### Questions seniors ask

**How do you tell stunting from wasting, and which is worse?**
Stunting is low height-for-age (chronic, mostly from the first 1000 days); wasting is low weight-for-height/MUAC (acute). Wasting carries the higher immediate risk of death; stunting has lifelong effects on cognition and adult health; children with both have the highest mortality.

**This child's weight has crossed two centile lines. What do you do?**
Re-measure carefully, take a feeding and illness history, test for HIV, screen for TB, malaria, UTI and anaemia, examine for chronic disease, counsel on feeding and review in 2–4 weeks; treat SAM/MAM if criteria are met.

**Why is head circumference important?**
It reflects brain growth; microcephaly (or a falling HC) suggests brain injury, congenital infection, genetic disease or HIV encephalopathy; macrocephaly or a rising HC suggests hydrocephalus or a mass.

**How do you assess development quickly on a ward round?**
Ask the parent what worries them and what the child does now; observe the child; check one or two skills per domain against the limit ages; ask about regression; note hearing and vision.

**A 2-year-old says no words but walks and plays well. What do you think of?**
Hearing loss first (test), then autism spectrum disorder (eye contact, pointing, response to name), global delay, lack of stimulation, or a specific language disorder.

**What is the difference between familial short stature and constitutional delay?**
Familial: short parents, normal velocity, normal bone age, normal puberty timing, short final height. Constitutional: often average parental heights with a family history of late puberty, normal velocity, **delayed bone age** and delayed puberty, normal final height.

**What advice would you give the mother of a 7-month-old on complementary feeding?**
Continue breastfeeding; give thick porridge enriched with groundnut/simsim paste, milk or egg, mashed beans, fish, vegetables and fruit; 2–3 meals a day now and 3–4 plus snacks from 9 months; feed patiently with a separate bowl; wash hands; continue feeding during illness.
