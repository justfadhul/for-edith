## In a nutshell

**Neonatal tetanus** (tetanus neonatorum) is generalised tetanus in a baby in the first 28 days of life. It happens when **spores of Clostridium tetani** contaminate the **umbilical stump** (cut with an unsterile razor or reed, or dressed with cow dung, ash, soil or herbs) in a baby whose **mother has no protective antibodies** because she was not immunised with **tetanus toxoid (TT/Td)**.

The story is classic: a baby who **sucked and cried normally for the first 2 days**, then on day 3-14 **stops sucking** (jaw spasm, trismus), cries excessively, becomes **stiff**, and develops **painful spasms** triggered by touch, noise or light, while remaining **fully conscious**.

Why it matters in Uganda: neonatal tetanus is almost **100% preventable** with maternal Td and clean cord care, yet cases still occur after home births and harmful cord practices, especially in hard-to-reach districts. Without intensive care it is usually fatal; with good nursing, sedation and antitoxin, many babies survive.

> [!PEARL]
> The one thing never to forget: **every case of neonatal tetanus is a failure of prevention**. When you admit one, also vaccinate the mother, report the case, and ask how the cord was cut and dressed.

## Definitions & classification

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Neonatal tetanus (WHO surveillance definition, confirmed case)** | Illness in an infant with **normal ability to suck and cry in the first 2 days**, who **loses this ability between days 3 and 28** and **becomes rigid or has spasms** |
| **Suspected case** | Any neonatal death between 3 and 28 days of unknown cause, or any illness reported as tetanus in the first month, where the infant fed normally after birth |
| **Maternal tetanus** | Tetanus during pregnancy or within 6 weeks after the end of pregnancy (birth or abortion) |
| **Incubation period** | Time from spore entry to first symptom: usually **3-14 days** in neonates (often 5-7) |
| **Period of onset** | Time from first symptom to first spasm; **below 48 h = severe** |
| **MNT elimination** | Fewer than **1 case per 1,000 live births in every district** per year (WHO target) |

Other forms of tetanus (for comparison): generalised (commonest overall), localised (spasm near the wound), cephalic (cranial nerves, from head wounds/otitis).

## Epidemiology & Uganda context

- WHO: in **2021 just over 7,700 newborns died of neonatal tetanus**, an **84% reduction since 2000** (about 46,900 deaths), mainly due to immunisation of women with tetanus-toxoid-containing vaccine (TTCV) and clean birth practices. Disruption of immunisation services (e.g. during COVID-19) caused resurgence in some places.
- **Risk factors**: unimmunised or incompletely immunised mother (no ANC, first pregnancy without Td2 before delivery, teenage mother), **home or roadside birth**, unclean cutting instrument, **substances on the cord** (cow dung, ash, mud, herbs, ghee), cultural practices, rural residence, poverty.
- **Uganda**: Td is given at ANC (first contact) and in the UNEPI schedule for girls/women of childbearing age; Uganda was validated by WHO as having achieved MNT elimination (2011), which must be sustained through Td coverage, facility births and clean cord care. Sporadic cases still present to regional and national referral hospitals.
- **Neonatal tetanus is a notifiable disease** (UCG 2023): notify the district surveillance focal person; every case triggers a case investigation (mother's Td history, place of birth, cord practices) and community response.
- **Facilities**: TIG/antitoxin, metronidazole, diazepam and chlorpromazine are available at most general and referral hospitals; **ICU ventilation** for neonates is rare outside Kampala.

## Pathophysiology

1. **Spores** of C. tetani (an anaerobic Gram-positive bacillus, found in soil and animal faeces) enter the **necrotic umbilical stump**, an ideal low-oxygen environment.
2. Spores germinate and the bacteria produce **tetanospasmin** (and tetanolysin). The bacteria stay local; the **toxin** causes the disease.
3. Tetanospasmin binds to **motor nerve terminals** and travels by **retrograde axonal transport** to the **spinal cord and brainstem**.
4. In the central nervous system it is taken up by **inhibitory interneurons** and cleaves **synaptobrevin**, blocking the release of the inhibitory neurotransmitters **GABA and glycine**.
5. Without inhibition, motor neurons fire excessively → **rigidity** and **reflex spasms**; the shortest nerves (cranial: jaw, face) are affected first → **trismus**, **risus sardonicus**, then neck, back (**opisthotonus**), abdomen and limbs.
6. **Autonomic** disinhibition → tachycardia, labile blood pressure, sweating, hyperpyrexia, arrhythmias.
7. **Toxin binding is irreversible**: recovery needs **new nerve terminal sprouting**, which takes **3-6 weeks**. Antitoxin only neutralises **unbound** toxin, so give it **early**.
8. **Why the mother matters**: maternal IgG antibodies from TT/Td cross the placenta and neutralise toxin in the baby. That is why maternal immunisation prevents neonatal tetanus.
9. **Why the disease does not protect**: the amount of toxin that causes disease is too small to induce immunity, so survivors must be vaccinated.

Dirty cord  ->  spores germinate (anaerobic stump)

                    |

                    |

## Clinical acumen

### Focused history

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Age now and day symptoms began** | Onset day 3-28 fits; shorter incubation (below 7 days) = worse prognosis |
| **Did the baby suck and cry normally in the first 2 days?** | Key part of the WHO case definition (separates from HIE and congenital problems) |
| **When did the baby stop sucking? First spasm when?** | Period of onset below 48 h = severe |
| **Place of birth, who delivered, what was used to cut and tie the cord** | Unclean birth is the source |
| **What was applied to the cord?** (cow dung, ash, herbs, ghee, powder) | Source of spores |
| **Mother's Td/TT doses and timing (Td card), ANC attendance** | Unprotected mother; plan her vaccination |
| **Previous babies who died at 1-2 weeks** | Suggests previous tetanus in an unimmunised mother |
| **Feeding since onset**: any fluids, how given? | Dehydration, hypoglycaemia, aspiration risk |
| **Breathing pauses, colour change during spasms** | Apnoea, laryngospasm: life-threatening |
| **Fever, convulsions with loss of consciousness, jaundice** | Points to sepsis/meningitis or other diagnoses |
| **Maternal HIV status, syphilis** | Other risks; prophylaxis |

### Examination

- **Observe first, touch later.** Watch the baby quietly before handling (handling triggers spasms).
- **Trismus**: the mouth cannot open; baby cannot suck; try to insert a finger or spatula gently (spatula test: the baby bites down reflexively in tetanus instead of gagging).
- **Risus sardonicus** (fixed grimace), wrinkled forehead, pursed lips ("carp mouth").
- **Generalised rigidity**: board-like abdomen, stiff limbs, **clenched fists**, flexed arms, extended legs, toes dorsiflexed.
- **Opisthotonus** (arched back) during spasms.
- **Spasms** provoked by touch, noise, light, injection: count frequency, duration, and whether associated with cyanosis/apnoea.
- **Level of consciousness**: **alert between spasms** (key difference from seizures/meningitis).
- **Fontanelle**: normal (bulging suggests meningitis).
- **Umbilicus**: dirty, infected or septic stump (may look normal by the time of presentation).
- **Vitals**: temperature (fever from spasms or infection), HR (tachycardia), RR, SpO2, BP if possible.
- **Hydration and weight**: dehydration, weight loss.
- **Complications**: aspiration pneumonia (crackles), sepsis signs.

### Red flags

> [!REDFLAG]
> - **Apnoea, cyanosis or laryngospasm** during spasms
> - **Very frequent or prolonged spasms** despite sedation
> - **Short incubation (below 7 days)** or **period of onset below 48 h**
> - **Fever, tachycardia above 180, labile BP** (autonomic instability)
> - **Aspiration pneumonia**, sepsis
> - **Over-sedation**: no spontaneous breathing, floppy, SpO2 falling after diazepam
> - **Hypoglycaemia, hypothermia, dehydration** from not feeding

### Thinking like a clinician

- **Conscious baby + spasms + trismus + onset day 3-14 + dirty cord = tetanus.** A baby with seizures from meningitis or hypoglycaemia has altered consciousness between episodes; the tetanus baby looks anxious and alert.
- **"Could suck at birth, cannot suck now"** separates tetanus from HIE (abnormal from birth) and from congenital problems.
- **Do not do things that provoke spasms** without sedation (LP, NGT insertion, IV line, examination): sedate first and cluster procedures.
- **The biggest killers are apnoea, aspiration and exhaustion**, not the toxin itself. Good nursing saves lives.
- **Sedation is a balance**: enough to stop spasms, not so much that the baby stops breathing. Know where the bag and mask are.
- **Always ask about the mother's Td** and vaccinate her before she leaves.

## Clinical workup

### Step 0: First 5 minutes

1. **Airway and breathing**: suction the mouth gently; oxygen if cyanosed or SpO2 below 90%; **bag-mask ventilation** if apnoeic.
2. **Stop the spasm**: **diazepam 0.5 mg/kg rectally** or **0.2 mg/kg IV** (UCG) if spasms are ongoing; watch breathing.
3. **Quiet, dark, cool corner/room**; minimal handling.
4. **Glucose** (when sedated) and temperature.
5. **IV access** after sedation; start IV fluids.
6. **Call for help**; plan TIG, metronidazole and nursing.
7. **At HC III/IV**: sedate, give the first dose of antibiotic (metronidazole/benzylpenicillin) and TIG if available, and **refer immediately** to hospital (UCG: "Refer to hospital immediately") with the mother.

### Step 1: Focused history

- [ ] Age and day of onset; normal suck/cry in first 2 days?: confirms case definition.
- [ ] Day of first spasm: prognosis.
- [ ] Place and attendant of birth; instrument for cord; substances on cord: source and public health response.
- [ ] Mother's Td doses and dates: case investigation; vaccinate her.
- [ ] Feeding, urine output: hydration.
- [ ] Apnoeas, fever, other symptoms: severity and complications.
- [ ] Mother's HIV/syphilis status; baby's immunisations (BCG/OPV0).

### Step 2: Focused examination

- Observe: posture, spasms, frequency, consciousness, colour.
- Trismus, risus sardonicus, fist clenching, opisthotonus, abdominal rigidity.
- Vitals (T, HR, RR, SpO2), CRT, hydration, weight.
- Umbilicus and skin.
- Chest (aspiration), fontanelle (meningitis), jaundice.
- Document the **spasm score/frequency** each shift to guide sedation.

### Step 3: Bedside tests

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| **Glucose** | Hypoglycaemia from not feeding (mimic and complication) |
| **Temperature** | Fever (spasms, infection) or hypothermia |
| **SpO2** | Hypoxaemia during spasms/apnoea; over-sedation |
| **Weight** | Dehydration; fluid calculation |
| **Spasm chart** | Number and duration of spasms per hour to titrate sedation |

### Step 4: Laboratory & imaging

| Test | When | Expected / abnormal finding | How it changes management | Availability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **None is diagnostic** | | Tetanus is a **clinical** diagnosis | Treat on clinical grounds | |
| **FBC** | Baseline, suspected sepsis | Often normal; leucocytosis if secondary infection | Antibiotic choice | HC IV and above |
| **Blood culture** | Fever, umbilical sepsis | Secondary bacterial sepsis (S. aureus, Gram-negatives) | Add/adjust antibiotics | Referral hospitals |
| **Electrolytes, calcium** | Doubt about diagnosis | Hypocalcaemia (tetany mimic) | Calcium gluconate | Referral hospitals |
| **Lumbar puncture** (after sedation) | Only if meningitis cannot be excluded clinically | **Normal CSF** in tetanus | Meningitis needs 21 days of antibiotics | Hospital |
| **Wound swab culture** | Rarely useful | C. tetani is hard to grow and may be present without disease | Not used to diagnose | Limited |
| **Chest X-ray** | Suspected aspiration pneumonia | Consolidation | Antibiotics, oxygen | Most hospitals |

### Step 5: Putting it together

**Model summary line:** "A **9-day-old** male born at **home in Kayunga** by a traditional birth attendant, cord cut with a **used razor blade** and dressed with **cow dung**, who **sucked well until day 6**, then **stopped sucking**, and since day 7 has **generalised spasms** triggered by touch with **trismus, risus sardonicus and opisthotonus**, alert between spasms. Mother is a **19-year-old primigravida** who attended ANC once and received **one dose of Td**. Weight 2.8 kg, T 38.0 C, HR 172, SpO2 92% in air."

**Assessment:** **neonatal tetanus**, incubation 6 days and period of onset about 24 h (**severe**), with possible omphalitis; risks of apnoea, aspiration and dehydration.

**Problem list:** (1) neonatal tetanus with frequent spasms; (2) source: infected umbilical stump; (3) feeding/hydration; (4) risk of apnoea/aspiration; (5) unprotected mother; (6) notification and community follow-up.

## Differential diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing features | Key investigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Neonatal tetanus** | Normal suck days 1-2, then trismus, rigidity, spasms provoked by stimuli, **conscious** between spasms, dirty cord | Clinical |
| **Neonatal sepsis/meningitis** | Fever or hypothermia, lethargy, **reduced consciousness**, bulging fontanelle, seizures not triggered by touch | Blood culture, LP |
| **Hypocalcaemia (neonatal tetany)** | Jitteriness, carpopedal spasm, seizures, often IDM/preterm/asphyxia; no trismus | Serum calcium |
| **Hypoglycaemia** | Jittery, lethargic, seizures; responds to glucose | Glucose |
| **HIE seizures** | Abnormal from birth after a difficult delivery; altered consciousness | History, Sarnat |
| **Kernicterus** | Deep jaundice, opisthotonus, high-pitched cry, lethargy | Bilirubin |
| **Neonatal abstinence/drug withdrawal** | Maternal drug use; jittery, hypertonia, poor feeding, sneezing | History |
| **Congenital neuromuscular/structural problems** | Present from birth; no spasms | Clinical, imaging |

## Investigations

| Test | What you are looking for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Glucose | Hypoglycaemia | Complication and mimic |
| FBC, blood culture | Secondary infection | Antibiotic plan |
| Calcium | Hypocalcaemia | Mimic |
| CSF (after sedation, only if in doubt) | Meningitis | Normal CSF supports tetanus |
| CXR | Aspiration | Complication |

## Management

### 1. Overview: the five pillars

1. **Airway, breathing and spasm control** (sedation, suction, oxygen, ventilation if available).
2. **Neutralise unbound toxin** (TIG or antitoxin, as early as possible).
3. **Remove the source** (metronidazole; clean/debride the stump).
4. **Supportive and nursing care** (quiet dark room, fluids, NGT feeding, temperature, skin, eyes, monitoring).
5. **Prevention**: vaccinate the baby (and complete the schedule) and the **mother**, **notify**, and educate.

### 2. Drug table (UCG 2023, with WHO/international notes)

| Drug | Dose | Route | Frequency | Duration / notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Human tetanus immunoglobulin (TIG)** | **500 IU** | **IM**, divided between **at least 2 sites**, away from the vaccine site | **Once** | Neutralises unbound toxin; give as early as possible. Intrathecal TIG has been studied in neonatal tetanus but is **not routine** |
| **Equine tetanus antitoxin (ATS)** (if TIG unavailable) | **5,000 IU** (WHO/older protocols; check local product) | IM (or IV per product) | Once | Test dose and **adrenaline ready** (anaphylaxis, serum sickness) |
| **DTP-containing vaccine** (UCG: "1st dose of DPT"; in Uganda the pentavalent DTP-HepB-Hib) | Standard dose | IM, **different limb** from TIG | At diagnosis | Disease does **not** confer immunity; continue the routine schedule |
| **Metronidazole** (first line) | **Loading 15 mg/kg** over 60 min, then **7.5 mg/kg** | IV (oral/NGT when possible) | **Every 12 h if under 4 weeks old**; **every 8 h if over 4 weeks** | UCG 2023 lists a 14-day course (many references use 7-10 days) |
| **Benzylpenicillin** (second line) | **100,000 IU/kg** | IV/IM | **Every 12 h** | **10-14 days** (UCG). Metronidazole is preferred (penicillin is a GABA antagonist) |
| **Diazepam** (spasm control) | **0.2 mg/kg IV** or **0.5 mg/kg rectal** | IV/rectal | **Every 1-4 h** as needed (UCG) | Titrate to spasms; watch **apnoea**, hypotension; some units use a continuous infusion with monitoring; taper slowly over weeks as spasms settle |
| **Chlorpromazine** (adjunct) | **1 mg/kg** | Oral via **NGT** | **Every 8 h** (UCG) | Often alternated with diazepam to reduce total benzodiazepine dose; hypotension |
| **Phenobarbital** (alternative/adjunct sedative) | Loading 20 mg/kg, then 5 mg/kg/day | IV/IM/NGT | Daily | Per local protocol; additive respiratory depression |
| **Magnesium sulphate** | Specialist/ICU use only | IV | Infusion | Reduces spasms and autonomic instability in adults; limited neonatal evidence |
| **Cloxacillin + gentamicin** | 50 mg/kg 6-hourly + 5 mg/kg (7.5 mg/kg after week 1) daily | IV | | If **omphalitis with flare** or secondary sepsis |
| **Oxygen** | To SpO2 above 90% | Nasal prongs | As needed | During spasms/apnoea |

> [!DRUG]
> **Worked example (UCG doses) for a 2.8 kg, 9-day-old baby:**
> TIG **500 IU IM** (250 IU into each thigh, separate from vaccine site); first pentavalent (DTP-containing) dose in another site; **metronidazole 42 mg IV** over 60 min, then **21 mg IV 12-hourly**; **diazepam 0.56 mg IV** (or **1.4 mg rectally**) every 1-4 h as needed; **chlorpromazine 2.8 mg via NGT 8-hourly**.

> [!UGANDA]
> UCG 2023 places neonatal tetanus management at **hospital level (H)** and ventilation at **regional/national referral level (RR)**. If TIG is out of stock, use equine antitoxin with anaphylaxis precautions, or IV immunoglobulin (which contains tetanus antitoxin) where available. Do not delay sedation, metronidazole and nursing while you look for TIG.

### 3. Nursing care (the most important part)

1. **Environment**: a **quiet, dark, cool** room or corner (UCG); cover the cot; no radios; restrict visitors (but keep the mother close).
2. **Minimal handling**: **cluster care** (feeds, observations, injections, turning) **about 30 minutes after a sedative dose** (UCG: suction and turn 30 min after sedative).
3. **Airway**: keep a **mucus extractor/suction** at the bedside; suction the mouth as needed; position on the side.
4. **Breathing**: oxygen and pulse oximetry; **bag and mask at the bedside** for apnoea; observe breathing continuously after diazepam.
5. **Fluids** (UCG): **start with IV fluids** (UCG: half-strength saline with 5% dextrose; follow neonatal fluid volumes by weight and day of life, e.g. 120-150 mL/kg/day after the first days), then **NGT feeds of expressed breast milk from 24 h after admission, in small frequent feeds**; reduce IV as feeds increase.
6. **Mother expresses breast milk** every 3 h to keep lactation going; she can do gentle care when sedation allows.
7. **Temperature**: monitor 4-hourly; treat fever by environmental measures; avoid hypothermia.
8. **Cord care**: clean and **debride** the stump; chlorhexidine; nothing else.
9. **Skin, eyes, mouth**: turn 2-3-hourly (pressure areas), eye lubrication if lids do not close, mouth care.
10. **Monitoring chart**: spasms per hour, sedation doses, RR, HR, SpO2, temperature, intake/output, weight daily.
11. **Wean sedation slowly** over 2-4 weeks as spasms reduce; the baby may stay rigid for weeks; full recovery of sucking may take 2-4 weeks.
12. **Discharge** when sucking well, no spasms, gaining weight; give BCG/OPV0 if not given and continue the UNEPI schedule.

### 4. Prevention: individual and public health

| Level | Action |
|---|---|
| **Mother (now)** | Give **Td** before discharge (start or continue the 5-dose schedule) |
| **Women of childbearing age** | **Td1** at first contact; **Td2** at least 4 weeks later; **Td3** 6 months later; **Td4** 1 year later; **Td5** 1 year later (lifelong protection). Give **Td2 at least 2 weeks before delivery** |
| **Childhood** | DTP-containing vaccine (pentavalent) at **6, 10 and 14 weeks** (UNEPI), plus boosters per the current national schedule so that girls arrive at childbearing age protected |
| **Birth** | Facility delivery or a skilled attendant; the "**cleans**": clean hands, clean perineum, clean delivery surface, **clean blade**, **clean cord tie**, **clean cord care** |
| **Cord care** | **Chlorhexidine 7.1% digluconate gel daily for 7 days** (UCG), keep dry and clean, **nothing else on the stump** |
| **Surveillance** | **Notify** every case (and any neonatal death on day 3-28 of unknown cause), case investigation, community Td response |

> [!UGANDA]
> Uganda's ANC Td schedule is the same one in your Obs & Gyn quick reference (Td1 at first contact, Td2 4 weeks later, Td3 6 months later, Td4 and Td5 a year apart). When you see a mother with a tetanus baby, give her **Td today** and explain that she needs the remaining doses to protect future babies.

## Complications

| Acute | Later |
|---|---|
| **Apnoea, laryngospasm, respiratory failure** (main cause of death) | Most survivors recover fully |
| Aspiration pneumonia | Some have **neurological sequelae** from hypoxia during spasms (developmental delay, CP) |
| Hypoglycaemia, dehydration, malnutrition | Recurrence possible without immunisation |
| Secondary sepsis (umbilical, line, hospital-acquired) | Family grief; future pregnancies at risk if mother not vaccinated |
| Autonomic instability: tachycardia, hyperthermia, arrhythmia | |
| Fractures (vertebral) from violent spasms (rare) | |
| Over-sedation, hypotension | |
| Death: high case fatality, especially with short incubation and no ventilation | |

**Poor prognostic features**: incubation **below 7 days**, period of onset **below 48 h**, frequent severe spasms, fever, tachycardia, LBW/prematurity, apnoea, no access to ventilation.

## Ward-round & exam pearls

### Model presentation

"**Baby Kato**, a **10-day-old** male, weight **2.9 kg** (birth weight not known; home birth), **admitted yesterday with neonatal tetanus**. He breastfed normally until **day 6**, then stopped sucking; spasms began on **day 7**. The cord was cut with a **razor blade** and **cow dung** was applied. Mother is 20, had **one dose of Td** in this pregnancy, **HIV-negative** at ANC, RPR negative. He has had no BCG or OPV0.

On examination he has **trismus, risus sardonicus, clenched fists and opisthotonic spasms** provoked by touch, **alert between spasms**; T 37.8 C, HR 168, RR 52, SpO2 95% in 1 L/min oxygen; umbilicus moist with surrounding redness 0.5 cm.

He has received **TIG 500 IU IM in two sites**, the **first pentavalent dose**, **metronidazole 43.5 mg loading then 22 mg 12-hourly**, and is on **diazepam 0.6 mg IV 2-hourly** alternating with **chlorpromazine 3 mg 8-hourly by NGT**. Spasms have reduced from **12 to 5 per hour**. He is on IV fluids at 120 mL/kg/day and started **EBM 5 mL 2-hourly** by NGT today.

**Problems:** severe neonatal tetanus (incubation 6 days, onset to spasm 1 day); umbilical infection; feeding; risk of apnoea and aspiration; unimmunised mother. **Plan:** continue sedation titrated to spasms, cluster care, advance NGT feeds, monitor SpO2 and apnoea, **Td for the mother today**, **notify** the district surveillance officer."

### Viva questions (with answers)

1. **What is the WHO case definition?** Normal suck and cry in the first 2 days, then loss of ability to suck between days 3 and 28, with rigidity or spasms.
2. **How does tetanus toxin cause spasms?** It travels retrogradely to the CNS and blocks release of GABA and glycine from inhibitory interneurons.
3. **Why is the baby conscious?** The toxin affects inhibitory motor pathways, not the cortex.
4. **What is the dose of TIG?** 500 IU IM, divided into two sites, away from the vaccine (UCG).
5. **Why metronidazole rather than penicillin?** Equal or better activity against C. tetani, and penicillin is a GABA antagonist; metronidazole is associated with lower mortality.
6. **How would you prevent neonatal tetanus?** Maternal Td (5 doses; Td2 at least 2 weeks before delivery), clean delivery, clean cord care with chlorhexidine.
7. **Does surviving tetanus give immunity?** No. Vaccinate the baby and complete the schedule.

### OSCE tips

- Expect a **counselling station** on cord care or Td for a pregnant woman, or a **spot diagnosis** from a picture of opisthotonus/risus sardonicus.
- In a history station, always ask: "**Could the baby suck after birth?**", "**What was used to cut the cord, and what was put on it?**", "**How many tetanus injections did you get?**"

### Classic exam traps

- **"Baby with spasms on day 7: treat as neonatal seizures with phenobarbital only."** → Think **tetanus** if the baby is conscious, has trismus and spasms provoked by touch; give TIG, metronidazole, sedation.
- **"Do an LP and insert an NGT straight away."** → Sedate first and cluster procedures; stimulation triggers spasms and apnoea.
- **"Penicillin is the antibiotic of choice."** → **Metronidazole** is first line (UCG); penicillin is second line.
- **"The baby survived, so he is now immune."** → No. Give DTP-containing vaccine and complete the schedule.
- **"Treat the baby and discharge the mother."** → Give the **mother Td**, notify, and educate about cord care.
- **"Inject TIG into the same limb as the vaccine."** → Use **different sites**.
- **"Feed by bottle as soon as he is admitted."** → He cannot suck and may aspirate: IV fluids first, then **NGT EBM** from 24 h.

### Questions seniors ask

1. **"How do you know this is tetanus and not meningitis?"** Normal suck days 1-2, trismus, spasms triggered by stimuli, alert between spasms, normal fontanelle; meningitis has reduced consciousness, fever, bulging fontanelle.
2. **"What were the incubation period and period of onset?"** Give the days and hours; short = severe.
3. **"What has he received, and in what dose?"** TIG 500 IU, metronidazole mg/kg, diazepam mg/kg and frequency.
4. **"How are you titrating sedation?"** By spasm frequency and breathing; bag and mask at the bedside.
5. **"How is he being fed?"** IV fluids then NGT EBM; mother expressing.
6. **"What about the mother?"** Td today and the full schedule; ANC for future pregnancies.
7. **"Who have you told?"** Notified district surveillance; case investigation.
