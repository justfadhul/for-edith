## In a nutshell

The **newborn examination** is a structured, head-to-toe check that answers three questions:
1. **Is this baby well now?** (danger signs, vital signs, feeding)
2. **Is anything abnormal that needs action or referral?** (malformations, hips, eyes, heart, birth injury, infection risk)
3. **Is the baby the expected size for gestation?** (weight, length, head circumference, Ballard score)

It is done **at birth** (quick check), **within 24 hours and before discharge** (full exam), at the **3-7-day** postnatal visit and at **6 weeks**. UCG 2023 (section 17.1.1) asks every health worker to examine all babies after delivery, before discharge, and whenever a baby is seen in the first week.

Why it matters in Uganda: many babies go home within hours of birth, so this may be the **only full examination** a baby gets. Missing a danger sign (sepsis, jaundice, hypothermia), a cleft palate (feeding failure), an imperforate anus, a dislocated hip or a white pupil (retinoblastoma) has lifelong consequences.

> [!PEARL]
> The one thing never to forget: **observe before you touch, and keep the baby warm**. Most of the exam (colour, breathing, posture, movements) is done by looking at a calm baby; examine in a warm room, uncover one part at a time, and finish with the things that make babies cry (hips, Moro, mouth).

## Definitions & classification

### Normal values for a term newborn

| Parameter | Normal range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Weight** | **2.5-4.0 kg** | Below 2.5 kg = LBW; above 4.0 kg = macrosomia (think maternal diabetes). Plot by gestation: SGA below 10th centile, LGA above 90th |
| **Length** | About **48-53 cm** | Use a length board (two people) |
| **Head circumference (OFC)** | About **33-37 cm** (mean about 35 cm) | Largest of 3 measurements over the occiput and above the eyebrows; microcephaly below **-2 SD** (severe below -3 SD) |
| **Respiratory rate** | **30-60/min** | Count for a full minute when calm (UCG); periodic breathing (pauses under 20 s) is normal |
| **Heart rate** | **100-160/min** | Sleep can be 90-100; crying up to 180 |
| **Temperature** | **36.5-37.5 C** axillary | Hypothermia below 36.5 C |
| **SpO2** | **95% or more** after the first hours | Pre- and post-ductal difference 3% or less |
| **Capillary refill** | Under **3 seconds** | Press on the sternum |
| **Weight change** | Loss up to **7-10%** in the first 3-5 days; regain birth weight by **10-14 days** | More than 10% = feeding problem |
| **First urine** | Within **24 h** | |
| **First meconium** | Within **24 h** (almost all term babies by 48 h) | Delay = obstruction until proven otherwise |

### Gestational age categories

| Category | Gestation |
|---|---|
| Preterm | Below 37 weeks |
| Term | 37+0 to 41+6 weeks |
| Post-term | 42 weeks or more |

## Indications & contraindications

### When to examine

| Time | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **At birth (first minute and first hour)** | Breathing, HR, colour, tone, obvious malformations; Apgar; skin-to-skin; vitamin K, eye ointment, cord care |
| **Within 24 h and before discharge** | Full head-to-toe exam, measurements, Ballard if gestation uncertain, hips, red reflex, palate, anus, spine, femoral pulses, feeding observation |
| **3-7 days** (postnatal visit) | Jaundice, feeding, weight, cord, eyes, skin, danger signs |
| **6 weeks** | Repeat hips, red reflex, heart, testes; growth; development (social smile); immunisation |
| **Any sick visit in the first month** | Danger-sign assessment (IMNCI) |

### Contraindications and cautions

- **Defer a full examination** while a baby is being resuscitated or is unstable: stabilise first (ABC, warmth, glucose).
- **Do not perform Barlow/Ortolani forcefully or repeatedly** (can damage the hip), and **avoid them** in a baby with a suspected limb fracture.
- **Do not force the legs** of a baby born by breech (UCG).
- In **suspected neonatal tetanus**, minimise stimulation (examine after sedation).
- Always obtain the **mother's permission** and explain what you are doing.

## Procedure step by step

### A. Preparation

1. **Read the records**: maternal age, parity, ANC findings, HIV (last VL), syphilis, HBsAg, blood group, gestation by dates/scan, labour events (ROM, fever, meconium, mode of delivery), resuscitation, Apgar, birth weight, drugs given (vitamin K, eye ointment, NVP).
2. **Introduce yourself**, confirm the baby's identity (name band), explain the exam, get consent; ask the mother about **feeding, urine, stool, and any concerns**.
3. **Wash hands**; warm hands and stethoscope; warm room, no draught.
4. **Observe the baby quietly** in the mother's arms or cot before undressing.

### B. General inspection (hands off)

5. **Colour**: pink, pale, **central cyanosis** (tongue/lips), peripheral acrocyanosis (normal in first 24-48 h), **jaundice** (in daylight; blanch skin), plethora.
6. **Breathing**: count **RR for 60 seconds**; look for **grunting, flaring, chest indrawing**, apnoea.
7. **Posture and movement**: term baby lies **flexed** with symmetrical spontaneous movements; note a limp or "frog-leg" posture (hypotonia), asymmetry (palsy, fracture), jitteriness, seizures.
8. **Cry**: normal, weak, high-pitched (CNS), hoarse (hypothyroidism, laryngeal).
9. **Dysmorphic features**: overall appearance (e.g. Down syndrome: upslanting palpebral fissures, flat facial profile, epicanthic folds, single palmar crease, sandal gap, hypotonia).

### C. Measurements

10. **Weigh** naked on a zeroed scale; record to the nearest 10-20 g.
11. **Head circumference**: tape over the occiput and just above the eyebrows; take the largest of three.
12. **Length**: two people, baby supine on a length board, legs extended.
13. **Temperature** (axillary, 3-5 min or digital beep).
14. **Plot** all three on the appropriate chart; classify **SGA/AGA/LGA**; note microcephaly/macrocephaly.

### D. Head-to-toe examination

15. **Head**: shape, **moulding**, **caput**, **cephalhaematoma**, **subgaleal swelling**; palpate **sutures** (overriding, widely open, craniosynostosis ridge) and **fontanelles** (size, tension: flat, bulging, sunken).
16. **Face**: symmetry at rest and crying (**facial palsy**), dysmorphism.
17. **Eyes**: open spontaneously when the baby is held upright and gently rocked; look for discharge, swelling, subconjunctival haemorrhage (benign), coloboma, cloudy cornea; **red reflex** (see section F).
18. **Ears**: position (top of ear in line with the outer canthus), shape, **pre-auricular tags/pits** (consider hearing screening; renal anomalies if with other features).
19. **Nose**: patency of each nostril (watch misting on a cold surface or listen; choanal atresia → cyanosis at rest that improves with crying).
20. **Mouth**: **inspect the palate with a torch and tongue depressor and palpate with a clean gloved finger** (assessing suck at the same time); cleft lip/palate, **Epstein pearls** (benign), natal teeth, tongue-tie, thrush, micrognathia (Pierre Robin).
21. **Neck**: lumps (cystic hygroma, sternomastoid tumour, goitre), webbing, skin folds; **clavicles** (run fingers along: crepitus, step, swelling = fracture).
22. **Chest**: shape, symmetry, nipples (supernumerary, breast engorgement is normal); **RR, recession**; auscultate both sides (air entry, crackles).
23. **Heart**: apex position (right-sided in dextrocardia/pneumothorax), **HR and rhythm**, heart sounds, **murmurs** (many soft systolic murmurs in the first days are innocent/transitional, but any murmur with cyanosis, poor feeding, tachypnoea or weak pulses is serious); **femoral pulses** (and brachial-femoral comparison); **pulse oximetry** right hand and a foot where available.
24. **Abdomen**: shape (distended or scaphoid: diaphragmatic hernia), **liver** (normal edge up to 2 cm below the costal margin), spleen tip, kidneys (balloting), masses, **umbilical cord** (**2 arteries and 1 vein**; single umbilical artery → look for renal/other anomalies), cord stump (clean, dry, chlorhexidine), hernias (umbilical hernias are common and usually close by 2-5 years).
25. **Groins**: inguinal hernia, femoral pulses (if not already).
26. **Genitalia (male)**: penis length, **meatus position** (hypospadias, epispadias: **do not circumcise**), foreskin, **both testes in the scrotum** (palpate from groin down), hydrocele (transilluminates, usually resolves), scrotal swelling.
27. **Genitalia (female)**: labia, clitoris size, vaginal opening; **white discharge or small withdrawal bleed** is normal (maternal oestrogen). **Ambiguous genitalia** (clitoromegaly, fused labia, impalpable gonads) = possible **congenital adrenal hyperplasia**: do not assign sex, check glucose and electrolytes, urgent senior referral.
28. **Anus**: **patency and position**; ask whether meconium has passed (a fistula can pass meconium yet still be abnormal: look at the position).
29. **Limbs**: count **fingers and toes**, look for **polydactyly** (postaxial extra digit on the little-finger side is common and often familial in African babies), syndactyly, **palmar creases**, **talipes** (positional talipes corrects fully with gentle passive movement; structural club foot does not → refer for Ponseti casting), limb length, movement of each limb, **brachial plexus palsy** (waiter's tip), fractures.
30. **Hips**: Barlow and Ortolani (section G).
31. **Back and spine**: turn the baby prone, supported over your hand (ventral suspension also tests tone); inspect the whole spine for **dimples** (especially above the natal cleft or deep with no visible base), **hair tufts, haemangiomas, lipomas, sinuses**, scoliosis, **open neural tube defects** (myelomeningocele: cover with sterile saline-soaked gauze, nurse prone, refer).
32. **Skin**: jaundice extent, pallor, bruising, petechiae, rashes, pustules; **birthmarks** (Mongolian blue spots: grey-blue patches over the sacrum/buttocks, benign, document them), stork marks, port-wine stain, café-au-lait macules (6 or more = neurofibromatosis), haemangioma (usually appears after birth), **erythema toxicum** (blotchy red with yellow-white centres, day 1-3, baby well), **milia**, **miliaria** (heat rash), peeling (post-term).

### E. Neurological examination and primitive reflexes

33. **Alertness and state**: alert, sleepy, irritable, lethargic.
34. **Tone**: posture; **pull-to-sit** (head lag: term babies show some head lag but should not be floppy); **ventral suspension** (term baby holds head briefly in line with the trunk, limbs flexed); scarf sign.
35. **Primitive reflexes** (do Moro last because it makes the baby cry):

| Reflex | How to elicit | Normal response | Disappears by | Abnormal if |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rooting** | Stroke the cheek near the mouth | Turns head toward the stimulus, opens mouth | About **4 months** | Absent: CNS depression, prematurity |
| **Sucking** | Clean finger on the palate | Strong rhythmic suck | About **4 months** (becomes voluntary) | Weak/absent: prematurity, sepsis, HIE, tetanus (trismus) |
| **Palmar grasp** | Press a finger into the palm | Fingers flex and grip | **3-6 months** | Absent in one hand: Klumpke palsy; persistent: CP |
| **Plantar grasp** | Press the sole below the toes | Toes curl | **9-12 months** | Absent: spinal lesion |
| **Moro (startle)** | Support head and back, let the head drop about 1-2 cm suddenly (or pull slightly off the bed and release) | Arms **abduct and extend**, hands open, then **adduct** (embrace) with a cry | About **4-6 months** | **Asymmetric**: **Erb's palsy, clavicle/humerus fracture**; **absent**: severe CNS depression, HIE; **persistent**: CP |
| **Stepping/placing** | Hold upright with soles touching the surface | Stepping movements | About **2 months** | |
| **Asymmetric tonic neck reflex (ATNR)** | Turn the head to one side (supine) | "Fencing" posture: arm and leg extend on the face side, flex on the occiput side | About **6 months** | Obligatory or persistent: CP |
| **Galant (trunk incurvation)** | Stroke along one side of the spine (prone) | Trunk curves toward the stroked side | About **4-6 months** | Absent: spinal cord lesion |

### F. Red reflex

36. Dim the room; set the ophthalmoscope to **0** dioptres; stand **about 30-50 cm** away; shine the light into both pupils at once (and each eye separately).
37. **Normal**: a **symmetrical red or orange-red glow** in both pupils (it can look darker/orange in babies with more pigmented fundi: compare the two sides).
38. **Abnormal**: **absent or dull reflex**, **white pupil (leukocoria)**, dark spots, or **asymmetry** → **cataract, retinoblastoma**, glaucoma, vitreous haemorrhage, retinal detachment → **urgent ophthalmology referral** (UK NIPE: seen within 2 weeks; leukocoria same week).

### G. Hip examination (DDH)

39. Baby **supine, relaxed** (after a feed is ideal), on a firm surface, nappy removed; examine **one hip at a time**, stabilising the pelvis with the other hand.
40. **Barlow test (provocation)**: hip and knee flexed to 90 degrees, hip **adducted**; apply **gentle downward (posterior) pressure** through the knee. A palpable "clunk" as the femoral head **slips out** of the acetabulum = **dislocatable hip**.
41. **Ortolani test (relocation)**: from the same position, **abduct** the hip while **lifting the greater trochanter forward** with the fingers. A "clunk" as the femoral head **slides back in** = **dislocated, reducible hip**.
42. Look also for **limited abduction**, asymmetric thigh/gluteal skin folds, **leg-length discrepancy** (Galeazzi sign: knees at different heights with hips and knees flexed). Soft "clicks" without movement are usually ligamentous and not significant.
43. **Risk factors** for DDH: **breech** presentation (especially after 36 weeks), **family history** in a first-degree relative, **female**, **first-born**, **oligohydramnios**, large baby, **tight swaddling with hips extended** (a traditional practice in some communities).

### H. Gestational age (New Ballard score)

44. Score **6 neuromuscular signs**: posture, square window (wrist), arm recoil, popliteal angle, scarf sign, heel-to-ear.
45. Score **6 physical signs**: skin, lanugo, plantar surface (creases), breast, eye/ear, genitals.
46. Each sign scores **-1 to 5**; total **-10 to 50**. **Gestation (weeks) = (2 × score + 120) ÷ 5.**

| Total score | Weeks |
|---|---|
| -10 | 20 |
| 0 | 24 |
| 10 | 28 |
| 20 | 32 |
| 25 | 34 |
| 30 | 36 |
| 35 | 38 |
| 40 | 40 |
| 45 | 42 |
| 50 | 44 |

Best done within **12-20 hours** (valid to about 2 weeks; neuromuscular items are unreliable in sick or sedated babies). Accuracy about ± 2 weeks.

| Feature | Preterm (about 32 weeks) | Term |
|---|---|---|
| Skin | Thin, red, smooth, visible veins | Thick, pale, cracking/peeling, few vessels |
| Lanugo | Abundant | Thinning, bald areas |
| Plantar creases | Faint red marks, anterior transverse crease only | Creases over the whole sole |
| Breast | Flat areola, no bud or 1-2 mm bud | Full areola, 5-10 mm bud |
| Ear | Soft, slow recoil | Formed, firm, instant recoil |
| Genitals (male) | Testes in upper canal, few rugae | Testes down, good rugae |
| Genitals (female) | Prominent clitoris, small labia minora | Labia majora cover minora and clitoris |
| Posture | Arms and legs more extended | Fully flexed |
| Square window | 45-60 degrees | 0 degrees |
| Scarf sign | Elbow passes midline | Elbow does not reach midline |

### I. Finish

47. Dress and wrap the baby, return to the mother (skin-to-skin), **observe a breastfeed** (attachment, effective suckling: UCG).
48. **Explain findings** to the mother in plain words; address concerns.
49. **Document** everything (including normal findings and benign birthmarks) and plan follow-up.
50. **Check routine care**: vitamin K, tetracycline eye ointment, chlorhexidine cord care, **BCG and OPV0** before discharge (hepatitis B birth dose where introduced), HIV prophylaxis (NVP or AZT + NVP) for HIV-exposed babies, risk-based antibiotics, **danger-sign counselling** and the **3-7-day** review.

## Clinical acumen

### Focused history (what to find out before you touch the baby)

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Gestation (LNMP, early scan)** | Expected size and maturity; compare with Ballard |
| **Maternal illness**: diabetes, hypertension, HIV (VL), syphilis, HBsAg, TB, fever | Hypoglycaemia, SGA, infection risk, prophylaxis |
| **Drugs in pregnancy/labour**: anticonvulsants, opioids, MgSO4 | Malformations, depressed baby |
| **Liquor volume**: oligohydramnios (renal anomalies, DDH, lung hypoplasia), polyhydramnios (oesophageal atresia, GI obstruction) | Directs the exam |
| **Labour**: ROM duration, maternal fever, meconium, breech, instrumental delivery, shoulder dystocia | Sepsis risk, DDH, birth injury |
| **Resuscitation and Apgar** | HIE risk |
| **Family history**: DDH, cleft, heart disease, deafness, sickle cell, early childhood eye problems | Targeted screening |
| **Feeding since birth, urine and meconium passed** | Obstruction, feeding problems |
| **Mother's concerns** | Mothers often notice the problem first |

### Examination: what matters most

Danger signs, vital signs and measurements first; then the "**easy-to-miss five**": **palate**, **red reflex**, **hips**, **femoral pulses**, **anus/spine**.

### Red flags

> [!REDFLAG]
> - **Any IMNCI danger sign**: not feeding, convulsions, RR 60 or more or below 30, grunting, severe indrawing, central cyanosis, temperature above 37.5 or below 35.5 C, floppy or no movement
> - **Jaundice in the first 24 h**, or yellow palms/soles
> - **Absent or white red reflex**
> - **Absent femoral pulses**, SpO2 below 95% or a difference above 3% between hand and foot, cyanosis, a loud or harsh murmur
> - **Bilious vomiting**, abdominal distension, **no meconium by 48 h**, imperforate anus
> - **Bulging or very large fontanelle**, OFC above +2 SD or below -2 SD
> - **Ambiguous genitalia** (possible salt-losing CAH)
> - **Open neural tube defect**, gastroschisis/omphalocele
> - **Excessive drooling and choking with feeds** (oesophageal atresia: pass an orogastric tube)
> - **Weight loss above 10%** or a mother worried that the baby "is not right"

### Thinking like a clinician

- **A "normal" exam in a baby who is not feeding is not normal.** Feeding problems are the commonest early sign of sepsis, hypoglycaemia, HIE and malformations.
- **Use the history to target the exam**: polyhydramnios → pass a tube to exclude oesophageal atresia; breech → careful hip exam; diabetic mother → glucose, heart, sacrum (caudal regression); instrumental delivery → scalp and facial nerve.
- **Peripheral cyanosis (hands and feet) is normal in the first days; central cyanosis (tongue) is never normal.**
- **Distinguish benign from serious skin findings**: Mongolian spots vs bruises (spots are present at birth, uniform, non-tender, do not change quickly); erythema toxicum vs pustular infection (well baby, centres sterile, changes location).
- **One anomaly → look for more** (e.g. single umbilical artery → kidneys; ear tags → hearing and kidneys; Down syndrome features → heart (AVSD), duodenal atresia, hypothyroidism).
- **Re-examine at 6 weeks**: hips and testes can change, cataracts may become visible, murmurs appear as pulmonary pressure falls.

## Clinical workup

For a skill topic, the "workup" is the pre-examination preparation and the post-examination checks.

### Step 0: First 5 minutes (at birth or on receiving a baby)

1. **Breathing or crying?** If not, start HBB resuscitation (see Neonatal Resuscitation).
2. **Dry, warm, skin-to-skin**, cover the head.
3. **Quick look**: colour, tone, obvious malformation (cleft, NTD, abdominal wall defect, limb anomaly, ambiguous genitalia).
4. **Danger signs** present? → act (oxygen, glucose, antibiotics, SCBU).
5. **Breastfeed within 1 hour**.
6. **Vitamin K 1 mg IM** (0.5 mg preterm/LBW per local protocol), **tetracycline eye ointment**, **chlorhexidine** to the cord, identification band.

### Step 1: Focused history

- [ ] Maternal records reviewed (HIV/VL, syphilis, HBsAg, blood group, diabetes, hypertension, fever).
- [ ] Gestation by best dates.
- [ ] Liquor, labour, delivery mode, resuscitation, Apgar.
- [ ] Feeds, urine, meconium since birth.
- [ ] Family history; mother's concerns.

### Step 2: Focused examination

- [ ] Vitals and measurements, plotted.
- [ ] Head-to-toe with the "easy-to-miss five" (palate, red reflex, hips, femorals, anus/spine).
- [ ] Neuro: tone, alertness, reflexes.
- [ ] Ballard if gestation uncertain.
- [ ] Observe a breastfeed.

### Step 3: Bedside tests

| Test | Indication | Normal / action |
|---|---|---|
| **Glucose** | At risk (preterm, SGA, LGA/IDM, asphyxia, sepsis, hypothermia, poor feeding) | Treat below **2.6 mmol/L** |
| **Pulse oximetry** | All babies where available (CCHD screening), and any with cyanosis/distress | **Pass**: 95% or more in right hand or foot with difference 3% or less; **fail**: below 90%; 90-94% or difference above 3% → repeat once, then echo/senior review |
| **Transcutaneous bilirubin** | Visible jaundice after 24 h | Plot; serum if high |
| **Orogastric tube** | Polyhydramnios, frothy secretions, choking | Tube coils in the oesophagus (on X-ray) = oesophageal atresia |
| **Transillumination** | Scrotal swelling, suspected pneumothorax | Hydrocele transilluminates |

### Step 4: Laboratory & imaging

| Test | When | Expected / abnormal finding | How it changes management | Availability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hip ultrasound** | Positive Barlow/Ortolani, or risk factors (breech, family history) | Dysplasia/dislocation (Graf classification) | Pavlik harness; orthopaedic follow-up | Regional/national referral, private |
| **Echocardiography** | Murmur with symptoms, failed oximetry, weak femorals, Down syndrome | Structural heart disease | Prostaglandin/surgery referral | National referral (Uganda Heart Institute), some regional |
| **Renal ultrasound** | Single umbilical artery with other anomalies, ear tags with other features, abdominal mass | Renal anomalies | Urology/nephrology | Most hospitals |
| **Spinal ultrasound** | Atypical sacral dimple, tuft, haemangioma | Tethered cord | Neurosurgery (CURE Hospital Mbale, Mulago) | Referral hospitals |
| **Karyotype/genetics** | Dysmorphism (e.g. Down syndrome), ambiguous genitalia | Trisomy 21, DSD | Counselling, CAH management | Limited (Kampala/private) |
| **Glucose, electrolytes, 17-OH-progesterone** | Ambiguous genitalia | Salt-wasting CAH | Hydrocortisone, fluids | Limited |
| **Hearing screen (OAE)** | Risk factors (meningitis, severe jaundice, gentamicin, ear anomalies, family history) | Refer result | Audiology | Few centres |
| **Thyroid function** | Prolonged jaundice, Down syndrome, features of hypothyroidism | Low T4, high TSH | Levothyroxine | Referral hospitals |

### Step 5: Putting it together

**Model summary line (normal):** "A **20-hour-old** term (39 weeks by early scan; Ballard 38) male, birth weight **3.25 kg (AGA)**, OFC 34.5 cm, length 50 cm, born by SVD after an uncomplicated labour, mother HIV-negative, RPR negative, HBsAg negative. He is breastfeeding 8 times in 24 h with good attachment, has passed urine and meconium. Examination is normal: RR 44, HR 136, T 36.9 C, SpO2 98% right hand and 97% foot, palate intact, red reflexes present and symmetrical, hips stable, femoral pulses normal, anus patent, spine intact, Mongolian spots over the sacrum documented. Vitamin K, eye ointment, chlorhexidine, BCG and OPV0 given. Plan: discharge after 24 h with danger-sign counselling and 3-7-day review."

**Worked example (Ballard):** a baby whose LNMP is unknown scores **25** → (2 × 25 + 120) ÷ 5 = **34 weeks**. If she weighs 1.6 kg, plot at 34 weeks: about the 10th centile → borderline SGA; she needs KMC, feeding support and glucose monitoring.

## Management

The "management" of the newborn examination is **what you do with what you find**.

### 1. Abnormal findings: action table

| Finding | Likely meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Any **danger sign** | Sepsis, hypoglycaemia, hypothermia, HIE, CHD | Stabilise; **ampicillin + gentamicin**; glucose; warmth; SCBU/referral |
| **Jaundice day 1** / palms and soles yellow | Haemolysis, sepsis | **TSB urgently**, phototherapy per thresholds |
| **Weight below 2.5 kg** | LBW | KMC, feeding support; **below 1.5 kg or below 32 weeks: refer** (UCG) |
| **Absent/abnormal red reflex** | Cataract, retinoblastoma | **Urgent ophthalmology referral** (e.g. Mulago, Ruharo, Mengo eye units) |
| **Positive Barlow/Ortolani**, limited abduction | DDH | Ultrasound and **orthopaedic referral** (CoRSU, Mulago, regional ortho); avoid tight swaddling; Pavlik harness if confirmed |
| **Breech or family history, normal exam** | DDH risk | Hip ultrasound at about 6 weeks (where available) and careful 6-week exam |
| **Club foot (structural)** | Congenital talipes equinovarus | Refer early for **Ponseti casting** (clubfoot clinics at CoRSU, Mulago and many regional referral hospitals) |
| **Positional talipes** | Intrauterine moulding | Reassure; passive stretching |
| **Cleft lip/palate** | Feeding problem | Support breastfeeding or **cup/spoon EBM** (UCG); refer to cleft surgery (CoRSU, Mulago); check for other anomalies |
| **Absent femoral pulses / failed oximetry / cyanosis** | Duct-dependent CHD, coarctation | Senior review, oxygen cautiously, **echo**, refer (Uganda Heart Institute) |
| **Imperforate anus / no meconium by 48 h / bilious vomiting** | Anorectal malformation, Hirschsprung, malrotation | **Nil by mouth**, NGT on free drainage, IV fluids, urgent surgical referral |
| **Undescended testis** | Cryptorchidism | Recheck at 6-8 weeks; refer for orchidopexy by **6-12 months** if not descended (bilateral impalpable with hypospadias = DSD, urgent) |
| **Hypospadias** | Urethral meatus on the underside | **Do not circumcise**; refer to paediatric surgery |
| **Ambiguous genitalia** | CAH, DSD | Do not assign sex; glucose, electrolytes; urgent referral |
| **Open neural tube defect** | Myelomeningocele | Cover with sterile saline-soaked gauze, nurse prone, avoid latex, **refer** (CURE Children's Hospital Mbale); folic acid counselling for the next pregnancy |
| **Sacral dimple** (deep, above natal cleft, with tuft/haemangioma) | Occult spinal dysraphism | Spinal ultrasound; neurosurgery |
| **Gastroschisis/omphalocele** | Abdominal wall defect | Cover with cling film/sterile bag, keep warm, NGT, IV fluids, urgent surgical referral |
| **Down syndrome features** | Trisomy 21 | Sensitive disclosure with both parents; echo; thyroid; feeding support; refer |
| **Asymmetric Moro / limp arm** | Erb's palsy, clavicle fracture | See Common Neonatal Disorders (birth injuries) |
| **Large tense fontanelle, fast-growing OFC** | Hydrocephalus, meningitis | Senior review; cranial US; LP if infection suspected |
| **Polydactyly (postaxial, narrow stalk)** | Often familial, isolated | Refer for removal; avoid tying off at home with thread unless your unit's protocol supports it |
| **Natal teeth (loose)** | Aspiration risk | Dental review |
| **Tongue-tie with feeding problems** | Ankyloglossia | Assess feeding; frenotomy if feeding affected |

### 2. Benign findings: reassure and document

| Finding | Explanation to the mother |
|---|---|
| **Mongolian blue spots** | Normal pigment, very common in African babies, fade over years; **record them** |
| **Erythema toxicum** | Harmless rash in the first days; comes and goes |
| **Milia** | Tiny white spots on the nose; disappear |
| **Epstein pearls** | White cysts on the palate; disappear |
| **Breast engorgement / "witch's milk"** | Mother's hormones; **do not squeeze** (abscess risk) |
| **Vaginal discharge/small bleed** | Mother's hormones; settles in days |
| **Subconjunctival haemorrhage** | From birth pressure; clears in 1-2 weeks |
| **Caput, moulding** | Settle in 1-3 days |
| **Umbilical hernia** | Common; usually closes by 2-5 years; no coins or binders |
| **Acrocyanosis** | Blue hands and feet in the first days; keep warm |
| **Hydrocele** | Fluid around the testis; usually resolves by 1 year |

### 3. Communication and documentation

- Use plain, honest language; deliver unexpected findings (e.g. Down syndrome, cleft) **with both parents present** where possible, in private, with a senior.
- Do not blame the mother; explain the next steps and referral clearly; write a referral letter.
- Document **measurements with centiles**, **all systems** including normal findings, and **benign marks**; record vaccines and prophylaxis given.

### 4. Discharge checklist (UCG)

- **At least 24 h old**, feeding well (8 or more feeds/24 h), temperature normal, no danger signs.
- Do **not** plan early discharge for **small (LBW/preterm)** babies or babies not feeding well.
- **BCG and OPV0** given; HIV prophylaxis started if exposed; vitamin K and eye ointment done.
- Counsel on **danger signs** (refusal to feed, excessive crying, cord bleeding, fever, bulging fontanelle, abdominal distension, grunting, yellow palms/soles), **cord care**, **warmth**, **exclusive breastfeeding**, **sleeping under a treated net**.
- **Next visit at 3-7 days**; immunisation at **6 weeks**.

## Complications

The examination itself is safe if done gently; risks and pitfalls include:
- **Hypothermia** from a long exam with the baby uncovered.
- **Hip injury** from forceful or repeated Barlow/Ortolani.
- **Infection** from unwashed hands or equipment.
- **Missed diagnoses**: posterior cleft palate (looked but did not feel), cataract/retinoblastoma (no red reflex check), coarctation (femorals not felt), imperforate anus (not inspected), DDH (hips not examined), sepsis (danger signs not checked).
- **False reassurance** when a mother's concern is dismissed.
- **Mislabelling** of Mongolian spots as bruises (child-protection harm) when not documented.

## Ward-round & exam pearls

### Model presentation

"**Baby boy of Nabirye**, **18 hours old**, born at **38 weeks** by early scan (Ballard 36-38) by SVD after a 9-hour labour, ROM 4 hours, clear liquor, **Apgar 8 and 10**; no resuscitation. Birth weight **3.1 kg (50th centile, AGA)**, OFC **34 cm (25th centile)**, length **49 cm**.

Mother is a 24-year-old para 2, **HIV-positive on TLD with a suppressed viral load (below 50 copies) at 34 weeks**, RPR negative, HBsAg negative, blood group O positive. The baby is **low-risk HIV-exposed** and received **NVP 15 mg** at 2 hours; plan **NVP daily for 6 weeks** and **DNA PCR at 6 weeks**. He is **exclusively breastfeeding**, 7 feeds so far, good attachment; passed urine and meconium.

On examination he is pink, active, with a normal cry; RR 46, HR 140, T 36.8 C, SpO2 98% hand and 97% foot. Anterior fontanelle 2 × 2 cm, flat. Palate intact on inspection and palpation. **Red reflexes present and symmetrical.** Heart sounds normal, no murmur, **femoral pulses normal**. Abdomen soft, 3 cord vessels, stump clean with chlorhexidine. Both testes descended, meatus normal, anus patent. **Hips stable** on Barlow and Ortolani. Spine intact. **Mongolian spots** over the sacrum, documented. Normal tone; rooting, suck, grasp and symmetrical Moro present.

**Vitamin K, tetracycline eye ointment, BCG and OPV0** given. **Assessment:** healthy term AGA HIV-exposed infant. **Plan:** discharge after 24 h, counsel on danger signs and exclusive breastfeeding, NVP adherence, review at 3-7 days, 6-week immunisation and DNA PCR."

### Viva questions (with answers)

1. **What is the normal RR and HR of a newborn?** RR 30-60/min; HR 100-160/min.
2. **How do you perform the Ortolani test, and what does a positive test mean?** Flex hip and knee to 90 degrees, abduct while lifting the greater trochanter; a clunk as the head relocates = a dislocated but reducible hip.
3. **Why examine the red reflex?** To detect cataract and retinoblastoma early; absent or white reflex needs urgent referral.
4. **When does the Moro reflex disappear and what does an asymmetric Moro suggest?** About 4-6 months; brachial plexus palsy or clavicle/humerus fracture.
5. **How do you calculate gestation from the New Ballard score?** (2 × score + 120) ÷ 5.
6. **Why feel the femoral pulses?** To detect coarctation of the aorta or other left-sided obstructive lesions.
7. **Name five benign findings in a newborn.** Mongolian spots, erythema toxicum, milia, Epstein pearls, breast engorgement (also vaginal discharge, subconjunctival haemorrhage, umbilical hernia).

### OSCE tips

- Talk to the mother throughout; keep the baby warm; wash hands at the start and end.
- Do the **hips and Moro last** (they make the baby cry).
- **State normal findings aloud** ("palate intact on inspection and palpation; red reflexes present bilaterally").
- End with a **one-line summary and plan**.

## Mnemonics & memory aids

**Order of the exam: "Look, Measure, Head-to-Toe, Turn, Test"**: look (colour, breathing, posture), measure (weight, OFC, length, temperature), head-to-toe (supine), turn (back and spine), test (reflexes, hips last).

**Easy-to-miss checks: "Please Remember Five Hidden Abnormalities, Seriously"**: **P**alate, **R**ed reflex, **F**emoral pulses, **H**ips, **A**nus, **S**pine.

**Reflex disappearance: "2-4-6-12"**: stepping by **2** months, rooting/sucking by **4**, Moro/ATNR by about **6**, plantar grasp by **12** months.

**Barlow vs Ortolani: "Barlow = Bye-bye (out), Ortolani = Only back In"**.

### Classic exam traps

- **"Palate looks fine on inspection, so it is intact."** → You must also **palpate** (and see the back with a torch): posterior/submucous clefts are missed.
- **"Bluish patches on the buttocks: suspect abuse."** → Usually **Mongolian spots**: present at birth, benign; **document** them.
- **"Blue hands and feet at 6 hours: cyanotic heart disease."** → **Acrocyanosis** is normal; check the **tongue** for central cyanosis and do oximetry.
- **"A click on hip examination = DDH."** → Soft ligamentous clicks are common; the significant finding is a **clunk** of dislocation/relocation or limited abduction.
- **"Absent red reflex can wait for the 6-week check."** → Wrong. **Refer urgently** (retinoblastoma, cataract).
- **"He passed meconium, so the anus is normal."** → A fistula can pass meconium; **inspect the anal position and patency**.
- **"Circumcise to correct hypospadias."** → Never circumcise: the foreskin is needed for repair.
- **"Squeeze the swollen breasts to remove the milk."** → Wrong: causes mastitis/abscess.

### Questions seniors ask

1. **"Is the baby the right size for gestation?"** Give weight, OFC and length with centiles and the Ballard estimate.
2. **"Did you check the palate, red reflex, hips, femorals and anus?"** Say yes, and how.
3. **"What is the mother's HIV status and what prophylaxis has the baby received?"** VL, risk category, NVP ± AZT dose, PCR plan.
4. **"Has the baby fed, passed urine and meconium?"** Times and number of feeds.
5. **"What benign findings did you note?"** e.g. Mongolian spots, erythema toxicum, documented.
6. **"Can this baby go home today?"** Over 24 h, feeding well, normal exam, vaccines and prophylaxis given, mother counselled, follow-up booked.
7. **"What would make you refer?"** Danger signs, absent red reflex, unstable hip, absent femorals/failed oximetry, imperforate anus, ambiguous genitalia, NTD, cleft with feeding failure.
