## In a nutshell

**Infant and young child feeding (IYCF)** covers how babies and toddlers are fed from birth to 2 years: **breastfeeding** and **complementary feeding**. It is the cheapest and most powerful child-survival intervention we have. Optimal breastfeeding alone could prevent about **400,000 under-five deaths a year** worldwide (WHO), mostly from diarrhoea and pneumonia, and poor complementary feeding is the main reason Ugandan children become **stunted** between 6 and 24 months.

The rules are short: **breastfeed within 1 hour**, **only breast milk for 6 months**, **add the right foods at 6 months** (thick, energy-dense, diverse, with animal-source foods every day), **keep breastfeeding to 2 years or beyond**, **feed more during and after illness**, and **feed responsively**.

> [!PEARL]
> The one thing never to forget: **when a mother says "my milk is not enough", look at a feed before you believe it.** In almost every case the baby is poorly attached or fed too rarely. Fix attachment and frequency and the supply follows. Reaching for formula or "glucose water" is how exclusive breastfeeding ends.

## Definitions & classification

| Term | Definition (WHO/UNICEF) |
|---|---|
| **Early initiation of breastfeeding** | Putting the newborn to the breast **within 1 hour** of birth |
| **Exclusive breastfeeding (EBF)** | Only breast milk (including expressed or wet-nurse milk); **ORS, drops and syrups** of vitamins, minerals or medicines allowed. **No** water, other liquids or foods |
| **Predominant breastfeeding** | Breast milk plus water or water-based drinks (not other milks or food) |
| **Mixed feeding** | Breast milk plus other milk or foods **before 6 months** |
| **Pre-lacteal feeds** | Anything given before breastfeeding starts (sugar water, glucose, herbs, cow's milk): harmful |
| **Complementary feeding** | Giving other foods and fluids **in addition to breast milk** from 6 months |
| **Replacement feeding** | Feeding a child who receives **no** breast milk with a diet that provides all needs |
| **Breast-milk substitute (BMS)** | Any food marketed as a partial or total replacement for breast milk (infant formula, follow-up formula, growing-up milks, and baby bottles and teats under the Code) |
| **Minimum dietary diversity (MDD)** | **5 or more of 8 food groups** in the previous day: (1) breast milk, (2) grains, roots, tubers and plantains, (3) pulses, nuts and seeds, (4) dairy, (5) flesh foods (meat, fish, poultry, organ meat), (6) eggs, (7) vitamin A-rich fruits and vegetables, (8) other fruits and vegetables |
| **Minimum meal frequency (MMF)** | **2** meals (breastfed 6–8 months), **3** (breastfed 9–23 months), **4** (non-breastfed 6–23 months, including at least 2 milk feeds) |
| **Minimum acceptable diet (MAD)** | Meets both MDD and MMF |
| **Responsive feeding** | Feeding that responds to the child's hunger and fullness cues, encourages self-feeding and is patient and interactive (WHO 2023) |

## Epidemiology & Uganda context

- Globally **fewer than one-third (30.8%)** of children aged 6–23 months meet minimum dietary diversity, and only about half get enough meals (WHO fact sheet, 2018–2024 data).
- **Uganda**: about **66%** of babies are put to the breast within 1 hour and about **66%** of infants under 6 months are exclusively breastfed; continued breastfeeding falls to about **43% at 2 years**; only about **15%** of children 6–23 months receive a **minimum acceptable diet** (UDHS 2016, cited in MIYCAN 2021). Facility data for FY 2022/23 show **92%** initiation within 1 hour among facility births, but home births lag.
- **Stunting (26%, UDHS 2022)** rises steeply from 6 to 24 months: the complementary-feeding window.
- **Common Ugandan practices that harm**: discarding **colostrum**, **pre-lacteal feeds** (sugar water, glucose, herbs), giving **water or cow's milk** in the first months, **thin porridge** as the main complementary food, sharing a plate with siblings, **stopping breastfeeding abruptly** when the mother becomes pregnant, bottles, and early return to work without support.
- **Uganda policy**: the MoH **Maternal, Infant, Young Child and Adolescent Nutrition (MIYCAN)** guidelines and training (2021), **BFHI** in maternity units, the national **Regulations on Marketing of Infant and Young Child Foods**, and growth monitoring and promotion through the child health card.

> [!UGANDA]
> On the ward and in clinic you will mostly meet **grandmothers and older siblings** feeding children while mothers work in the garden or market, so **counsel whoever actually feeds the child**. Maternity leave is short and informal-sector mothers often have none; teach **expressing and cup-feeding** early. Locally available, affordable foods for good complementary feeding include **silverfish (mukene/omena) powder, eggs, beans and groundnut paste (ebinyebwa), sim-sim, dark green leaves (dodo/amaranth, nakati, cassava leaves), orange-fleshed sweet potato, pumpkin, pawpaw, mango, avocado** and **millet or maize porridge enriched with groundnut paste, milk or oil**.

## Physiology of lactation

### Hormones and reflexes

| Hormone | Trigger | Action | Practical lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prolactin** (anterior pituitary) | **Suckling**; higher at **night** | Milk **production** for the next feed | Frequent feeds, including at night, make more milk |
| **Oxytocin** (posterior pituitary) | Suckling, seeing/hearing/thinking of the baby | **Let-down (milk ejection)**: myoepithelial cells contract; also uterine contraction ("afterpains") | Stress, pain and doubt **inhibit** let-down; a calm, confident mother flows better |
| **Feedback inhibitor of lactation (FIL)** | Milk left in the breast | **Reduces** production locally | **Emptying the breast** keeps supply up; engorgement shuts it down |

**Supply follows demand**: the more milk removed, the more is made. Supplements (water, formula) reduce suckling and therefore supply.

### What is in breast milk

| Component | Features |
|---|---|
| **Colostrum** (first 2–4 days, yellow, small volume) | Rich in **secretory IgA, lactoferrin, white cells, vitamin A**, growth factors; laxative (clears meconium, reduces jaundice); "the first immunisation" |
| **Transitional and mature milk** | Volume rises to about **500–800 mL/day**; about **65–70 kcal/100 mL** |
| **Foremilk vs hindmilk** | Foremilk is watery (quenches thirst); **hindmilk is fat-rich** (energy, satiety). Let the baby finish one breast before offering the other |
| **Protein** | Easily digested **whey-dominant** protein; less renal solute load than cow's milk |
| **Fat** | Long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids (DHA, ARA) for brain and retina; **lipase** aids digestion |
| **Carbohydrate** | **Lactose** and **human milk oligosaccharides** (feed protective bifidobacteria, block pathogens) |
| **Protective factors** | IgA, lactoferrin, lysozyme, macrophages, bifidus factor, anti-inflammatory factors |
| **Micronutrients** | **Iron** (low amount but highly absorbed), zinc; adequate vitamin A if mother well nourished. **Vitamin K** is low (hence vitamin K at birth); vitamin D low |
| **Water** | Enough for a baby in hot weather: **no extra water needed** under 6 months |

### Why 6 months?

Breast milk meets all energy and nutrient needs for about 6 months. After that, needs for **energy, iron, zinc and vitamin A** exceed what breast milk provides; the infant's gut, kidneys and oral-motor skills (sitting with support, chewing movements, loss of tongue-thrust) are ready. Starting earlier displaces breast milk and adds infection (contaminated food and water); starting later causes **growth faltering** and **iron deficiency**.

### The energy gap from 6 to 24 months

| Age | Energy needed from complementary foods (breastfed child, about) |
|---|---|
| 6–8 months | **200 kcal/day** |
| 9–11 months | **300 kcal/day** |
| 12–23 months | **550 kcal/day** |

A baby's stomach holds about **30 mL/kg**, so foods must be **energy-dense** (thick porridge, with oil, groundnut paste or milk added). Thin, watery porridge fills the stomach with water, not energy.

### Benefits of breastfeeding

| For the child | For the mother | For family and society |
|---|---|---|
| Fewer and less severe **diarrhoea, pneumonia, otitis media, sepsis**; less NEC in preterms | **Less PPH** (oxytocin), faster uterine involution | Free, safe, always available |
| Lower infant mortality, including SIDS | **Lactational amenorrhoea** (LAM, > 98% protection when all 3 criteria met) | Less spending on formula and health care |
| Higher IQ scores | Lower risk of **breast and ovarian cancer** and **type 2 diabetes** | Environmentally clean |
| Less **obesity and diabetes** later | Bonding, convenience | |

## Clinical acumen

### Focused history (feeding history)

| Ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **When was the baby first put to the breast?** Pre-lacteal feeds? | Delayed initiation and pre-lacteals predict early stopping and infection |
| **Breastfeeding now?** How often by day and **by night**? How long? One or both breasts? | Frequency drives supply; < 8 feeds/24 h is too few for a young infant |
| **Anything else** given (water, glucose, cow's milk, formula, herbs, porridge)? From what age? | Mixed feeding before 6 months: infection risk, lower supply, HIV transmission risk |
| **Feeding utensils**: cup, bottle, spoon? How cleaned? | Bottles carry infection and interfere with suckling |
| **Wet nappies, stools**, weight gain on the card | Adequacy of intake |
| **Breast problems**: pain, cracked nipples, lumps, fever | Attachment problems, mastitis |
| **6–23 months: 24-hour recall**: what, how much, how thick, how many meals and snacks, **animal-source foods**, vegetables and fruit, oil | Energy density, diversity (MDD), frequency (MMF) |
| **Who feeds the child? Own plate? Helped to eat?** | Responsive feeding; sibling competition |
| **Feeding during illness** | Many families withhold food during diarrhoea |
| **Mother's HIV status, ART, viral load**; child's EID results | Feeding duration, infant prophylaxis, testing schedule |
| **Mother's health**: nutrition, mood (depression), work, contraception, new pregnancy | Support needs; abrupt weaning risk |
| **Immunisation, vitamin A, deworming** | Missed prevention opportunities |

### Examination

1. **Observe a breastfeed** (at least a few minutes): **positioning** (4 points) and **attachment** (4 signs), **effective suckling** (slow deep sucks with pauses, **audible swallowing**, cheeks round not drawn in), baby releases the breast and looks satisfied.
2. **Baby**: weight, length, head circumference, **plot**; hydration; jaundice; mouth (**oral thrush**, cleft palate, **tongue-tie**); tone and alertness; signs of illness.
3. **Mother's breasts** (with consent and privacy): engorgement, nipple shape (flat or inverted), **fissures**, redness, tender lump (blocked duct, mastitis), fluctuant mass (abscess), thrush.
4. **Older child**: anthropometry (WHZ, HAZ, MUAC), oedema, pallor, development (can sit? self-feed?), and **watch a meal** if possible.

### Red flags

> [!REDFLAG]
> **A baby who is not feeding well is sick until proven otherwise** ("unable to drink or breastfeed" is an IMCI danger sign). Also: **weight loss > 10% of birth weight** in the first week, not back to birth weight by **2 weeks**, **fewer than 6 wet nappies** after day 5, **lethargy or jaundice** with poor feeding (hypernatraemic dehydration, sepsis), a **flat or falling growth line**, a mother with **fever and a hot, red breast** (mastitis/abscess), and a **depressed or overwhelmed mother**.

### Thinking like a clinician

- **"Not enough milk" is usually poor attachment or infrequent feeding**, sometimes pain, a sleepy jaundiced baby, or a separated mother–baby pair. True primary low supply is rare.
- **Crying is not proof of hunger.** Check weight gain and wet nappies before suggesting supplements.
- **Sore nipples mean poor attachment** until proven otherwise (or thrush if painful after weeks of comfortable feeding).
- **A breastfed baby with frequent loose yellow stools and good weight gain is normal**, not diarrhoea.
- **At 6 months, ask "what is in the bowl?"**: thin porridge alone will not do; add oil, groundnut paste, milk, egg or fish powder.
- **The HIV question changes duration, not the method**: exclusive breastfeeding on maternal ART is the safe choice in Uganda.
- **Common student mistakes**: advising water for a hot baby under 6 months; telling a mother with mastitis to stop breastfeeding; recommending formula to an HIV-positive mother; giving the age-blind advice "feed porridge three times a day" without quantities, density or diversity; forgetting to ask who feeds the child.

## Clinical workup

For a feeding topic, the "workup" is the **feeding assessment and counselling session** (IMCI "assess the child's feeding" and "counsel the mother").

### Step 0: First 5 minutes

1. **Check general danger signs** (IMCI): unable to drink or breastfeed, vomits everything, convulsions, lethargic or unconscious. Any → treat and refer; feeding counselling can wait.
2. **Young infant** (< 2 months): temperature, jaundice, hypoglycaemia risk; **weigh**.
3. **Classify nutrition**: weight, length/height, **MUAC** (≥ 6 months), **oedema**. SAM → nutrition pathway (see the Malnutrition topic).
4. Create **privacy and a comfortable seat** for the mother; introduce yourself; ask permission to watch a feed.

### Step 1: Focused history

- [ ] **Breastfeeding**: initiation, frequency day and night, both breasts → supply and attachment problems.
- [ ] **Other foods/fluids** and age started → mixed feeding risk; counselling target.
- [ ] **Bottle use** → infection; switch to cup.
- [ ] **Output and weight gain** → adequacy.
- [ ] **Breast symptoms** → mastitis, thrush, engorgement.
- [ ] **24-hour recall (6–23 months)** → MDD, MMF, density.
- [ ] **Who feeds, own plate, responsive?** → counsel the real feeder.
- [ ] **Feeding during illness** → advice on extra feeding.
- [ ] **HIV status and ART of mother; EID of baby** → duration and testing.
- [ ] **Mother's health, work, pregnancy, mood** → support plan.

### Step 2: Focused examination

Observe a breastfeed (positioning, attachment, suckling), examine the baby (anthropometry, mouth, hydration, jaundice), examine the breasts if there is a problem, and for older children assess growth, development and a meal.

| Document these positives | And these negatives |
|---|---|
| "Observed feed: good positioning, 4/4 attachment signs, audible swallowing" | "No nipple fissures, no breast lump or redness" |
| Weight and Z-scores; trend on the card | "No oral thrush; no tongue-tie; palate intact" |
| Number of feeds and wet nappies in 24 h | "No other fluids given" |
| 24-h recall: food groups and meals | "No oedema; MUAC 13.8 cm" |

### Step 3: Bedside tests

| Test | How | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **Weight on a calibrated scale and plotting** | Undressed baby | Adequacy of intake; faltering |
| **Wet nappy count** | Ask; check a nappy | ≥ 6/day after day 5 = adequate fluid |
| **Observed breastfeed (BFHI/IMCI form)** | Watch 3–5 min | Attachment, suckling, positioning |
| **MUAC and oedema** (≥ 6 months) | Tape, press feet | Acute malnutrition |
| **RBS** in a poorly feeding young infant | Glucometer | Hypoglycaemia |
| **Bilirubin** (transcutaneous or serum) if jaundiced | | Breastfeeding vs breast-milk jaundice, pathological causes |
| **HIV rapid test** for mother (retesting) or **DNA PCR** for exposed infant | Per schedule | Feeding plan and prophylaxis |

### Step 4: Laboratory & imaging

| Test | When | Expected / abnormal finding | How it changes management |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Serum sodium** | Poorly feeding, jaundiced, lethargic newborn with weight loss > 10% | **Hypernatraemia** (> 150 mmol/L) | Breastfeeding failure dehydration: slow rehydration, intensive lactation support, temporary supplementation with EBM/formula. RRH/NRH |
| **Hb** (HemoCue) | Pallor, 6–24 months with poor diet | Anaemia (iron deficiency) | Iron-rich foods, iron supplement, deworm. HC III and above |
| **Maternal viral load** | HIV-positive breastfeeding mother: **every 3 months until breastfeeding ends** (Uganda; see HIV in Pregnancy topic) | Non-suppressed (≥ 1000 copies/mL) | Adherence support, extended infant prophylaxis, enhanced EID |
| **Infant DNA PCR** | HIV-exposed: 4–6 weeks, 9 months, 6 weeks after stopping breastfeeding; rapid test at 18 months | Positive | Start ART; continue breastfeeding to 24 months |
| **Breast ultrasound / aspiration** | Suspected abscess | Fluid collection | Needle aspiration or incision and drainage |
| **Pus culture** | Recurrent or non-responding mastitis | *S. aureus* (MRSA) | Targeted antibiotic |

### Step 5: Putting it together

**Model one-line summary**
> "A 7-week-old girl, weight 3.9 kg (birth weight 3.2 kg; WAZ −1.6, crossing down one line), breastfed 6 times a day plus water and glucose 'because of the heat', mother with cracked nipples from poor attachment, HIV-negative mother: **feeding problem (mixed feeding, infrequent feeds, poor attachment) with slow weight gain**, no danger signs."

**Problems**: (1) poor attachment causing sore nipples and ineffective milk transfer; (2) infrequent feeds; (3) water and glucose (mixed feeding); (4) slow weight gain.

**Plan**: show positioning and attachment, feed **8–12 times** in 24 h including nights, **stop water and glucose**, nipple care (expressed milk on the nipple, air-dry), reassure, **review weight in 5–7 days** (IMCI: follow up a feeding problem in 5 days, low weight in 14 days).

> [!EXAM]
> **Worked example: a 24-hour recall in a 10-month-old.**
> Yesterday: breastfed 4 times; **thin maize porridge** (cup, morning); **matooke with bean sauce** (½ cup, evening). Food groups: breast milk ✔, grains/roots/plantains ✔, pulses ✔ = **3 of 8** (MDD needs 5). Meals: 2 (MMF for a breastfed 9–23-month-old is 3). **Neither MDD nor MMF → no minimum acceptable diet.**
> Counsel: **3–4 meals + 1–2 snacks**, **½ cup per meal** (250 mL cup), make porridge **thick** and add **groundnut paste, milk or oil**; add **an animal-source food daily** (egg, silverfish powder, liver, milk), **dark green leaves and an orange fruit or vegetable daily** (dodo, pawpaw, mango, orange sweet potato). Snacks: banana, avocado, boiled egg. Give her **her own bowl** and help her eat.

## Differential diagnosis

Differentials for **"poor feeding"** and **"faltering growth"** in infancy:

| Condition | Distinguishing features | Key investigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Feeding/attachment problem** | Sore nipples, short or infrequent feeds, unsettled baby, otherwise well | Observed feed |
| **Inadequate complementary diet** | Faltering after 6 months, thin porridge, few food groups | 24-hour recall |
| **Neonatal sepsis / meningitis** | Sudden poor feeding, fever or hypothermia, lethargy, apnoea | FBC, blood culture, LP |
| **Neonatal jaundice** (sleepy, poor suck) | Yellow skin, especially day 1 (pathological) or with dehydration | Bilirubin |
| **Hypernatraemic dehydration** | Weight loss > 10%, irritability, fever, few wet nappies | Sodium |
| **Oral problems**: thrush, cleft palate, tongue-tie | Visible on inspection; nasal regurgitation with cleft | Examination |
| **Congenital heart disease** | Sweating and breathlessness with feeds, murmur | Echo |
| **Neurological disorder** (cerebral palsy, birth asphyxia) | Poor suck and swallow, abnormal tone | Neurological exam |
| **HIV infection** | Faltering, thrush, recurrent infections, lymphadenopathy | DNA PCR |
| **TB** | Faltering, cough, contact | CXR, Xpert |
| **Recurrent infections** (diarrhoea, malaria, worms) | History of repeated illness | Malaria test, stool |
| **Gastro-oesophageal reflux** | Effortless vomiting after feeds, thriving | Clinical |
| **Pyloric stenosis** | Projectile non-bilious vomiting at 2–8 weeks, hungry | Ultrasound, electrolytes |
| **Maternal depression / neglect** | Withdrawn mother, missed visits | Screen (EPDS) |

## Investigations

| Test | What you are looking for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Weight, length, MUAC (plotted) | Growth faltering, wasting, stunting | Severity and follow-up |
| Observed feed | Attachment, suckling | Commonest fixable cause |
| 24-hour recall | MDD, MMF | Specific counselling |
| Hb | Anaemia | Iron, deworming |
| Maternal HIV status and VL; infant EID | HIV exposure and infection | Feeding duration, ART |
| Sodium, bilirubin (newborn) | Dehydration, jaundice | Urgent treatment |

## Management

### A. Supporting breastfeeding from birth (labour ward and postnatal ward)

1. **Immediate skin-to-skin** after birth (vaginal or CS) for at least 1 hour; **initiate breastfeeding within 1 hour**; delay weighing and bathing.
2. **Colostrum is precious**: no pre-lacteal feeds.
3. **Rooming-in 24 hours**; **feed on demand** (responsive feeding), at least **8 times in 24 hours**, including nights; watch for early hunger cues (rooting, sucking fingers, moving lips) rather than waiting for crying.
4. Help with **positioning and attachment** at the first feeds:

| Positioning (4 points) | Attachment (4 signs) |
|---|---|
| Baby's **head and body in a straight line** | **More areola visible above** the baby's mouth than below |
| Baby held **close** to the mother's body | **Mouth wide open** |
| Baby **facing the breast**, nose opposite the nipple | **Lower lip turned outward** |
| Whole body **supported** (not just neck and shoulders) | **Chin touching the breast** |

*Effective suckling: slow deep sucks with pauses, swallowing heard, cheeks rounded. Positions: cradle, cross-cradle, football (rugby) hold (good after CS or for twins), lying down.*

5. **After CS**: skin-to-skin in theatre or recovery as soon as the mother is responsive; side-lying or football hold; help with lifting.
6. **Small and preterm babies**: **kangaroo mother care**, expressed breast milk by **cup, spoon or NG tube**, then breast as suck matures (see Neonatal topics).
7. **Teach expressing by hand** to every mother before discharge (thumb above, fingers below the areola, press back toward the chest, compress, release; rotate around the breast).
8. **Contraception**: LAM, progestogen-only methods, IUD; **avoid combined pills in the first 6 weeks** while breastfeeding (see Puerperium topic).

### B. The Baby-Friendly Hospital Initiative: Ten Steps (WHO/UNICEF 2018)

| Step | Content |
|---|---|
| **1a** | Comply fully with the **International Code of Marketing of Breast-milk Substitutes** and World Health Assembly resolutions |
| **1b** | Have a **written infant feeding policy** routinely communicated to staff and parents |
| **1c** | Establish ongoing **monitoring and data-management** systems |
| **2** | Ensure staff have sufficient **knowledge, competence and skills** to support breastfeeding |
| **3** | Discuss the importance and management of breastfeeding with **pregnant women and their families** |
| **4** | Facilitate **immediate and uninterrupted skin-to-skin** contact and support mothers to **initiate breastfeeding as soon as possible** after birth |
| **5** | Support mothers to **initiate and maintain** breastfeeding and **manage common difficulties** |
| **6** | Do not provide breastfed newborns any **food or fluids other than breast milk**, unless **medically indicated** |
| **7** | Enable mothers and infants to remain together and practise **rooming-in 24 hours a day** |
| **8** | Support mothers to recognise and respond to their infants' **cues for feeding** |
| **9** | Counsel mothers on the use and risks of **feeding bottles, teats and pacifiers** |
| **10** | Coordinate **discharge** so that parents and infants have timely access to **ongoing support and care** |

*Steps 1–2 are "critical management procedures"; steps 3–10 are "key clinical practices". The Code also bans advertising of BMS to the public, free samples to mothers, and gifts to health workers.*

> [!UGANDA]
> Uganda enforces the Code through its **Regulations on Marketing of Infant and Young Child Foods**: no free formula samples or promotion in health facilities, labels must carry warnings and preparation instructions, and facilities must **not accept donations of formula or complementary foods** from manufacturers, including during emergencies (MIYCAN 2021). As a student you must not accept gifts or sponsored materials from BMS companies.

### C. Exclusive breastfeeding 0–6 months: counselling messages

1. **Breast milk is all your baby needs**, even in hot weather; no water, glucose, herbs, juice or porridge.
2. Feed **whenever the baby wants**, day and night, **at least 8 times** in 24 hours; let the baby finish one breast (hindmilk) before offering the other.
3. Signs your baby is getting enough: **6 or more wet nappies a day**, regular stools, **gaining weight** on the card, content after feeds.
4. **Going back to work**: express milk before leaving; caregiver gives it by **cup**; breastfeed more at night and on days off. Storage (CDC): **room temperature up to 4 hours**, **fridge up to 4 days**, **freezer about 6 months**; thawed milk use within 24 h (fridge) and within 2 h once warmed; never refreeze.
5. **Medicines and illness**: most maternal illnesses and medicines (including ART, antimalarials, antibiotics, TB drugs) are compatible with breastfeeding; continue breastfeeding through the mother's and baby's illnesses.

### D. Complementary feeding from 6 months (WHO 2023, UCG 2023 IMCI)

**WHO 2023 guideline recommendations (6–23 months):**
1. **Continue breastfeeding to 2 years or beyond** (strong).
2. **Milks** for non-breastfed children: **6–11 months**: infant formula **or** (full-fat) animal milk; **12–23 months**: **animal milk**; **follow-up/growing-up formulas are not recommended**; no flavoured or sweetened milks.
3. **Introduce complementary foods at 6 months (180 days)** while breastfeeding continues (strong).
4. **Dietary diversity**: **animal-source foods (meat, fish, eggs) daily**; **fruits and vegetables daily**; **pulses, nuts and seeds frequently**; minimise starchy staples; prefer whole grains.
5. **Unhealthy foods**: no foods high in **sugar, salt or trans fats**; **no sugar-sweetened beverages**; **no non-sugar sweeteners**; **limit 100% fruit juice**.
6. **Supplements and fortified foods** where needs cannot be met (micronutrient powders, fortified cereals, small-quantity LNS) as part of counselling, not instead of a diverse diet.
7. **Responsive feeding** (strong).

**Practical feeding recommendations (UCG 2023 IMCI, 250 mL cup):**

| Age | Texture | Frequency | Amount per meal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **6–9 months** | Thick porridge; well-mashed foods | **2–3 meals** + **1–2 snacks** | Start with **2–3 tablespoons**, increase to **½ cup** |
| **9–12 months** | Finely chopped or mashed family food; finger foods | **3–4 meals** + **1–2 snacks** | **½ cup** |
| **12–24 months** | Family foods, chopped if needed | **3–4 meals** + **1–2 snacks** | **¾ cup** |
| **2 years and over** | Family foods | **3–4 meals** + **1–2 snacks** | **At least 1 full cup** |
| **Not breastfed (6–23 months)** | As above | **Add 1–2 extra meals** and **2 or more milk feeds** daily (MMF 4) | |

*Snacks in UCG: egg, banana, bread, avocado. UCG also says give a nutritious snack 3 times a day if breastfed and 5 times a day if not breastfed at 6–9 months.*

**Making a good Ugandan complementary meal ("enrich the porridge"):**
- **Thick** enough to stay on a spoon.
- Add **energy**: a teaspoon of **oil**, **groundnut or sim-sim paste**, **milk**.
- Add **protein and iron/zinc**: **egg**, **silverfish (mukene) powder**, **liver**, meat, fish, **beans** (mashed).
- Add **vitamin A-rich** foods: **orange sweet potato, pumpkin, carrots, pawpaw, mango, dark green leaves**.
- Use **iodised salt** in small amounts only for older children (no added salt is ideal under 1 year); **no sugar-sweetened drinks or sweets**.

**Hygiene**: wash hands (caregiver and child) with soap; clean utensils; feed with a **cup and spoon** (no bottles); serve food immediately after cooking or store it safely; boil or treat drinking water.

**Responsive feeding**: sit with the child, give **their own bowl**, encourage but do not force, talk and make eye contact, offer new foods repeatedly (several "tastes"), minimise distractions.

### E. Feeding during and after illness

1. **Breastfeed more often and for longer** at each feed.
2. Offer **extra fluids** (ORS in diarrhoea; see Diarrhoea topic) and **soft, favourite foods**, small amounts often.
3. **Never withhold food** during diarrhoea or fever.
4. After illness: **an extra meal a day for at least 2 weeks** (catch-up).
5. Sick HIV-exposed child: at least **2 extra meals and 1 snack** (MIYCAN 2021).

### G. HIV and infant feeding

**Principle**: with the mother on effective **ART (TLD)** and **virally suppressed**, the risk of transmission through breastfeeding is very low, while not breastfeeding greatly increases death from diarrhoea, pneumonia and malnutrition in Uganda. So **breastfeeding is recommended for all women irrespective of HIV status** (MIYCAN 2021).

1. **Mother**: lifelong **ART**, **viral load monitoring** during breastfeeding (every 3 months until breastfeeding ends, per the HIV-in-pregnancy topic), adherence support, healthy breasts (treat mastitis and cracked nipples promptly; they increase transmission).
2. **Infant**: risk-based **prophylaxis** (NVP, or AZT + NVP if high risk), **co-trimoxazole** from 6 weeks, **EID**: DNA PCR at **4–6 weeks**, **9 months**, and **6 weeks after complete cessation** of breastfeeding; rapid test at **18 months**.
3. **Feeding**:
   - **Exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months**; **no mixed feeding** under 6 months (gut inflammation lets virus cross).
   - From 6 months: **complementary foods with continued breastfeeding**.
   - **Duration**: **WHO 2016**: breastfeed for **at least 12 months**, and may continue **up to 24 months or longer** (like the general population) with full ART adherence support. **Uganda MIYCAN 2021**: for **HIV-negative exposed infants**, encourage **stopping at 12 months** and give **at least 500 mL/day of other milk** (cow, goat or soya milk); for **HIV-positive infants**, **continue breastfeeding on demand to 24 months**.
   - **Stopping**: gradually over **1–2 weeks** (MIYCAN): express and cup-feed, gradually replace with other milk; never abrupt. (UCG advises about 1 month for stopping breastfeeding in general.)
   - **Replacement feeding** only if breastfeeding is impossible and it is **acceptable, feasible, affordable, sustainable and safe**, with a guaranteed supply and safe preparation; never combine with breastfeeding.

> [!UGANDA]
> The **UCG 2023 HIV chapter still contains older wording** ("exclusive breastfeeding stopping at 3–6 months if replacement feeding is possible"). Current Ugandan practice follows the **consolidated HIV guidelines and MIYCAN 2021**: **exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months on ART, continued breastfeeding with complementary foods**, stopping at about **12 months** for HIV-negative infants (WHO allows up to 24 months), and **24 months** if the infant is HIV-positive. Do not advise formula as the default for an HIV-positive mother.

### H. Common breastfeeding problems

| Problem | Features | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **"Not enough milk" (perceived)** | Crying, frequent feeds, mother's doubt; weight gain often normal | Observe a feed, check weight and wet nappies, **improve attachment**, **feed more often** (incl. nights), stop supplements, build confidence; review in 5–7 days |
| **True low intake** | Poor weight gain, < 6 wet nappies | Correct attachment; express after feeds to increase supply; skin-to-skin; temporary supplement with **expressed milk by cup**; treat causes (pain, separation, sleepy jaundiced baby) |
| **Sore or cracked nipples** | Pain during feeds, fissures | **Correct attachment**; do not stop feeding; start on the less sore side; rub **hindmilk** on the nipple and air-dry; avoid soap and creams; if very painful, express from that side for 1–2 days and cup-feed |
| **Engorgement** | Both breasts full, hard, painful, shiny; milk not flowing | **Frequent feeding** with good attachment; **hand-express** a little to soften the areola before feeding; **warm compress before**, **cold compress after**; paracetamol; support bra |
| **Blocked duct** | Tender lump, mother well | Continue feeding (start on that side), gentle massage toward the nipple, vary positions, avoid tight clothing |
| **Mastitis** | Red, hot, tender wedge, **fever, malaise** | **Continue breastfeeding or expressing** from the affected side (essential); rest, fluids, **paracetamol or ibuprofen**; if bacterial (fissure, no improvement in 24 h, very ill): **flucloxacillin/cloxacillin 500 mg orally 6-hourly for 10–14 days** (erythromycin if penicillin-allergic) |
| **Breast abscess** | Fluctuant, very tender swelling, fever | **Needle aspiration or incision and drainage**, antibiotics, continue feeding from the other breast and resume the affected side once drained |
| **Flat or inverted nipples** | Nipple does not protrude | Good attachment matters more than nipple shape (baby suckles the areola); skin-to-skin; try positions; draw out with a syringe before feeds |
| **Thrush (candida)** | Burning nipple pain after weeks of comfortable feeds; white patches in the baby's mouth | Treat **both**: **nystatin oral suspension** for the baby (1 mL 4 times daily for 7 days) and **miconazole cream** or nystatin to the nipples |
| **Breastfeeding (suboptimal intake) jaundice** | First week, poor feeding, weight loss | More frequent effective feeds; treat dehydration; phototherapy per thresholds |
| **Breast-milk jaundice** | Well, thriving baby, jaundice beyond 2 weeks | Exclude pathological causes (conjugated bilirubin, thyroid, infection); **continue breastfeeding** |
| **Low birth weight / preterm** | Weak suck | KMC, EBM by cup or NG, breastfeed as soon as able |
| **Twins** | | Both can be exclusively breastfed; football hold, feed together or one after the other |
| **Cleft lip/palate** | | Upright position, EBM by cup or special feeder; refer for surgery (Uganda: CoRSU, Mulago) |
| **Mother separated / working** | | Expressing, cup feeding by caregiver, night breastfeeding |

### I. When is breastfeeding not advised?

**Very few** situations (WHO acceptable medical reasons; CDC):
- **Infant**: **classic galactosaemia**; some other rare metabolic diseases (e.g. maple syrup urine disease, phenylketonuria: partial breastfeeding with special formula).
- **Mother**: **Ebola virus disease** (in Uganda's outbreaks: separate and give replacement feeds); certain **drugs** (cytotoxic chemotherapy, radioactive iodine, some radiopharmaceuticals; check each drug); **herpes simplex lesions on the breast** (avoid that breast until healed); severe maternal illness preventing care (express if possible).
- **Not contraindications in Uganda**: **HIV on ART**, **hepatitis B** (baby gets birth-dose vaccine), **TB** (baby gets TB preventive therapy; mother on treatment can breastfeed), **malaria**, mastitis, most antibiotics and antimalarials, **ART**.

### J. When formula is truly needed (safe preparation)

1. Use **commercial infant formula** under 12 months (WHO 2023 allows full-fat animal milk from 6 months for non-breastfed infants).
2. **Wash hands**; **boil water** and use it while still at least **70 °C** (kills *Cronobacter*), then cool.
3. **Measure exactly**: usually **1 level scoop per 30 mL** of water (follow the tin); **never add extra scoops or sugar** (hypernatraemia, obesity) and **never dilute** (malnutrition).
4. **Feed by cup**, not bottle; **discard** any prepared milk not used within **1 hour** (UCG).
5. Approximate volume: **150 mL/kg/day** for young infants.
6. Teach and **observe** preparation; ensure a **guaranteed supply** (an interrupted formula supply is dangerous).

## Complications

Complications of **suboptimal feeding**:

| Group | Consequences |
|---|---|
| **Infant (0–6 months)** | Diarrhoea, pneumonia, otitis media, sepsis and death (mixed or no breastfeeding); hypernatraemic dehydration and jaundice (insufficient intake); higher HIV transmission with mixed feeding |
| **Young child (6–24 months)** | **Growth faltering, stunting, wasting**, micronutrient deficiencies (**iron-deficiency anaemia**, vitamin A deficiency, zinc deficiency), developmental delay |
| **Long term** | Lower school achievement and income; overweight, obesity and diabetes (especially with formula and sugary foods) |
| **Mother** | Engorgement, mastitis, abscess, early return of fertility, higher risk of breast and ovarian cancer and type 2 diabetes when not breastfeeding |
| **Formula-related** | Contamination and diarrhoea, over-concentration (hypernatraemia), over-dilution (malnutrition), cost |

## Ward-round & exam pearls

**Model case presentation:**
> "Baby Kato is a **5-month-old boy**, **birth weight 3.0 kg** at term by SVD at a HC IV. He was put to the breast **2 hours after birth** after being given **glucose water**. He is breastfed about **6 times a day**, and since 3 months has also had **water, gripe water and diluted cow's milk** by bottle because his mother returned to her market stall. He is **HIV-exposed**: mother on TLD since ANC, **last viral load 4 months ago < 50 copies/mL**; he completed NVP prophylaxis and had a **negative DNA PCR at 6 weeks**. Immunisations are **up to date to 14 weeks**. Two episodes of diarrhoea in the last month.
> **Weight 5.6 kg (WAZ −2.2), length 63 cm (LAZ −1.3, WLZ −2.0)**; his card shows the line **flattening since 3 months**. Alert, no danger signs, no thrush. Observed feed: baby's body turned away, **chin off the breast, more areola below than above**, quick shallow sucks.
> **Summary**: **mixed feeding with poor attachment and bottle use in an HIV-exposed infant**, with **growth faltering** (borderline moderate wasting) and recurrent diarrhoea. **Plan**: correct positioning and attachment, **exclusive breastfeeding** (stop water, cow's milk and bottle), **express breast milk** for the caregiver to give by **cup** while the mother is at the market, feed on demand day and night; maternal **viral load now**; **review in 5 days** and weigh again in 14 days; prepare for **complementary feeding at 6 months**; next **DNA PCR at 9 months**."

**Likely viva questions (with answers)**

1. *What does exclusive breastfeeding allow?* Breast milk only, plus ORS, drops or syrups of vitamins, minerals and medicines.
2. *Name the 4 signs of good attachment.* More areola above than below, mouth wide open, lower lip turned out, chin touching the breast.
3. *Why start complementary foods at 6 months?* Breast milk no longer meets energy, iron, zinc and vitamin A needs; the infant is developmentally ready; earlier starts add infection and displace breast milk.
4. *What does WHO 2023 say about milks for non-breastfed children?* 6–11 months: formula or animal milk; 12–23 months: animal milk; follow-up formulas not recommended.
5. *How should an HIV-positive mother in Uganda feed her baby?* On ART with viral load monitoring: EBF for 6 months, then complementary foods with continued breastfeeding (to 12 months if the infant is HIV-negative per MIYCAN; up to 24 months per WHO; 24 months if the infant is HIV-positive); no mixed feeding; stop gradually; EID 6 weeks after stopping.
6. *A mother with mastitis asks if she should stop breastfeeding.* No: continued emptying of the breast is the treatment; add analgesia and flucloxacillin/cloxacillin if bacterial.
7. *List the BFHI Ten Steps' key clinical practices.* Antenatal counselling, immediate skin-to-skin and early initiation, support to breastfeed and manage problems, no supplements unless medically indicated, rooming-in, responsive feeding, counselling on bottles/teats/pacifiers, coordinated discharge.

**OSCE tips**: the classic station is **"counsel a mother on breastfeeding"** or **"assess a breastfeed"**. Greet and sit at her level, **ask permission**, use **open questions**, **praise** what she does well (builds confidence), **observe before advising**, give **2–3 relevant suggestions** not a lecture, use the **4 positioning points and 4 attachment signs**, check understanding (teach-back), and arrange follow-up. For "counsel on complementary feeding", ask the child's age, do a **24-hour recall**, give **age-specific frequency, amount, thickness and variety**, mention **hygiene, responsive feeding and feeding during illness**.

## Mnemonics & memory aids

- **"1-6-24"**: breastfeed within **1 hour**, exclusively for **6 months**, continue to **24 months** or beyond.
- **Attachment "ALLC"**: **A**reola more above, **L**ips (lower) turned out, **L**arge (wide) open mouth, **C**hin touching.
- **Positioning "In line, Close, Facing, Supported"**.
- **Complementary feeding "FATVAH"** (WHO): **F**requency, **A**mount, **T**hickness (density), **V**ariety, **A**ctive/responsive feeding, **H**ygiene.
- **"2-3-4"** meals: 2–3 at 6–8 months, 3–4 at 9–23 months, plus 1–2 snacks.
- **Cup size**: ½ cup at 6–12 months, ¾ cup at 12–24 months, 1 cup from 2 years.
- **"Mastitis: keep milk moving."**
- **Storage "4–4–6"**: 4 hours room, 4 days fridge, 6 months freezer.

### Classic exam traps

- **"Give water to a breastfed baby in hot weather."** Wrong: breast milk contains enough water under 6 months.
- **"Stop breastfeeding because of mastitis."** Wrong: keep milk moving; it is the treatment.
- **"Formula is safer for babies of HIV-positive mothers."** Wrong in Uganda: EBF on maternal ART is recommended.
- **"Introduce porridge at 4 months because the baby cries."** Wrong: complementary foods at 6 months.
- **"Thin porridge three times a day is adequate at 9 months."** Wrong: thick, enriched, diverse foods, 3–4 meals plus snacks.
- **"Follow-up formula for a non-breastfed 15-month-old."** Wrong (WHO 2023): animal milk; follow-up formulas not recommended.
- **"Sore nipples are normal and will settle."** Wrong: they signal poor attachment.
- **"Mix-feed while the HIV-positive mother returns to work."** Wrong: exclusive breastfeeding with expressed milk by cup.

### Questions seniors ask

**This mother says she has no milk on day 2 after CS. What do you do?**
Explain that colostrum comes in small amounts and is enough; put the baby skin-to-skin and to the breast often (8–12 times), help with the football hold, hand-express colostrum and give by cup or spoon if needed; check the baby's weight, output and jaundice; avoid formula unless medically indicated.

**How do you assess a breastfeed?**
Watch for the 4 positioning points (in line, close, facing, supported), 4 attachment signs (more areola above, wide mouth, lower lip out, chin to breast) and effective suckling (slow deep sucks with pauses and swallowing).

**What foods would you recommend for a 9-month-old in rural Uganda?**
Breastfeed on demand; 3–4 meals of ½ cup plus 1–2 snacks; thick millet or maize porridge enriched with groundnut paste, milk or oil; mashed matooke or sweet potato with beans and silverfish powder or egg; dark green leaves; pawpaw, mango or banana; own bowl; clean hands and utensils.

**How long should an HIV-positive mother breastfeed?**
On ART with suppressed viral load: EBF for 6 months, then complementary foods with continued breastfeeding; Uganda MIYCAN 2021 advises stopping at 12 months if the infant is HIV-negative (WHO allows up to 24 months), and continuing to 24 months if the infant is HIV-positive; stop gradually over 1–2 weeks; PCR 6 weeks later.

**What are the BFHI Ten Steps, and which one do Ugandan wards break most?**
Policy, staff competence, antenatal counselling, immediate skin-to-skin and early initiation, support, no unnecessary supplements, rooming-in, responsive feeding, counselling on bottles and teats, discharge support. Common breaches: delayed initiation after CS, pre-lacteal glucose water, and separating mother and baby.

**A breastfed 6-day-old has jaundice and has lost 12% of birth weight. What are you worried about?**
Suboptimal intake (breastfeeding failure) with hypernatraemic dehydration and hyperbilirubinaemia; check sodium and bilirubin, observe a feed, rehydrate, support lactation and supplement with expressed milk (or formula if needed) by cup, and look for sepsis.
